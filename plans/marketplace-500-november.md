## 1. The model: three engines, no money needed

In Myron Golden's terms you start out trading time for money. This month you begin moving toward *owning a system*: a repeatable flow of free inventory and paid jobs.

| Engine | How it works | Cash needed | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Sell what you already own** | Declutter: electronics, tools, furniture, kids' items, clothes in bundles, unused gifts | $0 | **$150** (5–6 items × ~$25–40) |
| **B. Free-to-flip** | Pick up items listed in Marketplace **Free**, Buy Nothing groups and curb alerts. Clean them, photograph them well and relist | $0 (gas only) | **$180** (4 flips × ~$45) |
| **C. Paid service: "Guest-Ready Haul & Help"** | Haul away and clear out before Thanksgiving guests arrive. After Black Friday, assemble furniture and set up holiday décor and lights. Junk you haul away often becomes free inventory for Engine B | $0 | **$200** (2 jobs × ~$100) |
| **Total** | | | **$530** (about 6% cushion) |

Why three engines: this is **inversion** (Mental Models). The fastest way to miss $500 is to depend on one channel that stalls. Engine A brings cash this weekend. Engine B grows over the week. Engine C pays the most per hour.

**Levitt's question:** why should a buyer choose you over the other 200 listings?
- Your items are **clean, tested, honestly described and photographed in daylight**.
- You can **deliver locally** for a small fee.
- You **answer within 10 minutes**.

That is "giving more without charging more."

## 2. Your offers (Hormozi)

### Service offer: the "Guest-Ready Garage & Basement Clear-Out"

| Value Equation part | How the offer covers it |
|---|---|
| **Dream outcome** | Space cleared before family arrives Thursday |
| **Perceived likelihood** | Before-and-after photos from your own home first. Then reviews from job #1 |
| **Time delay** | Done the same or the next day, in 2–3 hours |
| **Effort for the customer** | They point at things, you carry them out, you sort them (donate, dump or haul away) |

- **Price:** $85 for a "car-load" clear-out (about 2 hours), $150 for a half-day. Don't compete on the cheapest price. Sell speed and zero hassle.
- **Guarantee:** "If the space isn't cleared by the time we agreed, you don't pay."
- **Scarcity and urgency:** "Only 4 slots before Thanksgiving: Sun 22, Mon 23, Tue 24, Wed 25."
- **Bonus:** "I'll photograph and list your sellable items for you at no charge. You keep 70%." This is also an Engine B pipeline.
- **MAGIC name:** *"Guest-Ready in 3 Hours: Pre-Thanksgiving Clear-Out."*

### After Black Friday (Nov 27–30): "Assembled Tonight"
Offer flat-pack furniture and toy assembly, plus TV mounting **without** electrical work. Charge $40 per item or $90 for three items, same day.

Marketplace limits service listings. Post the service offer in **local community and Buy/Sell groups and on your personal profile**, and point people there from your listings. This is David Frey's local Facebook referral play (Brick & Mortar Funnels).

## 3. Listings that sell (Bly)

**Title formula** (Bly's ultra-specific headline, 4 U's): `[Brand] [Item] – [Key condition proof] – [Benefit] – [Price hook]`

- ❌ "Table for sale"
- ✅ "Solid Oak Dining Table, Seats 6 – No Wobble, Just Cleaned – Ready for Thanksgiving"

**Body**, using the motivating sequence (attention, need, satisfy, prove, action):
1. One line on the benefit ("Host 6 people this Thursday").
2. Specifications: size, brand, age, any flaws (honesty builds trust).
3. Proof: "tested today", a photo of it working, measurements.
4. Action: "Pickup today 4–8pm near [cross streets], or I deliver within 5 miles for $10. Message 'AVAILABLE'."

**Photos:** 5–8 photos in daylight against a plain wall, with one photo showing scale.

**Pricing rule:** look at **sold** comparable listings, then price about 10% below the cluster for quick cash. Expect buyers to offer 15–20% under your price.

**Timing:** post and renew in the evenings (6–9pm) and on weekend mornings {unverified, common seller practice}.

**Holiday sellers (Nov 21–30):**

- folding tables
- roasting pans and slow cookers
- space heaters (check that they are not recalled)
- Christmas trees and stands, lights
- kids' toys and games
- bikes

## 5. Build the asset, not just the cash (Naval and Lead Funnels)

- **Reputation is your leverage.** Marketplace ratings compound, so treat every buyer as a long-term game.
- **Start a "First Dibs" list:** a text or Messenger list of buyers who want to hear about new items before you post them. At the end of each sale, ask: *"Want first dibs on my next items?"* This is your first lead funnel, and the list is the asset.
- **Specific knowledge:** notice which categories sell in hours. That becomes your niche for December ("you are the niche", Dan Koe).

## 7. Risks and how to handle them (inversion)

- **Scams:**
  - Meet in a public place. Police-station "safe exchange zones" are common {unverified, check for one near you}.
  - Take cash, or a payment you can see has cleared before the item leaves.
  - Never accept overpayment or "send a code to verify" requests.
  - Don't ship local sales.
- **Safety:**
  - Bring someone with you to pickups and service jobs.
  - Tell someone where you are going.
  - Don't do electrical, gas or roof work.
- **Prohibited or unsafe items:**
  - Don't sell recalled products. Check cribs, car seats and space heaters at CPSC.gov.
  - Check Marketplace's commerce policies {unverified, read them before listing}.
- **Records and taxes:**
  - Write down every sale: date, item, price, cost.
  - Payment apps may send tax forms above certain thresholds {unverified, look up the current IRS 1099-K threshold}.
  - Selling your own used items below what you paid for them is usually not taxable income, but flipping for profit is {unverified, not tax advice}.
  - The Legal Research skill can walk you through checking your state's rules.
