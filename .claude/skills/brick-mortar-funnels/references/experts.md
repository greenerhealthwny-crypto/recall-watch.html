# The 15 Expert Playbooks

All points are from the book *(book)* unless marked *(std)*. Numbers are the authors' own examples.

## Dr. Adam Sewell (physicians, med spa): "The 7-Step Framework"

He built and sold a 7-figure clinic and founded Physician In Charge. His 100 days are about 14 weeks, split into **7 two-week blocks**:
1. Define the target patient and validate the market.
2. Build the message and the 3 secrets.
3. Choose the event location and build the presentation.
4. Build the landing page and follow-up sequence.
5. Build the follow-up phone sequence, and hold the event.
6. Book patients at the event and collect testimonials.
7. Follow up with attendees and non-buyers.

- **Market rules:**
  - Never create an offer without proven demand.
  - Being first or alone in a market is risky.
  - **Competition is good**, because it proves people are buying.
  - The market must be buying, large, and easy to reach with ads.
- **Validation:** Google the niche keyword ("thyroid doctor"). Several competitors paying for ads, with dedicated landing pages, means a validated market.
- **Narrow niche:** thyroid patients, not "everyone who needs internal medicine".
- **Education-based marketing**, on a scale from education to entertainment ("edutainment").
- **The 3 secrets:** change the prospect's belief patterns. He shows the *internal* belief ("it won't work for me"; answered with success rates). Brunson's framework also covers *external* beliefs and beliefs about the *vehicle* *(std)*.
- **The event:**
  - Start at 6–7 pm.
  - Book the venue at least 2 weeks out, and market it 7–10 days before.
  - **Serve food first, then present.** If someone eats and leaves, "I consider it charity."
- **Hooks to test** (all sending people to the landing page):
  - "Feeling tired and gaining weight even though you diet and exercise?"
  - "Do you have D.E.R.F. (Diet and Exercise Resistant Fat)?"
- **Landing page:** a video explains the situation, the 3 secrets and the benefits of attending. Keep the follow-up "edutaining".
- **Follow up with everyone.** Prospects research for a long time before they buy.
- "Done > Perfect": doctors are especially prone to perfectionism.

## Alex Hormozi (gyms): "100 Gym Members With Only $1,000 In The Next 100 Days"

Gym Launch had worked with about 2,100 gyms.
- **Client-financed acquisition:** make more at the point of sale than it costs to acquire that customer *and the next*.
  - Example: a $300 cost per sale and $1,100 collected. That's $300 for this customer, $300 for the next, and $500 to fulfill and profit.
- **Sell high-ticket.** Trust is the gym's advantage. A $130-a-month membership is a commodity. "Better to close 50% at 5x the price than 100% at 1x."
- **Service tiers per week:** $149 semi-private plus 1-on-1 accountability, down to $49 accountability only.
  - Pricing example: losing 50 lb takes 25 weeks at 2 lb a week. Double that to 50 weeks, × $99 a week = $4,950.
- **Expected results from 10 prospects:** 5 sales, made up of 1 "whale" ($2,500 up front), 1 above-average ($1,200) and 3 normal ($600), each plus continuity. That's $5,500, or **about $1,100 cash per sale**.
- **The five-sale sequence:**
  1. **Service.**
  2. **Food:** at a nutrition orientation 24–48 hours later, 60–80% buy meal delivery.
  3. **Supplements:** "75%+ of USA residents buy supplements."
  4. **12-month commitment**, for anyone who was downsold.
  5. **Paid in full:** 14 days later, 20–30% take the "Year of Change" pay-in-full discount.
- **Spend per show:** at about a 50% close rate, a show is worth about $550, so he can **spend about $275 per show**.
- **Funnel:** ad, then click, then opt-in, then schedule, then show.
  - **Challenge promotions:** 28-Day Bikini Blueprint, 6-Week Mommy Makeover Challenge, 42-Day Detox.
  - The 3-step funnel: opt in → a scheduling page (consults 3–8 pm weekdays, *including Friday*, and 9 am–noon Saturday) → a free Facebook group.
  - **Contact everyone** who opted in, scheduled or not: confirm and excite them.
- **Rinse and repeat.** Spend $1 to make $3: $1 repays the ads, $1 buys the next customer, $1 goes to the bank. Top gyms simply spend more.

## Andrew Argue (accountants): "100 Clients Using Your 'New Opportunity'"

He has worked with over 7,000 accountants.
- **The problem:** the offer. Business owners dread "tax prep and bookkeeping".
- **The new opportunity:** a **free 45-minute tax assessment** that gives an estimated savings on the first call (prior-year, current-year and future overpayment).
  - Topics: deductions, entity structure, retirement, insurance, loopholes, Tax Cuts and Jobs Act updates.
- **Target business owners** ($250k–$500k+ revenue), not individuals. Use local ads, or niche ads nationwide.
- **The value ladder:** tax plan → business and individual preparation → implementation → accounting, controller and CFO services → wealth management.
  - Example: charging 640% more than "John Smith" accountant still makes sense when the client saves $22,000 in taxes.
- **Case study:**
  - $2,774 in ad spend → 532 clicks → 94 leads at $29.52 each → 52 sessions booked → 26 completed → $19,750 sold, $17,650 collected.
  - Two clients later grew to about $35,700 and $41,750 in first-year fees.
- **The method:**
  1. Serve business owners.
  2. Make tax plans the first offer.
  3. Facebook ads → a ClickFunnels page → book on the calendar → an **education-based sales consultation**.

## Dr. Anissa Holmes (dentists): four phases

1. **Days 1–45: build warm audiences.**
   - **Gift-card giveaway** with local businesses: ask each business for a card, then give away one prize a day for 12 days. Announce on Facebook Live. Entry = share and comment (on Instagram, comment only).
   - A **smile makeover competition** for someone in need.
   - Set up **retargeting audiences:** page and Instagram engagers, website visitors, patients, video viewers.
   - **Train the team on the phone.** Questions like "What is your biggest challenge with your teeth or smile?" through "If you can fit [cost] into your budget, are you ready to start right away?"
2. **Days 46–50:** funnels and email nurture for high-value treatments (implants, Invisalign, people at risk of cavities).
3. **Days 51–80:** a **30-day floss challenge** with weekly prizes, plus video testimonial ads.
4. **Days 81–100:** more traffic, for example holiday clothing drives and more ads, and convert the leads.

## Dr. Chad Woolner (chiropractors): sprint, surge, marathon

He avoids "the new patient roller coaster".
- **Sprint (weeks 1–6): the "Reboot" lunch-workshop funnel ("The 30-Minute Reboot").**
  - Give real value, not a "thinly veiled sales pitch", and build an ongoing conversation instead of "one shot is all you got".
  - Book workshops through existing relationships first: organic social, email, flyers, conversations.
  - Then the **lead box method:** "win a free lunch" boxes in local restaurants.
  - Then the **"welcome guest" approach:** contact businesses weekly, planning on a 90% failure rate.
  - **Case value** = collections ÷ new patients. At 2 new patients per workshop, a workshop is worth at least $2,000 (on a $1,000 case value).
  - The target is about 3 workshops a week × 2 patients = **6 new patients a week**.
- **Surge (weeks 7–12): the "Epic Event" funnel.**
  - A free movie screening for your audience (Russell Brunson attended the first one).
  - Three email invites → RSVP page (name, email, phone, number of guests) → reminders.
  - **"Glue and Offer":** a raffle at the end, with prizes donated by local joint-venture partners, keeps people seated for a limited-time new-patient offer.
  - Result: about 200–300 attendees and 20 new patients.
- **Marathon (weeks 7–14 onward): a "portfolio of proof".**
  - Film at least one success story a week.
  - The **Position Ascension Loop:** positioning → perception → expectation → anticipation → superior outcomes → reputation → positioning.
  - A **proof funnel** sends any traffic source to "proof pages", supported by an ongoing communication system.

## David Frey (any local business): the "local celebrity social media referral machine"

1. **A personal profile for local friends.** It allows up to 5,000 friends, reaches further than a page, and is trusted more. Check Facebook's Terms of Service first.
   - Give it a lead-generating banner (your photo, an irresistible offer, a URL containing "free" and the city name, for example FreeFriendswoodMothersDayDinner.com).
   - Fill in the "Details About You" section and add funnel links.
2. **Friend everyone local you know:** teachers, doctors, neighbors, church and PTA members.
3. **Friend your local Dream 100 influencers.** Comment on their posts for a couple of days before sending a request, then engage with them for the first 30 days.
4. **Join local Facebook groups.**
5. **Build a community:** at least **20 friend requests a day**, tracked in a sheet. Content rules: never political or religious, never disagree, always positive. Mostly engaging posts, with soft promotion sprinkled in.
6. **Fast customers from referrals:**
   - "Refer a friend": ask friends to introduce you.
   - "Share a post": share a success story.
   - "Can I use your profile?"
   - A **"Team 12" referral group** of 12 local professionals.
   - The **"viral deal"**: a deal too good not to share.
- **Bonus: the restaurant fishbowl.** Offer to buy two free meals a week for a restaurant's customers, drawn from a business-card fishbowl, to build a local email list.

## Emily Gang (insurance / Medicare): ten steps

Phase 1 (steps 1–4) is the hardest:

## Joe McCall (real estate investors and agents): "100 leads, fastest way possible"

- **Focus on investor buyers:** they are more educated and don't need driving around. Use **one market and one strategy**, and be the "category king".
- "A good plan violently executed now is better than a perfect plan executed next week" (Patton).
- **Lead magnets:**
  - "How to buy your next investment property in <City>…"
  - "Top 10 Biggest Mistakes Investors Make In <City>"
  - "Top 5 Worst <City> Zip Codes"
  - A cash-flow calculator, a rehab estimator, eviction-rate zip codes.
- **After the download, get the phone number** with an "ethical bribe", for example a texted list of trusted lenders, property managers or private money sources, or "smoking hot deal" alerts.
- **Four strategies:**
  1. **Call existing landlords**, found on Zillow and Craigslist.
  2. **Produce regular local content:** YouTube and podcasts, "Deal of the Day" walkthroughs (get the listing agent's permission), always with an offer.
  3. **Attend local investor groups:** Meetups and REIAs (Real Estate Investment Associations).
  4. **Follow up, follow up, follow up.** 90% or more of business comes from follow-up, which takes 6–7 touches over 3–4 months.

## Joshua Latimer (home services): "Maximize profits with a service-based business"

- **Systems** changed his business: a window-cleaning company reaching over $180k a month.
- **Maximum CAC, for recurring services:** annual revenue × profit margin.
  - Lawn care: $40 × 30 jobs = $1,200 a year, × 25% margin = **$300 maximum CAC**.
- **For one-off jobs:** decide how much of the profit to give away. Roofing: a $15,000 job at 20% margin = $3,000 profit.
- **Value ladder:** "Amateurs focus on the front end but professionals focus on the back end." Start with low-priced **front-end offers**:
  - maid service: a $20 45-minute power clean;
  - carpet cleaning: a free spot clean;
  - window cleaning: 10 exterior windows for $99.
- **Seven steps:**
  1. Choose one irresistible front-end offer, often tied to a holiday such as the 4th of July.
  2. Write the copy: a hook headline, body text, benefits, and urgency or scarcity ("only 10 spots").
  3. Build a simple landing page for **that one offer only.** Your broad website is failing you.
  4. Choose great images, including before-and-after shots.
  5. Repeat for each offer you want to test.
  6. Drive traffic: 3–5 emails to existing clients, Facebook Live and posts, Facebook ads.
  7. Measure and record the data, unemotionally.
- One good client moving up the ladder can be worth about $3,850 a year.

## Mike Arce (fitness studios): Loud Rumor

He has worked with about 1,400–2,000 studios.
- **The story:** make the studio's concept easy for members to explain, so referrals multiply.
- **Know the ideal member:** ad language differs for women and men. Younger audiences explore around and are less loyal.
- **The offer matters most.** A **free offer gets 3× the leads.** The most successful studios "almost exclusively run free" offers. Paid offers only win for studios with weak sales skill.
- **The funnel:**
  1. **Campaigns** by offer: Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Google Display, Waze, plus retargeting.
  2. **1–4 ad sets** by targeting (for example women 25–55 within a set radius).
  3. **At least 2 A/B-tested ads** differing in one variable. Judge after about $50. Copy should include specificity, scarcity, exclusivity, rarity, popularity and urgency. Example: "WOMEN IN SPRINGFIELD!…"
  4. **A ClickFunnels landing page**, not a lead-form ad, so you can retarget. Ask for first and last name, email and phone. Call to action: "Yes, I Want a Free Week Pass!"
  5. **Automations:** pixels build audiences; remarketing changes after opt-in.
  6. **Email and text** that grow more urgent until the person opts out or comes in.
- **Lead costs** (his figures at the time):

  | Platform | Cost per lead |
  |---|---|
  | Facebook | $5–$35 |
  | Instagram | $10–$50 |
  | Instagram Stories | $1–$7 |
  | YouTube | $25–$65 |

  Focus on Facebook and Instagram Stories. Instagram feed ads work best for retargeting. The goal is about 1,000 leads.
- Example minimum lifetime customer value: $1,050.

## Ryan D. Lee & Bradley Gibb (financial advisers): "The Top Grade Method"

- "If you control the rain, you control the game": generate your own leads instead of depending on partners.
- **"The best customer is one you already have"**, and "pick up the pile of money in front of you".
- **The steps:**
  1. **Top-grade the client list** by the value each client has provided.
  2. **Analyze it for segments.** For them, the youngest clients were the least valuable and the pre-retirees the most.
  3. **Ask where that segment wants to go.** Their questions were "How does everything I've accumulated turn into retirement income?"
  4. **Add value first, then sell.** A video series ("1040% more income in retirement"), a membership site, and **live webinars** with Q&A. "Nobody likes to be sold to, but everyone loves to buy."
- **Three things that held back growth:** time wasted educating non-buyers (a low close rate), few referrals, and clients shopping around.
- **The 3 M's:** become the **Master** (find your X factor and build a movement), craft your **Message**, identify your **Market** (start with your existing clients).

## Tyler Shaule (any local business): the Phone Funnel System

- **Local businesses need conversations with qualified prospects.** "Conversations = Conversions."
- **Online now means on a smartphone.** Text messages have about a 98% open rate, and replying is natural.
1. **The reactivation funnel:**
   - Upload past leads and customers (with mobile numbers).
   - "Offer hack" competitors for an appealing offer: a free 30-minute massage with a chiropractic consultation; a no-cost initial legal consultation; a free window cleaning from a siding company.
   - Use SMS follow-up and two-way texting (Twilio integration; conversations.io).
   - **Repackage the offer every 90 days.**
2. **Ad-to-appointment funnels:**
   - Use a proven offer ("not the time to test").
   - Convert with a ClickFunnels page with SMS opt-in, or a lead-form ad.
   - **Survey-to-appointment** suits businesses that need a consultation (loan officers, contractors, law firms, cosmetic surgery). Use 3–5 questions, the first two easy, one or two that qualify. His survey funnel did over $1.3M in 7 months.
3. **Rate, review and referral funnel** (implement this early):
   - An **NPS survey:** "How likely… to recommend (1–10)?" and "How could we achieve a score closer to 10?"
   - Send 9s and 10s to Google reviews; follow up personally with everyone else.
   - Aim for **about 300 five-star reviews.**
   - Then ask for referrals.
- **SMS tips:** a text extension on Google Ads, a "Text Me" button on Instagram, a texting number on sales pages, an outbound "Hi!" call.
- **"Closest to the customer wins"** for businesses that sell in person.
