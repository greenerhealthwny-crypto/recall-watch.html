# Cross-Cutting Tactics, Math, and Templates

All points are from the book *(book)* unless marked *(std)*.

## §1 Acquisition math

| Formula | From | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Max CAC** (recurring) = annual revenue per client × profit margin | Latimer | $1,200 × 25% = $300 |
| **Max CAC** (one-off job) = the share of the job's profit you'll give up | Latimer | A $15,000 roof at 20% margin = $3,000 profit available |
| **Case value** = collections ÷ number of new patients (yearly is better) | Woolner | A $1,000 case × 2 patients per workshop = a $2,000 workshop |
| **Value per show** = cash per sale × close rate. **Spend per show** ≈ half of that | Hormozi | $1,100 × 50% = $550, so spend about $275 per show |
| **Client-financed acquisition:** cash at the point of sale ≥ 2 × CAC + fulfillment | Hormozi | $300 + $300 + $500 = $1,100 |
| **Return target:** spend $1 to make $3 (repay the ads, buy the next customer, bank $1) | Hormozi | |
| **Funnel math:** impressions → clicks → opt-ins → appointments → shows → sales. Work backward from 100 customers | Hormozi, Argue | Argue's case: 532 clicks, 94 leads, 52 booked, 26 completed, 6 sold |
| **Lead costs** (Arce, at the time) | Arce | Facebook $5–35, Instagram $10–50, IG Stories $1–7, YouTube $25–65 |

**Working backward** *(std, using the book's logic)*: 100 customers at a 50% close rate needs 200 shows. At about a 60% show rate, that's about 333 appointments. If a third of leads book, that's about 1,000 leads. This matches Arce's goal of about 1,000 leads. Use your own rates.

## §2 Front-end offer ideas by industry

- **Gyms and studios:**
  - Hormozi: paid challenges such as the 6-Week Mommy Makeover Challenge, 28-Day Bikini Blueprint, 42-Day Detox.
  - Arce: a free week pass.
- **Accountants:** a free 45-minute tax assessment with estimated savings (Argue).
- **Dentists:** contests (gift cards, a smile makeover), a floss challenge, implant and Invisalign funnels (Holmes).
- **Chiropractors and doctors:** a free lunch workshop, a seminar with dinner, a movie-night event (Woolner, A. Sewell).
- **Med spas:** educational video, then an application (J. Sewell).
- **Home services:** a $20 power clean, a free spot clean, 10 windows for $99, holiday promotions (Latimer). Free window cleaning from a siding or roofing company (Shaule).
- **Real estate:** city-specific reports, calculators, an open-house list, "What's my home worth?", a buyer and seller seminar (McCall, Mashore).
- **Retail:** a ticketed class ($47–$97) with a free challenge and a bring-a-friend gift (Cross).
- **Insurance and financial:** a webinar, a value video series, a membership (Gang; Lee and Gibb).
- **Consultation businesses:** a 3–5 question survey that leads to an estimate or appointment (Shaule).
- **Restaurants and local partners:** two free meals a week via a fishbowl draw, which builds your list (Frey).

## §3 Ad copy and landing pages

- **Copy elements:**
  - Latimer: a hook headline, body context, a benefit list, urgency or scarcity.
  - Arce: specificity, scarcity, exclusivity, rarity, popularity, urgency. Call out the audience and city ("WOMEN IN SPRINGFIELD!").
- **Test hooks:** A. Sewell writes four hooks. Arce A/B tests at least 2 ads that differ in one variable, judged after about $50.
- **One offer per page.** A broad website fails (Latimer). Use a ClickFunnels page rather than a lead-form ad if you want to retarget (Arce). Exception: Shaule allows lead-form ads with SMS.
- **Forms:** name, email and phone. Add a scheduling calendar on the page or the next page (Hormozi, Argue).
- **Domains** with the city and "free" in them (Frey).

## §4 Follow-up and conversion

- **Contact every lead,** booked or not: confirm and excite them (Hormozi).
- **Phone scripts that qualify and ask** (Holmes): biggest challenge → biggest benefit → imagine the result → "If you can fit [cost] into your budget, are you ready to start right away?"
- **Get a phone number after the opt-in** with an ethical bribe (McCall).
- **Texting:** about a 98% open rate; two-way SMS; increasingly urgent texts and emails (Shaule, Arce). *Get TCPA consent first (std).*
- **6–7 touches over 3–4 months.** 90% or more of the business comes from follow-up (McCall).
- **Retarget** opt-ins and engagers with testimonial videos (J. Sewell, Holmes).
- **Upsell in sequence after the first sale:** service → food → supplements → 12-month commitment → pay in full (Hormozi). Tax plan → preparation → CFO services (Argue).

## §5 Events, workshops and seminars

- **Seminar:** start at 6–7 pm, book the venue 2+ weeks ahead, market 7–10 days out, **serve food first**, keep it educational and entertaining, record it, follow up with everyone, collect testimonials (A. Sewell, Mashore).
- **Lunch workshop:** lead with real value rather than a pitch, and build an ongoing relationship. Book through existing contacts, restaurant lead boxes and weekly business outreach (Woolner).
- **Big event:** 3 email invites, RSVP, reminders, "glue and offer" (a raffle with donated prizes keeps people seated for a limited-time offer) (Woolner).
- **Ticketed retail class** with an in-store experience (Cross).
- **Live webinar** with the Perfect Webinar script and Q&A (Gang; Lee and Gibb).

## §6 Warm audiences and referrals

- **Giveaways with local businesses:** one prize a day, Facebook Live, share and comment to enter, which builds retargeting audiences (Holmes). *Follow platform promotion rules and contest law (std).*
- **Local Facebook networking:** a personal profile, the Dream 100, local groups, 20 friend requests a day, positive content only (Frey).
- **Referral methods:** refer-a-friend, share a post, use a friend's profile, a "Team 12" group, a viral deal (Frey). Bring-a-friend bonuses (Cross).
- **Existing clients first:** reactivation (Shaule), top-grading and segmenting (Lee and Gibb), emailing past clients (Latimer).
- **Reviews:** an NPS survey, then reviews, then referrals; aim for about 300 five-star reviews (Shaule).
  - *Caution (std, verify):* Google's policies prohibit "review gating", that is, asking only satisfied customers for reviews. Send the review request to everyone, and use the NPS answers for service follow-up.
- **Proof portfolio:** film one success story a week and use it on proof pages (Woolner).

## §7 A 100-day calendar template (a blend of the experts)

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Niche and avatar, market validation, offer, goals (Mashore day 1), testimonials. **Reactivate the existing list** and use referrals (Shaule, Frey, Lee and Gibb) |
| 3–4 | Landing page or funnel, video content, local groups, the first workshop or seminar. Warm-audience giveaway (Holmes) |
| 5–6 | Paid ads within your maximum CAC, A/B tests, an SMS and email follow-up machine, phone conversion |
| 7–10 | Scale what works. Hold a big event (Woolner) or a webinar (Gang). Add a second niche or offer |
| 11–14 | Portfolio of proof, review and referral funnel, membership or value content for retention. Repeat |

**Daily habits:** a morning routine and 6 must-dos (Mashore, J. Sewell); 20 local friend requests and engagement (Frey); call and text all new leads the same day (Mashore, Hormozi).
