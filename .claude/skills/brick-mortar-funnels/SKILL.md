# Brick & Mortar Funnels (ClickFunnels, about 2020)

A working guide distilled from the ClickFunnels compilation *Brick & Mortar Funnels: If You Had To Get 100 Customers In The Next 100 Days… What Would You Do To Keep Your Doors Open… Even In Times Of Uncertainty?* *(book)*.

The format: Russell Brunson's challenge to 15 local-business experts is to get **100 new customers in 100 days** *(book)*. Each chapter is one expert's plan for their industry.

**Source:** the user's PDF has an embedded text layer (183 pages). The PDF is dated 2021. The book cites 2017–2019 figures, so platform features, ad costs and prices will have changed *(std)*.

## The experts

| Expert | Industry | Core play |
|---|---|---|
| Dr. Adam Sewell | Medical / med spa | **A 7-step, 14-week plan** in two-week blocks. Pick a narrow patient type (thyroid), check the market by looking at paid ads, write a message using Brunson's "3 secrets", run an **education-based seminar** with food served first, follow up with every attendee |
| Alex Hormozi | Gyms | **Client-financed acquisition**: cash at the point of sale pays for that customer *and the next*. High-ticket challenges, a five-sale sequence, about $1,100 cash per sale. Spend $1 to make $3 |

| Dr. Chad Woolner | Chiropractors | **Sprint, surge, marathon.** A "Reboot" lunch-workshop funnel (about 3 a week, 2 patients each), then an **"Epic Event"** movie night, then a **proof funnel** of case studies (the "Position Ascension Loop") |
| David Frey | Any local business | A **"local celebrity" Facebook referral machine.** A lead-generating personal profile, friend your local **Dream 100**, local groups, 20 friend requests a day, positive content, five referral methods, and a restaurant fishbowl list |

## Principles they share

1. **A narrow target and a specific message beat a broad one.** Examples: thyroid patients; investor buyers in one city; women 25–55 near the studio *(book)*.
2. **Lead with an offer the market wants, not your commodity service.** Examples: a free tax assessment instead of tax prep; a 6-week challenge instead of a $130-a-month membership; a $99 ten-window clean *(book)*.
3. **Educate before you sell.** Use seminars, workshops, videos, webinars and value posts *(book)*.
4. **One offer → one landing page → book a call or appointment → follow up hard** by phone, text and email. Most sales come from follow-up *(book)*.
5. **Know the economics:**
   - your maximum CAC (Latimer);
   - what you can spend per person who shows up (Hormozi);
   - case value (Woolner).

   Then spend more on whatever works. "He who can spend the most to acquire a customer wins" (Dan Kennedy, quoted by Hormozi) *(book)*. Shaule disagrees for businesses that close in person: "the closest to the customer wins".
6. **Warm the audience first:** giveaways, local partners, groups, content, retargeting. Use **your existing list first** for reactivation, top-grading and email *(book)*.
7. **Proof compounds:** collect testimonials, case studies and reviews (Shaule: aim for about 300 five-star reviews) *(book)*.
8. **Done beats perfect.** Test, measure, repeat *(book)*.

**Workflow for building a 100-day plan:**
1. Identify the business, its average customer value, profit margin and close rate. Calculate the maximum CAC.
2. Pick the closest expert or experts and their main funnel (event or workshop, webinar, free offer, assessment, application, front-end offer, reactivation).
3. Define a narrow avatar, an irresistible front-end offer and 3–4 hooks.
4. Lay out a week-by-week (or two-week-block) calendar:
   - weeks 1–2: existing list and warm audience (reactivation, referrals, groups);
   - weeks 3–6: funnel and paid traffic;
   - weeks 7–14: scale, a big event, a portfolio of proof, reviews and referrals.
5. Set targets: leads, appointments, shows, closes, and the 100-customer total, worked backward.

## Caveats

- **Results are the experts' own claims and examples,** not guarantees *(book)*. Ad costs have risen since the book *(std, verify)*.
- **Comply with:**
  - **texting and calling law** (TCPA: get prior express written consent for marketing texts) and email law (CAN-SPAM) *(std)*;
  - **platform rules:** Frey himself warns to check Facebook's Terms of Service before creating a second personal profile, and contest and giveaway rules apply *(book; std)*;
  - **health-advertising and patient-privacy rules** (HIPAA, state boards, FTC testimonial rules) *(std)*;
  - financial and insurance marketing rules (Medicare marketing rules, SEC/FINRA) *(std)*.
- **Never invent testimonials or review gating** that breaks platform rules. Google discourages selectively asking only happy customers for reviews *(std, verify)*.
