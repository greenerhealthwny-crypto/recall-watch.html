# From The Trash Man To The Cash Man (Myron Golden, 2007)

A working guide distilled from Myron Golden's *From The Trash Man To The Cash Man: How Anyone Can Get Rich Starting From Anywhere* (Skillionaire Enterprises, 2007) *(book)*. Golden, a former trash collector, dedicates the book to his parents and credits his mentor Jerry Clark *(book)*.

**Source:** OCR text of a scanned copy (Internet Archive digitization, 164 pages). Some words were garbled; uncertain details are flagged.

**Chapters** *(book)*: 1 What are your chances of becoming rich? · 2 The difference between the rich and the poor · 3 The Window of Opportunity · 4 What kind of house do you live in? · 5 The Hands-Free Money business plan · 6 All businesses are not created equal · 7 Leverage your business through technology · 8 It's better to be a V.M. than a V.P. · 9 Know what business you're really in · 10 Find a financial guide who has already found their fortune · 11 You have no competition · 12 Since you can't beat 'em, join 'em · 13 You can if your cans say you can · 14 Eleven mental money traps · 15 Thirteen practical money traps · 16 How to get started on the road to riches · resource directory.

## Key ideas *(book)*

1. **The odds:** of 100 people working from age 20 to 65, **1 becomes rich, 4 financially independent, 5 are still working, 28 are dead, 62 are dead broke** (insurance-industry figures from Social Security data Golden used in the 1980s). So about 5% make it.
2. **Rich vs poor thinking:** the rich understand how money works; think "both/and" not "either/or"; focus on opportunity, not obstacles; and choose advisors who are where they want to be.
3. **The Window of Opportunity: four ways to make money**
   - **H.A.J. = Have A Job** and **O.A.J. = Own A Job** (self-employment): the two worst ways, because income stops when you stop.
   - **S.M.S. = Sow Money Seeds** (investing): the second best way.
   - **O.A.S. = Own A System** (a business that runs without you): the best way.
4. **The financial house** has four windows: **Income**, **Outgo**, **WRL (Wealth Reducing Liabilities)**, **IPA (Income Producing Assets)**.
   - **Poor:** every dime of income goes straight out the Outgo window.
   - **Middle class:** buy wealth reducing liabilities and live in debt to keep up with the Joneses.
   - **Rich:** buy income producing assets (for example a rental building), whose cash flow eventually covers expenses.
   - The difference is not how much you make but what you do with money after you make it.
5. **Hands-Free Money business plan:** build a home business producing **$10,000 a month within two years** that needs no more than "one finger's worth of effort" to run; then start another, different business to do the same, and repeat. (Golden draws it from 1 Thessalonians 4 and Ecclesiastes 5:3.)
6. **Product over service:** a service business sells your time; a product business can sell 1 or 100,000 units in the same time.
7. **Leverage technology:** use the internet to market, research and sell.
8. **V.M. (Virtual Millionaire) vs V.P.:** three kinds of millionaire: **cash (liquid)**, **asset**, and **virtual**, someone whose income gives a million-dollar lifestyle (for example, business income equal to what $1 million at 6% would pay) without a million in the bank. Virtual millionaires have cash flow, which many asset millionaires lack.
9. **You are in the marketing business:** a plumber is not in plumbing, an author not in writing; everyone is in the business of marketing what they do.
10. **Find a financial guide** who has already found their fortune: take advice from the rich, not the broke.
11. **You have no competition:** reject the "economic pie" model; wealth is created, so promote friends in your field.
12. **Since you can't beat 'em, join 'em:** don't hate the rich ("be a participator, a celebrator"); you won't become what you despise.

## The six cans (chapter 13) *(book)*
Buy six paint cans and divide every paycheck:
1. **"I can tithe"**: 10% of gross to God's work through the church (or charity, if that is your belief).
2. **"I can finish free"**: 10%, never spent; used only to buy income producing assets or businesses; you may spend their profits.
3. **"I can educate myself"**: 10% for seminars, books, audio programs.
4. **"I can save for what I want"**: for vacations, down payments, furniture, emergencies.
5. **"I can have fun"**: must be spent every month on something you enjoy.
6. **"I can pay my bills"**: **50%** for living expenses.
*(The text checked states the percentages for cans 1–3 and 6; cans 4 and 5 share the remaining 20%, likely 10% each.)*

## Money traps *(book)*
**Eleven mental money traps** ("poor people…"): 1 mistake negativism for skepticism · 2 are too myopic about money · 3 don't desire or like money · 4 value consumption more than production · 5 don't trust or like people with money · 6 are technophobic · 7 value security more than freedom · 8 value formal education over self-education · 9 have no financial education · 10 don't understand and use the power of principles · 11 have a renter's mentality vs an owner's mentality.

**Practical money traps** (titled "thirteen"): 1 don't protect their minds · 2 don't invest in their greatest asset (their mind) · 3 have no financial assets, only wealth-reducing liabilities · 4 entertain themselves more than educate themselves · 5 are paralyzed by a paycheck · 6 do everything themselves (don't outsource) · 7 don't have good money management practices · 8 operate on a cash basis · 9 don't have a financial mentor or coach · 10 don't set a good financial example for their children · 11 don't teach their children how to become financially free · 13 don't use good debt; bad debt uses them. *(The copy checked jumps from #11 to #13; no #12 was found.)*

## Getting started (chapter 16) *(book)*
Invest in self-education; study what rich and poor people do; stop trading time for money; follow the advice of the rich; find a business that fits the hands-free plan; avoid the money traps.

## How to use this skill
- Map the user's income to the four window panes and push toward Own A System and Sow Money Seeds.
- Draw their financial house: what goes to outgo, liabilities and income producing assets.
- Set up the six cans for their income.
- Diagnose mindset with the trap lists.

## Caveats
- The book promotes the author's businesses, network marketing and programs, and its income claims carry a disclaimer that results vary *(book)*. Encourage due diligence on any business opportunity *(std)*.
- Its tithing and faith framing is the author's personal belief; adapt respectfully *(book)*.
- The 100-people statistic is old insurance-industry data; treat it as illustrative *(std)*.
- Not individualized financial, tax or investment advice *(std)*.
