# The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (Eric Jorgenson)

A working guide distilled from *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant: A Guide to Wealth and Happiness*, compiled and edited by **Eric Jorgenson** from Naval's tweets, tweetstorms, podcasts and interviews, with a foreword by **Tim Ferriss** *(book)*. Jorgenson notes the material was transcribed, edited and rearranged, and that bolded questions come from interviewers *(book)*.

**Source:** the user's PDF has a text layer (242 pages). Points marked *(book)* were checked in it.

**Structure** *(book)*: Foreword (Tim Ferriss) · Timeline · **Part I Wealth**: *Building Wealth* (understand how wealth is created; find and build specific knowledge; play long-term games with long-term people; take on accountability; build or buy equity; find a position of leverage; get paid for your judgment; prioritize and focus; find work that feels like play; how to get lucky; be patient) and *Building Judgment* (judgment; how to think clearly; shed your identity to see reality; learn the skills of decision-making; collect mental models; learn to love to read) · **Part II Happiness**: *Learning Happiness* (happiness is learned, a choice, requires presence and peace; every desire is a chosen unhappiness; success does not earn happiness; envy is the enemy; built by habits; find it in acceptance), *Saving Yourself* (choosing to be, care for, build, grow and free yourself), *Philosophy* (the meanings of life; live by your values; rational Buddhism; the present is all we have) · recommended reading · Naval's writing.

## Wealth: the core ideas *(book)*

- **"Seek wealth, not money or status."** Wealth is assets that earn while you sleep; money is how we transfer time and wealth; status is your place in the social hierarchy. Ignore people playing status games; they attack people playing wealth-creation games.
- **"You're not going to get rich renting out your time."** You must own **equity**, a piece of a business.
- **"Arm yourself with specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage."**
- **Specific knowledge** is "knowledge you cannot be trained for"; it is found by pursuing your genuine curiosity and passion rather than whatever is hot. Figuring out what you can uniquely provide may take the better part of a decade.
- **Long-term games:** "Pick an industry where you can play long-term games with long-term people." "Play iterated games. All the returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest."
- **Accountability:** take business risks under your own name. "Society will pay you for creating things it wants" that it doesn't yet know how to create.
- **Leverage:** "Fortunes require leverage. Business leverage comes from capital, people, and products with no marginal cost of replication (code and media)." "Code and media are permissionless leverage", the leverage behind the newly rich.
- **Judgment:** with leverage, judgment matters most; be paid for your judgment, not your hours. "Earn with your mind, not your time."
- **Productize yourself:** "Yourself" brings uniqueness, accountability and specific knowledge; "productize" brings leverage. Ask: Is this authentic to me? Am I scaling it with labor, capital, code or media?
- **Escape competition through authenticity:** competition comes from copying; no one can compete with you on being you.
- **Learn to sell. Learn to build.** "If you can do both, you will be unstoppable."
- **Hourly rate:** set an aspirational hourly rate and spend ruthlessly to save time at that rate; "You will never be worth more than you think you're worth."
- **Luck, four kinds:** (1) blind luck; (2) luck from hustle and energy, for luck to find you; (3) being "good at spotting luck": skilled enough in a field to notice a lucky break others miss; (4) luck that finds you because of your unique character, brand and mindset; the goal is the fourth.
- **Patience:** "Impatience with actions, patience with results." "Inspiration is perishable": act on it immediately.

## Judgment and decision-making *(book)*
- Clear thinking requires shedding your identity and ego to see reality.
- "If you can't decide, the answer is no."
- Collect mental models; the book discusses compound interest, game theory and the principal-agent problem among others *(book)*.
- **"Read what you love until you love to read."** Reading is the foundation of learning.
- The three big early-life decisions: **where you live, who you're with, and what you do**.

## Happiness *(book)*
- "Happiness is a choice you make and a skill you develop." It can be learned, like fitness.
- Happiness is the state when nothing is missing; peace is happiness at rest.
- **"Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want."** Pick desires carefully, one at a time.
- Success does not earn happiness; envy is its enemy.
- Happiness is built by habits (meditation features heavily, along with exercise and less screen time).
- "Retirement is when you stop sacrificing today for an imaginary tomorrow."
- A good life, in Naval's words: "A calm mind, a fit body, and a house full of love."
- The three big things in life: wealth, health and happiness.

## How to use this skill
- **Career or wealth questions:** diagnose specific knowledge (what feels like play to you but work to others?), the leverage type available (labor, capital, code, media), path to equity, and the long-term game; suggest productizing yourself.
- **Decisions:** apply "if you can't decide, the answer is no", long-term people, and identity-free thinking.
- **Happiness:** reframe desire as chosen unhappiness, build habits (meditation, exercise, reading), practice presence and acceptance.
- Quote short lines and attribute them to Naval, noting the book is a compilation.

## Caveats
- The book is free-form and compiled from many sources; wording was edited by Jorgenson *(book)*.
- Not financial advice; Naval's path (startups, angel investing) is unusual *(std)*.
