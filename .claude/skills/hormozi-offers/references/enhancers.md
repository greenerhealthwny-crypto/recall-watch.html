# Enhancing the Offer: Scarcity, Urgency, Bonuses, Guarantees, Naming (Section IV)

All points are from the book *(book)* unless marked *(std)*.

**Why enhance at all:** supply and demand are a "delicate dance of desire". Satisfy no demand and you make nothing; satisfy all of it and you "kill your golden goose". The five enhancers each work on demand differently:
1. **Scarcity** decreases supply, which raises price and adds exclusivity.
2. **Urgency** increases demand by lowering the action threshold.
3. **Bonuses** increase demand and perceived exclusivity.
4. **Guarantees** increase demand by reversing risk.
5. **Names** re-stimulate demand and expand awareness among the target audience.

## §1 Scarcity (FOMO from limited supply)

**Three types:**
1. A limited supply of seats or slots, in general or per period.
2. A limited supply of bonuses.
3. Never available again.

**How to use them:**
- **Physical products:** limited releases of flavors, colors or designs. **Always sell out**, since it's better to sell out consistently than to overorder. About once a month is the sweet spot.
- **Services:** cap the clients per period, per coach or per cohort. Tell prospects your real limits "and let psychology do the rest".
- **Pro tip, extreme scarcity:** sell a very limited, very high-priced tier of one-to-one access to you, through a medium you don't hate. "Once you're out, you can never come back" works for small groups.

## §2 Urgency (limits on *when*, not *how many*)

**Four ethical methods:**
1. **Cohort-based rolling urgency.** Start clients on a regular cadence: "If you sign up today, I can get you in with our next group that kicks off on Monday." The less often you start, the stronger the effect.
2. **Rolling seasonal urgency**, tied to seasons or holidays.
3. **Promotional or pricing urgency:** a discount or bonus that expires.
4. **Exploding opportunity:** an offer that disappears after a set time.

The fear of losing sales is unfounded. In a launch, the last 4 hours of the last day bring up to 50–60% of sales. Real deadlines make more money.

## §3 Bonuses ("but wait… there's more")

**Core idea:** one offer is worth less than the same offer broken into components and stacked as bonuses. The rules:
1. Always offer them, drawn from your trimmed and stacked bundle.
2. Give each a special name with a benefit in the title.
3. For each, tell them: how it relates to their issue; what it is; how you discovered or created it; and how it makes things faster, easier or less effortful.
4. Provide proof: a stat, a past client or personal experience.
5. Paint a vivid picture of life after using it.
6. Always give it a price tag, and justify the price.
7. **Tools and checklists beat more training**, because they cost the buyer less effort and time, so they're worth more.
8. Each bonus should answer a specific doubt about why they can't succeed.
9. Solve their *next* problem before they meet it.
10. **The total bonus value should eclipse the core offer.**
11. Add scarcity or urgency to the bonuses themselves.

**Advanced:** get other businesses to provide bonuses (tools or services) at no cost to you, sometimes for an affiliate commission. Hormozi credits Jason Fladlien's influence.

## §4 Guarantees (reversing risk)

**The formula:** "If you do not get X result in Y time period, we will Z." Without the "or what", a guarantee sounds weak.
- Weak: "We will get you 20 clients guaranteed."
- Strong: "You will get 20 clients in your first 30 days, or we give you your money back plus your advertising dollars spent with us."

**Four types:**

| Type | What it is | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Unconditional** | A no-questions-asked refund, essentially a paid trial. The strongest | Low fulfillment cost, confident results |
| **Conditional** | Has terms. Best as "better than money back" (double or triple your money, or service until the result) tied to the **key actions** that produce success | High-cost services. It drives the customer behavior that gets results |
| **Anti-guarantee** | "All sales are final", with a credible reason why (you expose proprietary material or do heavy customization) | Consumables, info that loses value once given, custom high-ticket work |
| **Implied (performance)** | Revenue share, profit share, pay per result, triggers, ratchets | Only where outcomes can be measured transparently and you have trust or control |

- **The math:** 100 sales with 5% refunds is 95 net. A guarantee that brings 130 sales with 10% refunds is 117 net, **23% more**. "Don't be emotional, just do the math."
- **Warning:** buyers who buy *only* for a guarantee can be poor customers. Tie conditions to the work they need to do.
- **Stacking:** for example, an unconditional 30-day guarantee plus a conditional 90-day "triple your money" guarantee.
- **Always "hit your guarantee hard, even if you don't have one."** State it boldly, with a reason why.
- **Caution** *(std)*: guarantees and refund terms must be honored and must comply with consumer-protection law in your jurisdiction.

## §5 Naming: the M-A-G-I-C formula

A good name attracts the right avatar and repels bad ones. Good naming can bring "2x, 3x, or 10x the response rate". Use three to five of these components, in any order that sounds punchy:

| Letter | Component | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | **Make a magnetic reason why** | Why the offer exists now, for example "Grand Opening", "Free", "Summer", "New Year" (attention) |
| **A** | **Announce your avatar** | Who it's for, and who it isn't (for example, "for busy moms") |
| **G** | **Give them a goal** | The specific dream outcome (purpose) |
| **I** | **Indicate a time interval** | "in 6 weeks", "in 10 days". Avoid it where compliance forbids outcome-plus-time claims; use an action goal instead (for example, "Make Your First Sale in 10 Days") |
| **C** | **Complete with a container word** | Signals a bundled system: challenge, blueprint, bootcamp, program, accelerator, intensive, masterclass, system |

- Rhyme or alliteration helps.
- Renaming an old offer re-stimulates demand.
- Use the same headline formula to name each bonus in your stack.

## §6 Execution: your first $100,000

- Hormozi's emotional milestone was $101,018 in personal savings (March 2017). It meant "relief", security for three years, and was the richest he ever felt.
- **The "In a nutshell" recap:**
  1. Don't be a commodity.
  2. Pick a normal or growing market, and niche down.
  3. Charge a lot.
  4. Use the four value drivers.
  5. Build the offer in five steps.
  6. Stack it, deliver it and make it profitable.
  7. Add scarcity.
  8. Add urgency.
  9. Add bonuses.
  10. Reverse risk with a guarantee.
  11. Name it for your avatar.
