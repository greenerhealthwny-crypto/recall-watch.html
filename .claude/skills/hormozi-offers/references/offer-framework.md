# Market, Pricing, the Value Equation, and the Five-Step Offer Build (Sections I–III)

All points are from the book's text *(book)* unless marked *(std)*.

## §1 The commodity problem and the market

- **A commodity** is "a product available from many places", so buyers choose on price. Commodities are priced at market efficiency, leaving owners just enough margin to "become a slave to their business". A Grand Slam Offer makes the same fulfillment look like a different product.
- **Demand is channeled, not created.** A "normal" market is growing with the marketplace and has unmet needs in **health, wealth or relationships**. Avoid dying markets: his example is "Lloyd" selling to newspapers.
- **The four indicators of a starving crowd:**

| Indicator | Meaning | Hormozi's example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Massive pain** | They "desperately need", not merely want, the offer. Price is proportional to pain. "The pain is the pitch." | Back pain, bad marriage, being broke |
| 2. **Purchasing power** | They can afford it | A resume service for the unemployed failed because the customers had no money |
| 3. **Easy to target** | They gather somewhere you can reach: associations, mailing lists, groups, channels | Rich doctors are easy to find, but don't show the ads to nursing students |
| 4. **Growing** | Growth is a tailwind; decline is a headwind | Newspapers had three of the four and were shrinking |

- **Order of importance: Starving Crowd > Offer Strength > Persuasion Skills.**
  - A great market saves a bad offer: hot dogs at 2 a.m. outside bars.
  - In a normal market, a Grand Slam Offer wins even with weak persuasion. That is most readers.
  - In a normal market with a normal offer, you need exceptional persuasion.
- **Commit to the niche.** "Riches are in the niches." You can charge up to 100× more for the same product to a specific avatar. Below about $10M a year, niching down almost always pays.
  - Hormozi's avatar was a microgym owner with about 100 members, a signed lease and at least one employee, who wanted to help clients lose weight.
  - He turned away everyone else.
- **Resilience:** try many offers. Failing once is normal.

## §2 Pricing: charge what it's worth

- "Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile" (Dan Kennedy, quoted).
- **Price-to-value discrepancy:** people buy when perceived value far exceeds price. Widen the gap by raising value, not by cutting price.
- **The virtuous cycle of price:** a higher price brings more buyer commitment and better results. It also means more margin for better service and better acquisition, which means more value, and so more justification for a high price. Low prices bring the reverse.
- **His experience:** Gym Launch charged the most in its industry and gave the most value. Clients still "got a deal" because the price was a fraction of what they earned.

## §3 The Value Equation

**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Perceived Time Delay × Perceived Effort & Sacrifice)**

| Driver | Goal | Buyer's question | Levers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | ↑ | "What will I make?" | Bigger, status-raising result |
| Perceived likelihood | ↑ | "How will I know it's going to happen?" | Proof, testimonials, guarantees, third-party edification |
| Time delay | ↓ | "How long will it take?" | Fast wins, quick early results ("fast beats free") |
| Effort and sacrifice | ↓ | "What is expected of me?" | Done-for-you, tools, simplicity |

- **"Get the bottom to zero."** Bigger claims are "easy, and it's lazy". The durable advantage is reducing time and effort.
- **Logical versus psychological solutions:** the logical fix for slow elevators is faster elevators; the psychological fix is mirrors. Perception is reality.
- People pay for speed and ease (make-up, supplements, shapewear, surgery) far more than for the "right" slow path.

## §4 Creating the Grand Slam Offer (Ch 8–10)

**The thought process:**
- Think divergently, as in the brick exercise: list many uses for a brick (paperweight, doorstop, weight for exercise, wedge…).
- Then go through the customer's whole journey and find every obstacle **before, during and after** they buy.

**Step 1: Identify the dream outcome.** What they really want, in their language, including the status it brings.

**Step 2: List problems.** For each thing they must do to reach the outcome (for weight loss: buy, cook and eat healthy food, and exercise), list every obstacle. Score each against the four value drivers:
- It isn't worth it (outcome).
- It won't work for me (likelihood).
- It's too hard (effort).
- It takes too long (time).

**Step 3: Solutions list.** Turn each problem into a solution, typically by writing "how to" and reversing the problem. Name each solution.

**Step 4: Create solution delivery vehicles ("the how").** This is the most important step: brainstorm everything you could do. **The cheat codes:**
1. Level of personal attention: one-on-one, small group, one-to-many.
2. Effort expected from them: DIY, DWY (done with you) or DFY (done for you).
3. If live, the medium: in person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat.
4. If recorded, the format: audio, video or written.
5. Response speed and hours: 24/7, 9–5, within 5 minutes, within an hour, within 24 hours.
6. The **10x to one-tenth test:** what would you provide if they paid 10× (or $100,000)? How would you still make them successful at one-tenth of the price?

His worked example, for "buying healthy food is hard":
- one-on-one store tours;
- personalized lists;
- full-service shopping;
- text or phone support while shopping;
- group tours;
- a recorded tour;
- predetermined lists;
- a grocery buddy system;
- prebuilt Instacart carts.

**Solve every perceived problem.** He lost sales by refusing to help clients who ate out, rather than solving that problem.

**Step 5: Trim and stack.**
- Remove items that are high cost and low value, then items that are low cost and low value.
- Keep items that are **low cost and high value**, and **high cost and high value**.
- Judge value by the four drivers: financial value, belief they'll succeed, less effort, less time.
- Bundle the survivors into "the ultimate high value deliverable".
- **The sales-to-fulfillment continuum:** the easier something is to sell, the harder it usually is to fulfill, and vice versa. Aim for a sweet spot: easy to sell and easy enough to fulfill. His mantra: "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction." Over-deliver first to get demand and cash flowing, then trim what you offer for the same price.

**Outcome:** a differentiated offer that prospects judge on value, not price.
