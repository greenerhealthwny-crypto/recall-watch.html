# $100M Offers (Alex Hormozi, 2021)

A working guide distilled from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*, Acquisition.com Volume I (Acquisition.com LLC, 2021) *(book)*. Hormozi built and sold gym-turnaround and licensing businesses (Gym Launch). The book is about making "an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no" *(book)*.

## The core framework

1. **Grand Slam Offer:** "an offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available". It combines an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price and an unbeatable guarantee, with a money model (payment terms) that lets you get paid to acquire customers *(book)*.
   - Commoditized means price-driven purchases, "a race to the bottom".
   - Differentiated means value-driven purchases: you sell in a **category of one**, "in a vacuum".
   - The result is higher response rates, higher conversion and premium prices. The fulfillment can be identical; only the offer changes *(book)*.
2. **There are only three ways to grow:** get more customers, get them to pay more, and get them to buy more times *(book)*.
3. **Starving Crowd (market) > Offer Strength > Persuasion Skills.** A "great" rating higher in that order overpowers weaknesses lower down. A bad market stops everything *(book)*.
4. **Charge a premium.** "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow." Premium prices fund better delivery and better results, which justify the price: **the virtuous cycle of price**. "Profit is oxygen" *(book)*.
5. **The Value Equation** *(book)*:

   > **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Perceived Time Delay × Perceived Effort & Sacrifice)**

   Increase the top, and decrease the bottom toward zero. Beginners chase bigger claims on the top; the best gains are on the bottom, making results faster and easier. "Perception is reality."
6. **Build the offer in five steps:**
   1. Identify the dream outcome.
   2. List every problem in the way.
   3. Turn each problem into a named solution.
   4. Create delivery vehicles.
   5. Trim and stack.

   "You will solve every problem" *(book)*.
7. **Enhance the offer** with scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees and naming. These shift the demand curve without changing the core product *(book)*.
8. **Persistence:** "If you try one hundred offers, I promise you will succeed… If your offer doesn't work, it doesn't mean you suck. It means your offer sucks" *(book)*.

**Workflow for building an offer with a user:**
1. Check the market against the four indicators. Pick a narrow avatar.
2. Write the dream outcome.
3. List the problems before, during and after the purchase. Score each against the four Value Equation drivers.
4. Convert each problem into a "how to…" solution, then brainstorm delivery vehicles across the delivery cube:
   - one-to-one, small group or one-to-many;
   - DIY, DWY or DFY (do it yourself, done with you, done for you);
   - medium, response time;
   - the 10x / one-tenth price test.
5. Trim to high-value items, both low-cost and high-cost. Stack them into a bundle.
6. Add scarcity and urgency, bonuses (with names and price tags), a guarantee (using "if you don't get X in Y time, we'll Z"), and a M-A-G-I-C name.
7. Present a price well below the stacked value.

## Caveats

- Hormozi's tactics should be applied **ethically**. He insists scarcity and urgency be real ("without being phony"). Never invent fake deadlines or fake limits, and keep guarantee claims truthful and legally compliant, for example consumer-protection and FTC rules *(std)*.
- The numbers are illustrative. His own disclaimer says "the numbers in this book are theoretical" *(book)*.
- Quote short phrases only. The book contains profanity; keep outputs professional unless the user prefers otherwise.
