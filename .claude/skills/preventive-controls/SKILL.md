# FSPCA Preventive Controls for Human Food (PCQI course)

A working guide distilled from the **Food Safety Preventive Controls Alliance (FSPCA)** *Preventive Controls for Human Food* Participant Manual, 1st edition (2016). This is the standardized curriculum FDA recognizes for training a **Preventive Controls Qualified Individual (PCQI)** under FSMA's rule *Current Good Manufacturing Practice, Hazard Analysis, and Risk-Based Preventive Controls for Human Food* (21 CFR Part 117).

The course follows a hypothetical **E.G. Food Company** making frozen omelets, and builds its Food Safety Plan step by step. The manual has 16 chapters and 6 appendices:

1. Introduction and the PCQI role
2. Food Safety Plan overview
3. GMPs and other prerequisite programs
4. Biological hazards
5. Chemical, physical and economically motivated hazards
6. Preliminary steps
7. Resources
8. Hazard analysis and preventive controls determination
9. Process controls
10. Allergen controls
11. Sanitation controls
12. Supply-chain controls
13. Verification and validation
14. Record-keeping
15. Recall plan
16. Regulation overview

Appendices: the regulation text, worksheets, the full omelet example plan, pathogen data, sanitation basics, and hygienic zoning and environmental monitoring.

**Source coverage:** Built from the user's photographed copy (288 spreads), which was OCR'd. The printed manual OCR'd well, about 138k words. Figures marked *(manual)* were checked against that text.

**Currency caution:** the manual reflects the 2016 rule. Since then, **sesame** became the 9th major food allergen (FASTER Act, labeling required from 1 January 2023) *(std)*. FDA has also issued further guidance, e.g. the hazard analysis draft guidance chapters and the FSMA 204 traceability rule. Check current FDA guidance before relying on details for compliance.

## The core logic of a Food Safety Plan

1. **Prerequisite programs first.** cGMPs (Subpart B) plus programs such as hygienic zoning, supplier specifications, preventive maintenance, pest control and training. They handle most hazards without a preventive control *(manual)*.
2. **Preliminary steps:**
   - Assemble the food safety team.
   - Describe the product (formulation, pH, a_w, packaging, storage, shelf life, labeling, intended use and consumers, including vulnerable groups).
   - Draw and **verify on site** the flow diagram.
   - Describe the process *(manual)*.
3. **Hazard analysis:** for each step and each incoming ingredient, list the *known or reasonably foreseeable* biological, chemical (including radiological) and physical hazards. Include economically motivated ones. Then decide whether each **requires a preventive control**, based on **severity × probability in the absence of controls**, and **justify** every decision *(manual)*.
4. **Preventive controls** for each "yes": process, food allergen, sanitation, supply-chain or other (e.g. transportation, training). Each has **management components** — monitoring, corrections or corrective actions, verification and records — "as appropriate to the food, the facility, and the nature of the preventive control and its role in the facility's food safety system" *(manual)*.
5. **A recall plan** is required whenever a hazard requiring a preventive control is identified *(manual)*.
6. **Verify and keep it alive:**
   - Validate process controls.
   - Verify monitoring and corrective actions.
   - Calibrate instruments.
   - Review records within **7 working days**.
   - Test product and environment where appropriate.
   - **Reanalyze at least every 3 years**, or when anything significant changes *(manual)*.

**Key distinctions:**
- **Not every preventive control is a CCP** *(manual)*. Allergen, sanitation and supply-chain controls are managed differently from process controls.
- **Validation is required for process controls.** It is **not required** for allergen, sanitation or supply-chain controls, or for the recall plan, though it may be useful *(manual)*.
- **Correction vs corrective action:** a *correction* is a quick fix of a minor, isolated problem with no product safety impact, e.g. re-gowning an employee or re-cleaning a surface before production starts. A *corrective action* follows a deviation that may affect safety, and includes product evaluation and disposition plus root-cause prevention *(manual)*.
- **PCQI duties:** to perform or oversee preparing the Food Safety Plan, validating preventive controls, reviewing records and reanalyzing the plan *(manual)*.

**Workflow for drafting or reviewing a Food Safety Plan:**
1. **Gather:** the facility, products, ingredients (with suppliers and any that are "sensitive"), process steps, packaging, storage and distribution, intended use and consumers, existing GMPs and prerequisite programs, and history (complaints, testing, recalls).
2. Run the hazard analysis step by step using the six-column form, with ingredients included and a justification for every yes and no.
3. For each "yes", pick the control type and write its parameters or critical limits, monitoring (**what, how, frequency, who**), corrections or corrective actions, verification and records.
4. Add a recall plan, then a validation and verification schedule.
5. State what's illustrative: models are teaching aids. Each plan must reflect the actual facility, validated by a PCQI.

## Output style and caveats

- Use the regulation's own vocabulary: "hazard requiring a preventive control", "significantly minimize or prevent", "supply-chain-applied control", "receiving facility", "PCQI".
- Use tables for hazard analyses and control summaries, mirroring the FSPCA worksheet columns.
- This is training and drafting help, not legal advice or a substitute for a PCQI, a process authority or FDA guidance. Critical limits for lethality steps must be validated (scientific studies, process authority, regulatory guidance).
- Flag where the 2016 manual may be outdated (sesame, later guidance, traceability rule), and where USDA-FSIS rules (meat, poultry, egg products), juice HACCP, seafood HACCP or LACF rules apply instead of, or alongside, Part 117.
- Don't reproduce long passages of the manual; paraphrase and apply.
