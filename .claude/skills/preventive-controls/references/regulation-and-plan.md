## §1 The rule (21 CFR Part 117)

- FSMA's *Current Good Manufacturing Practice, Hazard Analysis, and Risk-Based Preventive Controls for Human Food* rule was published in September 2015 *(manual)*. It updates the cGMPs and adds hazard analysis and risk-based preventive controls (HARPC) *(manual/std)*.
- **Main subparts** *(manual/std)*:
  - A: general provisions, definitions, qualifications, training, records.
  - B: cGMPs.
  - C: hazard analysis and risk-based preventive controls.
  - D: modified requirements.
  - E: withdrawal of a qualified facility exemption.
  - F: records.
  - G: supply-chain program.
- **Who is covered:** facilities that manufacture, process, pack or hold human food and must register with FDA under section 415 of the FD&C Act. Some activities and facilities are exempt or have modified requirements *(manual/std)*:
  - **Qualified facilities** (including very small businesses, defined in the rule as under $1 million a year, adjusted for inflation, averaged over the prior 3 years, in human food sales plus the market value of food held without sale) *(manual)* file an attestation and follow modified requirements rather than full Subpart C *(std)*.
  - Facilities already covered by **seafood HACCP**, **juice HACCP** or **low-acid canned food** rules are exempt from Subpart C for those products (LACF only for the microbiological hazards) *(std)*.
  - Certain farm activities, storage of unexposed packaged food (modified requirements for refrigerated products), dietary supplements and others have specific exemptions *(std)*.
  - **Meat, poultry and egg products under USDA-FSIS are outside FDA's rule** *(std)*.
- **Hazard definitions** *(manual)*:
  - **Hazard:** any biological, chemical (including radiological) or physical agent that has the potential to cause illness or injury.
  - **Known or reasonably foreseeable hazard:** one known to be, or with the potential to be, associated with the facility or the food.
  - **Hazard requiring a preventive control:** a known or reasonably foreseeable hazard for which a knowledgeable person would, based on the hazard analysis (severity and probability in the absence of controls), establish one or more preventive controls to significantly minimize or prevent it, together with management components appropriate to the food, the facility, and the control's nature and role.
  - **Preventive controls:** risk-based, reasonably appropriate procedures, practices and processes that a knowledgeable person would use to significantly minimize or prevent the hazards, consistent with current scientific understanding.

## §2 PCQI

- A **preventive controls qualified individual** has successfully completed training in risk-based preventive controls at least equivalent to the FDA-recognized standardized curriculum (this course), or is otherwise qualified through job experience *(manual)*.
- **The PCQI must perform or oversee** *(manual)*:
  1. Preparing the Food Safety Plan.
  2. Validating the preventive controls.
  3. Reviewing records.
  4. Reanalyzing the plan.
  - Also some written justifications, e.g. for completing validation later than 90 days after production starts, reviewing records later than 7 working days, or deciding that validation isn't required.
- **Two kinds of training are required:**
  1. Food hygiene and food safety training appropriate to each individual's duties, for everyone.
  2. PCQI training where applicable.
  - Keep training records *(manual)*.

## §3 Food Safety Plan

**Required written elements** (the plan must be facility-specific) *(manual)*:
1. **Hazard analysis.**
2. **Preventive controls** (process, food allergen, sanitation, supply-chain, other), when the hazard analysis identifies hazards requiring them.
3. **Supply-chain program** (Subpart G), where the receiving facility relies on a supplier to control a hazard.
4. **Recall plan**, when any hazard requires a preventive control.
5. **Monitoring procedures.**
6. **Corrective action procedures.**
7. **Verification procedures**, including validation as required.
8. **Implementation records** demonstrating the system runs as designed.

**Optional but useful** *(manual)*: facility description, team list and training records, product description, flow diagram, process description. Anything in the plan may be subject to regulatory review.

**Format:** there is no required format. A plan can be grouped by product family, if hazards and controls are the same and differences are clearly identified, or organized by unit operation. The manual models a five-tab notebook *(manual)*:
1. Background.
2. Hazard analysis.
3. Preventive controls.
4. Recall plan.
5. Implementation records and procedures.

**Scope:** define the products and processes, the part of the food chain (retail vs foodservice vs manufacturing vs consumer) and all three hazard types *(manual)*.

**Why it matters** (the manual's cases) *(manual)*:
- **Peanut products / *Salmonella*:** the roasting process was unvalidated, *Salmonella* was in the processing environment, and hundreds of downstream companies used the ingredient with no kill step. Lessons: process validation, sanitation controls and **supplier controls**.
- **1989 UK botulism from hazelnut conserve in yogurt:** a fruit-type process was applied to low-acid hazelnuts, and the ingredient was held at room temperature. Lessons: validation, and understanding **supplier capabilities**.
- **Undeclared allergens** cause at least a third of FDA food safety recalls, mostly labeling errors.
- **Contamination sources:** ingredients, the processing environment and equipment, and people.

**Timeline and number cheat-sheet** *(manual unless noted)*:

| Requirement | Value |
|---|---|
| Records review (monitoring, corrective action and verification records) by or under a PCQI | Within **7 working days** of creation (or a justified longer time) |
| Process-control validation | Before implementation, or within the **first 90 days of production** (or a justified reasonable time) |
| Reanalysis | At least **every 3 years**, and whenever there is a significant change, new hazard information, an unanticipated problem, or a control found ineffective. Complete it before the change, or within 90 days with justification. |
| Record retention | At least **2 years** from creation. Records on the adequacy of equipment or processes, including scientific studies, are kept **2 years after their use is discontinued** |
| Food Safety Plan location | **Onsite**; electronic records count as onsite if accessible from the site |
| Offsite records | Retrievable within **24 hours** of an official request |
| Supplier onsite audit (SAHCODHA hazards) | Before first use and **at least annually**, unless written justification supports other activities |
| Qualified facility supplier assurances | Written assurance at approval and **at least every 2 years** |

## §4 cGMPs and prerequisite programs (Ch 3)

- **Subpart B cGMPs** cover *(manual/std)*:
  - Personnel: disease control, cleanliness, hand washing, clothing, jewelry, eating and drinking areas.
  - Plant and grounds.
  - Sanitary operations: cleaning and sanitizing, pest control, toxic chemical storage.
  - Sanitary facilities: water supply, plumbing, sewage, toilets, hand-washing stations, rubbish.
  - Equipment and utensils (cleanable design).
  - Processes and controls.
  - Warehousing and distribution.
  - Holding and distribution of human food by-products for animal food.
  - **Defect action levels.**
- The 2015 rule made some former recommendations binding, such as **protecting against allergen cross-contact** and **training in food hygiene and food safety** *(manual/std)*.
- **Prerequisite programs** beyond GMPs *(manual)*:
  - Hygienic zoning (especially for ready-to-eat products).
  - Supplier and product specifications.
  - Preventive maintenance (e.g. it can make metal a hazard not requiring a preventive control in one facility, while another facility needs metal detection).
  - Glass and brittle plastics policy.
  - Allergen signage and icons.
  - Color-coded tools.
  - Pest control, chemical control, training, and receiving and storage.
- If a hazard analysis finds a GMP element essential to control a hazard (e.g. environmental pathogens in ready-to-eat foods, allergen cross-contact), that element becomes a **sanitation or allergen preventive control** with written procedures and management components *(manual)*.
