## §1 Validation, verification, reanalysis (Ch 13)

- **Validation** obtains and evaluates scientific and technical evidence that a control measure, combination of measures, or the whole plan, properly implemented, can effectively control the hazards *(std/manual)*.
  - It is **required for process preventive controls**.
  - It is **not required** for food allergen controls, sanitation controls, the supply-chain program or the recall plan, though it may be useful *(manual)*.
  - A PCQI may also determine in writing that validation isn't required for a particular process control *(manual)*.
  - **Timing:** before implementation, or **within the first 90 days of production** (on-line validation may be needed to capture process variation), or within a justified reasonable time *(manual)*.
  - **Sources of evidence:** scientific literature, regulatory guidance (e.g. FDA, USDA lethality tables), challenge studies, process authority studies, predictive models with caution, and in-plant data *(manual/std)*.
- **Verification** consists of the activities, other than monitoring, that establish the validity of the plan and that the system operates as planned *(std)*. Required as appropriate *(manual)*:
  - Validation (process controls).
  - **Verification of monitoring** and of corrective actions being performed.
  - **Calibration**, or accuracy checks, of process monitoring and verification instruments, e.g. the omelet thermometer's daily accuracy check and annual calibration.
  - **Product testing and environmental monitoring**, as appropriate to the food, facility, control and its role. Environmental monitoring is required for ready-to-eat foods exposed to the environment where an environmental pathogen requires a control *(manual)*.
  - **Records review**, by or under the oversight of a PCQI, of monitoring, corrective action and calibration/testing/EM/supplier records, within **7 working days** of creation (or a justified longer time) for monitoring and corrective action records *(manual)*.
  - Written procedures for these activities.
- **Reanalysis** is a verification procedure to confirm the plan stays valid *(manual)*. Do it:
  - **at least every 3 years**;
  - when there's a significant change in activities that creates a reasonable potential for a new hazard or a significant increase in one;
  - when new information about potential hazards arises;
  - after an unanticipated food safety problem;
  - when a preventive control is found ineffective.
  - **Complete it before the change takes effect**, or within **90 days** after production starts (or a justified timeframe) *(manual)*.
  - Frequent recurring deviations are a trigger *(manual)*.
  - Manage major changes formally: reissue forms, retrain, phase in, update labels, inform suppliers *(manual)*.

## §2 Record-keeping (Ch 14)

- **Required records** *(manual/std)*:
  - The written Food Safety Plan (signed and dated by the owner, operator or agent on approval and on modification).
  - Hazard analysis.
  - Preventive controls.
  - Supply-chain program and its records.
  - Recall plan.
  - Monitoring, corrective action and verification records (validation, calibration, product testing, EM, records review, reanalysis).
  - Training records (PCQI and food hygiene and safety training).
- **Record content:** original records made at the time of observation, with the facility name and location, date and time of the activity, a signature or initials of the person performing it, and the product identity and lot code where applicable *(std)*.
- **Retention** *(manual)*:
  - At least **2 years** from the date the record was created.
  - Records on the general adequacy of equipment or processes, including scientific studies and evaluations, are kept for at least **2 years after their use is discontinued** (e.g. after a plan update).
- **Location and access** *(manual)*:
  - The Food Safety Plan must be **onsite**. Electronic records are onsite if accessible from an onsite location.
  - Other records may be offsite if retrievable within **24 hours** of a request for official review.
  - All required records must be **promptly available to FDA** for official review and copying.
- Electronic records are allowed. 21 CFR Part 11 does not apply to these records *(std)*.
- **Good practice:**
  - Design forms that capture what, how, when and who.
  - Don't backfill or pre-fill.
  - Correct errors with a single line, initials and date.
  - Check that records are reviewed and signed by a qualified person within 7 working days *(manual)*.

## §3 Recall plan (Ch 15)

- **Required** whenever a hazard requiring a preventive control is identified. The recall plan itself isn't a control, but it limits illnesses when contaminated product is removed quickly *(manual)*.
- **Recall classes (FDA)** *(manual)*:
  - **Class I:** a reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences or death.
  - **Class II:** may cause temporary or medically reversible effects; the probability of serious consequences is remote.
  - **Class III:** not likely to cause illness or injury.
- **Required written procedures** (assign responsibility for each) *(manual/std)*:
  1. **Notify direct consignees** about the recalled food, including how to return or dispose of it.
  2. **Notify the public** when necessary to protect public health.
  3. Conduct **effectiveness checks** to verify the recall was carried out.
  4. **Dispose of** the recalled food appropriately: reprocess, rework, divert to a use without a safety concern, or destroy.
- **Supporting elements:**
  - A recall team with contact list.
  - FDA district or recall coordinator contacts.
  - Product coding and lot identification.
  - **Traceability**: one step forward (customers) and one step back (suppliers) *(manual)*.
  - Distribution records.
  - Templates for press releases and customer notices.
  - Reporting via the **Reportable Food Registry** where required (reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences or death) *(manual/std)*.
- **Mock recalls:** test the plan periodically. Verify contacts and information are current and test the team; trace products and ingredients one step forward and one step back. Don't usually contact real customers or suppliers *(manual)*.
- The FSMA 204 traceability rule (2022) adds Key Data Elements for foods on the Food Traceability List *(std)*.

## §4 Worked example: E.G. Food Company (App 3)

**Product:** frozen omelets (plain, cheese, and a variety served on biscuits), sold to foodservice and retail, with heating instructions; "keep frozen or thaw under refrigeration (< 41 °F)" *(manual)*.

**Selected storage details** *(manual)*:
- Pasteurized liquid eggs and milk in designated areas in a cooler at **< 40 °F (4.4 °C)**, used within code date. No open containers go back to the cooler, which minimizes allergen cross-contact.
- Frozen biscuits stored separately at **< 0 °F (−18 °C)**.

**Hazards requiring preventive controls and the chosen controls** (summary) *(manual)*:

| Hazard | Where | Control type | Key parameters / monitoring |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vegetative pathogens (e.g. *Salmonella* from eggs) | Cook step | **Process control (CCP)** | Omelet surface ≥ 158 °F (70 °C) instantaneous before transfer; each batch checked by a trained operator with a calibrated thermometer; cook log. Verification: records review within 7 working days, daily accuracy check, annual calibration |
| *S. aureus* growth/toxin in hydrated batter | Batter holding | Process control | Batter ≤ 50 °F (10 °C), and not above it for more than 12 h |
| Allergens (egg, milk, wheat, soy) | Labeling / fill line | **Allergen control**, plus storage segregation and scheduling as cross-contact controls | Operator matches the label to the product number at start-up and each changeover; corrective action = hold and relabel plus root cause |
| Metal | Metal detector after packing | Process control | Detector on and reject working, observed at the start, middle and end of each shift; investigate kick-outs |
| Environmental pathogens (*L. monocytogenes*) in ready-to-eat exposed product | Post-cook assembly, freezing and packing | **Sanitation control**, verified by environmental monitoring | Pre-op inspection, sanitizer checks; *Listeria* spp. EM; corrections or corrective actions |
| Ingredient hazards controlled by the supplier (e.g. a ready-to-eat ingredient with no kill step at E.G.) | Receiving | **Supply-chain program** | Approved supplier, third-party audit and records review |

(Cell values are a synthesis of the manual's example. Use Appendix 3 itself for the exact wording of each form.)

- Flow diagram and process description.
- **Hazard analysis** (six columns, see `hazards-and-analysis.md` §4).
- **Process preventive controls** (hazard; parameters and critical limits; monitoring what, how, frequency, who; corrective action; verification; records).

- **Sanitation preventive controls.**
