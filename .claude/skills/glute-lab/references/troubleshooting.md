## §1 The seven most common mistakes

1. **Just squatting.** Squats stretch the glutes under load (lower glute max) but don't hit upper glutes much, produce little metabolic stress, and don't give peak activation. Add hip thrusts and other glute-dominant work.
2. **Training glutes only once a week.** Twice minimum; three days is likely optimal for most.
3. **Nothing for the upper glutes.** Add bridges, hip thrusts, kickbacks, pull-throughs and abduction.
4. **No horizontally loaded exercises** (bridges, thrusts, 45° hypers).
5. **Thinking cardio grows glutes or is needed for fat loss.** Diet drives weight change; cardio may help appetite for some, but it competes for recovery (the interference effect). Lift first, then do cardio you enjoy. For advanced trainees cardio adds no glute growth.
6. **High-risk activities for glute growth** (plyometrics, sprinting): useful for athletes, but riskier and not the best muscle builders.
7. **Not having fun.** Adherence is everything; drop exercises you hate.
"Abs are made in the kitchen and glutes are made in the gym." Strength training is the cake; cardio, stretching and core work are icing; too much icing spoils it.

## §2 Training around discomfort, soreness and injury

Train around pain, not through it; consider prior injuries; don't get so sore that you can't train often; let the body recover. When in doubt, take the day off; never push through "bad" pain.

| Issue | Try | Avoid or reduce |
|---|---|---|
| Back | Bodyweight deficit reverse lunges, step-ups, Bulgarian split squats; knee-banded bridges, frog pumps, abduction (unless SI joint pain worsens with abduction) | Heavy erector-loading lifts |
| Hip (usually deep flexion) | Partials and mid-range squats or bridges, wall-squat isometrics, comfortable abduction, stance changes | Deep hip flexion (rarely full extension) |
| Knee, front | Abduction, straight-leg hinges (45° hypers) | Knee-dominant work |
| Knee, back | High-rep goblet squats, abduction | Straight-leg hinges |
| Knee (general) | Stiff-leg/Romanian deadlifts, leg curls, glute-ham raises, Nordics, back extensions, reverse hypers; sometimes feet-elevated bridges, frog pumps, thrusts | Squats, lunges, step-ups, split squats |
| Ankle/foot | Open-chain: quadruped hip extension, side-lying abduction, cable kickbacks and abduction, reverse hypers; often back extensions, bridges, sometimes RDLs or box squats | Standing loaded work that hurts |
| Very sore glutes | Train other areas, isolation leg work, walk, sleep | Glute training until recovered |
| Sore erectors | High-rep bodyweight or light dumbbell lunges, step-ups, Bulgarian split squats, single-leg RDLs; maybe bridges, frog pumps, abduction | Deadlifts, good mornings, heavy squats |
| Sore adductors | Narrow-stance squats, deadlifts, hip thrusts; partials; leg curls/extensions; abduction; knee-banded bridges | Wide stances |
| Sore quads | All hinges (deadlifts, RDLs, back extensions, swings, reverse hypers, good mornings, pull-throughs); feet-elevated bridging | Squat and lunge patterns and hip thrusts |
| Sore hamstrings | Upright-torso knee-dominant squats (heel-elevated goblet, front), short-stride lunges and split squats, lots of abduction | Hinges, leg curls (bridges and thrusts also use hamstrings) |

**Recovering from injury** (Contreras's own severe upper glute max tear, back to full strength in under 6 weeks versus a typical 12): be "hurt" vs "injured"; see a professional for real injuries. Tools he used: **mental imagery training** (daily, mostly first-person, vivid full workouts, building to 12–15 minutes, adding small movements as able); **cross-education** (train the healthy side; strength carries over); careful **isometric contractions** (don't overdo them); **eccentric** work later; light massage, heat and cold as optional extras. Lesson: he ignored warning signs (feeling ill, a lump) and pushed hip thrusts.

## §3 Gluteal imbalances

Normal and never perfectly symmetrical. Causes: handedness and leg dominance, old injuries and pain inhibition (e.g., glute inhibition after ankle sprains), nerve issues, anatomical variation.
- **Prevent:** work the weaker side first and match reps with the stronger; film bilateral lifts to catch shifting; use free weights, not only machines.
- **Normalize:** single-leg reverse hypers with light ankle weights (≈5–10 lb women, 10–20 lb men) on the weaker side only, 2–3 sets up to 5 times a week (or ankle-weight bent-over kickbacks against a wall); symmetrical bilateral technique; isometric squeezes of the weaker glute through the day; an extra unilateral set for the weak side.

## §4 Plateaus and not seeing results

Plateau strategies: personalize the program; follow a specialization program (one lift or pattern per 4-week cycle, e.g., DUP); include deload weeks (and a few deload weeks per year); reduce glute training (e.g., 4x15 = 60 sets/week down to 2x12 = 24 sets/week can unmask fitness); change it up (new plan every 3–6 weeks, even a new gym); hire a coach or training partner; add advanced methods (without abandoning basics); clean up sleep, diet and stress (eustress, not distress); find the fun; don't put a timeline on progress.
Not seeing results checklist: sleeping enough? training hard enough (or too hard)? need a program change? is diet the issue? Assess yourself as you'd assess someone else; ideally get a coach.

## §5 Common obstacles

- **New to glute training:** just start; bodyweight first, prioritize thrusts and bridges, add squat and hinge patterns; learn form; be consistent for years; experiment; have fun. Starter: Mon/Wed/Fri 3x20 bodyweight squats, hip thrusts or bridges, and RDLs; a hard set of lunges and a few minutes of band walks.
- **Embarrassed to hip thrust:** face a wall, use a quiet corner or empty studio, or train at home.
- **No time:** 2–3 days a week; split into shorter sessions (e.g., four 30-minute sessions); train early; home workouts with a mini band and Glute Loop.
- **No equipment:** high-rep bodyweight work to near failure (pistols, dead-stop single-leg hip thrusts, side-lying hip raises); at minimum get bands.
- **Physically demanding job:** train in the morning, or nap, eat and relax first.
- **Travel:** bodyweight and band work everywhere; hotel-gym dumbbells and cables. Sample couch/ottoman circuit, 3 rounds, 1 min rest: feet-elevated hip thrust 20; single-leg foot-and-shoulder-elevated hip thrust 12/leg; over-bench bird dogs 20; frog pumps 50; deficit reverse lunges 20/leg; extra-range side-lying abduction 30/leg. Strength and muscle are easy to maintain; plan a vacation after a hard week ("functional overreaching").
- **Not feeling glutes:** activation ability varies by person and muscle and doesn't track glute size; it improves with practice (low-load activation helps). Feeling quads during a thrust doesn't mean glutes aren't working. Use the hip thrust corrections (`exercises.md` §3), stay positive, get stronger with variety.
- **No pump or burn:** aim for at least once a week. Factors: hydration, supplements (e.g., creatine), pre-workout carbs, sodium and electrolytes, full systemic and local recovery, novelty, short-muscle-length exercises (band walks), constant-tension exercises (hip thrusts), piston-like reps, rep ranges, rest periods.

## §6 Physique questions

- **Saggy and weak → perky and strong:** a nice booty is a strong booty; train hard instead of excessive cardio and restriction (which yields "skinny-fat").
- **Looks great one day, not the next:** stress, sleep, carbs, salt, hormones and soreness shift water and fullness; judge the big picture.
- **Hip divots:** mostly skeletal anatomy and leanness; not a problem to fix.
- **Saddlebags:** fat storage is genetic and you can't spot-reduce; build glute muscle, diet soundly, stay active, prioritize glute-dominant work, don't replace lifting with cardio, be patient. Bigger glutes make thighs look smaller.
- **Cellulite:** normal in women; no treatment reliably removes it; train, eat well in a deficit if leaning out, sleep, get some sun, manage stress.
- **Grow glutes without growing legs:** reduce squats, lunges, step-ups, leg press, hack squats, leg extensions and heavy hinges (RDLs, back extensions…), and emphasize low-load activation, hip external rotation, abduction, **barbell glute bridges** (the "money" lift), American deadlifts (shorter range), pull-throughs, reverse hypers, swings, feet-elevated hip thrusts, frog pumps. Usually the issue is leg fat, not leg muscle.
- **Grow glutes without growing the waist:** prioritize hip thrusts with ribs down; avoid excessive squat/deadlift volume; avoid direct ab/oblique work; favor single-leg exercises; bodyweight, band, cable and ankle-weight abduction; higher reps with pristine form.
- **Want a smaller butt:** usually means losing fat, so keep training glutes, avoid excess high-intensity cardio (about 30 minutes of steady low-intensity 3–5 days a week is fine), control diet with ample protein. Lean people who truly want less glute muscle must stop glute-focused training.
- **Losing a lot of weight (e.g., 70 lb):** prioritize progressive overload, ~1 g protein per lb of lean body mass, stay in a deficit (individualized, ideally with a professional), and lose gradually (e.g., faster early, slower later over ~12 months) to keep muscle.

## §7 Pregnancy

The book's view, from coaching many pregnant women (always defer to the user's obstetric provider and stop anything that feels wrong):
- Training while pregnant is generally safe and healthy if you listen to your body; first trimester often brings nausea, the second often feels great, the third gets harder.
- Most women should reduce load as pregnancy progresses: lighter weights, higher reps; volume and frequency can often stay similar if they feel right.
- Hip thrusts: Contreras considers a few sets a couple of times a week reasonable (he cites research finding no conclusive evidence that supine exercise is unsafe), but some doctors advise avoiding supine work; follow the provider's advice if unsure. Modify: feet slightly farther from the bench, shallower depth, bar lower on the hips, push the bar forward away from the abdomen.
- Good options: bodyweight work, bands, then dumbbells, kettlebells and barbells as tolerated; wide-stance leg press, dumbbell sumo squats, knee-banded bridges, lateral band walks; dumbbell RDLs or stiff-leg deadlifts if barbell deadlifts feel off.
- Avoid whatever feels uncomfortable; some women find single-leg work painful (possibly relaxin-related pelvic laxity).
- Keep the habit; it's much harder to restart postpartum.

## §8 Goals, diet and lifestyle

**Mindset:** praise effort regardless of looks; stay optimistic; set realistic goals based on genetics and experience; progress comes in waves; be proud of how hard you train, not of how you compare; prioritize strength goals over aesthetic goals; judge results by strength, weight, size and the mirror together; control what you can; treat workouts as mini-competitions.

**Diet:**
- All weight-loss diets work through a caloric deficit; adjust as you go (the body adapts). Lose gradually to keep glute muscle (dieting shrinks the butt unless you're a beginner).
- **Protein:** about 1 g per lb of lean body mass per day (or roughly 0.8 g × body weight in lb), split across 3–4 feedings. Lean body mass = body weight − (body weight × body-fat %); e.g., 150 lb at 20% = 120 lb → ~120 g.
- **Flexible dieting:** ~85% of calories from whole, minimally processed foods, ~15% discretionary; at least ~0.4 g fat per lb of body weight per day on average; track as much or as little as suits the person (tracking can be harmful for some).
- **Meal timing** matters little for most (no strict anabolic window); eat 1–2 hours before training; carb timing matters for athletes and multiple daily sessions (Aragon's nutrient-timing continuum).
- **Recomp:** eat at maintenance with adequate protein and get much stronger for months (his client example: 132 lb, ~1,600 kcal and 110 g protein for a year; hip thrust 95 → 365 lb, waist shrank). Maintenance estimate: body weight (lb) × 14–15, or Mifflin–St Jeor; or a prepackaged-food day; or a weighed 7-day average. Fat loss ×10–12; gain ×16–18 (rare extremes 8 and 25).
- People underestimate intake and overestimate activity. You don't need to bulk to grow glutes; a slight surplus ("gaintain", 1–3 lb a year) is an option.
- Eat enough to fuel workouts. No supplement is mandatory. Cold water immersion and cryotherapy are for athletes (may blunt hypertrophy).
- **Sleep 7–9 hours.** If very stressed, take the day off or lower volume.
- **Cardio:** optional for physique; limit to about three 30-minute relaxed sessions a week; walking and cycling interfere less than jogging; a step goal (e.g., 10,000) is an alternative. Take at least one full rest day a week; rest days are for rest, not "active recovery".
