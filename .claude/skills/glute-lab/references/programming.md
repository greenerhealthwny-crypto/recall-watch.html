## §1 The eight program design variables

**Load.** Heavy loads aren't required for hypertrophy: anything above ~40% 1RM taken close to failure through full range builds similar muscle. Heavy may give a small extra benefit (maybe ~5%) and is essential for strength (skill and specificity). 8–12 reps likely captures ~95% of hypertrophy results. Find your "sweet spot" load where you feel the glutes most, and occasionally go heavier to expand it. Go heavy on lifts that feel good; stay lighter with high reps on lifts that don't (e.g., if heavy squats or deadlifts bother your back due to long femurs or poor hip mobility). Heavy loads beat up joints and limit frequency; lighter loads allow 3–5 days a week.

**Tempo.** Written as lower/bottom pause/raise/top pause (e.g., squat or deadlift 2/0/1/0). Hip thrusts are naturally quick, about 0.75/0/0.75/0 (10 reps in under 15 seconds) unless pausing or accentuating the lowering. Don't think about tempo except when doing pause or accentuated-eccentric reps.

**Rest periods.** 2–3 min for big progressive-overload lifts (hip thrust, squat, deadlift, bench, chin-ups); up to 5 min for a 1RM (Contreras sometimes rests up to 10 for big PRs). ~2 min for medium lifts (rows, push-ups, back extensions). 30–60 s (as low as ~20 s) for pump/burn work at the end. Self-selected rest works about as well.

**Exercise selection.** Match goals, experience, anatomy and injury history. Upper glutes: hip thrusts, back extensions, abduction. Lower glutes: lunges, squats, deadlifts. Experiment with stances, setups and loads; use the mind-muscle connection and palpation. Quick self-test: compare a **prone bent-leg hip extension** and a **standing glute squeeze**. More activation standing → straight-leg exercises (back extensions, reverse hypers) may suit you; more activation prone bent-leg → hip thrusts, bridges, quadruped hip extensions.
Seven ways to judge an exercise: performance feel (pump/burn, where tension is), biomechanical analysis, functional analysis, palpation, next-day soreness, feedback from others, research (EMG and training studies). There's no single perfect glute exercise; the hip thrust is the best all-rounder but lacks loaded stretch, which squats and deadlifts provide.

**Exercise order.** What comes first gets the best results. Prioritize weak areas or the weak side; otherwise primary lifts first (hip thrust, squat, deadlift variations), then accessories (single-leg, other variations), then high-rep pump/burn finishers (abduction, frog pumps, back extensions, reverse hypers). Sample: hip thrust 3x10 → single-leg RDL 2x10, back extension 3x30, walking lunge 2x20 → burnout of banded abduction, kickbacks, band walks, bridges, half-squat isohold (30 s each).

**Volume.** "Not all volume is created equal": deadlifts tax you more than band walks; lunges more than bridges; bands and short-muscle-length exercises allow more sets. Guidelines: ~16 hard sets per session as an upper limit (10–22 depending on exercise selection and recovery); 3–4 sets per exercise (prefer 3x5 exercises or 4x4 over 5–6 sets of 2–3 exercises). The first set gives the most stimulus (maybe ~80%), with diminishing returns after. **Weekly glute sets: 10 to 40** depending on tempo, genetics, exercise selection, effort, frequency and load (the book's top-guidelines list also cites 30–36 sets over 3–6 workouts for dedicated glute training). Most experts say 10–20 sets/week/muscle; glutes can handle a bit more. Less is sometimes more: many lifters progress after cutting volume and raising effort. One all-out set of 6 glute exercises, 5–6 days a week, also works for some people.

**Effort.** How close to failure (RPE / RIR). Novices usually can't gauge failure; it's a learnable skill. Volume and effort trade off: 2x8 at RPE 10 ≈ 4x8 at RPE 7 in fatigue. Push big lifts to failure or 1 shy, accessories 2–3 shy, burnouts and banded abduction nowhere near failure (they feel like failure but are limited by motivation). Don't take every set to failure.

**Frequency.** Per week: total sessions, sessions per exercise, sessions per muscle. Contreras's rough illustration: once a week ≈ 60% of gains, twice ≈ 90%, three times ≈ 98%; a fourth or fifth day helps or hurts depending on recovery. Three glute days suits most people (e.g., a quads-and-glutes day, a hams-and-glutes day, a glutes-only day). Daily glute training is possible only with low-damage work (bridges, bands, abduction, light dumbbells) and not to failure. Upper body: maintenance is about one day a week; growth needs more.

**Interrelationships.** You can have high frequency, high volume *or* high effort, but not all three at once. Example: 4 sets of heavy deadlifts to failure ≈ once a week; knee-banded glute bridges close to failure can go 4 days a week; loaded walking lunges to failure 4 days a week would be counterproductive.

**Set/rep schemes the Glute Lab uses:** 1x20 or 2x20 (squat, hip thrust, deadlift; goblet squat, KB deadlift); 3x1, 3x3, 3x5, 5x3, 5x5 (squat, deadlift, hip thrust, Nordic curl); 3x8, 3x10, 3x12, 4x8, 4x10 (most exercises); pyramids 10/8/6/15 or 15/10/5/20 (hip thrust, leg press); 3x20 or 3x30 (knee-banded bridges, glute-dominant back extensions); 4x30–50 frog pumps (sometimes 2x100). Strength: sets of 5 or fewer reps. Hypertrophy: 5–100 reps, 8–12 typical. Pump: many sets of 20–50. AMRAP across 3 sets and beat the total over time.

**Set loading options for "3x8":** straight sets all to failure (155x8, x5, x4); straight sets, last to failure (140x8x3); descending sets all to failure (155x8, 145x8, 135x8); ascending sets, last to failure (135x8, 145x8, 155x8). Choose by how you feel and where you are in the cycle.

## §2 The Rule of Thirds

Applied across the week, not every session:
- **Vector:** ~⅓ horizontal (hip thrust, bridge variations), ~⅓ vertical (squat, deadlift, lunge), ~⅓ lateral/rotary (abduction, external rotation).
- **Load:** ~⅓ heavy (1–5 reps), ~⅓ medium (6–12), ~⅓ light (13–50). You can drop a rep range you hate if volume and effort are high.
- **Effort:** ~⅓ of sets to failure or 1 shy, ~⅓ 2–3 shy, ~⅓ nowhere close.

Benefits: develops upper and lower glute max, type I and II fibers, every vector (transfer to sport and daily life), and tolerates higher workloads. Don't obsess over perfect balance; it's a template meant to be modified.

## §3 Evidence-based hypertrophy guidelines (book's summary table)

| Variable | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Twice a week beats once; little evidence for more except to split volume when specializing |
| Volume | 10–20 sets/week/muscle; higher for short specialization periods |
| Effort | Most sets close to failure; actual failure not necessary and can backfire |
| Load | All loads build muscle; moderate loads are practical; mixing rep ranges may help |
| Exercise selection | Prioritize multi-joint, but single-joint is needed for full development |
| Exercise order | Muscles trained earlier gain slightly more |
| Tempo | 2–6 s per rep similar; control the eccentric; avoid 10 s+ super-slow reps |
| Rest | 2–3 min appears best; ~3 min big lifts early, ~90 s smaller lifts later |
| Split | All popular splits work; total-body equals body-part splits for hypertrophy |
| Periodization | Having a plan beats winging it; no single best model; allow day-to-day flexibility |

## §4 How muscle grows

- **Three mechanisms:** mechanical tension (the main driver: heavy compound lifts, done first), metabolic stress (pump and burn: constant-tension or short-muscle-length work like bridges, hip thrusts, banded work at the end), muscle damage (loaded stretch: lunges, squats, Bulgarian split squats, RDLs, deficit deadlifts, good mornings; limit to one or two such exercises a week, mid-workout; soreness that stops you sitting is overkill).
- **Muscle actions:** isometric (holding the bottom of a squat or top of a hip thrust), eccentric (lowering), concentric (lifting).
- **Mind-muscle connection** (internal focus) increases activation and, in Schoenfeld & Contreras's study, growth. Use it for hypertrophy; use external focus ("push the floor away", "drive the bar through the roof") for max strength and performance. Contreras lifts heavy on the first one or two exercises, then trains "for the feel".
- **Fiber type:** the glutes are roughly half slow-twitch, half fast-twitch (studies: 52% and 68% slow). Don't train by fiber-type tests; train all rep ranges.
- **Strategies for maximizing growth:** go close to failure (above ~40% 1RM, 1–2 reps shy); accumulate "stimulating reps" (roughly the last 5 reps before failure); use variety (shotgun approach); create tension slowly through a large range of motion; squeeze the glutes for about a second at lockout on hip thrusts, bridges, kickbacks, back extensions (but don't over-tuck at the top of squats/deadlifts); training for the pump probably helps; hip extension is the best joint action (bridges/thrusts alone might give ~90% of results); prioritize resistance training over sprinting and plyometrics for glute growth (more injury risk, less tension).

## §5 Progressive overload

Doing more over time. Ten rules:
1. Start with whatever you can do with perfect form (weakest beginner: bodyweight high box squats about 8 inches deep; within 6 months she did full goblet squats, barbell hip thrusts and 95 lb deadlifts). Retest old 1RMs before using percentages.
2. Beginners: progress range of motion first (e.g., a box an inch lower each week), master body weight (about 3x20 full-range reps) before adding load, and use rep jumps when load jumps are big (10→15 lb dumbbells is a 50% jump).
3. Twelve ways to overload: more range of motion; better form/control/efficiency; more reps; more load; less rest (density); more speed (effort); more work in the same time; same work in less time; more sets; more frequency; same performance at lower body mass (relative); extending sets past failure (forced reps, negatives, dropsets, holds, rest-pause, partials, supersets). Form and range first, reps and load second.
4. It's never linear; adaptations come in waves.
5. Gains are fastest in the first ~3 months.
6. Veterans need strategy and specialization: rotate a monthly focus (deadlift, squat, single-leg, hip thrust) while maintaining the rest.
7. It's harder while losing weight; maintaining strength in a deficit is progress.
8. Sometimes it has a mind of its own.
9. Never prioritize it over form.
10. Standardize technique (squat to parallel or deeper, lock out hip thrusts, control full range).
Retest baselines with perfect form once a year. Lifting heavy is safe with good mechanics, smart programming and listening to your body.

## §6 Advanced methods

Use after about 3–6 months of training; at most two per session (the last being a burnout); never abandon straight-set progressive overload. "Advanced" routines haven't been shown to beat basic ones; a basic session of hip thrust 3x8, back squat 3x6, stiff-leg deadlift 3x10 and seated abduction 3x20 is brutal if pushed hard.

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| Constant-tension reps | Piston-like reps without rest at top or bottom; lighter load, 20–30 reps |
| Rest-pause | E.g., 6 reps, ~10 s rest, 2, ~10 s, 1, ~5 s, 1 = 10 reps with a 6RM |
| Isoholds | Hold top of hip thrust or bottom of squat (e.g., 30 s), often with a knee band; good for sticking points or training around injury |
| 20-rep squat | 10RM load for 20 rest-paused reps; classic ~6-week program |
| Pyramids | BC Pyramid: ~15RM, ~10RM, ~5RM, then ~20RM with a band; stick 3–4 weeks and beat reps |
| Enhanced eccentrics | Heavier lowering (two-up/one-down, partner pushes on bar, weight releasers, flywheel) |
| Accentuated eccentrics | Slow 4–6 s lowering; great around strains |
| Torque doubling | Band around knees during hip extension (bridges, thrusts, squats) to add abduction |
| Pulses / 1¼ reps | Small-range reps at the hardest point / a full rep plus a quarter rep |
| Clusters | E.g., 6x5 with 1 min rest instead of 3x10 with 2 min; easier on the body, good for athletes, less hypertrophy |
| Dropsets | Usually a triple drop (e.g., hip thrust 235→185→135); also bands or machine pins; beat total reps weekly |
| Pause reps | 3–5 s at the bottom of squats or top of hip thrusts; use ~15–20% less load; count honestly |
| Accommodating resistance | Bands or chains; more tension at lockout, less damage |
| Dynamic effort | Light, maximally fast, 3–5 reps for power (athletes) |
| Ladders | Knee-banded bridges + supine abduction from 15 (beginners 12) down to 1 |
| Pre-exhaustion / synergist supersets | Fatigue a helper (e.g., Nordic curls) before the target (back extensions) to feel glutes more; experiment |
| Supersets | Agonist, antagonist, synergist, upper/lower (pair non-competing lifts) |
| Burnouts | 3 minutes nonstop with a band: 60 s hip extension (30 s per side), 30 s frontal-plane abduction, 30 s transverse-plane abduction, 30 s combined extension + abduction (squat or bridge isohold), 30 s "random" (e.g., RKC plank with glute squeeze) |

How to place them: power methods first, strength next, hypertrophy anywhere, endurance last. Once-a-week trainees: straight sets or a pyramid. Three times a week: a different method each day (e.g., dropsets day 1, constant tension day 2, pause single-leg day 3).

## §7 Periodization, deloads, monthly focus

- **Terms:** microcycle (~1 week), mesocycle (~4 weeks), macrocycle (~16 weeks). Strategies: linearity (progress a quality steadily), undulation (vary stressors: daily or weekly undulating periodization), conjugation (several methods in a week), block (focus blocks of 3–6 weeks). Periodize any variable, not just sets, reps and load.
- **4-week cycles:** week 1 ≈ 60–70% effort (learn exercises, set loads, no failure; especially easy on deadlifts), week 2 typical but no failure, week 3 to failure with a little in the tank, week 4 go for PRs. Then repeat. Deloads are not "wimpy".
- **Rotate the monthly focus** ("same but different"): well-rounded → squat → hip thrust → deadlift → single-leg → repeat. Strength is easy to maintain and hard to gain, so specialize in one or two lifts per month while keeping the others on maintenance. Change the plan every 3–6 weeks.
- **Hip thrust DUP example (up to ~6 weeks):** Day 1: 3x10 @ ~75% + AMRAP; Day 2: 4x6 @ ~85% + AMRAP; Day 3: 2x15 @ ~65% + AMRAP. Progress reps or load weekly.
- **Training intuitively** works for experienced lifters who keep structure (e.g., the Rule of Thirds).

## §8 Training splits

All work; choose what you enjoy and recover from. The book gives a one-week template for each, repeated for a month with the deload-to-PR ramp.
- **Body part split:** 4-day (pecs/lats; quads/glutes; delts/arms; hams/glutes) or 5-day (glutes; chest/shoulders/triceps; quads/glutes; back/rear delts/biceps; hams/glutes; also the bodybuilder template).
- **Upper/lower:** 4 days.
- **Push/pull:** 4 days.
- **Squat/bench/deadlift/hypertrophy:** 4 days (powerlifting template).
- **Total body:** 3 days (medium/light/heavy). Contreras's own preference; Schoenfeld & Contreras found it slightly better for gains in one study.
- **Compound/bro:** days 1, 3, 5 compound lifts at 1–10 reps with ~3 min rest and high effort; days 2, 4 (and optionally 6) isolation work at 10–30 reps, shorter rest, pump and burn, not to failure. For people who love daily lifting.
- **Sport, CrossFit, bodybuilding, powerlifting:** add glute-dominant thrust/bridge and abduction work to whatever the athlete does; for sport, avoid failure, keep reps explosive, try clusters; hip thrusts help horizontal force (sprinting, pushing). Yoga, spin and Pilates are fine for enjoyment and health but don't "tone" or grow glutes.

## §9 Templates and program builder

**Basic Strength Training Template (2–3 days a week, any level):** one hip-dominant exercise, one upper-body pull, one knee-dominant exercise, one upper-body press, one core exercise; 2–3 sets of 8–12; optionally single-joint work or a burnout at the end.

**Beginner starter (from the troubleshooting chapter):** Mon/Wed/Fri: 3x20 bodyweight squats, hip thrusts or glute bridges, and RDLs; finish with a hard set of lunges and a few minutes of lateral band work.

**The book's 12-week programs (summary):** 4 sessions per week, each 4-week phase starting with a deload week, 10 optional minutes at the end of each session for isolation work or a ≤3-minute burnout (no extra heavy glute lifts).
- *Novice:* weeks 1–8 bodyweight only, weeks 9–12 add dumbbells and a resistance band; days 1/3 and 2/4 repeat to groove patterns; single-leg work starts with the weaker leg.
- *Intermediate:* barbell, band, adjustable bench, rack, chin-up bar, rings, dumbbells, 45° hyper, cable. Four different workouts weekly; focus rotates: hip thrust + military press, then squat + chin-up, then deadlift + bench. The "home base" program.
- *Advanced:* commercial gym; adds dropsets, pre-exhaustion, clusters, supersets, ladders, tempo changes. Run two intermediate-style cycles per advanced cycle.

**Program builder checklist (use to write original programs):**
1. Days per week → split (§8). Default: 3 full-body or lower-emphasis days.
2. Per glute session at 3x/week: about 4 glute exercises: 1–2 horizontal, 1–2 vertical, 1–2 lateral-rotary; rotate frontal vs transverse abduction between days.
3. Order: main lift (heavy or pyramid) → accessories → single-leg → pump/abduction or burnout.
4. Sets: 3–4 per exercise; ~12–20 sets per session; weekly glute sets 10–40 by person.
5. Loads, reps and effort by the Rule of Thirds (§2).
6. 4-week ramp (§7); log loads and reps; beat 3-set totals.
7. Next month: rotate focus.
