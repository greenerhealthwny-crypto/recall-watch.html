## §1 Glute anatomy and function in brief

- **Gluteus maximus** (largest, with upper and lower subdivisions), **gluteus medius**, **gluteus minimus**. Skeletal anatomy (pelvis width, femoral neck angle, attachment points) shapes appearance (e.g., hip divots) and movement (e.g., squat depth and stance), and differs between individuals and between sexes.
- **Joint actions:** hip extension (main), posterior pelvic tilt, hip external rotation, hip abduction. Upper glute max is worked well by abduction, hip thrusts and back extensions; lower glute max by squats, deadlifts and lunges (loaded stretch); both together by bridges and thrusts.
- **Genetics** sets the ceiling and how fast you grow and recover; you control mindset, effort and consistency.

## §2 Exercise categories

The categories combine force vector (relative to the body), plane of motion, and knee action (bent-knee vs straight-leg hip extension). Use the Rule of Thirds to balance them.

| Category | Character | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertically loaded** | Hardest on the body; more lower than upper glute max | Squats, deadlifts, good mornings, lunges, step-ups/step-downs, Bulgarian split squats, pistol/single-leg box squats, skater squats, single-leg RDLs, King deadlifts, Olympic lifts; lying leg press; kneeling squats |
| **Horizontally loaded** | Less taxing; highly activates upper and lower glute max; fairly constant tension | Band/dumbbell/barbell single- and double-leg hip thrusts and glute bridges, frog pumps and frog thrusts; back extensions and ankle-weight reverse hypers; quadruped hip extensions and band/cable kickbacks; cable pull-throughs, standing band/cable hip thrusts and kickbacks; kneeling band/cable hip thrusts |
| **Combined vertical + horizontal** | Vector at an angle or changing through the rep | Kettlebell swings, sled pushes, walking lunges, hip-banded RDLs, four-way hip machine; pendulum reverse hypers, 45° hypers, reverse hack squats; 45° leg press, hack squat; hip-banded kneeling squats/thrusts, pendulum donkey kicks |
| **Frontal-plane abduction** | Targets upper glute max | Side-lying hip abduction (knee-banded, ankle weight, extra-range off a bench), side-lying hip raise with top-leg abduction; knee-banded supine abduction at top of a bridge; lateral band/sumo/X walks; standing hip abduction (band, cable, ankle weight); lateral sled drags |
| **Transverse-plane abduction / external rotation** | Upper and lower glute max | Seated hip abduction machine or knee-banded seated abduction (3 torso positions: leaning back, upright, leaning forward); hip-hinge knee-banded abduction; supine abduction at the bottom of a bridge; fire hydrants; clams; bent-hip raises |
| **Combined frontal + transverse** | Upper and lower | Knee-banded squats, jump squats and bridges; lateral band squat walks; monster/zigzag walks; side-lying hip raises |
| **Vertical/horizontal + lateral** | Upper and lower | Knee-banded goblet/front/back squats, lateral lunges and step-ups; knee-banded bridges and hip thrusts |
| **Rotary** | Upper and lower | Cable/band standing hip external rotation, anti-rotation presses, landmine rotations, cuff/dip-belt cable hip rotation, half-kneeling woodchops |

**Glute-, quad- and hamstring-dominant continuum (Part 5):** glute-dominant (hip thrusts, bridges, kickbacks, abduction, back extensions done glute-style), quad-dominant (squats, lunges, step-ups, split squats, leg press, sled push), hamstring-dominant (deadlifts, RDLs, good mornings, swings, reverse hypers, Nordic ham curls, leg curls). Variables within each: limb number (bilateral/unilateral), range of motion, load position, equipment.

## §3 Hip thrust

**Ten reasons to hip thrust:** constant glute tension; large hip range of motion; high tension at peak activation (end-range extension); bent knees reduce hamstring contribution; stable and easy to learn; suits all body types; allows heavy loading and many set/rep schemes; the safest heavy lower-body lift and easy on the lower back done correctly; builds confidence (big numbers); versatile (stances, loads, equipment).

**Setup and cues:**
- **Bench height:** bottom of the shoulder blades at the bench edge. Ideal height ~14 in (35.5 cm); tall lifters 15–16 in; ≤5'2" or long legs/short torso ~12 in. Too high → more quads; too low → hinging over the bench. A padded bench, Hip Thruster, adjustable decline bench, or aerobic step with risers (against a wall) all work. Use a bar pad.
- **American hip thrust:** edge of the bench at mid-back, feet a little closer; more hamstrings, less quads, more pelvic-tilt focused; good if hip thrusts bother your back.
- **Foot distance:** at the top, shins roughly vertical.
- **Stance and pressure:** about shoulder width, feet fairly straight or slightly out, knees out; push through the heels (toes may lift).
- **Knees:** push out and track over the middle toes; a little inward drift at the very top can be anatomical.
- **Hips:** reach full hip extension (shoulders, hips, knees roughly in line), squeeze about 1 s at the top.
- **Spine:** "move from your sternum down", ribs down, slight posterior pelvic tilt at the top.
- **Head:** look forward and let the chin tuck as you rise (about 1 in 10 prefer neutral head).
- **Brace** at the bottom.

**Spinal-pelvic strategies (pick what feels best):** neutral spine (least favored: tends to hyperextend at lockout); neutral at the bottom with posterior tilt at the top; posterior tilt throughout; **anterior tilt at the bottom → posterior tilt at the top** (Contreras's and most clients' favorite, lets you lift more).

**Faults and corrections:**
- *Spinal hyperextension at lockout* → ribs down, posterior tilt, torso about parallel to the floor at the top.
- *Sliding up and down the bench* → keep the shoulder-blade position fixed.
- *Insufficient hip extension* → lighter load, 1 s squeeze, stop the set when you lose range.
- *Feeling it too much in the quads* → feet farther out; knee band; drive through heels; shorter bench; (some people) look up with hip hyperextension; American hip thrust.
- *Feeling it too much in the hamstrings* → feet closer; knee band; push through the whole foot; taller bench; look forward with posterior tilt.

**Hip thrust pain:** lower back → avoid hyperextension with anterior tilt at the top; look forward, ribs down, chin tucked. SI joint → listen to symptoms; the same exercises can help or aggravate. Knee → usually from high quad activation; vary exercises, fewer heavy barbell thrusts, try bridges or B-stance, lighter load, feet elevated, band. Neck → usually a weak neck holding the tuck; neutral head is fine. Upper-back scrapes → more padding.

**Equipment notes:** all you need is a stable bench. Machines (Skorcher, Hip Thruster, Booty Builder, Glute Builder, Nautilus Glute Drive) make setup and loading easier; none is a clear favorite at the Glute Lab.

**Pregnancy modifications:** see `troubleshooting.md` §7.

## §4 General technique faults (and fixes)

1. **Lumbar hyperextension / excess anterior tilt** (top of deadlifts, thrusts, back extensions): learn to extend the hips without overextending the spine, including when heavy and near failure.
2. **Valgus collapse (knees caving)**, especially rising from squats: cue knees out; ankle dorsiflexion mobility; transverse-plane abduction work; banded squats; practice lighter.
3. **Lumbar flexion / butt wink** (bottom of squats and deadlifts): don't go deeper than your hip anatomy allows (parallel squats, block pulls); hip and ankle mobility; erector strength; practice lighter.
4. **Pushing through the balls of the feet:** set the feet, "push through the heels"; ankle mobility; heel wedges help but shouldn't be a crutch.
5. **Hips shooting up out of the hole:** lighter practice and stronger quads.
6. **Inconsistent range and form rep to rep:** set up the same way every time, pre-lift ritual.
7. **Asymmetric setup:** use bar markings, center the pad over the hips, align the feet; don't look sideways at a mirror (film instead).
8. **Suboptimal breathing:** high reps, breathe freely; moderate reps, in on the way down and out on the way up; heavy, brace (360° with diaphragm, pelvic floor, abs, obliques, erectors) and exhale after lockout.

Other form guidance: devote extra focus if movement is hard; anything that isn't good form slows progress and invites injury; start with basic variations and progress load, volume and range slowly; improve mobility if range is limited; experiment with stances; don't obsess over range of motion; discipline on every rep. Use activation drills to warm up. Do both full-range and shorter-range exercises; stretches the book highlights: deep lunge, rectus femoris, hip external rotation, twisting glute stretch.

## §5 The exercise library (Part 5) at a glance

The book details setup, cues, variations, faults and corrections for:
- **Glute-dominant:** hip thrusts (double-leg, single-leg, B-stance, feet-elevated, foot-and-shoulder-elevated, American, PPT, kneeling/standing band and cable, banded barbell, knee-banded, dumbbell); glute bridges (barbell, B-stance, feet-elevated, double-leg straight-leg bridge, glute march); frog pumps, frog thrusts, frog reverse hypers; quadruped hip extensions and kickbacks (horizontal quadruped, cable/band standing kickbacks); hip abduction (frontal-plane variations, seated hip abduction machine, cuff/dip-belt and cable/band hip external rotation); PPT activation work (PPT RKC plank, short-lever RKC plank, PPT activation test); horizontal back extension; glute activation drills.
- **Quad-dominant:** squats (bodyweight/free, box, bench, back with high- or low-bar, front, goblet and other front-loaded, half squats, hang squats, pistol, kneeling squat, pendulum/lever machines, leg press, heel elevation; a squat-learning progression and eight ways to improve squat depth; "full squats grow the glutes better than half squats"); lunges and split squats (forward, reverse, deficit, walking, curtsy, Bulgarian split squats); step-ups (bodyweight, dumbbell, ring/human-assisted accentuated); sled pushes (low-, mid- and high-handle, banded) and sled drags; quad strengthening.
- **Hamstring-dominant:** deadlifts (conventional, sumo, trap bar, RDL, stiff-leg, American, single-leg, kettlebell/dumbbell/landmine variations; deadlift categorization); good mornings (dowel, banded, barbell, B-stance); hip-dominant swings (kettlebell, banded, B-stance, American); back extension variations (45° hyper, glute-dominant rounded-back style) and equipment hacks; reverse hypers (pendulum, bent-leg); pull-throughs (cable, kneeling); knee flexion (Nordic ham curls with regressions and progressions, lying/kneeling/prone curls); glute-ham tie-in work.
- **Equipment:** buying advice (Glute Loop/mini bands, dumbbells, a bench, barbell and pad, hip thrust machines). Ankle and hip mobility drills.

When a user asks how to do a specific exercise, give setup, 3–5 key cues, common faults and a regression/progression, built from the principles above.
