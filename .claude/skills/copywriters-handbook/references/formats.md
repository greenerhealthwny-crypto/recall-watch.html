# Selling, Research, and Formats (Ch 4–15)

All points from the book *(book)* unless marked *(std)*.

## Writing to sell (Ch 4)
- **Motivating sequence:** attention → show a need → satisfy the need and position the product → prove it (testimonials, results, track record, guarantees) → ask for action (tell the reader exactly what to do; make it easy).
- AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) is the classic predecessor.
- **Features/benefits list:** list every feature, then the benefit to the buyer (Bly's example: a No. 2 pencil).
- **USP:** three Reeves requirements; if no unique feature, claim an important benefit competitors have not publicized.
- **Know the buyer:** BFD formula; 22 copy motivators; understand what prospects want before writing.
- **Positioning** (Trout and Ries) complements, not replaces, features, benefits and sales arguments.

## Getting ready to write (Ch 5)
- Gather background: previous ads, brochures, specs, press coverage, customer letters; ask questions about the product, the audience and the objective.
- **Interviewing tips:** say who you are and who referred you; let the subject pick the time; book early; do homework; be punctual; take only the notes you need; build rapport; keep a list of sources and save notes; say thank you.
- **Generating ideas (9 steps):** identify the problem; assemble pertinent facts; gather general knowledge; look for combinations; sleep on it; use a checklist; get feedback; team up; give new ideas a chance.

## Print ads (Ch 6)
- Four purposes: sell directly (mail order), generate leads, build product awareness, build corporate image.
- **Checklist:** headline has a benefit, news, curiosity or a promise; visual illustrates the main benefit; lead paragraph expands the headline; layout invites reading; body covers all sales points in order; copy gives enough information to convince; is interesting; is believable; **asks for action**.

## Direct mail (Ch 7)
- A personal, one-to-one medium.
- **15 ways to open a sales letter:** state the offer; highlight the free literature; make an announcement; tell a story; flatter the reader; write peer-to-peer; a personal message from the president; use a provocative quote; ask a question; make it personal; identify the reader's problem; stress a benefit; use human interest; let the reader in on inside information; sweepstakes.
- Envelope teasers can raise or lower response; test them.
- **"The letter sells, the brochure tells"**: the letter carries the persuasion, the brochure the details.

## Brochures, catalogs, sales materials (Ch 8)
- Know the brochure's purpose (leave-behind, response to inquiry, point of sale, direct-mail enclosure); give complete information; organize logically; tell readers what to do next.

## PR materials (Ch 9)
- Press releases must be news, written in journalistic style (who, what, when, where, why), not advertising *(book; std detail)*.

## Commercials and multimedia (Ch 10)
- Radio and TV are linear; repeat the name and offer; write for the ear and eye *(book; std detail)*.

## Web (Ch 11)
- Formats: microsite (long-copy landing page), short-copy landing page, transaction page; long-copy, teaser and lead-generation e-mails; e-mail conversion series; online and banner ads; pop-unders.
- A site should give the information prospects need to buy and the information that convinces them.
- SEO: one (at most two) unique keywords per page.

## E-mail marketing (Ch 12)
1. Write FROM and SUBJECT lines with care; the FROM line identifies you (house list) or the list owner (rented list).
2. Be careful with "free" in subject lines (spam filters).
3. Lead with a killer headline or benefit.
4. First paragraph = a mini-version of the whole message, with offer and a response link.
5. Then expanded copy: features, benefits, proof.
6. Repeat the offer and response mechanism at the close; multiple response links help.
7. Wide margins, sparing all-caps; short is generally better; get key points across quickly.
8. Tone: helpful, informative, educational, not hard-sell ("information is the gold in cyberspace").
9. Include an opt-out statement.
- Deliverability: valid reverse DNS, limited send rate, valid HTML, monitor bounces by ISP.
- Formats grid: short or long e-mail × short landing page or microsite.
- **Free-content model:** create free content, offer it online, then sell paid products to those who accept.
- **E-newsletter tips:** be a conduit; collect problems and questions; distill into a tip: state the problem, give the solution (action steps), describe the benefit.
