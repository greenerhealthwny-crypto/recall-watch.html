# Headlines and Clear Writing (Ch 2–3)

## Eight basic headline types
1. **Direct**: states the selling proposition plainly ("Pine Shirts 20% Off").
2. **Indirect**: makes its point obliquely, arousing curiosity.
3. **News**: announces something new (new product, improvement, new use).
4. **How-to**: promises useful information ("How to…").
5. **Question**: must address the reader's interest, not be a quiz.
6. **Command**: tells the reader what to do.
7. **Reason-why**: lists reasons ("7 reasons…").
8. **Testimonial**: the customer does the selling, in their words.

## Headline rules (selected)
- Put the strongest benefit up front; Ogilvy recommends including the promise and brand name.
- Avoid **blind headlines** that make no sense without the body copy ("Give It a Hand").
- Avoid irrelevant wordplay, puns and gimmicks — "they do not sell products."
- Avoid negatives: not "Contains No Sodium" but "100% Sodium-Free."
- Write several; most copywriters keep a **swipe file** of proven ads.

## The 4 U's
- **Urgent** (a reason to act now), **Unique** (new or different), **Ultra-specific** (concrete numbers and details), **Useful** (a clear benefit).
- Score 1–4 on each; aim for 3+ on at least three; rewrite to lift two or three categories by a point.

## 11 tips for writing clear copy
1. **Put the reader first** — "you-orientation"; picture a real person like your reader.
2. **Carefully organize your selling points** (logical order; follow the motivating sequence).
3. **Break the writing into short sections** (subheads, bullets).
4. **Use short sentences.**
5. **Use simple words.**
6. **Avoid technical jargon** (unless writing to specialists who use it).
7. **Be concise** — cut redundancy.
8. **Be specific** — facts, figures, details sell; generalities don't.
9. **Go straight to the point.**
10. **Write in a friendly, conversational style.**
11. **Avoid sexist language.**
