# The Copywriter's Handbook (Robert W. Bly, 3rd edition, 2005)

A working guide distilled from Robert W. Bly's *The Copywriter's Handbook*, 3rd edition (Owl Books / Henry Holt, 2005; first published 1985) *(book)*.

**Source:** the user's PDF has a text layer. Points marked *(book)* were checked in it; *(std)* is general knowledge.

**Chapters** *(book)*: 1 Introduction; 2 Headlines; 3 Writing to communicate; 4 Writing to sell; 5 Getting ready to write; 6 Print ads; 7 Direct mail; 8 Brochures, catalogs and sales materials; 9 PR materials; 10 Commercials and multimedia; 11 Web; 12 E-mail marketing; 13 Getting a copywriting job; 14 Hiring copywriters; 15 Graphic design; appendices.

## Core frameworks

1. **Copy's three jobs:** get attention, communicate, persuade *(book)*.
2. **Four functions of a headline:** get attention; select the audience; deliver a complete message; draw the reader into the body copy *(book)*.
3. **The 4 U's** (from Michael Masterson): **urgent, unique, ultra-specific, useful**. Rate a headline 1–4 on each; if it doesn't score 3–4 on at least three, rewrite it *(book)*.
4. **Bly's headline method:** ask (1) Who is my customer? (2) What are the product's important features? (3) Why will the customer buy (which feature matters most)? The answer to 3 is the key selling proposition to state in the headline *(book)*.
5. **The motivating sequence** (Bly's alternative to AIDA): **1 Get attention** (lead with the strongest benefit, not a pun) → **2 Show a need** → **3 Satisfy the need and position your product as the solution** → **4 Prove it can do what you say** → **5 Ask for action** *(book)*.
6. **Features vs benefits:** customers buy benefits; translate every feature into a reason to buy ("so what?") *(book)*.
7. **USP** (Rosser Reeves): every ad makes a proposition ("buy this, get this benefit"); one the competition cannot or does not offer; strong enough to move the mass millions. If you have no unique feature, stress an **under-publicized benefit** competitors ignore (a brewery whose bottles are washed in live steam; the book doesn't name the brand) *(book)*.
8. **BFD formula** (Masterson's "Core Complex"): write out your market's **Beliefs, Feelings, Desires** before writing copy *(book)*.
9. **22 copy motivators:** to be liked, appreciated, right, important; make money, save money, save time, make work easier; be secure, attractive, sexy, comfortable, distinctive, happy; have fun; gain knowledge; be healthy; gratify curiosity; convenience; fear; greed; guilt *(book)*.

**Workflow for writing copy:**
1. Research: product features, customer (BFD, motivators), competitors' claims; interview experts *(book)*.
2. Build a features → benefits list; pick the USP or strongest benefit.
3. Write 5–10 headlines using the 38 techniques; score each on the 4 U's; keep the best.
4. Write body copy in the motivating sequence; apply the 11 clarity tips; end with a specific call to action.
5. Check against the format checklist (print ad, letter, e-mail, landing page).

## Caveats

- 2005 edition: web and e-mail chapters predate social media, smartphones and modern deliverability rules; principles hold, tactics may not *(std)*.
- Make only truthful, substantiated claims (FTC rules on advertising, testimonials and endorsements; CAN-SPAM for e-mail) *(std)*.
