# The Daily Laws (Robert Greene)

A working guide to Robert Greene's *The Daily Laws* (2021): 366 one-page meditations, one per calendar day, drawn from all of his books (*Mastery*, *The 48 Laws of Power*, *The Art of Seduction*, *The 33 Strategies of War*, *The Laws of Human Nature*) plus a new essay, "The Law of the Sublime," used in December. Each month opens with Greene's personal essay on the theme. Each day has:
1. A **title** (e.g., *October 4 — Analyze, Scrutinize, Question*).
2. An epigraph and a short **meditation** (adapted from one of his books).
3. A one-sentence **Daily Law** that distills it, and the source chapter.

**Source coverage:** Built from the full PDF the user supplied. The calendar of all 366 titles is in `references/calendar.md`. The meditations themselves are not reproduced; write your own reflection in Greene's spirit.

## The year's arc

The book moves from **inner work** (finding your purpose and mastering a craft) to **social power** (court politics, toxic types, indirection, seduction, persuasion, strategy) and back inward to **human nature** (emotions, rationality, mortality, the sublime). Greene's preface frames it: the dangers of modern life come from people and their psychology, and naïveté and cultural fantasies about how people *should* be leave us unprepared. Each day aims to strip away one illusion.

## Monthly themes in brief

- **January — Life's Task.** Everyone is born with a unique seed; childhood inclinations and the inner "Voice" point toward your calling. Embrace your weirdness, occupy your own niche, avoid false paths (money, status, others' expectations), and let purpose guide you. Greene's own story: sixty jobs and years of wandering before *The 48 Laws*.
- **February — Apprenticeship.** Submit to reality: value learning over money, practice deeply, find the right mentor, absorb the rules of your field, enter the cycle of accelerated returns, and move toward resistance and pain rather than comfort.
- **March — The Master at Work.** After 10,000-plus hours, intuition and creativity emerge. Return to childlike curiosity, combine fields, think in wholes, use failure, and keep reinventing. There are no shortcuts; intensity and love of the work matter.
- **April — The Perfect Courtier.** The world is a court; power is unavoidable. Never outshine the master, find out who holds the strings, make accomplishments seem effortless, guard your reputation, use absence, master your emotions.
- **May — Supposed Nonplayers and Toxic Types.** Those who claim to be above power games often play hardest (displaying weakness or virtue as leverage). Judge people by behavior, not words; recognize deep narcissists, passive aggressors, enviers and the toxic types; determine the strength of people's character.
- **June — Indirection.** Wear the appropriate mask, control your image, play on people's trust in appearances, conceal intentions, control the options, let people feel superior, use absence and timing.
- **July — The Seductive Character.** See the world through a seducer's eyes: direct your attention outward, delay satisfaction, create mystery, mix pleasure and pain, appeal to fantasies and the inner child, and develop real charm (focus on the other person).
- **August — The Master Persuader.** Soften resistance: listen deeply, infect people with the right mood, confirm their self-opinion, play on competitive spirit, make them the star, use their resistance, and come to terms with your own self-opinion.
- **September — The Grand Strategist.** Rise above the battlefield: see the whole chessboard, attack the center of gravity, think in terms of the ending, avoid tactical hell, and know when to stop.
- **October — The Emotional Self.** The primary law of human nature: we are irrational, emotional, social animals. Cultivate the Inner Athena, analyze and question, beware group madness, recognize envy, aggression, grandiosity and the shadow, and see that *you* are often the obstacle.
- **November — The Rational Human.** There is hope: rationality is a skill. Increase your reaction time, keep free of the emotional whirlpool, assume you misjudge people, be patient, cultivate empathy, and find your higher self.
- **December — The Cosmic Sublime.** Open your mind to the infinite and awe-inspiring: deep time, the improbability of life, nature, other cultures, mortality. Death awareness gives urgency, dissolves petty differences and connects you to something larger than yourself.

## How to use this skill

1. **"What's today's law?" / a specific date.** Look up the date in `references/calendar.md`. Give the date and title, name the month's theme, then write an **original** short meditation (120–220 words) in Greene's voice and spirit: a historical touchstone or vivid image, the psychological insight, and a practical application. End with a one-line **Daily Law** in your own words and a **practice** for today (one concrete action or journal question). If the user shared context (job, conflict, goal), tailor the practice to it.
2. **A month or theme.** Summarize the theme and its arc using the table above, list a handful of key titles from that month, and suggest a weekly practice.
3. **A practice plan** (7, 30, 90 or 365 days). Sequence titles from the calendar that fit the user's goal (e.g., a career change → January–March; office politics → April–June; relationships → July–August; leadership → September and November; anxiety or reactivity → October–November). Give each day a title and a one-line action.
4. **A recurring reminder** (e.g., "send me a law every morning"). Offer to set it up with whatever scheduling tool is available, using today's date to pick the entry.

## Output style and ethics

- Don't reproduce Greene's meditations or long passages; paraphrase and write original reflections. Short phrases and the titles are fine.
- Keep daily entries brief and actionable; this is a contemplative format.
- The power, seduction and indirection months describe manipulation. Frame them as self-awareness and defense: recognize these tactics, compete fairly, persuade honestly. Don't help users deceive, coerce or exploit partners, employees or vulnerable people.
- For heavy topics (mortality in December, the shadow in October), stay grounded and compassionate; if the user seems to be in crisis, set the book aside and respond to them directly.
