# The Ten Scrolls in Detail

All points are from the book *(book)* unless marked *(std)*. Quotations are kept short.

## Scroll I: Habits (and the reading method)
- The only difference between those who failed and those who succeeded is **their habits**. "Good habits are the key to all success"; bad habits are "the unlocked door to failure."
- First law: **I will form good habits and become their slave.** Only a habit can subdue another habit.
- Patience: an olive tree takes a hundred years, an onion nine weeks: "I have lived as an onion plant."
- Experience is "overrated"; begin unencumbered by unnecessary knowledge.
- The method: one scroll, 30 days; silently on rising and after the midday meal, aloud before bed. Repetition reaches the "mysterious mind" that never sleeps; an act repeated becomes easy, then a pleasure, then a habit.
- *Practice (std):* calendar block three 2-minute readings; tick off 30 days; start the next scroll on day 31.

## Scroll II: Love
- Love is "the greatest secret of success in all ventures"; muscle can split a shield, only love opens hearts.
- **Look on all things with love**: the sun, the rain, light and darkness.
- **Speak with love:** "laud mine enemies and they will become friends; … encourage my friends and they will become brothers." Dig for reasons to applaud; bite your tongue before criticizing.
- **Act with love:** love the ambitious (they inspire), failures (they teach), kings (they are human), the meek, the rich, the poor.
- Greet everyone by silently saying **"I love you"**; it shows in your eyes and voice.
- *Practice (std):* before each call, silently say the phrase and find one genuine thing to praise.

## Scroll III: Persistence
- Young bulls are rated by how many times they charge despite the lance; **"I am a lion and I refuse to talk, to walk, to sleep with the sheep."**
- Never consider defeat; remove words like *quit, cannot, unable, impossible, hopeless* from your vocabulary.
- Each day's effort is "one blow of my blade" against an oak.
- **Law of averages:** each failure increases the chance of success at the next attempt; "Each nay I hear will bring me closer to the sound of yea." "I must fail often to succeed only once."
- **One more attempt:** when tired at day's end, resist going home; try again, and if that fails, try once more.
- *Practice (std):* set a daily minimum of attempts plus one "persistence call" after you want to stop.

## Scroll IV: Self-worth
- No one before, now or after has your mind, heart, eyes and voice: **you are rare, and rarity has value**.
- Capitalize on your difference; do not imitate others.
- You were not born to fail; multiply your talents, and apply all efforts to the present.
- Look beyond people's garments; see value in yourself and others.
- *Practice (std):* list your 3 differentiators; build your pitch around them.

## Scroll V: Live each day as if it is your last
- Seal the day's "container of life"; waste no moment on yesterday's defeats or tomorrow's worries. "Will the sun rise twice this morning?"
- Avoid time-killers; idleness is stealing food and warmth from those you love.
- Make every hour count; labor until muscles cry for relief, then continue; make more calls, sell more goods.
- "And if it is my last, it will be my greatest monument."
- *Practice (std):* each morning write the one result that would make today your best day.

## Scroll VI: Master your emotions
- Moods move in cycles like the tides and seasons; today's dead flower carries the seed of tomorrow's bloom.
- **Counter-actions:** "If I feel depressed I will sing. If I feel sad I will laugh. If I feel ill I will double my labor. If I feel fear I will plunge ahead. If I feel inferior I will wear new garments. If I feel uncertain I will raise my voice. If I feel poverty I will think of wealth to come. If I feel incompetent I will remember past success. If I feel insignificant I will remember my goals."
- And the reverse, against overconfidence: complacency → remember competition; great wealth → remember one unfed mouth; pride → remember a moment of weakness; unmatched skill → look at the stars.
- **Read others' moods:** make allowances for a buyer's anger today; tomorrow he may be different. Recognizing moods is "my key to great wealth."
- *Practice (std):* write your own "If I feel X, I will Y" list on a card.

## Scroll VII: Laugh at the world
- Only humans can laugh; laughter aids digestion, lightens burdens, lengthens life.
- **Laugh at yourself**: "man is most comical when he takes himself too seriously."
- Four words restore balance: **"This too shall pass."** Apply to sorrow and to success alike.
- Laughter reduces failures and successes to their true size; shed only tears of sweat.
- *Practice (std):* when a deal falls through, say the four words, then make the next call.

## Scroll VIII: Multiply your value a hundredfold
- A mulberry leaf "touched with the genius of man becomes silk"; you can do the same with yourself.
- **Set goals** for the day, week, month, year and life. Take your best past performance and **multiply it a hundredfold**.
- Aim high: better to aim the spear at the moon and hit an eagle than aim at the eagle and hit a rock.
- Stumbling is normal ("Only a worm is free from the worry of stumbling").
- **Surpass your own deeds, not others'.** Announce goals to the world, then live up to them; receive praise with humility.
- *Practice (std):* write goals at four horizons; review weekly against yesterday's performance.

## Scroll IX: Act now
- "My dreams are worthless, my plans are dust, my goals are impossible. All are of no value unless they are followed by action."
- No map ever carried its owner an inch; no law prevented a crime.
- Repeat **"I will act now"** hourly until it is as habitual as breathing; use it on waking, when tempted to delay, when facing a door you fear to open.
- "Tomorrow is the day reserved for the labor of the lazy." It ends: "This is the time. This is the place. I am the man."
- *Practice (std):* use the phrase as a two-minute rule: when you think of a task, start it immediately.

## Scroll X: Pray for guidance
- In disaster every creature cries out to God; that instinct is a gift.
- Pray **only for guidance**, never for gold, love, health or victories.
- **The salesman's prayer:** ask not for opportunities equal to your ability, but for **ability equal to your opportunities**; teach me to hunt with words and prosper with love; keep me humble in failure; give me courage to laugh at my fears; guide my words; silence me from gossip. It ends: "Guide me. Help me. Show me the way."
- *Practice (std):* use it as a morning reflection; adapt the language for non-religious users (an intention or reflection).

## The ending
- Hafid hands the scrolls to **Paul**, the one he had waited for, completing the chain begun by Pathros *(book)*.
- The book closes with a preview of *Part II: The End of the Story*, in which Hafid, at sixty, resolves to begin "a second life" of helping others *(book)*.
