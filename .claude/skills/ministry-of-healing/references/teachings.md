## §1 The True Medical Missionary

- **Christ our example.** Jesus spent more time healing than preaching; he went to the homes of the poor, healed the sick, and taught. Health ministry is gospel ministry.
- **Days of Ministry.** Pictures of Christ among crowds of the sick and children; tireless, personal and compassionate.
- **With Nature and with God.** Christ found renewal in early-morning prayer and in nature; workers need the same rhythm of withdrawal and service.
- **The Touch of Faith.** Distinguishes casual contact from living faith that receives healing; faith is trusting God's word and acting on it.
- **Healing of the Soul.** Physical healing and forgiveness go together (the paralytic, the woman at the well); guilt and sin weigh on health.
- **Saved to Serve.** Those who are healed become witnesses (the demoniac of Gadara): "go home to thy friends, and tell them."

**Application:** frame any health program as compassionate service first; personal contact matters more than preaching.

## §2 The Work of the Physician

- **Source of healing.** All healing power is from God; the physician cooperates with God's laws.
- **Sin and disease.** White taught that disease often results from violating natural law ("the laws of nature, as truly as the precepts of the decalogue, are divine," 113). *Handle carefully*: present this as her theological view; never blame individuals for their illness.
- **The gospel of health; ministry to the soul.** The physician has unique access to people's hearts and should offer hope and spiritual support, with tact.
- **The Physician an Educator.** The physician should teach patients the causes of disease and how to prevent it.
- **The Use of Drugs (~126).** She criticized the "free use of poisonous drugs" and patent nostrums of her day, by which people sought relief from symptoms without addressing causes.
- **Natural Remedies (127).** "In case of sickness, the cause should be ascertained. Unhealthful conditions should be changed, wrong habits corrected. Then nature is to be assisted in her effort to expel impurities and to reestablish right conditions in the system." Then: "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power,—these are the true remedies. Every person should have a knowledge of nature's remedial agencies and how to apply them."
- **Preservation of health; training for life's conflict; teaching in the home.** Prevention and education in homes are central.
- **The Physician and the temperance work; the physician's personal needs and perils.** The doctor must model temperance and guard his own spiritual life.

## §3 Medical Missionaries and Their Work

- **Teaching and Healing (139).** Every church member can be a medical missionary. **Christ's method (143):** "Christ's method alone will give true success in reaching the people. The Saviour mingled with men as one who desired their good. He showed His sympathy for them, ministered to their needs, and won their confidence. Then He bade them, 'Follow Me.'" Also: more personal effort, less sermonizing; "little opportunities"; teach health principles; missionary nurses.
- **Helping the Tempted (161).** Tact and sympathy; "our want of compassion"; reach hearts by kindness; point out errors gently; don't be discouraged by relapses; people are "saved by hope."
- **Working for the Intemperate (171).** Addiction is a disease of body and will; help with food, rest, and a healthful environment as well as prayer. **The power of the will (~176):** "This is the governing power in the nature of man,—the power of decision, of choice. Everything depends on the right action of the will." We cannot change our hearts by willpower, but we can choose to serve God, and he works in us. Those rescued help rescue others.
- **Help for the Unemployed and the Homeless (183).** Industrial training; consideration for the poor; opportunities for the homeless; leave crowded city slums for homes in the country; establish industries; missionary families settling in needy areas; **help men to help themselves (~195)**; object lessons in thrift and work.
- **The Helpless Poor (201).** Care for "the household of faith," widows and orphans, and the aged.
- **Ministry to the Rich (209).** The wealthy are often spiritually hungry and neglected; reach them with tact and give them a vision of service.

**Application:** recovery ministries, job-skills programs, food security, community gardens, and senior care all fit this part.

## §4 The Care of the Sick

- **In the Sick-Room (219).** Attendants should be cheerful, calm and clean; keep the room quiet, ventilated and sunny; limit visitors who tire the patient; institutional nursing needs Christian character.
- **Prayer for the Sick (225).** Conditions of answered prayer; confession of sin; submission to God's will ("whatever the outcome of the case, let us not lose faith in God"). Praying for healing is not opposed to using means: "Christ made use of the simple agencies of nature … He sanctioned the use of simple and natural remedies" (233).
- **The Use of Remedies (233).** Rational remedies.
  - **Rest as a remedy (~236):** "Some make themselves sick by overwork. For these, rest, freedom from care, and a spare diet, are essential to restoration of health." Time in the country and nature helps the "brain weary and nervous."
  - **Water (237):** "In health and in sickness, pure water is one of heaven's choicest blessings." Drink freely; external applications (hydrotherapy) are simple and effective.
  - **Exercise (237–238):** "Action is a law of our being." "Inactivity is a fruitful cause of disease. Exercise quickens and equalizes the circulation of the blood."
- **Mind-Cure (241).** "The relation that exists between the mind and the body is very intimate. When one is affected, the other sympathizes. The condition of the mind affects the health to a far greater degree than many realize. Many of the diseases from which men suffer are the result of mental depression. Grief, anxiety, discontent, remorse, guilt, distrust, all tend to break down the life forces." And: "Courage, hope, faith, sympathy, love, promote health and prolong life. A contented mind, a cheerful spirit, is health to the body and strength to the soul" (241). Remedies: sympathy, hope, faith, cheerful occupation, helping others. White warned against hypnotism-like "mind controlling mind" (243). **Gratitude and Praise (251):** "Nothing tends more to promote health of body and of soul than does a spirit of gratitude and praise. It is a positive duty to resist melancholy, discontented thoughts and feelings." Sing praises; trust the healing promises.
- **In Contact with Nature (261).** Sanitariums should be in the country; outdoor life, gardening and flowers aid recovery; "Exercise in the open air should be prescribed as a life-giving necessity. And for such exercises there is nothing better than the cultivation of the soil" (265). Teach patients "to breathe deeply, and in breathing and speaking to exercise the abdominal muscles" (265).

## §5 Health Principles

- **General Hygiene (271).** Circulation of the blood; respiration; **pure air (274):** "The lungs are constantly throwing off impurities, and they need to be constantly supplied with fresh air." Thorough ventilation and **sunlight** in homes; prevention of disease; clean surroundings.
- **Hygiene among the Israelites (277).** Cleanliness, sanitation and dietary laws given to Israel; outdoor festivals and social life. "Gratitude, rejoicing, benevolence, trust in God's love and care,—these are health's greatest safeguard" (281).
- **Dress (287).** Simple, modest, healthful, warm (especially the extremities), loose enough for free breathing and movement; independent of fashion. She opposed corsets and heavy, dragging skirts that harmed women's health.
- **Diet and Health (295).** "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator. These foods, prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible, are the most healthful and nourishing" (296). Choose foods to fit climate, occupation and individual needs; variety across meals, but not too many dishes at one meal; learn to cook well ("she who has not the help of such facilities should put herself under the instruction of some good cook"). **Regularity (303):** "Regularity in eating is of vital importance. There should be a specified time for each meal." No snacking between meals. **Meal spacing (304):** "At least five or six hours should intervene between the meals; and most persons who give the plan a trial, will find that two meals a day are better than three." Don't eat food very hot or very cold; avoid **overeating**; eat in a cheerful, unhurried setting. **Abstemiousness (307):** "Abstemiousness in diet is rewarded with mental and moral vigor; it also aids in the control of the passions." Sedentary workers should eat sparingly and exercise.
- **Flesh as Food (311).** Meat was not part of the original diet; she gave reasons for discarding it (animal disease, second-hand nutrition, effects on the mind and passions). "Flesh was never the best food; but its use is now doubly objectionable, since disease in animals is so rapidly increasing" (~312). **Changing the diet:** change gradually; the initial weakness passes; replace meat with well-prepared nourishing foods.
- **Extremes in Diet (318).** "Let your moderation be known unto all men." Some "reformers" adopt an impoverished, meager diet that lacks variety; "their health suffers, their usefulness is impaired, and their example tells against rather than in favor of reform in diet." Provide wholesome, palatable food; individual needs differ ("Some can not use milk, while others thrive on it"); where other foods to supply the needed elements can't be obtained, "milk and eggs should not be wholly discarded," though she expected diet reform to be "progressive" and to move away from them over time (320–321); "the narrow ideas of some would-be health reformers have been a great injury to the cause of hygiene" (~323).
- **Stimulants and Narcotics (325).** Hot condiments and spices irritate and create cravings. **Tea and coffee (326):** stimulants that give a false lift followed by depression. Tobacco is a slow poison. Intoxicating drinks wreck lives; "milder intoxicants" (wine, beer, cider) lead toward stronger ones; parents' responsibility for children's appetites.
- **The Liquor Traffic and Prohibition (337).** Strong support for prohibition and against license laws; the church's responsibility.

## §6 The Home

- **Ministry of the Home (349).** The home is the first sphere of influence and hospitality.
- **The Builders of the Home (356).** Marriage requires careful choice, maturity, self-sacrifice and mutual respect; happiness comes from unselfish service.
- **Choice and Preparation of the Home (363).** Country homes with land for gardens; simplicity in furnishing; beautiful natural surroundings rather than costly display.
- **The Mother (371).** Her health and peace affect her children; avoid overwork; cheerfulness; her opportunity to shape character.
- **The Child (379).** Care of infants; simple, warm, unrestrictive clothing; regular, simple diet; care in sickness; teach physiology.
- **Home Influences (388).** Parents as teachers; responsibility; companionship with children.
- **True Education a Missionary Training (395).** Train children for service; home teaching; the school; "learning by imparting."

## §7 The Essential Knowledge

- **A True Knowledge of God (409).** God is a personal being revealed in creation, providence and supremely in Christ. "Nature is not God" (against pantheism).
- **Danger in Speculative Knowledge (427).** Warnings against pantheistic theories and speculation about God.
- **The False and the True in Education (439).** She cautioned against infidel authors, sensational literature, romance, and some myths and fairy tales; favored knowledge that can be used and "heart education."
- **Importance of Seeking True Knowledge (451); The Knowledge Received through God's Word (458).** Study Scripture experientially; it aids the study of nature; growth into "education in the life eternal."

## §8 The Worker's Need

- **Help in Daily Living (469).** The discipline of trial; true greatness is in service, not position; trust God with plans for the future; "God will provide."
- **In Contact with Others (483).** **Speak no evil (489):** "If we have a sense of the long-suffering of God toward us, we shall not be found judging or accusing others." Courtesy; the importance of little things; patience with the erring.
- **Development and Service (497).** Force of character; mental culture; the motive in service; singleness of purpose.
- **A Higher Experience (503).** The joy of the Lord; time with God; the privilege of prayer; God as counselor.

## §9 Modern-evidence notes and safety guardrails

**Well supported today (present confidently, with modern framing):**
- Mostly whole-food, plant-predominant diets (grains, legumes, fruits, vegetables, nuts) are associated with lower rates of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers. Adventist Health Studies found longer life expectancy among Adventist vegetarians in California (Loma Linda is a Blue Zone).
- Avoiding tobacco and limiting or avoiding alcohol; regular physical activity; fresh air and ventilation; adequate rest and sleep; time in nature and gardening; hydration; social support, purpose, gratitude and faith community, all of which are linked to better health outcomes.
- Mind–body connection: chronic stress, depression and loneliness affect physical health.
- Regular meal timing and avoiding constant snacking; some evidence for time-restricted eating, though "two meals a day" isn't right for everyone.

**Needs nuance or a modern update:**
- **"Drugs."** White's warnings targeted the toxic patent medicines of her era. Modern evidence-based medications, vaccines and surgery save lives. Never advise stopping prescribed treatment; encourage combining lifestyle changes with medical care.
- **Disease as the result of sin or broken natural law.** Lifestyle affects health, but much illness comes from genetics, infection, injury, environment and chance. Never imply that a sick person is to blame.
- **Vegetarian or vegan diets** need planning for vitamin B12 (supplement or fortified foods), vitamin D, iron, zinc, iodine, calcium and omega-3s, especially for pregnancy, infants, children and older adults. White herself warned against impoverished diets.
- **Two meals a day / long gaps between meals** may be unsuitable for children, pregnant or breastfeeding women, people with diabetes on certain medications, athletes, people with a history of eating disorders, and frail older adults.
- **Hot/cold food rules, tea and coffee, spices.** Modern evidence on moderate coffee and tea is mostly neutral to favorable; present her view as her conviction rather than as current science.
- **Hydrotherapy.** Useful for comfort and recovery; not a cure for serious disease; caution with extremes of temperature for people with heart conditions, diabetes or neuropathy.
- **Literature, fairy tales, fashion, gender roles.** Present as 1905 views in their historical context.

**Always refer to professionals** for diagnosis, medication changes, therapeutic diets, eating disorders, addiction treatment (pair spiritual support with evidence-based treatment), mental health crises, and pregnancy.

## §10 Turning the book into programs, classes and handouts

- **Eight-remedies series (8 sessions):** one remedy per week with a short reading (quote + MH page), the modern evidence, one practical habit and a small group discussion. The remedies map onto the NEWSTART acronym used by Adventist lifestyle programs: Nutrition, Exercise, Water, Sunlight, Temperance, Air, Rest, Trust in God.
- **Cooking class:** simple plant-based meals from grains, legumes, fruits, vegetables and nuts; emphasize flavor and variety (her warning against unappetizing "reform" tables); include B12 guidance.
- **Recovery ministry:** use "Helping the Tempted" and "Working for the Intemperate" (tact, hope, the power of choice, practical care) alongside professional addiction treatment.
- **Community outreach:** "Christ's method alone" as the model: meet needs (health screenings, food, job skills), build trust, and only then invite to faith, without making help conditional.
- **Devotional / Bible study:** pair chapters with their Scripture references (the book has a scriptural index); e.g., "The Touch of Faith" with Mark 5; "Gratitude and Praise" with Proverbs 17:22.
- **Handouts:** quote, plain-language explanation, one actionable step, and a line encouraging readers to consult their health-care provider before major changes.
