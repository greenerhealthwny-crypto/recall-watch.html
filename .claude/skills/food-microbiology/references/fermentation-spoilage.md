## §1 Lactic acid bacteria (Ch 19)

- **Fermentation** in the biochemical sense is energy generation in the absence of oxygen, using an internal organic compound as the electron acceptor. It yields only 1–2 ATP per glucose, far less than respiration *(book)*. In food use, the word broadly means microbial transformation of a food.
- **Lactic acid bacteria (LAB):** Gram-positive, non-sporing, acid tolerant, many GRAS. Genera include *Lactococcus*, *Streptococcus thermophilus*, *Lactobacillus* and related genera, *Leuconostoc*, *Pediococcus*, *Oenococcus*.
  - **Homofermentative** LAB make only lactic acid *(book)*.
  - **Heterofermentative** LAB make lactic acid plus CO₂ and ethanol or acetate (flavour, gas holes) *(std)*.
- **Why ferment:** preservation (acid, low pH), flavour and texture, and safety, since fast acidification stops *S. aureus* and other pathogens. That is why acid development is a critical control point in cheese and fermented sausage *(book)*.
- **Starter cultures:**
  - Pasteurizing first removes most native LAB, so the added starter faces little competition. This gives control of acidification rate and a consistent product *(book)*.
  - The typical inoculum is 0.5–5% starter for cultured dairy *(book)*.
  - **Bacteriophage** infection of starters is a major industrial problem (slow vats). It is handled by strain rotation and phage-resistant strains *(std)*.
  - LAB exchange genes (conjugation, transduction, transformation), and many key traits sit on plasmids *(book)*.
- **Dairy:**
  - **Yogurt:** milk with added solids, heated, cooled; *S. thermophilus* plus *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus* incubated until the target pH *(book)*.
  - **Cheese:** coagulate (rennet and/or acid), cut, cook, drain whey, salt, press, ripen. Cottage cheese can be "direct set" with acid instead of a culture *(book)*.
  - **Cultured buttermilk, sour cream:** *Lactococcus* plus aroma-producing *Leuconostoc* (diacetyl) *(std)*.
- **Vegetables (sauerkraut, pickles, kimchi):**
  - Salt (about 2–2.5% for kraut *(std)*) selects for LAB.
  - The natural succession runs *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* → *Lactobacillus brevis* / *L. plantarum*. Storage can last up to a year *(book)*.
- **Meats (fermented sausage):**
  - Add curing salts (nitrite < 150 ppm), about 3% glucose and starter at about 10⁷ CFU/g; blend; stuff into oxygen-impermeable casings; incubate *(book)*.
  - The starter drops pH from about 5.6 to 4.8 within about 8 h. This coagulates protein and speeds drying; some products are heated afterwards *(book)*.
  - **Starter history:** indigenous flora, then back-slopping, then freeze-dried *Pediococcus acidilactici* (homofermentative, tolerates 6% salt, grows at 43–50 °C, which is above where pathogens grow), then frozen cultures with short lag times *(book)*.
- **Alkaline fermentations** (e.g. "stinky tofu" brine) raise pH as ammonia is made; LAB and *Bacillus* dominate *(book)*.

## §2 Yeast-based and other fermentations (Ch 20)

- **Beer:**
  - **Malting** germinates the grain and frees hydrolytic enzymes.
  - **Mashing** converts starch to fermentable sugars.
  - The wort is boiled with hops.
  - Fermentation by *Saccharomyces* follows: ale yeast ferments warm at the top, lager yeast ferments cool at the bottom.
  - Then clarification, pasteurization and bottling *(book/std)*.
- **Wine:**
  - Technically simpler than beer, since the sugars are already in the grape *(book)*.
  - Inoculate *S. cerevisiae* or *S. bayanus* (about 10⁶–10⁷ CFU/g) for consistency. Native yeasts can give an extraordinary or a bad wine *(book)*.
  - White wines are fermented cooler and longer; red wines warmer (20–30 °C) and shorter (about 7 days) to extract colour *(book)*.
  - **Malolactic fermentation** by *Oenococcus oeni* removes malic acid *(book)*.
  - Pasteur discovered microbial fermentation and wine spoilage, and pasteurization was first invented for wine *(book)*.
- **Bread:** yeast CO₂ leavens dough; sourdough adds LAB *(std)*.
- **Vinegar:** an oxidative fermentation of ethanol to acetic acid by *Acetobacter* / *Gluconobacter*. Oxygen transfer is the rate-limiting step, solved by trickling (generator) and submerged fermentors, which cut the time from months to a day or two *(book)*.
- **Others:**
  - **Sake:** koji mold (*Aspergillus oryzae*) saccharifies rice, then yeast ferments *(book)*.
  - **Kefir:** grains of yeasts and bacteria give a sour, carbonated, slightly alcoholic drink after overnight fermentation at room temperature *(book)*.
  - **Soy sauce and miso** use koji *(std)*.
  - **Cocoa:** mucilage (pulp) is fermented by a yeast → LAB → acetic acid bacteria succession over about 2–8 days. This is essential to chocolate flavour and still "an art"; the succession prevents starter use. Beans are then sun-dried, roasted and ground *(book)*.
  - **Coffee:** fermentation removes mucilage and matters far less for quality than in cocoa *(book)*.
- **Indigenous fermentations** preserve local crops and can improve nutrition *(book)*.

## §3 Spoilage organisms (Ch 21)

**General.** Spoilage is any change that makes food unacceptable: off-odours, slime, gas, discolouration, texture loss. Which organisms win depends on intrinsic and extrinsic factors. Off-odours typically appear around 10⁷ CFU/cm² and slime around 10⁸ on meat *(std)*.

- **Fresh red meat and poultry:**
  - Muscle of healthy animals is essentially sterile; contamination comes from hide, hooves, gut contents, feathers and equipment *(book)*.
  - **Aerobic chilled storage:** psychrotrophic *Pseudomonas* dominates, using glucose first, then amino acids. That causes putrid odours and slime, with proteolysis only in very large populations *(book)*. *Acinetobacter*, *Moraxella* and *Shewanella putrefaciens* (H₂S, greening) also contribute.
  - **Vacuum or MAP:** slower growth, a lower final population and LAB dominance. Sour, cheesy odours come from short-chain fatty acids and amines. *Brochothrix thermosphacta* spoils faster than LAB *(book)*. Blown packs can come from psychrotrophic clostridia.
  - Ground meat: aerobic flora on the surface, LAB inside *(book)*.
  - **Interventions:** carcass washes (organic acids, chiefly lactic and acetic; chlorine) and low-dose irradiation (< 5 kGy) extend shelf life *(book)*.
- **Fish and shellfish:**
  - Flora reflect the water, season and handling *(book)*.
  - Spoilage by *Shewanella putrefaciens* (TMAO → TMA, "fishy"; H₂S) and *Photobacterium phosphoreum* *(std)*.
  - Scombroid fish (tuna, mackerel) can accumulate **histamine** from bacterial decarboxylation, causing scombroid poisoning *(std)*.
- **Milk and dairy:**
  - Raw milk must be cooled to 7 °C within 2 h of milking (US sanitary standards); most farms reach < 4 °C *(book)*.
  - **Psychrotrophs** (*Pseudomonas*) make **heat-stable proteases and lipases** that survive pasteurization, causing bitterness and rancidity even after the cells are killed *(book)*.
  - Pasteurized milk is expected to last 14–20 days. Post-pasteurization contamination of even one psychrotroph per container can spoil it *(book)*.
  - Coliforms and LAB are controlled by sanitation, cold raw milk, pasteurization and refrigeration *(book)*.
  - **Cheese:** lipases matter more than proteases. "Early gas" (early blowing) comes from coliforms when acidification is slow. Late gas comes from *Clostridium tyrobutyricum*. Heterofermentative lactobacilli also make gas in aged Cheddar *(book/std)*. Lysozyme is used in Europe against blowing *(book)*.
  - Joke from the book: yogurt is spoiled when it starts to look like cheese, and cheese is spoiled when it starts to look like regular cheese *(book)*.
- **Produce:**
  - Plant tissue defences (cuticle, cell walls) are breached by pectinolytic bacteria (*Pectobacterium* / *Erwinia*, *Pseudomonas* — soft rot) and molds *(book/std)*.
  - Fruits, being acidic, spoil mainly by **molds and yeasts**. Yeasts grow faster in juices *(book)*.
  - Orange juice can get a buttermilk-like flavour from LAB (diacetyl).
  - *Alicyclobacillus* causes a disinfectant taint in juice *(book, Ch 3)*.
  - Dried fruits spoil only by osmophilic yeasts or xerotolerant molds *(book)*.
- **Canned foods:** **flat sour** (thermophilic *Geobacillus*); swells from thermophilic anaerobes (e.g. *Thermoanaerobacterium thermosaccharolyticum*, H₂ + CO₂) *(book)*; leakage spoilage (mixed flora, usually from contaminated cooling water).
- **Grains and bakery:** molds (and mycotoxins); "rope" in bread from *Bacillus* *(std)*.

## §4 Molds and mycotoxins (Ch 22)

- **Key genera:** *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Fusarium* are the most common in foods *(book)*. Molds are identified by colony characteristics (diameter, conidial colour) and microscopic morphology, on media that suppress bacteria *(book)*. Some grow at low or very low temperatures and low a_w.
- **Mycotoxins** are toxic secondary metabolites, mostly from molds contaminating plant commodities, such as grains, nuts and oilseeds, before or after harvest *(book)*.
  - **Aflatoxins** (B₁, B₂, G₁, G₂; M₁ in milk):
    - From *A. flavus* and *A. parasiticus*. Acute liver damage; B₁ is a potent liver carcinogen *(book/std)*.
    - Found in peanuts, corn, tree nuts and cottonseed, especially under drought or poor storage *(book)*.
    - The 1960 **turkey "X" disease** in England (about 100,000 turkeys, from contaminated peanut meal) led to their discovery *(book)*. The book also describes a Kenyan maize outbreak with 125 deaths *(book)*.
    - Ammoniation and some oxidizers (ozone, H₂O₂) can reduce aflatoxin levels in commodities *(book)*.
  - **Ochratoxin A** (*Aspergillus ochraceus*, *Penicillium verrucosum*): kidney toxin in cereals, coffee and wine *(std)*.
  - **Patulin** (*Penicillium expansum*): in apple juice from rotten apples *(std)*.
  - ***Fusarium* toxins:**
    - **Trichothecenes:** type A (T-2, HT-2, diacetoxyscirpenol); type B (deoxynivalenol/"vomitoxin", nivalenol) *(book)*.
    - **Zearalenone:** estrogenic *(book)*.
    - **Fumonisins** (*F. verticillioides*, *F. proliferatum*): in corn; linked to esophageal cancer in high-incidence regions *(book)*.
  - **Ergot alkaloids** (*Claviceps purpurea* on rye and wheat). Ergotism has been suggested as a cause of the behaviour behind the 1692 Salem witch trials *(book)*.
  - Other genera: *Alternaria* (fruits, vegetables, grains), *Cladosporium*, *Trichothecium* and others *(book)*.
- **Detection:** TLC and HPLC are the most common, plus GC and GC–MS, and immunoassay kits for screening and quantitation (deoxynivalenol, zearalenone, T-2, fumonisins) *(book)*.
- **Control:**
  - Good agricultural practice, drying grain quickly to safe moisture, cool and dry storage, insect control, and sorting out damaged kernels.
  - Testing and regulatory limits *(std)*.
  - Cooking does **not** destroy most mycotoxins.
