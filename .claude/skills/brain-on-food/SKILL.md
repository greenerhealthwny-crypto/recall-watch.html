# This Is Your Brain on Food (Uma Naidoo, MD, 2020)

A working guide distilled from Dr. Uma Naidoo's *This Is Your Brain on Food*. Naidoo is a Harvard-trained psychiatrist, a professionally trained chef (Cambridge School of Culinary Arts) and a nutrition specialist. She calls her field **nutritional psychiatry** *(book)*.

Her starting point is that diet and mental health are "inextricably linked, and the connection between them goes both ways". Poor food choices raise the risk of mental-health problems, and those problems in turn lead to poor eating. "Until we solve nutritional problems, no amount of medication and psychotherapy is going to be able to stem the tide" *(book)*. She describes using her own advice during treatment for breast cancer *(book)*.

**Structure** *(book)*:
1. The Gut-Brain Romance
2. Depression: probiotics, omega-3s and the Mediterranean eating pattern
3. Anxiety: fermented foods, dietary fiber and the tryptophan myth
4. PTSD: glutamates, blueberries and "Old Friends" bacteria
5. ADHD: gluten, milk caseins and polyphenols
6. Dementia and brain fog: microgreens, rosemary and the MIND diet
7. OCD: NAC, glycine and the dangers of orthorexia nervosa
8. Insomnia and fatigue: capsaicin, chamomile and anti-inflammatory diets
9. Bipolar disorder and schizophrenia
10. Libido: oxytocin, fenugreek and the science of aphrodisiacs
11. Cooking and eating for your brain

## Core ideas

1. **The gut-brain axis.** Gut and brain develop from the same embryonic tissue and stay connected through the **vagus nerve**, the **HPA (stress) axis**, the immune system and inflammation, and neurotransmitters and other chemicals made by gut bacteria *(book)*. Changing the microbiome changes mood, anxiety, memory and sleep. The influence runs both ways.
2. **Whole eating patterns beat single foods.** The **Mediterranean eating pattern** is her default for depression and ADHD. The **MIND diet** is her pick for memory. A **keto diet** has evidence for bipolar disorder *(book)*.
   - **SMILES trial:** Felice Jacka's 12-week trial tested diet as an add-on treatment for depression *(book)*.
   - In a roughly 10-year observational study, people who followed the Mediterranean diet most closely were least likely to develop depression *(book)*.
3. **What recurs on the "avoid" lists:**
   - the **Western diet** (bad fats from red and fried meat, and high-glycemic carbs such as white bread, rice, potatoes and pasta);
   - added **sugar** and high-fructose corn syrup;
   - **fried foods**;
   - **trans fats** and too much omega-6;
   - **artificial sweeteners** (aspartame especially);
   - **gluten**, for people with celiac disease or non-celiac gluten sensitivity;
   - too much **caffeine** (under 400 mg a day) and **alcohol**;
   - **MSG and glutamates** for PTSD and OCD.

   [all *(book)*]
4. **What recurs on the "embrace" lists:**
   - **omega-3s** from fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring, sardines);
   - **probiotics and fermented foods**;
   - **prebiotics and fiber**;
   - low-glycemic carbs;
   - healthy fats (olive oil, nuts, avocado);
   - B vitamins, vitamins C, D and E, and magnesium and zinc;
   - **turmeric with black pepper**, saffron, and herbs such as chamomile, lavender and passionflower;
   - **polyphenols** (berries, green tea, colorful vegetables).

   [all *(book)*]
5. **The tryptophan myth.** Turkey alone won't make you sleepy or calm, because little dietary tryptophan reaches the brain. Pairing tryptophan with carbohydrates helps, for example turkey with mashed potatoes, or cereal with milk *(book)*.
6. **Balance, not obsession.** She warns about **orthorexia nervosa**, an unhealthy fixation on eating "correctly", and says treatment is individual *(book)*.
7. **Food supports treatment; it doesn't replace it.** She uses nutrition alongside medication and therapy, and recommends coordinating with the treating doctor *(book)*.

**Workflow for a meal plan or advice:**
1. Identify the concern (or concerns) and any medications, allergies, celiac disease, pregnancy or eating-disorder history.
2. Start from the matching cheat sheet. When concerns overlap, such as depression with anxiety, merge the lists. Naidoo herself says the anxiety advice also applies to OCD *(book)*.
3. Build meals around a Mediterranean-style base, adding the condition-specific foods, and remove the "avoid" items.
4. Add the relevant safety notes from `kitchen-and-safety.md`, for example passionflower with blood thinners or MAOIs, grapefruit and tyramine with some bipolar drugs, sodium with lithium, and side effects of keto.
5. Suggest gradual changes and tracking how they feel over weeks. Her patients typically improved over weeks to months *(book)*.

## Safety rules (always apply)

- **Educational, not medical advice.** Never tell someone to start, stop or change psychiatric medication. Frame supplements as things to discuss with their doctor.
- **Crisis first.** If someone mentions suicidal thoughts, self-harm, psychosis or mania, give crisis resources before any nutrition content. In the US, call or text **988** *(std)*.
- **Flag interactions.** These are in `kitchen-and-safety.md`.
- **Watch for disordered eating.** Don't push restriction on someone with signs of an eating disorder or orthorexia.
- Use Naidoo's vocabulary: "nutritional psychiatry", "gut-brain connection", "cheat sheet", "foods to embrace", "foods to avoid".
- Quote short phrases only, and point to the book's recipes by page instead of reproducing them.
