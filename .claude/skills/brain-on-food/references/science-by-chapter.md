# The Science and Reasoning, Chapter by Chapter

*(book)* = confirmed in the OCR of the user's copy. *(std)* = general knowledge, not from the book. For exact food lists, see `cheat-sheets.md`.

## Ch 1: The Gut-Brain Romance

- **Gut and brain share embryonic origins.** Once nineteenth-century medicine specialized, it lost sight of the "nervous sympathy" between organs. Nutritional psychiatry is one of the newest frontiers *(book)*.
- **How they talk to each other** *(book)*:
  - **The vagus nerve** runs from the brain stem to the gut and carries signals both ways.
  - **The HPA axis** (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal stress system).
  - **The immune system and inflammation.**
  - **Chemicals made by gut bacteria** (neurotransmitters, short-chain fatty acids) can reach the brain, much as a swallowed pill does.
- "More than 90 percent of serotonin receptors are found in the gut" *(book)*. Some researchers think the brain's serotonin deficit in depression is heavily influenced by the gut.
- **Dysbiosis** (an unbalanced microbiome) is associated with depression, anxiety, OCD, insomnia, low libido, schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. Some bacterial combinations shift dopamine and are linked with Parkinson's and Alzheimer's *(book)*.
- **Stress changes the microbiome.** For example, it depletes *Lactobacillus* *(book)*.
- **Scale of the problem** *(book)*:
  - 1 in 5 US adults has a diagnosable mental-health condition in a given year, and 46% will meet the criteria at some point.
  - About 70% of Americans are above an optimal weight.
  - About 30.3 million people have diabetes, diagnosed or not.

## Ch 2: Depression

- **The SMILES trial** (Supporting the Modification of Lifestyle in Lowered Emotional States), led by Felice Jacka at Deakin University's Food and Mood Centre. It was a 12-week trial testing dietary change as an *add-on* treatment for depression *(book)*.
  - The trial reported significantly greater improvement with diet than with social support *(std, check the paper for figures)*.
- **Long-term observational data:** about 10 years after one study began, 666 people had developed depression. Those who most closely followed the Mediterranean diet were significantly less likely to be among them *(book)*.
- **Other traditional diets:** the Norwegian (Nordic) diet and the traditional Japanese diet, which is rich in pickled and fermented foods, show similar associations. Most such studies are observational *(book)*.
- **Omega-6 to omega-3 ratio:** the typical Western ratio is very high, while the ideal is closer to 4:1. People eating more omega-6 than omega-3 have a higher risk of depression. Cut omega-6 (corn oil, full-fat cheese, fatty red meat, processed foods) and add fatty fish *(book)*.
- **Saffron:** a 2013 meta-analysis of 5 RCTs found saffron reduced depressive symptoms against placebo. A 2017 study found **15 mg of saffron as effective as 20 mg of Prozac** *(book)*. Don't present this as a reason to stop antidepressants.
- **Glycemic load:** high-glycemic diets raise the risk of depression *(book)*.
- **Case:** "Ted" switched to Mediterranean-style lunches and snacks, such as walnuts, dark chocolate, fruit, hummus, oven-baked salmon and kale pesto. Over about 3 years he reached his goal weight and no longer felt depressed *(book)*.

## Ch 3: Anxiety

- The gut-brain link is built into our language: "butterflies in your stomach", "a pit in your stomach" *(book)*.
- **Fermented foods and fiber** feed beneficial bacteria. **Hydration** matters *(book)*.
- **The tryptophan myth:** turkey's tryptophan doesn't reliably reach the brain. Pair tryptophan foods with carbohydrates, for example turkey with mashed potatoes, a whole-grain low-sugar cereal with milk, peanut butter on whole-grain toast, or cheese with whole-wheat crackers *(book)*.
- **Herbs** *(book)*:
  - Passionflower: 45 drops of liquid extract daily, or a 90 mg/day tablet. Avoid it with blood thinners (Coumadin, Plavix) or MAOIs.
  - Chamomile: 1–3 cups of tea a day is generally safe unless you take blood thinners or are about to have surgery. Pregnant women should ask their doctor.
  - Lavender oil.
- **Other helpful nutrients:** selenium (Brazil nuts), potassium (pumpkin seeds, bananas), flavonoids (dark chocolate), theanine (green tea), lysine-rich foods. Avoid wheat bran because its phytic acid blocks zinc *(book)*.
- **Case:** "Marisol", a mother of two, used fiber, fermented foods and family-friendly recipes. After about 6 months she was calmer *(book)*.

## Ch 4: PTSD

- Trauma repeatedly activates the **HPA axis**, which disrupts the gut and its bacteria *(book)*.
- **"Old Friends":** microbes humans evolved with that help regulate immune and stress responses *(book)*.
- **Blueberries** (½–1 cup a day), omega-3s, vitamin E, turmeric and ginkgo help. **Glutamates and MSG** may worsen symptoms, so a personalized plan is needed because many glutamate-rich foods are otherwise healthy *(book)*.
- **Case:** "Letitia", a lawyer who survived a shooting, as recovery support alongside therapy *(book)*.

## Ch 5: ADHD

- **The Mediterranean pattern** is the base *(book)*.
- **Feingold diet** (1976): pediatric allergist Benjamin Feingold eliminated additives and salicylates. It is also called the Kaiser Permanente diet *(book)*.
- **The few-foods diet** is an elimination diet, best used under supervision if milder changes fail *(book)*.
- **A1 milk casein** may be a problem. Switch to A2 milk, nut milks, or goat's or sheep's milk *(book)*.
- **Polyphenols, breakfast, and caffeine within limits** help. Sugar is "unfairly vilified" but still worth limiting *(book)*.
- **Medication caution:** stimulants can be habit-forming. Diet can work "alongside your other treatments" *(book)*.
- **Case:** "Sanjay", a programmer whose ADHD had been mislabeled as laziness *(book)*.

## Ch 6: Dementia and brain fog

- **The MIND diet** (a Mediterranean and DASH hybrid) is the most complete plan for memory *(book)*.
- **Calorie restriction:** one study cut calories by 35% and improved memory in healthy elderly people after 3 months. Naidoo suggests about 25% less, but only with a doctor *(book)*.
- **Olive oil in a sofrito** (sautéed vegetables) moves brain-protective polyphenols such as naringenin, ferulic acid and quercetin into the food *(book)*.
- **Brain fog nutrients:** luteolin, phosphatidylserine (soy-derived PS improved memory in elderly Japanese adults after 6 months, Kato-Kataoka 2010) and citicoline *(book)*.
- "It's never too late or too early to start eating in a way that gives you the best possible chance of staving off dementia" *(book)*.

## Ch 7: OCD

- **The anxiety advice applies here too** *(book)*.
- **NAC** (as a supplement), **myoinositol, glycine**, milk thistle, B12 and turmeric with black pepper help *(book)*.
- **Orthorexia nervosa** is an extreme fixation on eating "healthily". Naidoo admits the irony of warning about it in a nutrition book *(book)*.
- **Muscle dysmorphia:** her example is a bodybuilder who pushed protein and supplements (including anabolic steroids) to the edge of kidney failure, then recovered with balanced eating and therapy *(book)*.
- **Case:** "Adam", who had checking rituals *(book)*.

## Ch 8: Insomnia and fatigue

- **Circadian rhythms:** gut bacteria have their own daily cycles. Jet lag and night shifts disrupt them *(book)*.
- In 2014 Sarah Davies (Imperial College London) found that sleep deprivation changed the levels of 27 metabolites in young men *(book)*.
- **Capsaicin:** 2.5 mg per meal (7.68 mg a day) restored energy balance in humans in one study *(book)*.
- **Anti-inflammatory eating** for fatigue *(book)*.
- **Caffeine** under 400 mg a day, with none after 3 p.m. **Alcohol** disrupts sleep *(book)*.
- **Case:** "Dumisani", a police officer and new mother on night shifts. Schedule and diet changes improved her mood within about 3 months *(book)*.

## Ch 9: Bipolar disorder and schizophrenia

- These need serious medicine. Diet "can work alongside medications" *(book)*.
- **Keto diet for bipolar disorder:** high fat, moderate protein, very low carbohydrate. Expect "keto flu" (nausea, headache, fatigue, insomnia, constipation) for days to weeks. Do it with medical supervision *(book)*.
- **Eating problems are common:** a notable share of bipolar patients have binge-eating disorder or night-eating syndrome *(book)*.
- **Lithium:** keep sodium steady. **Grapefruit and tyramine foods** interact with some bipolar medications *(book)*.
- **Cases** *(book)*:
  - "Nancy" (bipolar) moved to keto: less coffee, gluten avoided, more fish and olive oil. Her symptoms resolved in about 6 weeks.
  - "Alice" (schizophrenia) cut out bread and alcohol and improved in about 7 weeks.

## Ch 10: Libido

- **Hormones:** estrogen, testosterone and oxytocin, the "bonding hormone" *(book)*.
- **Fenugreek** raised testosterone in men in one study. A double-blind trial found better libido and arousal *(book)*.
- **Saffron, pistachios, apples, pomegranate and dark chocolate** help *(book)*.
- **Avoid** heavy soy (for men with low libido), licorice and PFOA in nonstick pans and packaging *(book)*.
- **Alcohol limits** for libido differ slightly from the anxiety limits; see `cheat-sheets.md`.
