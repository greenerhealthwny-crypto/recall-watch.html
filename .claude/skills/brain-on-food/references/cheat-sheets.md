## Depression (p. 54–56)

"The Mediterranean eating pattern is a great guideline to give you a complete diet that will fight depression and keep your brain healthy."

**Foods to embrace:**
- **Probiotics:** yogurt with active cultures, tempeh, miso, natto, sauerkraut, kefir, kimchi, kombucha, buttermilk and certain cheeses.
- **Prebiotics:** beans, oats, bananas, berries, garlic, onions, dandelion greens, asparagus, Jerusalem artichokes, leeks.
- **Low-GI carbohydrates:** brown rice, quinoa, steel-cut oatmeal, chia seeds.
- **Medium-GI foods, in moderation:** honey, orange juice, whole-grain bread.
- **Healthy fats:** monounsaturated fats like olive oil, nuts, nut butters and avocados.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** fish, especially fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring and sardines.
- **Vitamins** B9, B12, B1, B6, A and C.
- **Minerals and micronutrients:** iron, magnesium, potassium, zinc, selenium.
- **Spices:** saffron and turmeric.
- **Herbs:** oregano, lavender, passionflower, chamomile.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **Sugar:** baked goods, candy, soda, or anything sweetened with sugar or high-fructose corn syrup.
- **High-GI carbs:** white bread, white rice, potatoes, pasta, anything made from refined flour.
- **Artificial sweeteners:** aspartame is particularly harmful. Use saccharin, sucralose and stevia in moderation and with caution.
- **Fried foods:** French fries, fried chicken, fried seafood, or anything deep-fried in oil.
- **Bad fats:** trans fats such as margarine, shortening and hydrogenated oils should be avoided totally. Omega-6 fats such as vegetable, corn, sunflower and safflower oil should be eaten only in moderation.
- **Nitrates:** an additive used in bacon, salami, sausage and other cured meats.

## Anxiety (p. 79–80)

**Foods to embrace:**
- **High-fiber foods:** beans, brown rice, berries, bran, pears, apples, bananas, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, carrots, artichokes, almonds, walnuts, amaranth, oats, buckwheat, pearl barley.
- **Aged, fermented and cultured foods:** yogurt, kombucha, miso, tempeh, apple-cider vinegar, pickled vegetables.
- **Tryptophan:** turkey, other meats, and chickpeas, especially when combined with carbohydrates.
- **Vitamins** D, B1, B6, A, C and E.
- **Minerals:** magnesium, potassium, selenium.
- **Spices:** turmeric.
- **Herbs:** lavender, passionflower, chamomile.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **The Western diet:** foods high in bad fats (red meat, fried foods) and high-GI carbs (white bread, white rice, potatoes, pasta, anything made from refined flour).
- **Caffeine:** keep it under 400 mg a day.
- **Alcohol:**
  - Men: under 14 drinks a week and no more than 2 drinks on any single day.
  - Women: under 7 drinks a week and no more than 1 drink on any single day.
  - "By cutting back slowly, you will help lower anxiety."
- **Gluten:** if you have celiac disease or non-celiac gluten sensitivity, avoid all wheat products, such as bread, pizza, pasta and many alcoholic drinks.
- **Artificial sweeteners:** aspartame is particularly harmful. Use saccharin, sucralose and stevia in moderation and with caution.

**Also in the chapter:**
- Stay hydrated, since dehydration can worsen anxiety.
- Passionflower and chamomile tea (1–3 cups a day) have evidence.
- Avoid passionflower if you take blood thinners or MAOIs.
- Avoid wheat bran, because its phytic acid blocks zinc absorption.
- Lysine-rich foods may help: lean beef and lamb, tempeh, seitan, lentils, black beans, quinoa.

## PTSD (p. 98)

**Foods to embrace:**
- **Blueberries:** ½–1 cup a day.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring and sardines.
- **Vitamin E.**
- **Spices:** turmeric.
- **Supplements:** ginkgo biloba.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **The Western diet** (bad fats and high-GI carbs, as above).
- **Sugar:** baked goods, candy, soda, or anything with sugar or high-fructose corn syrup.
- **MSG, other glutamates and glutamic acid:** fish sauce, oyster sauce, tomato sauce, miso, Parmesan cheese, savory snack chips, ready-to-eat meals, mushrooms, spinach, seaweed, cheeses, soy sauce, fermented beans, tomatoes, and high-protein foods like meats and seafood.
  - Naidoo notes that some of these foods help with other conditions. It is about a personalized plan.

## ADHD (p. 112–113)

"As with depression, the Mediterranean eating pattern is a great overall diet to follow for improving ADHD symptoms."

**Foods to embrace:**
- **Breakfast:** start the day right, for example with a smoothie (recipe p. 261).
- **Caffeine:** it can help with ADHD, but keep it under 400 mg a day.
- **Polyphenols:** berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, coffee, green tea.
- **Vitamins** C and B12.
- **Minerals:** zinc, iron, potassium, magnesium.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **Gluten:** if you have celiac disease or non-celiac gluten sensitivity, avoid all wheat products, such as bread, pizza, pasta and many alcoholic drinks.
- **Dairy, specifically A1 milk caseins:** drink and cook with products made from A2 milk, nut milk, or goat's or sheep's milk.
- **Sugar:** while sugar is "unfairly vilified" as a cause of ADHD, it's still best to limit it.
- **Food colorings and additives:** these can be eliminated with the Feingold diet or the few-foods diet if symptoms don't respond to less radical changes.

## Memory, dementia and brain fog (p. 136–137)

"The MIND diet is the most comprehensive eating plan for ensuring a healthy memory. Eat green leafy vegetables, colorful vegetables, berries, nuts, olive oil, whole grains, fish, beans, poultry; drink red wine [in moderation]."

**Foods and strategies to embrace:**
- **Calorie restriction:** work with your doctor to reduce total calorie intake by about 25 percent.
- **Alcohol:** don't abstain totally or drink too much. 3–5 drinks a week is ideal for women, and 5–7 for men.
- **Coffee:** it is beneficial, but keep total caffeine under 400 mg a day.
- **Olive oil:** it is protective, especially in a *sofrito* (vegetables sautéed in olive oil).
- **Herbs and spices:** turmeric, black pepper, cinnamon, saffron, rosemary, ginger, sage.
- **For brain fog:**
  - Luteolin-rich foods: juniper berries, fresh peppermint, sage, thyme, hot and sweet peppers, radicchio, celery seeds, parsley, artichokes, dried Mexican oregano.
  - Phosphatidylserine (PS) foods: white beans, eggs and dairy.
  - Citicoline-rich foods: beef liver, egg yolks.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **The Western diet** (bad fats and high-GI carbs).
- **Gluten**, if you have celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.

## OCD (p. 157–158)

"Since OCD is so closely related to anxiety, the dietary recommendations in chapter 3 also apply here."

**Foods and supplements to embrace:**
- **N-acetylcysteine (NAC):** NAC itself must be taken as a supplement, but cysteine-rich foods can also be effective. Try meat, grains, eggs, ricotta cheese, cottage cheese, yogurt, broccoli, red pepper and onion.
- **Myoinositol:** fresh vegetables, especially navy or green beans, Brussels sprouts and lima beans; peanut butter; whole-wheat bread; cantaloupe; citrus fruits.
- **Glycine:** meat, fish, dairy products, legumes, spinach, kale, cauliflower, cabbage, pumpkin, bananas, kiwi.
- **Milk thistle** (*Silybum marianum*): available as a supplement.
- **Vitamin B12.**
- **Spices:** turmeric with a pinch of black pepper.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **MSG and glutamates:** the same list as for PTSD.
- **Gluten**, if you have celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.

**Also in the chapter:**
- Beware **orthorexia nervosa**, an extreme fixation on "healthy" eating.
- Beware muscle dysmorphia (her example was a bodybuilder on extreme protein and supplements who was close to kidney failure).
- Avoid radical diet changes, unvetted supplements and extreme protein intake. Consult a doctor or nutritionist.

## Insomnia and fatigue (p. 178–179)

**Foods to embrace (sleep):**
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** fish, especially fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring and sardines.
- **Melatonin:** eggs, fish, milk, rice, barley and rolled oats, grapes, pomegranates, walnuts, sunflower seeds, mustard seeds, flaxseeds, asparagus, broccoli, cucumber.
- **Tryptophan:** turkey, other meats, and chickpeas, especially when combined with carbohydrates.
- **L-ornithine:** meat, poultry, fish, eggs, soybeans, quinoa.
- **Chamomile tea.**
- **Foods with helpful micronutrients:** lettuce, tart cherry juice, barley grass powder, maca, *Panax* ginseng, lingzhi, ashwagandha powder.

**For fatigue:**
- **Anti-inflammatory foods:** omega-3s, colorful vegetables for polyphenols.
- **Minerals:** magnesium and zinc.
- **Vitamins** B1, B5, B6, B12, C, D and E.
- **Capsaicin-rich foods:** chili peppers, including cayenne, serranos and jalapeños.
- **Spices:** black cumin and turmeric.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **Caffeine:** you don't have to eliminate it completely, but stick to the guideline of no more than 400 mg a day, and don't drink caffeine after 3 p.m.
- **Alcohol:** though it can put you to sleep, it also disrupts sleep.

## Bipolar disorder (p. 205–206)

"The keto diet has been shown to be a good whole-diet approach for patients with bipolar disorder."

**Foods to embrace:**
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring and sardines.
- **N-acetylcysteine (NAC):** supplement, or cysteine-rich foods (meat, grains, eggs, ricotta cheese, cottage cheese, yogurt, broccoli, red pepper, onion).
- **Vitamin B9** (folate).
- **Minerals:** magnesium, zinc.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **The Western diet** (bad fats and high-glycemic carbs).
- **Caffeine:** keep it under 400 mg a day.
- **Sodium:** for patients treated with lithium, keep sodium levels constant.
- **Gluten**, if you have celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.
- **Alcohol:** totally abstain or heavily restrict it.
- **Complications with medicine:** grapefruit juice, and foods containing tyramine (aged cheese, aged or cured meats, fava beans, Marmite, sauerkraut, soy sauce and tap beer), can interfere with some medications prescribed for bipolar disorder.

## Schizophrenia (p. 206–207)

**Foods to embrace:**
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring and sardines.
- **NAC:** supplement, or cysteine-rich foods (as above).
- **Alpha-lipoic acid:** spinach, broccoli, tomato, and meats, especially organ meats like heart, kidney and liver.
- **L-theanine:** green, black and oolong tea.
- **Melatonin foods:** eggs, fish, milk, rice, barley and rolled oats, grapes, pomegranates, walnuts, sunflower seeds, mustard seeds, flaxseeds, asparagus, broccoli, cucumber.
- **Vitamins** B9, B12 and C.

**Foods to avoid:**
- **The Western diet** (bad fats and high-glycemic carbs).
- **Gluten**, if you have celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.
- **Sugar:** baked goods, candy, soda, or anything with sugar or high-fructose corn syrup.
- **Alcohol:** patients should try to abstain or heavily restrict it.

## Libido (p. 228–229)

**Foods to embrace:**
- **Foods that boost oxytocin:** dark chocolate, magnesium, and essential amino acids (found in meat, grains, milk, dairy, and to a lesser degree vegetables and eggs).
- **Coffee:** keep total caffeine under 400 mg a day.
- **Red wine:** no more than 1 glass a day.
- **Nuts:** pistachios, almonds, walnuts.
- Apples, pomegranate juice, onions, avocados.
- **Herbs and spices:** saffron, fenugreek.

**Foods and compounds to avoid:**
- **The Western diet** (bad fats and high-glycemic carbs).
- **Soy protein:** men with low libido may want to cut down on tofu and soy proteins, such as those in vegetarian and vegan imitation-meat products.
- **Alcohol:**
  - Men: under 14 drinks a week and no more than 4 drinks on any single day.
  - Women: under 7 drinks a week and no more than 3 drinks on any single day.
- **Sugar:** baked goods, candy, soda, or anything with sugar or high-fructose corn syrup.
- **Licorice:** avoid candies and other products containing licorice extract.
- **PFOA:** beware of nonstick cookware and food packaging containing PFOA. Use stainless-steel or cast-iron cookware, eat PFOA-free microwave popcorn, and use unbleached paper snack bags.
