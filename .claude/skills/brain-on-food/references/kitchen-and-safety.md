## §1 Cooking and eating for your brain (Ch 11)

- **Why cook:** Naidoo learned from her grandmother and Julia Child on public TV, then trained at the Cambridge School of Culinary Arts. Many patients barely cook, so she walks them through the basics *(book)*.
- **Shopping** *(book)*:
  - "Don't shop when you are hungry."
  - Buy colorful produce, and widen your range (for example green and yellow peppers as well as red).
  - Frozen berries are fine when fresh ones aren't available.
  - Use healthy fats: olive oil, avocados, oily fish.
  - Plant proteins such as **organic tofu and tempeh** can be flavored with spices.
  - **Spices are "a no-calorie, guilt-free way to boost flavor"**, especially turmeric, black pepper, saffron and others.
- **Basic equipment** *(book)*:
  - A large chef's knife and a small utility knife, kept sharp (a sharp knife is less likely to slip).
  - A cutting board.
  - An aluminum sheet pan with parchment paper, and a glass casserole dish.
  - A large pot and a 10–12-inch sauté pan.
  - A food processor, a blender or immersion blender, and ice-pop molds for healthy frozen treats.
  - A food thermometer.
- **Kitchen hygiene list** (excerpts) *(book)*:
  - Keep nail polish from flaking into food.
  - Use a tasting spoon and wash it between uses.
  - Check protein temperatures with a thermometer.
  - Keep your prep area clean.
  - Change, flip or wash the cutting board between raw proteins and other foods.
  - Don't thaw proteins on the counter overnight.
  - Store poultry on the bottom shelf of the fridge.
- **Recipes** (from about p. 240) include:
  - kale pesto (p. 248);
  - a breakfast smoothie for ADHD (p. 261);
  - a folate-rich spinach dal (p. 271);
  - a turmeric tofu "scramble";
  - healthy hot chocolate.

## §2 Safety cautions in the book

| Food or supplement | Caution | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Passionflower | Avoid with blood thinners (Coumadin, Plavix) or MAOI antidepressants (Nardil, Parnate) | *(book)* |
| Chamomile | Generally safe at 1–3 cups a day. Avoid with blood thinners or before surgery. Pregnant women should consult their doctor | *(book)* |
| Lavender oil supplements | Consult your doctor first | *(book)* |
| Grapefruit juice; tyramine foods (aged cheese, aged or cured meats, fava beans, Marmite, sauerkraut, soy sauce, tap beer) | Can interfere with some bipolar medications. Tyramine with MAOIs can cause dangerous blood-pressure spikes *(std)* | *(book)* |
| Sodium with lithium | Keep sodium intake constant, because changes alter lithium levels | *(book)* |
| Keto diet | "Keto flu" (nausea, vomiting, headache, fatigue, dizziness, insomnia, constipation) plus long-term side effects. Use only under supervision, especially with psychiatric medicines | *(book)* |
| Calorie restriction | Only with a doctor's plan | *(book)* |
| Caffeine | Under 400 mg a day. None after 3 p.m. for sleep | *(book)* |
| Alcohol | Abstain or heavily restrict with bipolar disorder or schizophrenia. Weekly and daily limits apply for others | *(book)* |
| Gluten avoidance | Only for celiac disease or non-celiac gluten sensitivity. Get tested before removing gluten *(std)* | *(book)* |
| Protein and supplements (bodybuilding) | Extreme intake risked kidney failure in her patient. Avoid unvetted supplements | *(book)* |
| Orthorexia | Don't let healthy eating become an obsession | *(book)* |
| Saffron, NAC, ginkgo, milk thistle, myoinositol | Discuss with a doctor. Ginkgo can raise bleeding risk with anticoagulants *(std)*. NAC is a supplement dose, not a food | *(book/std)* |
| 5-HTP or tryptophan supplements with SSRIs | Risk of serotonin syndrome *(std)* | *(std)* |

## §3 How to frame advice

- Nutrition is **adjunctive**: it works alongside medication and therapy. Naidoo's patients improved over weeks to months *(book)*.
- Personalize. Some "avoid" foods (such as glutamate-rich tomatoes, spinach and mushrooms) are healthy for other conditions *(book)*.
- Check for crisis signs first, and refer to professionals as needed.
