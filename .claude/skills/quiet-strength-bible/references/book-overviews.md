## §3 Reading and devotional plans (built on the edition's advice)

- **New to the Bible:** read Mark, then Acts, as "A Life of Quiet Strength" recommends *(book)*. Then John, Romans, and a Psalm and Proverb a day.
- **A daily "playbook" rhythm (Dungy):** read daily and pray daily *(book)*.
  - Use observation, interpretation and application on each passage.
  - Pick one Verse Finder practice per week, such as "Thankfulness must be cultivated".
  - Proverbs has 31 chapters, so you can read one a day each month *(std)*.
- **Topic studies:** take one topic from the Verse Finder, read every reference in context, write the principle in your own words, then write one action for the week.
- **Group or team study:** read the chapter together, share observations, and make one application each. Dungy's point is that coaches are always available to answer questions *(book)*.

## §4 How to Know Jesus Personally (A39–A40)

**Five truths about God's desire for you** *(book)*:
1. **God has a plan for your life** (John 3:27).
2. **God's plan gives meaning** (John 6:35).
3. **God's plan gives direction** (John 8:12).
4. **God's plan brings peace** (John 14:27).
5. **God's plan is for you to live with him in heaven** (John 3:16).

Sin separated people from God, and God gave his Son to be the perfect payment for sins *(book)*.

**Three things you must do** *(book)*:
1. **Recognize that you are a sinner** (Romans 3:23, 3:10).
2. **Ask Jesus to forgive you** (Colossians 1:22). The page offers a simple sample prayer, with space to record your name and the date.
3. **Turn away from your sins** (1 John 3:9). Obeying God's commands is evidence of the change.

## §5 About the NLT (A Note to Readers, October 2007)

- First published in **1996**. The **second edition (2004)** refined the text through an 8-year review, and an update came in 2007 *(book)*.
- Goal: translate the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek into clear, natural contemporary English, accurate to the original meaning. Suited to study, devotional reading and worship *(book)*.
- Dynamic-equivalence translations like the NLT trade some word-for-word literalness for readability. Compare with a formal translation (ESV, NASB, NKJV) for close study *(std)*.
