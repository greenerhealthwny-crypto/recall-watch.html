## Dungy's framing

- **"The Bible is God's playbook for us."** In football, the coaches give players a playbook with everything they need to do their jobs. Players study it before the game, not after they've fallen behind. In the same way, "the Bible is God's playbook for life and to do his will" *(book)*.
- A player who isn't performing usually isn't sure what to do on the field, so coaches encourage players to "get in their book". God is always there through prayer, as a good head coach is, to answer questions about the "plays" we don't understand. Dungy encourages reading the Bible and praying daily: "that's the best way to be successful in the game of life" *(book)*.
- **A life of quiet strength:** people base decisions on fads and slogans that "shift and change as quickly as the clock ticks". The solid foundation is God, who doesn't change ("God is our refuge and strength", Psalm 46:1–3). "If we can get in line with God's principles, practices, and priorities… we will stand strong no matter what life throws at us" *(book)*.

## How to study (from "A Life of Quiet Strength")

**Where to begin** *(book)*: if the New Testament is new to you, start with **Mark** (p. 31), a short, fast-moving account written by John Mark, who probably drew on Peter's memories. Then read **Acts** (p. 100) to see how Christianity spread.

**Three steps to understand what you read** *(book)*:
1. **Observation:** what does the author actually say? Restate it in your own words, or underline a key phrase.
2. **Interpretation:** what did the author mean when he wrote it? Ask why it was written (for example, John 20:31). Use the book summaries in the Overview, and a commentary or Bible dictionary if needed.
3. **Application:** ask "So what?" and "What difference does this passage make in my life?" Scripture is useful to teach, correct and train (2 Timothy 3:16). Look at the topical listings in the Verse Finder.

**Tips** *(book)*: underline key statements, note verses with special meaning, and join a Bible study to share insights.
