# Getting Things Done (David Allen, revised edition, 2015)

A working guide distilled from David Allen's *Getting Things Done*, revised edition (Penguin, 2015; first edition 2001; foreword by James Fallows) *(book)*.

**Source:** the user's PDF has a text layer (508 pages). Points marked *(book)* were checked in it.

**Structure** *(book)*: Part 1 The Art of GTD (ch 1–3: new practice; the five steps; the five phases of project planning) · Part 2 Practicing Stress-Free Productivity (ch 4–10: setup; capturing; clarifying; organizing; reflecting; engaging; projects) · Part 3 The Power of the Key Principles (ch 11–15: capturing habit; next-action decision; outcome focusing; GTD and cognitive science; path of mastery) · glossary.

## Core idea
Your mind is for having ideas, not holding them. Get every "open loop" out of your head into a trusted external system you review regularly, decide the next physical action for each, and you can work with a clear head: **"mind like water"** (the martial-arts state of responding appropriately, neither over- nor under-reacting) *(book)*.

## The five steps of mastering workflow *(book)*

1. **Capture** everything that has your attention ("stuff") into a few collection tools (in-tray, notebook, app). Empty your head.
2. **Clarify** each item, one at a time, top down, getting "in" to empty:
   - **What is it?**
   - **Is it actionable?**
     - **No** → trash it, **incubate** it (Someday/Maybe or tickler), or file it as **reference**.
     - **Yes** → what's the desired outcome? If it needs more than one action, it's a **project**. **What's the next action?** (the very next physical, visible activity)
   - **Two-minute rule:** "If an action will take less than two minutes, it should be done at the moment it's defined."
   - Otherwise **delegate it** (track on Waiting For) or **defer it** (calendar if date-specific, else Next Actions list).
3. **Organize** into distinct buckets: the **seven primary types**: Projects list; project support material; calendar actions and information; Next Actions lists (by context); Waiting For list; reference material; Someday/Maybe list. Keep "hard edges" between them. A physical **tickler file** uses **43 folders** (31 days + 12 months).
4. **Reflect**: review the system often enough to trust it, especially the **Weekly Review**.
5. **Engage**: choose what to do with confidence.

**A project** = "any desired result that can be accomplished within a year that requires more than one action step" *(book)*.

## Three models for choosing work *(book)*
- **Four-criteria model** (in the moment), in this order: **context, time available, energy available, priority**.
- **Threefold model** of daily work: doing predefined work; doing work as it shows up; defining your work.
- **Six-level model** for reviewing your work (the **horizons of focus**):
  - Horizon 5: Purpose and principles
  - Horizon 4: Vision
  - Horizon 3: Goals
  - Horizon 2: Areas of focus and accountabilities
  - Horizon 1: Current projects
  - Ground: Current actions

## The natural planning model (five phases) *(book)*
1. Defining purpose and principles (why?)
2. Outcome visioning (what would success look like?)
3. Brainstorming (how? get all ideas out)
4. Organizing (components, sequences, priorities)
5. Identifying next actions (what's the next physical step, who owns it)

## The Weekly Review *(book)*
**Get clear:** collect loose papers and materials; get "in" to zero; empty your head.
**Get current:** review action lists; review previous calendar; review upcoming calendar; review Waiting For; review project (and larger outcome) lists; review any relevant checklists.
**Get creative:** review Someday/Maybe; be creative and courageous.

## How to use this skill
- **Set up a system:** walk the user through a full capture (mind sweep), then clarify with the decision tree, then create the seven lists in their tool of choice (paper, app) and contexts that fit their life *(contexts std examples: @calls, @computer, @errands, @home)*.
- **Overwhelm:** start with capture and the two-minute rule; turn each project into one next action.
- **Projects:** run the natural planning model; keep only the next action on the active list.
- **Priorities:** use the horizons from the top down, and the four criteria in the moment.
- **Maintenance:** schedule a weekly review (about an hour or two, same time each week) *(timing std)*.

## Caveats
- The revised edition is tool-agnostic; any app or paper works if it's trusted and reviewed *(book)*.
- GTD is a workflow method, not a prioritization formula; pair with the horizons for direction *(book)*.
