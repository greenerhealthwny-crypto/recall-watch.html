# Researching an Ohio Security Deposit Claim: A Nolo-Style Research Plan

This follows the method in Nolo's *Legal Research: How to Find & Understand the Law* (19th ed., 2021): learn the framework, frame the issue, start with secondary sources, find the primary law, check that it is still good law, then organize it for court.

> **Legal information, not legal advice.** If a lot of money is at stake, or the landlord sues you back, think about a short consultation with a lawyer or legal aid. Your own research will make that meeting cheaper and more useful.

---

## Before you start: facts to write down

Your research has to fit your facts, so gather these first:

- **Where the rental is** (city and county). That decides which small claims court you file in and whether any local ordinance applies.
- **Lease dates**, the **deposit amount**, and the **monthly rent**.
- **The date you moved out and returned the keys.** In legal terms that is when you "delivered possession".
- **Whether you gave the landlord a forwarding address in writing**, when, and how. Keep a copy.
- **What the landlord sent you, if anything:** an itemized list of deductions, a letter, or nothing at all.
- **Your evidence:** move-in and move-out photos or video, any checklist, texts and emails, receipts for cleaning or repairs.

---

## Step 1: Frame the issue (Ch. 3)

Nolo's four framing steps:

| Question | Your answer |
|---|---|
| **Civil or criminal?** | **Civil.** You want money back from a private party. No prosecutor is involved. |
| **Substantive or procedural?** | **Both.** *Substantive:* what Ohio law says about deposits and deductions. *Procedural:* how to file and win in Ohio small claims court. |
| **State or federal?** | **State.** Landlord-tenant law is on Nolo's "mainly state" list. The exception is federally subsidized housing, which may add federal rules. The book's rule of thumb is that if federal money is involved, some federal law applies. |
| **Area of law** | **Landlord-tenant / housing.** Contract law (your lease) is a secondary area. |

**Write your research question.** Nolo says to state your goal first, then list specific questions. Your goal (filing a lawsuit) calls for depth, not just an overview.

> *My goal is to sue my former landlord in Ohio small claims court to recover my security deposit, which was kept in full.*

The specific questions to answer:

1. What Ohio statute governs residential security deposits?
2. How many days does the landlord have to return the deposit or send an itemized list of deductions?
3. What may a landlord lawfully deduct? (Unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, other charges under the lease?)
4. Do I have to give a forwarding address in writing, and what happens if I didn't?
5. If the landlord wrongfully withheld the deposit, what can I recover? (The deposit only, extra damages, interest, attorney fees?)
6. Who has to prove what in court? For example, does the landlord have to justify each deduction?
7. **Procedure:** What is the Ohio small claims limit? Which court and venue do I use? What forms, fees and service rules apply? Is there a deadline for filing?

## Step 2: Pick search terms using the Cartwheel (Ch. 3)

Nolo warns that most failed research happens because the researcher "runs out of patience at the index". Brainstorm widely:

| Category | Terms |
|---|---|
| Major words from the facts | security deposit, landlord, tenant, withhold, refund, return |
| Broader | landlord and tenant, residential rental, lease, rental agreement |
| Narrower | itemized list, deductions, wrongful withholding, forwarding address, normal wear and tear |
| Synonyms | damage deposit, rental deposit, prepaid rent, lessor / lessee |
| Antonyms / opposite side | landlord's damages, tenant's liability for damage, unpaid rent |
| Closely related | "termination of the rental agreement", "delivery of possession", interest on deposit, double damages |
| Procedural | small claims, municipal court, county court, jurisdictional limit, venue, complaint |
| Agencies / organizations | Ohio Attorney General (consumer protection), local fair housing or tenant-rights groups, legal aid |
| Long shots | escrow, liquidated damages clause, cleaning fee, carpet replacement, abandonment |

**Sample Boolean queries** (adjust the syntax for each site):

- Google Scholar / Justia: `"security deposit" AND (Ohio) AND (withh* OR "itemized") AND tenant`
- Westlaw-style: `"security deposit" /p (wrongful! withh!) /p ("forwarding address" OR itemiz!)`
- To find cases on a statute once you have it: `"5321.16" AND "wear and tear"`

Keep changing the queries as new terms come up in what you read.

## Step 3: Start with secondary sources (Ch. 2, 4)

Nolo advises starting with secondary sources to learn the vocabulary and to get citations to the primary law. For a state-law issue, a **state-specific** source is best.

1. **Nolo resources**
   - Nolo.com articles on Ohio security deposit rules and its state-by-state deposit charts. These give quick deadlines and citations to check.
   - *Every Tenant's Legal Guide* (Nolo) for the substantive background.
   - *Everybody's Guide to Small Claims Court* (Nolo) for the procedure. The book recommends it by name.
2. **Ohio-specific plain-English sources**
   - **Ohio Legal Help** (ohiolegalhelp.org) and your local legal aid society's tenant pages.
   - **Your court's small claims self-help page.** Many Ohio municipal courts publish small claims guides and forms. Staff can't give legal advice, but they can explain procedure.
3. **Lawyer-level sources** (at a county or law school law library; call first to check public access)
   - **Ohio Jurisprudence 3d** (Ohio's state legal encyclopedia). Look under "Landlord and Tenant".
   - An **Ohio landlord-tenant practice manual or treatise**. Ask the librarian which one the library has.
   - **A.L.R. annotations** on tenants' remedies for wrongfully withheld deposits. These collect cases from many states. Ohio cases carry the most weight; others are only persuasive.

**Rule from the book:** never rely only on a secondary source. Courts follow primary law, and the law changes faster than commentary does. Use these sources to get your citations, then go read the law itself.

## Step 4: Find and read the primary law (Ch. 5–7)

### A. The statute

Ohio's residential landlord-tenant law is **Chapter 5321 of the Ohio Revised Code**. The security deposit section is generally cited as **R.C. 5321.16**. Confirm it yourself on the official site, **codes.ohio.gov**, or in an annotated code at the library (Baldwin's or Page's Ohio Revised Code Annotated).

As commonly summarized, that section covers:

- the landlord's deadline (commonly stated as **30 days** after the lease ends and you move out) to return the deposit or give an **itemized written list** of deductions;
- the tenant's duty to give a **forwarding address in writing**;
- **interest** on larger deposits held for longer tenancies;
- the **remedy** if money is wrongfully withheld (commonly summarized as the amount wrongfully withheld, **plus an equal amount in damages, plus reasonable attorney fees**).

Treat every one of those points as something to **verify in the current statute text**, not as settled.

**Apply Nolo's seven rules for reading a statute:**

1. Read it at least three times, then read it again.
2. Watch the **"ands" and "ors"**. For example, are the deadline's triggers "termination **and** delivery of possession"?
3. Assume every word and punctuation mark has meaning. What exactly counts as an "itemized" list? What does "wrongfully withheld" mean?
4. Read it **consistently with related sections** of Chapter 5321, such as the landlord's and tenant's duties and the definitions section. Track down every cross-reference.
5. (The strict-reading rule applies to criminal statutes, so it isn't relevant here.)
6. Read ambiguities **to further the law's purpose**. Tenant-protection statutes are often read to protect tenants.
7. Read it **so that it makes sense** and avoids results the legislature couldn't have intended.

**Check the pocket part** in the print volume, or the "current through" date on the website, for amendments.

### B. Cases that interpret the statute

Nolo says **court interpretations are part of the statute**. Find them through:

- **The annotations** under the section in Baldwin's or Page's Ohio annotated code. This is the fastest route.
- **Google Scholar** (choose "Case law", then select Ohio courts), **Justia**, or **FindLaw**. Search the section number plus terms like "forwarding address", "itemized", "wear and tear", "attorney fees".
- **West's Ohio Digest** under the Landlord and Tenant topic. Use the Descriptive Word Index and check the pocket part.
- **One good case leads to more.** Follow its citations and its key numbers.

**Look for cases on the questions that matter to you:**

- What happens if the tenant didn't give a written forwarding address?
- Is a landlord who sends no itemized list liable for the full amount?
- What counts as "normal wear and tear"?
- How are attorney fees handled when the tenant represents themselves?

**Weigh the cases correctly (Ch. 1, 7):**

- **Supreme Court of Ohio** decisions bind every Ohio court.
- **Ohio's courts of appeals** are organized into numbered appellate districts. A decision from your district binds the trial court that will hear your case. A decision from another district is strongly persuasive.
- **Out-of-state cases** are only persuasive.
- Read the **opinion itself**, not just the headnotes, which "tend to overly simplify issues". Separate the **holding** from the **dicta**.
- Compare the issues and facts with yours. Prefer **recent** cases.

### C. Procedural law

- The **Ohio small claims statute** (Chapter 1925 of the Ohio Revised Code) sets the **dollar limit**, which court hears the case, and the basic procedure. **Confirm the current limit**, since limits change.
- Your **local court's rules and forms**, available on the municipal or county court website or from the clerk.
- **Filing deadline:** look up which **statute of limitations** applies to a claim under the deposit statute or the written lease. Don't assume one, and file promptly anyway.

### D. Local law

Check whether your **city has a housing or rental ordinance** that adds rules. Look on the city website or its online municipal code.

## Step 5: Validate: is it still good law? (Ch. 8)

"Shepardizing is every bit as important as finding a case or statute."

- **Statute:** confirm the current version on codes.ohio.gov and check the annotated code's pocket part for amendments, and for cases that interpreted or limited it.
- **Every case you plan to cite:** run it through **Shepard's** (Lexis) or **KeyCite** (Westlaw). Most law libraries give the public free access. Watch for:
  - **red flags:** overruled or reversed. Don't rely on it.
  - **orange or yellow:** questioned, criticized or distinguished. Read why.
  - **"followed":** good support.
- Without Shepard's or KeyCite, use Google Scholar's "How cited" feature as a rough check, then verify at the library before your hearing.

## Step 6: Organize and use it (Ch. 9)

**Keep a folder for each research question:** deadline and itemization, deductions and wear and tear, remedies, and procedure. Mark where each case states its issue, facts, decision and reasoning.

**Write a short IRAC memo for yourself.** It doubles as your script for court.

```
Facts: Lease ended [date]; keys returned [date]; written forwarding
  address sent [date, method]; deposit $___; landlord kept all of it;
  [no itemized list / list received on date, claiming ___].
Question presented: Under Ohio law, did my landlord wrongfully withhold
  my security deposit, and what can I recover?
Brief answer: Probably yes / probably no, because ___.
Discussion:
  Issue 1 - Deadline and itemized notice
    Rule: R.C. 5321.16 [verified text] + interpreting case(s)
    Analysis: my dates vs. the deadline; was a list sent?
    Conclusion:
  Issue 2 - Were the deductions lawful (damage vs. normal wear and tear)?
  Issue 3 - Remedy: deposit + statutory damages + interest + fees?
  Issue 4 - Procedure: court, venue, dollar limit, forms
Next steps / open questions:
```

**For the hearing:**

- Bring copies of the **statute** and your **key cases** (one set for the judge, one for the landlord, one for you).
- Bring your evidence: the lease, photos and video, your forwarding-address letter and proof it was sent, any itemized list, and messages.
- Small claims procedure is simplified, and clerks often help with the forms. Confine your argument to **existing law**. Trial judges follow precedent.
- **A demand letter first** is often expected and can lead to settlement. It cites the statute, states the amount, and sets a deadline. Nolo's small claims book covers how to write one.

## Your one-page research plan

```
Goal: Sue former landlord in Ohio small claims for full security deposit
Jurisdiction: Ohio; county ___; city ___ (check local ordinance)
Type: Civil | substantive + procedural | landlord-tenant (+ lease/contract)
Questions: deadline/itemization; lawful deductions; forwarding address;
  remedies (damages, interest, fees); burden of proof; small claims
  limit/venue/forms; filing deadline
Search terms: security deposit, wrongful withholding, itemized, forwarding
  address, normal wear and tear, delivery of possession, small claims
Start with (secondary): Nolo deposit articles + Every Tenant's Legal Guide;
  Everybody's Guide to Small Claims Court; Ohio Legal Help; Ohio Jur. 3d
Primary law: R.C. Chapter 5321 (esp. 5321.16) - verify; Ohio cases via the
  annotated code / Google Scholar / Ohio Digest; R.C. Chapter 1925 + local
  court rules; city ordinance
Validate: Shepard's/KeyCite at county or law school library; pocket part;
  codes.ohio.gov "current through" date
Output: IRAC memo -> demand letter -> small claims filing + hearing binder
```

**A note on citations.** The section numbers above are the commonly cited ones, but I haven't checked them against the current text. Treat them as leads. Confirm each one on codes.ohio.gov or in an annotated code before you put it in a demand letter or court filing. I haven't named specific cases on purpose. Find them through the annotations and check them with Shepard's or KeyCite so you know they are still good law.

**When to get a lawyer.** Consider one if the landlord counterclaims for more than the deposit, if your claim is above the small claims limit, or if a filing deadline is close. Many Ohio legal aid offices handle deposit disputes for free if you qualify.
