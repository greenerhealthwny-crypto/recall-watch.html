**Short answer:** You're not necessarily being scammed, but you are being *worked*. What you describe is the standard high-pressure SaaS playbook, and each piece of it is meant to keep you from comparing, negotiating, or walking away. The product may still be fine. The deal is built to get your signature before you've done your homework.

## The tactics you're seeing

| What they're doing | What it really is |
|---|---|
| **"Free" setup and training** | The cost is built into a 3-year commitment. Nothing is free when you have to sign a contract to get it. It also gives you a sense that you owe them something. |
| **"Sign this week"** | Made-up urgency. End-of-quarter quota deadlines are real for the *rep*, not for you. Deals like this almost always come back next week, or next quarter. |
| **Two options, both 3 years** | A false choice. You're being steered into choosing *which* 3-year deal, not *whether* to commit or for how long. Shorter terms, monthly plans, or a pilot are missing on purpose. |
| **"You're so smart for seeing the value"** | Flattery to tie your sense of yourself as a smart buyer to saying yes. If you then hesitate, it feels like going back on your own good judgment. |

Any one of these on its own is ordinary sales behavior. All four together means the deal is set up to favor the vendor.

## What to do

**1. Slow it down, politely and firmly.**
> "We're interested, but we don't sign multi-year agreements without a full review. If the offer can't wait two to three weeks, we'll have to pass."

How they react tells you a lot. A good vendor will work with your timeline. If the offer "disappears," it wasn't much of an offer.

**2. Ask for what they left off the table.**
- Annual or month-to-month pricing
- A 1-year term, or a 90-day paid pilot with an exit clause
- The full price list, including what setup and training would cost on their own
- Per-user / per-provider / per-location costs and how they change as you grow

**3. Get the full 3-year cost in writing.** Ask for the total contract value, the annual price increase cap (push for 3–5% or less), and every possible add-on: support tiers, integrations, data storage, extra training, interfaces with your EHR, lab, billing, or clearinghouse.

**4. Read the contract for the clauses that hurt later:**
- **Auto-renewal:** how long is the notice window? (Calendar it if you sign.)
- **Early termination:** do you owe the rest of the contract if you leave?
- **Data ownership and exit:** can you get *all* your data out in a usable format, at no cost, when you leave? This matters a lot for a clinic.
- **Performance terms:** uptime guarantees, support response times, and what you get if they miss them.
- **Price increases** at renewal.

**5. Clinic-specific must-haves.** If this software touches patient information, require a **signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA)** before go-live. Ask about their HIPAA security practices, breach notification terms, and whether they've had a third-party security audit (e.g., SOC 2). Also confirm it works with your existing systems.

**6. Compare at least two alternatives.** Even a quick demo from a competitor gives you leverage and a reality check on price. Telling the rep "we're also evaluating X" often unlocks better terms on its own.

**7. Check references yourself.** Ask for 2–3 clinics of similar size, and also find one through your own network or online reviews. Ask them: "What surprised you after signing? How is support after the first 90 days?"

**8. Have someone else review it.** Before you sign a multi-year commitment, have your attorney, accountant, practice management consultant, or a trusted colleague look it over. A rep who resists this is telling you something.

## If you still like the product

That's fine. Use the pressure they've created to your advantage when you negotiate:
- "We'll sign a 3-year deal **if** we can terminate for convenience after year 1," or
- "We'll do 3 years at this rate with a 3% annual cap, free data export on exit, and setup/training included in writing."

Get every promise the rep made in writing in the contract. Verbal promises don't survive turnover at the vendor.

**Bottom line:** Don't sign this week. Take 2–3 weeks to get the full pricing, review the contract, and compare one or two alternatives. If it's a good deal, it will still be a good deal then. If it disappears because you asked reasonable questions, it was a deal you were better off without.
