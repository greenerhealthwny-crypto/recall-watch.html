## What *The Ministry of Healing* says about drugs and natural remedies

Your aunt read it correctly in one way: Ellen White does make a strong case for natural remedies. But the book doesn't support stopping a prescribed blood pressure medicine, and doing that on her own could be dangerous.

### What White actually wrote

In the chapter **"The Physician an Educator"** (MH 125 and following), White criticizes the "free use of poisonous drugs" (MH ~126). Her complaint was that people took drugs to cover up symptoms and never dealt with what was causing the illness. Then she lays out her alternative:

> "In case of sickness, the cause should be ascertained. Unhealthful conditions should be changed, wrong habits corrected. Then nature is to be assisted in her effort to expel impurities and to reestablish right conditions in the system." (MH 127)

> "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power,—these are the true remedies. Every person should have a knowledge of nature's remedial agencies and how to apply them." (MH 127)

Other parts of the book build on this:
- **Diet:** "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator." (MH 296)
- **Exercise:** "Inactivity is a fruitful cause of disease. Exercise quickens and equalizes the circulation of the blood." (MH 237–238)
- **Rest and the mind:** "Grief, anxiety, discontent, remorse, guilt, distrust, all tend to break down the life forces," while "a contented mind, a cheerful spirit, is health to the body." (MH 241)
- **Prayer and means together:** She says praying for healing doesn't mean refusing practical treatment. Christ "sanctioned the use of simple and natural remedies" (MH 233).

### What "drugs" meant in 1905

This context is the key to reading the book fairly. When White wrote, the "drugs" doctors and drugstores handed out were often actually poisonous. They included mercury compounds (calomel), arsenic, strychnine, opium and morphine, and "patent medicines" that were mostly alcohol. These were sold with no testing and no honest labels, and they often did more harm than good. Her warning was a sensible reaction to that.

Modern blood pressure medicines are a completely different kind of thing. Drugs like ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium-channel blockers, diuretics and beta-blockers have been tested in large clinical trials. Those trials show they lower the risk of stroke, heart attack, kidney failure and early death. Nothing in *The Ministry of Healing* addresses medicines like these, because they didn't exist yet.

The book also warns against going to extremes. In "Extremes in Diet" (MH 318 and following), she cautions against "narrow ideas" of health reform that end up hurting people's health. She also says health reform should be "progressive," meaning gradual rather than all-or-nothing.

### Why your aunt shouldn't stop on her own

- **High blood pressure usually causes no symptoms.** She might feel fine while her pressure is dangerously high. Feeling well doesn't show that the natural remedies are doing the job.
- **Stopping some medicines suddenly can be risky.** With beta-blockers and clonidine in particular, an abrupt stop can make blood pressure or heart rate jump sharply ("rebound").
- **Untreated high blood pressure is a leading cause of stroke, heart attack and kidney disease.** These are exactly the kinds of harm White wanted people to avoid.

### The good news: she can do both

The eight remedies really do help with blood pressure, and modern research backs many of them up:
- **Proper diet:** A mostly plant-based diet with plenty of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, beans and nuts, and less salt, can lower blood pressure noticeably. The DASH diet is a well-studied version of this.
- **Exercise:** Brisk walking most days helps.
- **Temperance:** Avoiding alcohol and tobacco helps.
- **Rest:** Good sleep matters.
- **Stress and trust in God:** Lowering stress through faith, gratitude and prayer helps.
- **Weight:** Reaching a healthy weight, if she needs to, helps.

Some people who make these changes faithfully see their blood pressure drop far enough that **their doctor** decides to lower the dose or, sometimes, stop a medicine. That decision has to be made with her doctor, based on actual blood pressure readings over time.

### A practical way forward

1. **Keep taking her pills exactly as prescribed for now.**
2. **Start the lifestyle changes** she's excited about. That enthusiasm is a good thing.
3. **Check her blood pressure at home** with a validated cuff and keep a log.
4. **Tell her doctor** that she wants to make lifestyle changes and would like to reduce her medicine over time if her numbers allow it. Many doctors will gladly work with a patient who is motivated like this. A registered dietitian can help her plan meals, and many Adventist churches and hospitals run lifestyle programs (often called NEWSTART or CHIP) that work alongside medical care.

So the book's real message is to deal with the causes of illness and help the body heal through good habits and trust in God. Today, the faithful way to follow that is to use both together: the natural remedies, and the medicine and medical care God has made available.

*This explains a historical religious text and isn't medical advice. Please have your aunt talk with her doctor or pharmacist before changing any medicine.*
