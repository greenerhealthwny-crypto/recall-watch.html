# What *The Ministry of Healing* says about gardening and working outdoors

Ellen G. White comes back to gardening and outdoor work many times in *The Ministry of Healing*, and her clearest statements are in the chapter **"In Contact with Nature"** (pp. 261–266). I checked every quotation below against a scan of the original 1905 Review and Herald edition. Its page numbers match the standard pagination used in Ellen G. White citations (for example, *MH 265*) and on EGWWritings.org.

**How to cite**
- Full note: Ellen G. White, *The Ministry of Healing* (Washington, D.C.: Review and Herald, 1905), 265.
- Short form (the usual Adventist style): *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 265, or simply **MH 265**.

---

## 1. The key passage: "nothing better than the cultivation of the soil"

From the chapter "In Contact with Nature":

> "Exercise in the open air should be prescribed as a life-giving necessity. And for such exercises there is nothing better than the cultivation of the soil. Let patients have flower beds to care for, or work to do in the orchard or vegetable garden. As they are encouraged to leave their rooms and spend time in the open air, cultivating flowers or doing some other light, pleasant work, their attention will be diverted from themselves and their sufferings." (MH 265)

If you only have room for one quote, use this one.

## 2. Gardening began in Eden

The same chapter opens under the heading **"Let us go forth into the fields"** (Song of Solomon 7:11):

> "God appointed them their work in the garden, 'to dress it and to keep it.' Each day's labor brought them health and gladness, and the happy pair greeted with joy the visits of their Creator, as in the cool of the day He walked and talked with them." (MH 261)

> "The plan of life which God appointed for our first parents has lessons for us." (MH 261)

## 3. Outdoor work heals body and mind

> "Nature is God's physician. The pure air, the glad sunshine, the flowers and trees, the orchards and vineyards, and outdoor exercise amid these surroundings, are health-giving, life-giving." (MH 263–264. The sentence starts at the bottom of p. 263.)

> "Physicians and nurses should encourage their patients to be much in the open air. Outdoor life is the only remedy that many invalids need." (MH 264)

> "Plans should be devised for keeping patients out-of-doors. For those who are able to work, let some pleasant, easy employment be provided. Show them how agreeable and helpful this outdoor work is. Encourage them to breathe the fresh air." (MH 264)

> "The more the patient can be kept out of doors, the less care will he require. The more cheerful his surroundings, the more hopeful will he be." (MH 265)

## 4. The garden points us to God

> "In nature may always be found something to divert the attention of the sick from themselves and direct their thoughts to God." (MH 265)

> "Out-of-doors, amid the things that God has made, breathing the fresh, health-giving air, the sick can best be told of the new life in Christ." (MH 266)

> "He who cares for the birds and the flowers will care for the beings formed in His own image." (MH 266)

## 5. Outdoor work as exercise

From "The Use of Remedies":

> "Outdoor exercise is the best; it should be so planned as to strengthen by use the organs that have become weakened; and the heart should be in it; the labor of the hands should never degenerate into mere drudgery." (MH 239)

> "Light employment in useful labor, while it does not tax mind or body, has a happy influence upon both. It strengthens the muscles, improves the circulation, and gives the invalid the satisfaction of knowing that he is not wholly useless in this busy world." (MH 240)

> "Those whose habits are sedentary should, when the weather will permit, exercise in the open air every day, summer or winter." (MH 240)

## 6. Tilling the soil as ministry to the poor

These passages fit a community garden ministry especially well. They come from "Help for the Unemployed and the Homeless":

> "The tilling of the soil, the employment that God appointed to man in Eden, opens a field in which there is opportunity for multitudes to gain a subsistence." (MH 189)

> "There are multitudes of poor families for whom no better missionary work could be done than to assist them in settling on the land and in learning how to make it yield them a livelihood." (MH 192)

> "Christian farmers can do real missionary work in helping the poor to find homes on the land, and in teaching them how to till the soil and make it productive. Teach them how to use the implements of agriculture, how to cultivate various crops, how to plant and care for orchards." (MH 193)

> "Keep up the culture of your own land. Drop a word to your neighbors when you can, and let the harvest be eloquent in favor of right methods. Demonstrate what can be done with the land when properly worked." (MH 193)

> "Real charity helps men to help themselves." (MH 195)

> "He who taught Adam and Eve in Eden how to tend the garden, desires to instruct men to-day. There is wisdom for him who drives the plow and sows the seed." (MH 200)

## 7. Children and the outdoors

> "Teach them to enjoy the beauties of nature, and in useful employments to exercise systematically all the powers of body and mind." (MH 386)

> "If children are well clothed, it will benefit them to exercise freely in the open air, summer or winter." (MH 382)

## Notes for an accurate handout

- **Spelling:** quotations keep the 1905 spelling and punctuation ("to-day," "out-of-doors"). Many modern reprints keep it too. If you change it to "today," say so or use a modern edition's wording consistently.
- **Scripture inside the quotes:** "to dress it and to keep it" is Genesis 2:15. The chapter heading "Let us go forth into the fields" is Song of Solomon 7:11. On p. 189, White also quotes Psalm 37:3 and Proverbs 12:11.
- **Final check:** I read these from a scanned copy, so it's worth one quick comparison with the free text at EGWWritings.org before you print. Search for a phrase such as "nothing better than the cultivation of the soil" and confirm the page number in the margin.
- **Context:** White was writing mainly about patients recovering at sanitariums (pp. 261–266) and about poor families in crowded cities (pp. 188–200). Applying her words to everyone who works in a church garden is a fair use of them, but present it as an application, not as her direct subject.

**A suggested handout pairing:** Put MH 265 ("nothing better than the cultivation of the soil") at the top. Add MH 193 ("let the harvest be eloquent") for the outreach side and MH 266 ("He who cares for the birds and the flowers...") for the devotional side. You might close with a simple line like: *"Garden work is meant to be light, pleasant work; volunteers with health concerns should check with their health-care provider about what's right for them."*
