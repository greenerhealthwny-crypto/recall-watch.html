# The Ministry of Healing on gardening and outdoor work

I'm working from memory, not from the text in front of me, so I can't promise the exact wording or page numbers below. For a printed handout, check each quote against the book before you use it. That takes about five minutes at **egwwritings.org**, which is free. Search a phrase and it shows the standard page number (the original 1905 pagination, cited as "MH 264"). Printings from Pacific Press and Review & Herald and the online edition all use these same page numbers.

Here is where to look and what you'll find.

## 1. Chapter "In Contact With Nature" (starts around p. 261)

This chapter is your main source. It opens with Eden as God's model for a healthy life:

> "The Creator chose for our first parents the surroundings best adapted for their health and happiness. He did not place them in a palace... He placed them in close touch with nature and in close communion with the holy ones of heaven." *(about MH 261)*

In the next few pages (about 263–265) she makes these points:
- **"Nature is God's physician."** Pure air, sunshine, flowers, trees, orchards and vineyards, and outdoor exercise among them, are described as "health-giving, life-giving." *(about MH 264)*
- Physicians and nurses should encourage patients to spend a lot of time outdoors. She says outdoor life is "the only remedy that many invalids need." *(about MH 264)*
- Light, useful outdoor work, including caring for flowers and working the soil, is recommended for the sick as better than sitting idle. It is said to occupy the mind, strengthen the body, and point the heart to God. *(about MH 265)*

## 2. Chapter "Help for the Unemployed and the Homeless" (starts around p. 183)

This chapter is the best support for a **community garden ministry** in particular. In it she:
- Urges that the poor and unemployed be taught to **cultivate the soil** and helped to settle on land, so they can support themselves instead of just receiving charity.
- Says that working the land well brings both physical and spiritual blessing, and that churches and Christian workers should teach practical skills along with the gospel.

The key gardening passages are a few pages into the chapter, roughly pp. 188–194. Look them up to get the exact wording.

## 3. Chapter "Choosing and Preparing the Home" (around pp. 363–370)

- She advises families to move out of crowded cities to places where they can have land, a garden, and fresh air.
- She says children especially gain from working with soil and plants, and from learning about God through the things they grow.

## Suggested citation format

> Ellen G. White, *The Ministry of Healing* (Mountain View, CA: Pacific Press, 1905), 264.

A shorter form that Adventist churches commonly use: **— *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 264** or **(MH 264)**.

## A good one-line pull-quote for the handout

**"Nature is God's physician."** This is short, easy to remember, and it opens the right passage. Confirm the page (I believe it's 264) and the surrounding sentence before you print.
