# "True Remedies": A 4-Week Church Health Class
*Based on Ellen G. White, The Ministry of Healing (1905)*

**Audience:** Adults in their 50s and 60s, several living with type 2 diabetes
**Format:** 4 weekly sessions, about 90 minutes each
**Anchor text:** "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power—these are the true remedies." (*The Ministry of Healing*, p. 127)

---

## Before you begin: a note for leaders

- **Partner with health professionals.** Invite a nurse, dietitian, or physician from the congregation to review the material and, if possible, attend. Participants with diabetes should tell their own doctor they're joining the class.
- **Medication safety comes first.** Remind people often: *do not stop or change diabetes medications on your own.* Eating more plants, cutting refined carbohydrates, and walking more can all lower blood sugar quickly. That is good news, but people on insulin or sulfonylureas (glipizide, glyburide, glimepiride) can go too low. Ask them to check their glucose more often during the series and to talk with their prescriber about adjusting doses.
- **Keep it gentle and grace-filled.** Ellen White warns against extremes in diet reform. Present the class as an invitation, not a set of rules. Aim for steady progress, not perfection, and leave no room for shame about weight or blood sugar numbers.
- **Optional baseline:** If you have a health professional available, offer voluntary weight, waist, and blood pressure checks in Week 1 and Week 4. Keep results private.

### Standard session flow (90 minutes)

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 10 min | Welcome, sharing from last week's challenge |
| 10 min | Devotional thought and the week's *Ministry of Healing* quote |
| 30 min | Teaching: principle, plus what current science says |
| 20 min | Hands-on segment: food demo, walk, or practice |
| 10 min | Small-group discussion |
| 10 min | Weekly challenge, prayer, dismissal |

## Week 1: The Way God Designed Us to Heal
**Theme:** An overview of the eight natural remedies and the mind–body connection

> "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power—these are the true remedies. Every person should have a knowledge of nature's remedial agencies and how to apply them." (*The Ministry of Healing*, p. 127)

**Scripture:** 3 John 2; 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

**Teaching points**
- Introduce all eight remedies. Many churches use the NEWSTART acronym: Nutrition, Exercise, Water, Sunlight, Temperance, Air, Rest, Trust.
- Health is "wholistic": body, mind, and spirit work together. Ellen White wrote that "the relation that exists between the mind and the body is very intimate" (p. 241).
- Type 2 diabetes is strongly shaped by lifestyle. Daily habits can make a real difference in blood sugar, blood pressure, and energy, and in some people can lead to remission, alongside medical care.
- Natural remedies work *with* your doctor, not against them.

**Hands-on segment**
- "Remedy self-check": each person rates themselves 1–5 on all eight remedies and picks the one or two they most want to grow in.
- Short demonstration: a simple, balanced breakfast (steel-cut oatmeal with walnuts and berries, or a whole-grain and bean breakfast bowl).

**Discussion questions**
1. Which of the eight remedies comes most easily to you? Which is hardest?
2. Why do you think God built healing into such simple, everyday things?

**Weekly challenge:** Choose one remedy to focus on this week and write down one specific action, such as "a 10-minute walk after supper."

## Week 2: Food as Medicine
**Theme:** Proper diet, regular meals, and temperance at the table, with a focus on blood sugar

> "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator. These foods, prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible, are the most healthful and nourishing." (*The Ministry of Healing*, p. 296)

**Scripture:** Genesis 1:29; Daniel 1:8–16

**Teaching points**
- **The Eden diet today:** whole plant foods are rich in fiber, which slows sugar absorption and improves insulin sensitivity. Beans, lentils, intact whole grains, vegetables, nuts, and seeds are especially helpful for diabetes.
- **Regularity matters.** Ellen White counseled a "specified time for each meal" with nothing between meals (p. 303). Regular mealtimes help steady blood sugar. Some participants on certain medications may still need a planned snack, so follow medical advice.
- **Whole vs. refined:** the problem is refined flour, sugar, and sweet drinks, not "carbs" as a whole. Choose fruit over juice, whole grains over white bread.
- **The plate method:** half non-starchy vegetables, a quarter beans or lentils or other plant protein, and a quarter whole grains or starchy vegetables, with a small amount of healthy fat.
- **No extremes.** *The Ministry of Healing* cautions against diets so restricted that they leave people weak. Change should be gradual and enjoyable.

**Hands-on segment**
- Cooking demo and tasting: lentil-vegetable soup, a big salad with chickpeas, and brown rice or barley.
- Label-reading practice: find added sugars and fiber on common packaged foods.
- Hand out a one-page "Diabetes-Friendly Plant Plate" guide and a simple shopping list.

**Discussion questions**
1. What food from your childhood would fit the "Creator's diet"?
2. What's one swap you could make this week that wouldn't feel like a sacrifice?

**Weekly challenge:** Eat at regular times every day this week and add one serving of beans or lentils daily. If you check your blood sugar, note any changes and share them with your doctor.

## Week 3: Move, Breathe, Drink, and Step into the Light
**Theme:** Exercise, pure air, sunlight, and water

> "Inaction is a fruitful cause of disease. Exercise quickens and equalizes the circulation of the blood." (*The Ministry of Healing*, p. 238)

**Supporting quote on water:** "In health and in sickness, pure water is one of Heaven's choicest blessings." (p. 237)

**Scripture:** Isaiah 40:31; Psalm 19:1–6

**Teaching points**
- **Exercise is diabetes medicine.** Working muscles take up glucose even without much insulin. A 10–15 minute walk after meals noticeably lowers the blood sugar spike that follows. The goal is about 150 minutes a week of moderate activity, plus light strength work twice a week (chair stands, wall push-ups, resistance bands).
- **Start low, go slow.** Anyone who has been inactive, or who has heart disease or diabetes complications, should get clearance from their doctor first.
- **Fresh air and sunlight:** time outdoors lifts mood, supports sleep rhythms, and helps the body make vitamin D. Morning light is especially helpful. Use sensible sun protection.
- **Water:** choose water over sugary or sweetened drinks. Higher blood sugar can increase thirst and dehydration. People with heart or kidney conditions should follow their doctor's fluid guidance.
- **Diabetes safety:** check feet daily and wear well-fitting walking shoes. Carry a fast-acting sugar source (glucose tablets) when exercising if you take insulin or sulfonylureas. Be careful with hot or contrast foot baths if you have neuropathy, because numb feet can burn without feeling it. Always test the water temperature with your hand or a thermometer.

**Hands-on segment**
- A 15-minute group walk outdoors, weather permitting, or a chair-based exercise routine indoors.
- Demonstrate 3–4 simple strength moves that can be done at home.
- Fill a water bottle for each participant to take home (consider church-branded bottles).

**Discussion questions**
1. What kept you from moving more in the past? What could help now?
2. Where in God's creation do you feel most refreshed?

**Weekly challenge:** Walk 10–15 minutes after at least one meal each day, and drink a glass of water first thing each morning.

## Week 4: Rest, Trust, and a Grateful Heart
**Theme:** Rest, temperance in all things, and trust in divine power

> "Nothing tends more to promote health of body and of soul than does a spirit of gratitude and praise." (*The Ministry of Healing*, p. 251)

**Supporting quote:** "Courage, hope, faith, sympathy, love, promote health and prolong life." (p. 241)

**Scripture:** Matthew 11:28–30; Mark 6:31; Philippians 4:6–7

**Teaching points**
- **Sleep and blood sugar:** poor sleep raises insulin resistance and increases cravings. Aim for 7–8 hours and a regular bedtime. Snoring with daytime sleepiness may mean sleep apnea, which is common with type 2 diabetes. Encourage people to get it checked.
- **The Sabbath as a gift of rest:** a weekly rhythm of stopping, worship, nature, and fellowship.
- **Temperance:** avoiding what harms us (alcohol, tobacco, excess caffeine) and using good things in moderation, including screens and work.
- **Stress and trust:** chronic stress raises cortisol and blood sugar. Prayer, Scripture, gratitude, and supportive friends are real, measurable helps to health.
- **Keeping going:** review all eight remedies and build a personal plan for the next 90 days.

**Hands-on segment**
- Guided gratitude exercise: write down three blessings, then pray in pairs.
- Each person completes a "My 90-Day Health Plan" card with 2–3 specific goals.
- Closing celebration: a plant-based potluck lunch with recipes shared from the series.
- Optional repeat health measurements (private).

**Discussion questions**
1. How have worry or stress affected your health?
2. What has changed for you over these four weeks?
3. Who will you ask to encourage you as you continue?

**Weekly challenge (ongoing):** Keep a short gratitude list each evening, go to bed at the same time each night, and meet with an accountability partner monthly.

## Ideas for after the series
- Start a weekly "walk and talk" group after church.
- Hold a monthly plant-based potluck with a short health talk.
- Run a recipe swap or put together a church cookbook.
- Offer the class again as community outreach, in the spirit of the book's emphasis on medical missionary work.

## Suggested handouts
1. The Eight Natural Remedies (one-page overview with quotes)
2. Diabetes-Friendly Plant Plate and shopping list
3. Safe Exercise with Diabetes (foot care, low blood sugar signs, what to carry)
4. My 90-Day Health Plan card
