# Healthy by Design: A 4-Week Class on the Natural Remedies
*A church health class drawn from Ellen G. White's* The Ministry of Healing *(1905)*

**Audience:** Adults in their 50s and 60s, including several with type 2 diabetes
**Format:** 4 weekly sessions of about 75 minutes each, plus a take-home habit each week
**Anchor text:** *"Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power,—these are the true remedies."* (MH 127)

The book lists eight natural remedies. This outline groups them into four themes so that each week builds on the one before:

| Week | Theme | Remedies covered |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Creator's Table | Proper diet, regular mealtimes |
| 2 | Made to Move | Exercise, pure air, sunlight |
| 3 | Balance and Renewal | Water, temperance (abstemiousness), rest |
| 4 | Peace That Heals | Trust in divine power, gratitude, the mind–body link |

---

## Before you start: notes for the class leader

These points matter a lot for this group.

- **This class adds to medical care. It does not replace it.** When White warned against "drugs," she meant the poisonous patent medicines of 1905, such as mercury, opium and alcohol-based tonics. Say this clearly in Week 1. **No one should stop or change metformin, insulin or any other prescription because of this class.** If lifestyle changes lower someone's blood sugar, their doctor may need to adjust their medication. That is good news, and the doctor should handle it.
- **Get a health professional on your team if you can.** A nurse, physician, registered dietitian or certified diabetes educator from your church or community could review the material and take questions. A dietitian is especially helpful in Week 1.
- **Never imply that illness is anyone's fault.** White sometimes linked disease to broken natural law. Type 2 diabetes involves genetics, age, medications and circumstances as well as habits. Present the remedies as tools for hope.
- **Respect the faith framing without pressure.** Guests from the community may attend. Make prayer and the devotional readings warm invitations, not requirements.
- **Each session follows the same rhythm:** Welcome and check-in (10 min), Reading (5 min), Teaching (20 min), Demonstration or activity (20 min), Small-group discussion (15 min), Take-home habit and prayer (5 min).

## Week 1: The Creator's Table
**Remedies:** Proper diet and regular mealtimes

### Quote for the week
> "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator. These foods, prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible, are the most healthful and nourishing." (MH 296)

Supporting reading: *"Regularity in eating is of vital importance. There should be a specified time for each meal."* (MH 303)

### Teaching points
1. **What White recommended:** simple, whole, plant-based foods, prepared well and eaten at regular times without constant snacking.
2. **What modern research says:** Whole-food, mostly plant-based eating patterns (whole grains, legumes, vegetables, fruit, nuts) are linked to lower rates of type 2 diabetes and heart disease, and can improve blood-sugar control. The Adventist Health Studies found longer life expectancy among Adventist vegetarians.
3. **Avoid extremes.** White warned that some "reformers" ate such a meager diet that "their health suffers, their usefulness is impaired" (chapter "Extremes in Diet," which begins on MH 318). Healthy eating should taste good and keep people well nourished.
4. **Changing gradually is fine.** White herself advised changing the diet step by step.

### Diabetes notes (important)
- **Not all "natural" foods affect blood sugar the same way.** Whole grains, fruit and starchy vegetables still contain carbohydrate. Teach the **plate method**: half the plate non-starchy vegetables, a quarter beans, lentils, tofu or other protein, and a quarter whole grains or starchy vegetables. Choose whole fruit over juice.
- **Fiber helps.** Beans, lentils, oats, barley and vegetables slow the rise in blood sugar.
- **Be careful with meal timing.** White suggested that "two meals a day are better than three" (MH 304). **That is not safe for everyone with diabetes.** People who take insulin or sulfonylureas (such as glipizide, glyburide or glimepiride) can get dangerously low blood sugar if they skip meals. The safe takeaway is regular mealtimes and less grazing. Anyone who wants to change how many meals they eat should ask their doctor or diabetes educator first.
- **Vitamin B12:** People who eat mostly or fully plant-based diets need a reliable B12 source. Long-term metformin use can also lower B12. Encourage everyone to ask their doctor about checking it.

### Activity: cooking demonstration
Make a simple, diabetes-friendly plant-based meal, such as lentil-vegetable soup, a barley or brown-rice salad with beans and greens, and fresh berries. Offer small tasting portions. Hand out the recipes with the carbohydrate per serving if a dietitian can calculate it.

### Discussion questions
- Which foods from the "Creator's table" do you already enjoy?
- What is one meal this week where you could add vegetables or beans?
- What makes regular mealtimes hard in your daily life?

### Take-home habit
Fill half your plate with vegetables at one meal each day, and keep a regular schedule for meals.

## Week 2: Made to Move
**Remedies:** Exercise, pure air and sunlight

### Quote for the week
> "Inactivity is a fruitful cause of disease. Exercise quickens and equalizes the circulation of the blood." (MH 238)

Supporting reading: *"Exercise in the open air should be prescribed as a life-giving necessity. And for such exercises there is nothing better than the cultivation of the soil."* (MH 265)

### Teaching points
1. **"Action is a law of our being"** (MH 237). White saw the body as designed for movement.
2. **What modern research says:** For type 2 diabetes, physical activity is one of the most powerful tools there is. Working muscles take up glucose, and regular activity improves insulin sensitivity. Guidelines generally recommend about **150 minutes a week of moderate activity** (for example, 30 minutes on 5 days), plus strength training 2 to 3 times a week. Even a **10–15 minute walk after meals** can lower the rise in blood sugar after eating.
3. **Pure air:** White wrote that "the lungs ... need to be constantly supplied with fresh air" (MH 274). Open windows, ventilate the home and practice deep breathing.
4. **Sunlight:** Morning light outdoors helps the body clock and sleep. Vitamin D is worth asking the doctor about. Be moderate with sun exposure, because skin cancer risk increases with age.
5. **Gardening** is White's favorite exercise, and it combines movement, fresh air, sunlight and contact with nature.

### Diabetes and age notes (important)
- **Check with your doctor** before starting a new exercise program, especially if you have heart disease, retinopathy, neuropathy or kidney disease.
- **Low blood sugar during exercise:** People on insulin or sulfonylureas should check their blood sugar before and after activity and carry a fast-acting carbohydrate such as glucose tablets.
- **Foot care:** Wear well-fitting shoes and socks. Check your feet every day for blisters or sores, especially if you have reduced feeling in your feet.
- **Start where you are.** Chair exercises, water aerobics and balance work all count. Balance and strength training also lower the risk of falls.

### Activity
Take a group 10–15 minute walk outdoors right after the refreshments, at whatever pace each person can manage. If the weather is bad, lead a seated and standing chair-exercise routine. Teach White's deep-breathing advice to "breathe deeply, and in breathing and speaking to exercise the abdominal muscles" (MH 265).

### Discussion questions
- What kinds of movement did you enjoy when you were younger? Could you bring any of them back?
- What gets in the way: pain, time, weather or motivation? What could help?
- Who could be your walking partner this week?

### Take-home habit
Take a 10-minute walk after your largest meal at least 5 days this week. Open a window at home every day.

## Week 3: Balance and Renewal
**Remedies:** Water, temperance (abstemiousness) and rest

### Quote for the week
> "In health and in sickness, pure water is one of heaven's choicest blessings." (MH 237)

Supporting readings:
- *"Abstemiousness in diet is rewarded with mental and moral vigor."* (MH 307)
- *"Some make themselves sick by overwork. For these, rest, freedom from care, and a spare diet, are essential to restoration of health."* (MH ~236)

### Teaching points
1. **Water:** Make water your main drink. Replacing sugary drinks (soda, sweet tea, juice, specialty coffees) with water is one of the simplest ways to improve blood sugar.
2. **Temperance:** White defined temperance as avoiding what is harmful (alcohol and tobacco) and using good things in moderation, including portion sizes. Modern research strongly supports avoiding tobacco and limiting or avoiding alcohol.
3. **Rest:** Short or poor sleep raises insulin resistance and increases appetite. Most adults need 7–9 hours. A weekly Sabbath rest also brings real renewal for body and mind.
4. **Tea and coffee:** White considered them harmful stimulants (MH 326). Present this as her conviction. Current research on moderate plain coffee and tea is mostly neutral to favorable. The real concern for this group is the **sugar and cream** added to them.

### Diabetes and age notes (important)
- **Fluid limits:** People with heart failure or advanced kidney disease may have a fluid restriction. Follow your doctor's guidance over the general "drink more water" advice.
- **Thirst and frequent urination** can be signs of high blood sugar. Mention this to your doctor instead of only drinking more.
- **Alcohol** can cause delayed low blood sugar, especially in people on insulin or sulfonylureas.
- **Snoring and daytime sleepiness** can be signs of sleep apnea, which is common with type 2 diabetes. Encourage people to get evaluated.
- **Hydrotherapy** (warm and cold water treatments) is in the book. People with **neuropathy cannot feel temperature well and can be burned**. Always test water with an elbow or thermometer, never just with the feet. People with heart conditions should avoid extreme hot or cold treatments.

### Activity
- **"Rethink Your Drink":** Show the sugar in common drinks by measuring teaspoons of sugar next to each bottle. Then serve fruit-and-herb infused water, such as cucumber-mint or lemon-berry.
- **Plate-size demo:** Serve the same portion on a large plate and a small plate to show how moderation can feel satisfying.

### Discussion questions
- What do you drink most during a typical day?
- Where do you see "too much of a good thing" in your life: food, work, screens or worry?
- What would a restful evening routine look like for you?

### Take-home habit
Replace one sugary or sweetened drink a day with water. Choose a regular bedtime and keep screens out of the last 30 minutes before it.

## Week 4: Peace That Heals
**Remedies:** Trust in divine power, gratitude and the mind–body connection

### Quote for the week
> "Nothing tends more to promote health of body and of soul than does a spirit of gratitude and praise." (MH 251)

Supporting reading: *"The relation that exists between the mind and the body is very intimate. When one is affected, the other sympathizes ... Courage, hope, faith, sympathy, love, promote health and prolong life."* (MH 241)

### Teaching points
1. **The mind–body link:** White wrote that "grief, anxiety, discontent, remorse, guilt, distrust, all tend to break down the life forces" (MH 241). Modern research agrees that chronic stress, depression and loneliness affect physical health.
2. **Stress and blood sugar:** Stress hormones such as cortisol raise blood glucose. Many people with diabetes notice higher readings during difficult weeks.
3. **Diabetes distress is real.** Managing a chronic condition every day can feel exhausting. Depression is more common in people with diabetes, and treating it is part of good care.
4. **Trust, gratitude and community:** Faith, purpose, social support and gratitude are linked to better health. White also saw service to others as a path to health and joy.
5. **Prayer and medicine work together.** White noted that "Christ ... sanctioned the use of simple and natural remedies" (MH 233). Praying for healing and seeing your doctor are not in conflict.

### Notes for the leader (important)
- If someone seems persistently low, hopeless or overwhelmed, gently encourage them to talk with their doctor or a counselor. Have local mental-health resources and the **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (in the U.S.) ready.
- Avoid any suggestion that "more faith" will cure diabetes or replace treatment.

### Activity
- **Gratitude practice:** Each person writes down three things they are thankful for, and volunteers share one with the group.
- **Simple calming practice:** Lead 3 minutes of slow breathing paired with a short Scripture, such as Proverbs 17:22 ("A merry heart doeth good like a medicine") or Philippians 4:6–7.
- **Celebration and commitment:** Review the four weeks. Each person chooses **one habit from each week** to continue and pairs up with an accountability partner.

### Discussion questions
- How do worry and stress show up in your body or your blood-sugar readings?
- When have you felt God's care during a health challenge?
- Who in this church or community could you encourage this month?

### Take-home habit
Write down three things you are grateful for each evening, and check in with your accountability partner once a week.

## Optional extras
- **Before and after (voluntary and private):** With a nurse present, offer optional blood-pressure checks in Weeks 1 and 4. Invite people to track their own glucose readings, steps or weight privately. Never compare people publicly.
- **Weekly handout:** Include the quote with its MH page number, a plain-language summary, one action step and this reminder: *"Talk with your health-care provider before making major changes to your diet, exercise or medications."*
- **Next steps:** Groups that want more could continue with a full 8-week series (one remedy per week, following the NEWSTART model), a monthly plant-based potluck with recipe cards, or a walking group.

*The Ministry of Healing is in the public domain. Page numbers (MH) follow the standard 1905 edition. This class gives general health education and is not individual medical advice.*
