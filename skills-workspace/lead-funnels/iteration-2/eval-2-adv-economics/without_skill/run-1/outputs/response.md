# Breaking Even on Day One at $2 per Lead

> Note: I don't have the "Lead Funnels Swipe File" itself in front of me, so this answer uses standard direct-response funnel economics. The concepts (self-liquidating offers, break-even day, RFM-style segmentation) are standard. Where the swipe file may define something more narrowly (especially the "S" in RFMS), check its wording against what's below.

---

## 1. The three concepts

### Self-Liquidating Offer (SLO)
A **self-liquidating offer** is a low-priced front-end product, plus its order bump and upsells, whose revenue **pays back (liquidates) the ad cost** of getting the lead and the buyer. The point is not profit on the front end. The point is to **acquire customers for free**, so that everything they buy later (your core offer, continuity, high-ticket) is pure margin, and so you can scale ad spend without running out of cash.

Typical structure:
1. **Lead magnet / opt-in.** The $2 lead comes in here.
2. **Tripwire.** An irresistible $7–$27 product, shown on the thank-you page.
3. **Order bump.** A one-checkbox add-on at checkout, usually $10–$27.
4. **One-time offer (OTO / upsell).** A $47–$197 product shown right after purchase.
5. **Downsell.** A cheaper version for people who decline the OTO.

A funnel "self-liquidates" when **net front-end revenue per lead ≥ cost per lead**.

### Break-Even Day
**Break-even day** is the number of days after acquisition when a cohort's cumulative net revenue equals what you spent to acquire it.

- Break-even day = **0/1** means the funnel self-liquidates. You can scale ads as fast as your card limit allows, because cash comes back the same day.
- Break-even day = **30** means you must float a month of ad spend before getting it back. That caps scaling by cash, not by demand.
- Break-even day = **never (within 90 days)** means the funnel is broken. Fix the offer before buying more traffic.

Your target, "break even on day one," means **Day-1 Earnings Per Lead (EPL) ≥ $2.00 after fees and refunds.**

### RFMS
This is the classic **RFM** customer-scoring model plus a fourth factor:

| Letter | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **R: Recency** | How recently did they buy (or engage)? | Recent buyers are the most likely to buy again. Hit them with the next offer while they're hot. |
| **F: Frequency** | How many times have they bought? | Repeat buyers are proven. They get first access and premium offers. |
| **M: Monetary** | How much have they spent in total? | Big spenders get your high-ticket offers and personal outreach. |
| **S: Source** (often used this way) | Which ad, channel or lead magnet brought them in? | Tells you which traffic produces *buyers*, not just cheap leads. That's what lets you judge whether a $2 lead is actually good. |

Score each customer 1–5 on each factor. Segment, for example: "R5 F3+ M4+" are VIPs who get the high-ticket offer; "R1" are lapsed and get a win-back. Then compare average RFM scores **by Source** so you put more budget into the campaigns that produce high-value customers. A $3 lead from Source A whose customers score high can be worth far more than a $1.50 lead from Source B.

How RFMS ties back to break-even: day-one self-liquidation covers your ad cost; RFMS segmentation is how you make the **back-end** profit from those free customers, and how you choose which $2 leads to buy more of.

## 2. Designing the numbers

### Target
- Cost per lead (CPL): **$2.00**
- Goal: **Day-1 net EPL ≥ $2.00**
- Working example: **1,000 leads = $2,000 ad spend**

### The offer stack

| Step | Product (example) | Price | Take rate (assumption) | Base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tripwire | Quick-win product (template pack, mini-course, starter kit) | **$9** | **7%** of leads | 1,000 leads |
| Order bump | "Done-for-you" add-on / swipe pack | **$19** | **40%** of tripwire buyers | 70 buyers |
| OTO (upsell) | Core implementation course / toolkit | **$97** | **12%** of tripwire buyers | 70 buyers |
| Downsell | Lite version / payment plan | **$37** | **20%** of OTO decliners | 61.6 decliners |

### Day-one revenue per 1,000 leads

| Step | Units | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Tripwire: 1,000 × 7% = 70 × $9 | 70 | $630.00 |
| Order bump: 70 × 40% = 28 × $19 | 28 | $532.00 |
| OTO: 70 × 12% = 8.4 × $97 | 8.4 | $814.80 |
| Downsell: 61.6 × 20% = 12.3 × $37 | 12.3 | $455.84 |
| **Gross front-end revenue** | | **$2,432.64** |
| Payment fees (2.9% + $0.30 × ~91 charges) | | −$97.71 |
| Refunds (5% allowance) | | −$121.63 |
| **Net front-end revenue** | | **$2,213.30** |
| Ad spend (1,000 × $2) | | −$2,000.00 |
| **Day-one net profit** | | **+$213.30** |

**Net EPL = $2.21 vs. CPL = $2.00, so front-end ROAS ≈ 1.11.** The funnel **self-liquidates on day one** with about 10% headroom. That's the minimum cushion you want, because ad costs move around.

Key derived numbers:
- **Average cart value per buyer** = $2,432.64 / 70 ≈ **$34.75** gross (≈ $31.62 net)
- **Break-even buyer conversion** = $2.00 / $31.62 ≈ **6.3%** of leads must buy the tripwire, at this cart value
- **Break-even CPL** at a 7% tripwire take rate = **$2.21**. If ad costs rise above this, you're no longer self-liquidating.

### Sensitivity: what moves the needle

| Scenario | Tripwire take | Net EPL | Day-one result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic | 5% | ≈ $1.58 | −$0.42/lead (break-even moves to roughly day 7–14 via email follow-up) |
| **Base** | **7%** | **≈ $2.21** | **+$0.21/lead, day-one break-even** |
| Optimistic | 10% | ≈ $3.16 | +$1.16/lead, scale hard |

(Assumes bump, OTO and downsell rates stay the same, so net EPL scales roughly linearly with the tripwire take rate.)

The levers, in order of usual impact:
1. **Tripwire take rate.** Make the offer impossible to refuse: specific outcome, deep discount, shown right on the thank-you page while attention is highest. Even 7% → 9% adds about $0.63/lead.
2. **OTO price × take rate.** This is the biggest single revenue line. Testing $97 vs. $147, or adding a payment plan, often swings EPL more than anything else.
3. **Order bump.** Nearly free to add. 30–50% take rates are common when the bump is directly tied to the tripwire.
4. **Lower CPL.** Better creative, a tighter audience and a better opt-in page. Every $0.10 off CPL is $0.10 of margin.
5. **Day-1 follow-up.** A same-day email or SMS to non-buyers ("your discount expires at midnight") counts toward day-one revenue.

### Cohort cash curve (what happens after day one)

Per 1,000 leads, with $2,000 spent:

| Day | Source of revenue | Cumulative net revenue | vs. spend |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Tripwire + bump + OTO + downsell + same-day email | $2,213 | **+$213 (break-even)** |
| 7 | Tripwire cart-close / follow-up sequence (+$0.30/lead) | ~$2,513 | +$513 |
| 30 | Core offer launch to buyers + list (+$1.00/lead) | ~$3,513 | +$1,513 |
| 90 | High-ticket / continuity to RFMS VIP segment (+$2.00/lead) | ~$5,513 | +$3,513 |

(Back-end figures are placeholders. Replace them with your real cohort data after 30–60 days.)

### Using RFMS on this cohort
After 30 days, score the 70 + buyers:
- **R5 / F2+ / M ≥ $100** (bought tripwire + OTO and opened recent emails): your high-ticket call/application offer
- **R4–5 / F1 / M < $30** (tripwire only): nurture toward the core offer
- **R1–2** (no engagement in 30+ days): win-back sequence or suppress (keeps deliverability up)
- **By Source:** calculate net EPL *and* 90-day value per lead for each ad set. Keep scaling ad sets where Day-1 EPL ≥ $2 **or** where 90-day value per lead is high enough to justify a later break-even day. Cut the rest.

## 3. Your rules of thumb
1. **Day-1 net EPL ≥ CPL × 1.1** before scaling. That gives a 10% buffer.
2. Keep **tripwire take ≥ ~6.5%** and **average cart ≥ ~$32 net** at a $2 CPL.
3. Track **EPL by Source daily.** If a source's CPL creeps above its break-even CPL, fix it or kill it.
4. Treat front-end profit as a bonus. The real business is the **RFMS-driven back end** you've just acquired for free.
