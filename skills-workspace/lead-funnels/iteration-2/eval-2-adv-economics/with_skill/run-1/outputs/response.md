# Breaking Even on Day One at $2 per Lead

Based on the *Lead Funnels Swipe File* (Russell Brunson / ClickFunnels). Labels: **(book)** means the idea comes from the book; **(adapted)** or **(assumption)** marks my additions and illustrative numbers you should replace with your real data.

---

## 1. The three concepts

### Self-liquidating offer (SLO) (book)
An SLO is a low-priced product offered right after the opt-in, usually on the thank-you page or a sales page. It's priced so that **front-end sales pay back the ad spend that bought the leads**. The ad campaign "liquidates" itself. You end up with:
- leads that cost you nothing net ("free leads," in the book's words), and
- a **customer list**, which is worth more than a list of people who only took a freebie.

**The book's example:** $100 of ads brings 100 leads ($1 per lead). If 10% buy a $10 product, that's $100 back, which is break-even **(book)**.

### Break-even day (book)
Break-even day is the day on which **total revenue from a group of leads equals what you paid for them**. Not everyone buys at once. The book's example: if only 5% buy at first, follow-up can bring in another 5% by day 3, so you break even **on day 3** **(book)**.

The book's other version runs on lead value: at about **$1 per name per month** and a **$3** lead cost, you break even in **month 3** **(book)**. At your **$2** cost and the same $1/month, that method gives month 2. Day one requires the SLO to do all the work.

### RFMS (book)
The book scores contacts in the follow-up funnel by **RFMS: Recency, Frequency, Monetary, Social** **(book)**. The book names the four factors. The definitions and point scheme below are my working version **(adapted)**:

| Factor | Meaning | Example signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Recency** | How recently they engaged or bought | Opened or clicked in the last 7 days; bought in the last 30 |
| **Frequency** | How often they engage or buy | Number of purchases; clicks in the last 10 emails |
| **Monetary** | How much they've spent | Lifetime spend; whether they took the bump or the upsell |
| **Social** | How they engage socially | Messenger replies, shares, comments, referrals |

RFMS doesn't help you break even on day one. It's how you **turn the leads who didn't buy on day one into profit later**, and decide who gets which offer.

## 2. What day-one break-even requires

At $2 per lead, the condition is:

```
Net revenue collected in the first 24 hours ÷ leads acquired ≥ $2.00
```

Equivalently: **front-end conversion rate × net revenue per buyer ≥ $2**.

Use **net**, not gross. Subtract, per sale:
- payment processing (I assume about 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction, a common US card rate; check your processor);
- refunds (I assume 5%; replace with your real rate);
- any per-unit cost (zero for digital; product, shipping and handling for physical, which matters for "free + shipping" books);
- affiliate commissions, if affiliates sent the traffic.

Fixed costs (software, your time) are left out of the per-lead break-even. Cover them from profit after break-even.

### Why a single cheap product is not enough at $2

Conversion needed to recover $2 per lead from **one product with no bump or upsell** (net of the assumed fees):

| Price | Net per sale | Conversion needed for day-one break-even |
|---|---|---|
| $7 | $6.50 | **30.8%** (unrealistic) |
| $17 | $16.21 | **12.3%** (hard) |
| $27 | $25.92 | **7.7%** |
| $47 | $45.34 | **4.4%** |

The book's example (10% buying at $10) works at **$1** per lead. At **$2** you need to double the money per lead: 10% at about $20 net, or 5% at about $40 net. The usual fix is to keep the front-end price low enough to convert and **raise the average cart value** with an order bump and a one-time offer. Raising the front-end price too far usually lowers conversion.

## 3. Designed numbers

### The offer stack (assumption: digital product)
| Step | Offer | Price | Take rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opt-in | Pain-point lead magnet, e.g. "The 7 Biggest Mistakes (audience) Make When (doing X)" **(book formula)** | free | landing page 20–30% is "good" **(book)** |
| Thank-you page / SLO | Core low-ticket product: a short course, template pack, or workshop recording (the "make the full product, give away the best piece" tip **(book)**) | **$17** | **10%** of leads (target) |
| Order bump (checkbox on the order form) | Add-on such as a swipe file, checklist, or audio version | **$9.95** | **30%** of buyers |
| One-time offer (after purchase) | The next-level product | **$47** | **15%** of buyers |

### Per 100 leads ($200 ad spend)

| Line | Base case (10% front-end) | Break-even case (≈8.2%) |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers | 10 | 8.2 |
| Front-end ($17) | $170.00 | $139.40 |
| Bumps (3 × $9.95) | $29.85 | $24.48 |
| OTOs (1.5 × $47) | $70.50 | $57.81 |
| **Gross** | **$270.35** | **$221.69** |
| Processing (≈2.9% + $0.30/charge) | −$11.29 | −$9.26 |
| Refunds (5%) | −$13.52 | −$11.08 |
| **Net** | **$245.54** | **≈ $201** |
| **Net per lead** | **$2.46** | **≈ $2.01** |
| vs. $2.00 CPL | **+$0.46 per lead (+23%)** | break-even |

- **Average cart value:** ≈ $27.04 gross, ≈ $24.55 net per buyer.
- **Break-even front-end conversion:** $2.00 ÷ $24.55 ≈ **8.2%** of leads must buy within 24 hours.
- **Sensitivity:** each percentage point of front-end conversion is worth ≈ $0.25 per lead. At 6% you net ≈ $1.47 per lead (−$0.53). At 12% you net ≈ $2.95 (+$0.95).

### The other levers, with the same math
- **Cut cost per lead.** The book says leads typically cost $1–$3 **(book)**. At $1.50 per lead, break-even drops to about 6.1% conversion. A better hook or ad creative often helps more than a pricing change.
- **Raise the bump take rate.** 30% to 40% adds ≈ $0.10 per lead.
- **Raise the OTO take rate or price.** A $47 OTO going from 15% to 20% of buyers adds ≈ $0.22 per lead.
- **Raise opt-in conversion.** This lowers cost per lead for the same click cost. If clicks cost $0.60, a 30% opt-in rate gives $2.00 per lead, and 40% gives $1.50.

## 4. Plan B: if day one falls short, aim for day 3 (book)

Day one is the strictest target. The book's own example breaks even on **day 3**, with the follow-up funnel producing the second half of the buyers **(book)**. Build the follow-up anyway:

| Day | Message (to non-buyers) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Delivery email: the magnet link and the offer again | Some buy from the email, not the page |
| 1 | Story email: how you discovered the solution | Builds belief |
| 2 | Proof email: a result or case study, plus the offer | Handles objections |
| 3 | Deadline email: the SLO price or bonus expires | Urgency, if it's true; no fake deadlines |

Example: if day one gives 6% front-end conversion (≈ $1.47 net per lead) and follow-up adds 3 more points by day 3 (≈ +$0.74), you're at ≈ $2.21 per lead, **break-even on day 3**, with leads still on the list after that.

## 5. Using RFMS after day one (adapted)

Score every contact 0–3 on each factor (0–12 total) and route:

| Segment | Typical RFMS | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyers who took the OTO** | High M, high R | Higher-ticket offer (coaching, membership, event). These pay for your next ad tests |
| **Front-end buyers only** | Medium M, high R | A replay of the OTO offer, then the next product |
| **Engaged non-buyers** | High R/F, M = 0, maybe high S | The day 1–3 sequence, a webinar invitation, more content |
| **Cold** | Low R, low F | Re-engagement email, then fewer sends or removal (protects deliverability) |

Over time this is how you raise value per name above the book's baseline of about **$1 per name per month**, toward its "$2–$50 with a strong relationship" range **(book; estimates, not guarantees)**.

## 6. How to measure day-one break-even

1. **Track by cohort.** Group leads by the day they opted in, and count only revenue from that group within 24 hours. Ad-platform dashboards often report late or with different attribution windows, so use your checkout and email-tool data.
2. **Judge on enough leads.** At ~8–10% conversion, 100 leads means only 8–10 buyers, which is noise. Wait for **300–500 leads** before deciding (at 500 leads and 8%, the 95% margin is roughly ±2.4 points).
3. **Watch the three numbers that matter:** cost per lead, front-end conversion, and average net cart value. Their combination decides break-even day.
4. **Refunds come later.** Recheck the cohort's net at day 30.

---

## 7. Caveats

- All prices, take rates, fees and refund rates above are **assumptions** for illustration. The book's revenue figures are also illustrative, not guarantees **(book)**.
- Don't let the day-one target push you into bait-and-switch. The thank-you page must **deliver the promised magnet first** **(book)**. Bumps and OTOs must be honestly described, and deadlines must be real.
- Follow CAN-SPAM, GDPR and TCPA for the email and SMS follow-up, and disclose affiliate links **(std)**.
- The book was written around 2019 for the ClickFunnels ecosystem. The math works with any funnel or checkout tool.

---

## Summary

At **$2 per lead**, day-one break-even requires about **$2 of net revenue per lead within 24 hours**. A single $7 SLO can't do it (it needs ~31% conversion). A **$17 front end + $9.95 bump (30%) + $47 OTO (15%)** gives about **$24.55 net per buyer**. That breaks even at **≈ 8.2%** front-end conversion and earns **≈ $0.46 per lead** at a 10% target. If you fall short, run the book's day-3 follow-up to close the gap, then use **RFMS** to increase what each name earns after that.
