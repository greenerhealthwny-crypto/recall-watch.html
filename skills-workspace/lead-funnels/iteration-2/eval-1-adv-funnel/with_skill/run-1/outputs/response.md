# Lead Funnel for a Local Furniture Flipper (Facebook Marketplace)

Built on the five-part lead funnel from Russell Brunson's *Lead Funnels Swipe File*: lead magnet, landing page, thank-you page, sales page, follow-up funnel. Labels: **(book)** means the idea comes from the book; **(adapted)** means I changed it to fit a local physical-goods business; **(assumption)** marks numbers I made up for illustration that you should replace with your own.

---

## 0. First, where the book's model does not fit as-is

The book is written mostly for **info products and online offers**: unlimited copies, national traffic, a $7–$37 product on the thank-you page. A furniture flipper is different in four ways, and the funnel has to account for them:

| Book assumption | Your reality | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Unlimited inventory (an ebook sells 1,000 times) | Every piece is one of a kind, and you can only flip so many a month | The list's job is to **sell each piece faster and closer to asking price**, and to **find inventory**, not to sell the same item to 10% of the list |
| National audience | Buyers have to live within driving distance | Your list will be smaller. Quality (local, active buyers) beats size |
| You own the traffic page | Marketplace is Facebook's platform. Buyers there are *Facebook's* contacts, not yours | The whole point of the funnel is to move buyers from Marketplace onto **your own list**: "the money is in the list" **(book)** |
| The "$1 per name per month" rule of thumb | That figure is Brunson's estimate for online info lists **(book)** | Work out your own value per name from your profit per flip (section 6) |

You actually have **two lists to build**:
1. **Buyers** (demand): people who want good used furniture before it hits Marketplace.
2. **Sellers** (supply): people moving, downsizing or clearing an estate who want furniture gone fast. For most flippers, steady supply is the harder problem.

The funnel below covers both. Start with the buyer funnel; add the seller funnel once the first one works. "Done is better than perfect" **(book)**.

## 1. Lead magnet

**Rules from the book:** keep it simple, attach a curiosity hook, focus on **pain**, and make several so you can test which hits **(book)**.

### Buyer-side pains
- Getting beaten to a good deal on Marketplace because someone messaged first.
- Buying something that turns out to be particleboard, wobbly, smelly, or carrying bed bugs.
- Not knowing whether a price is fair.
- Driving 40 minutes to a no-show or a scam.
- Buying a piece that does not fit through the door.

### Primary magnet: the "First Dibs" drop list (adapted)
A free VIP list that gets **new pieces 24 hours before they are posted on Marketplace**, with photos, measurements and price. This is a **newsletter-style magnet**, modeled on Groupon's local-deals opt-in (#18) and on the newsletter magnets in the swipe file (#9, #25) **(book)**. For a local reseller it's the strongest offer because it solves the biggest pain: missing out on good pieces.

### Secondary magnet: a pain-point guide
Use the book's formula, "The (#) Biggest Mistakes (audience) Make (when doing X)" **(book)**:

> **"The 7 Biggest Mistakes People Make Buying Used Furniture on Facebook Marketplace (and how to spot a solid-wood bargain in 30 seconds)"**

A one- or two-page PDF, in the spirit of Brendon Burchard's "1-Page Productivity Planner" **(book)**. Possible contents: the drawer-joint test (dovetail vs. staples), the solid-wood vs. veneer vs. particleboard check, bed-bug and smoke checks, measure-the-doorway first, fair-price ranges for common pieces in your area, and safe pickup and payment habits.

Which of the nine fast formats this uses **(book)**: a short text guide, plus an "iPhone how-to" video (#8 in the book's list) of you doing the 30-second wood check. Your before/after flips are also a natural "example book" (#2): "12 Flips That Sold in Under 24 Hours."

### Hooks to test (3–5, one "Mistakes" hook required)
1. "The 7 Biggest Mistakes People Make Buying Used Furniture on Marketplace"
2. "See Our Best Pieces 24 Hours Before Anyone Else in [Town]"
3. "Why the Good Dressers Are Gone Before You Even See Them"
4. "The 30-Second Test That Tells You if It's Real Wood"
5. "Get First Dibs: Restored Solid-Wood Furniture, Delivered in [Town]"

The same magnet can carry different titles **(book)**. Test 2 and 3 against each other first.

### Seller-side magnet
> **"Moving? Get a Same-Week Cash Offer and Free Pickup for Your Furniture"**

Format: a quote or booking request, modeled on the local-service lead funnels in the swipe file: Roto-Rooter's "Call Now / Schedule Service Online" (#54) and "Find Reliable, Low Cost Tree Service" (#74), both of which ask for name, email, phone and a few questions **(book)**.

## 2. Landing page

**Three elements only** **(book)**: an intriguing headline, an image of the lead magnet, an opt-in form. Spend 5–10 minutes building it, then test and adjust **(book)**.

### Buyer landing page (draft)

> **Headline:** See Our Best Furniture 24 Hours Before It Hits Marketplace
> **Subhead:** Free "First Dibs" list for [Town / 25-mile radius]. Plus: *The 7 Biggest Mistakes People Make Buying Used Furniture on Marketplace* (free PDF).
> **Image:** a before/after of a recent flip next to a mockup of the PDF cover.
> **Form:**
> - First name
> - Email
> - "What are you hunting for?" (checkboxes: dressers, tables, chairs/sofas, mid-century, outdoor, kids)
> - *Optional:* mobile number, with an unticked box: "Text me new drops. Message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out."
> **Button:** "Give Me First Dibs"
> **One line of proof:** "47 pieces sold to list members before they were ever posted." (Use your real number. Leave it off until you have one.)

Keep fields minimal. The interest question is worth its cost because it lets you send dresser people only dressers. The book shows several funnels asking "name + email + questions" (#4, #35, #63) **(book)**.

**Conversion benchmark:** the book says a good page converts about 20–30% (the Marketing Secrets Blackbook page hit 55.63%) **(book)**. Warm traffic from your own buyers should beat cold ad traffic; measure it.

### Seller landing page (draft)
> **Headline:** Moving or Downsizing? Get a Cash Offer for Your Furniture This Week
> **Image:** your truck/van, or you carrying a dresser out of a house.
> **Form:** name, email, phone, zip code, "What do you want gone?" plus a photo upload.
> **Button:** "Get My Offer"

### Where the traffic comes from (adapted)
- **Every Marketplace listing:** a line in the description such as "Want first dibs on pieces like this? Search '[Your Business Name] First Dibs'" or a link to your Facebook Page. **Check Facebook's current Commerce Policies first.** Marketplace has rules on external links and on moving buyers off-platform, and they change. A Facebook Page or Group you own is a safer bridge.
- **At every pickup or delivery:** the highest-quality lead you will ever get is someone who just bought from you. Hand them a card with a QR code to the opt-in page, or ask: "Want me to text you when I get another piece like this?" Then collect consent properly (see compliance below).
- **A sticker or tag on every piece you sell** ("Restored by ___ · first dibs at [QR]"). The piece sits in a living room, and guests ask where it came from.
- **Free list-building before ads** **(book, adapted)**: the book suggests guesting on podcasts and blogs. For you, the local equivalents are Nextdoor, local Facebook groups (where rules allow), realtors and stagers, moving companies and estate-sale organizers. The last three are also seller-lead sources.
- **Paid:** small local Facebook/Instagram ads to the "Mistakes" guide, radius-targeted. Only after the organic version converts.

## 3. Thank-you page

**Two jobs only** **(book)**: deliver what you promised (a download button right on the page), then push one next step. **No bait-and-switch** **(book)**.

### Buyer thank-you page (draft)

> **You're on the First Dibs list.**
> Your guide: **[Download "The 7 Biggest Mistakes…" PDF]**
> You'll get our next drop by email [and text] before it goes on Marketplace.
>
> **One next step:** Here's what's available *right now*, before Marketplace sees it → **[See This Week's Pieces]**

The next step goes to your **live inventory page** (the "sales page" below). It's the physical-goods version of the book's thank-you-page offer: the reader is at peak interest, so show them what they can buy today.

If you have no inventory listed that day, use the book's beginner fallback: send them to the place where you publish, such as your Instagram or TikTok of before/after flips **(book)**.

### Seller thank-you page
> "Got it. I'll text you within 24 hours with an offer or a few questions. Want a faster offer? Reply with photos of the back and the drawers." Plus a link to two or three before/after flips so they see you're real. Fisher Investments (#76) and the local service funnels (#54, #74) use a similar "here's what happens next" thank-you page **(book)**.

## 4. Sales page (adapted: there is no ebook to sell)

The book's sales page sells a **self-liquidating offer (SLO)**: a low-price product whose front-end sales pay for the ads, and which turns leads into *customers* **(book)**. The book also says you can skip this part until you have a product **(book)**.

For a reseller, the honest adaptation is:

### A. The "First Dibs" inventory page (core)
- One page (a simple site, a Shopify page, or even a public photo album) listing current pieces: photos, dimensions, condition notes, price, pickup/delivery area.
- **"Reserve with a deposit"** button (for example, a refundable $25 deposit holds it 48 hours). This beats "is this still available?" messages and no-shows.
- Each piece shows "List members see this 24 hours early" so buyers who come from Marketplace learn the list exists.

### B. Optional true SLO-style add-ons (low price, high volume)
These are the closest thing you have to the book's $7–$15 thank-you-page products (for example "44 Sensational Salads" leading to a $15 cleanse, #27) **(book)**:
- **Delivery + placement** for $39–$79 (assumption: set your own price). It's high margin if you already own a truck.
- **A furniture care kit** (wax, felt pads, touch-up marker, microfiber cloth) for $15–$20. Unlike your furniture, you can sell this item many times.
- **Custom refinish or paint on request** ("Pick the color before I finish it"). This is a pre-sale, which means no inventory risk.
- Affiliate products such as cleaners or furniture-safe paint are possible too **(book: affiliate offers are allowed)**. Disclose affiliate links.

### C. Seller side
The "sales page" is your **offer**: a cash price and a pickup time. Close by text or phone.

## 5. Follow-up funnel

The book uses automated email, SMS and Messenger plus broadcasts, and scores contacts by **RFMS: recency, frequency, monetary, social** **(book)**.

### Buyer sequence (automated)
| When | Channel | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Email | Deliver the guide again plus "Here's what's available now" |
| Day 1 | Email | **Story:** one flip, start to finish. "Found this $20 dresser on a curb. Here's what I did, and the mistake I almost made." Document the journey **(book)** |
| Day 3 | Email | **Mistake #1 in depth** + one current piece that passes the test |
| Day 5 | Email | **Offer:** "This week's drop" + delivery add-on |
| Day 7 | Email | "Reply and tell me what you're hunting for. I'll keep an eye out." This feeds the custom-request list, and replies help deliverability |
| Ongoing | Email + SMS (opted-in only) | **Weekly "Thursday Drop"** broadcast: 3–8 pieces, 24 hours before Marketplace. SMS only for pieces matching what they asked for |

### Seller sequence
- Instant auto-text: "Got your request. Offer within 24 hours."
- If they don't book: day 2 "still need it gone?" and day 7 "moving date coming up?" Then quarterly: "Know anyone moving? I pay $X for referrals." **(adapted)**

### RFMS scoring (adapted for furniture)
| Factor | Score up when… |
|---|---|
| **Recency** | they opened or clicked a drop recently, or bought recently |
| **Frequency** | they've bought more than once, or click most drops |
| **Monetary** | total spent, and whether they buy big pieces (sofa or dining set vs. a side table) |
| **Social** | they referred a buyer or seller, left a review, shared a post, or tagged you |

Use the scores to give your top 20–50 buyers an **"inner circle" text** an hour before the email drop. Re-engage contacts with low recency scores, or prune them.

### Daily publishing (book)
Pick one medium you can stand and post daily **(book)**. For a flipper, short-form video is the obvious choice: before/after, the "find," the repair, the reveal. Every video ends with "Get first dibs: link in bio." You only need to be one or two steps ahead of your audience **(book)**. Showing your process is the content.

## 6. List math (rebuilt for a local reseller)

The book's rule of thumb is about **$1 per name per month** if you do the relationship poorly, and $2–$50 with a strong relationship **(book)**. Those figures come from online info marketing. **Don't assume them for a local furniture list.** Calculate your own from your numbers.

### Formula
```
Value per name per month = (list sales per month × average profit per sale) ÷ list size
```

### Worked example (all numbers are assumptions; replace them)
- List: **500** local buyers
- Average **profit** per flip (sale price minus purchase, materials and gas): **$110**
- List members buying per month: **2%** = 10 pieces
- Add-ons: 4 deliveries × $40 profit = $160

Monthly list profit = 10 × $110 + $160 = **$1,260**, so **≈ $2.52 per name per month**.

| Scenario | % of list buying/month | Pieces | Profit/month | $/name/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weak | 0.5% | 2.5 | $275 | $0.55 |
| Base | 2% | 10 | $1,260 | $2.52 |
| Strong | 4% | 20 | $2,360 | $4.72 |

### The capacity ceiling (the part info marketers skip)
If you can only source and restore **15 pieces a month**, you can't sell 20 pieces to the list. Past the point where the list buys everything you make, list growth doesn't add revenue in a straight line. It pays you in other ways:
- **Faster sales:** pieces sell in hours, not weeks, so less storage and more turns.
- **Higher prices:** list buyers get first look and don't haggle the way cold Marketplace buyers do. You can test pricing 10–15% higher, or "first dibs at full price, then Marketplace."
- **Less time** spent answering "is this available?" messages.
- At that point the **bottleneck is supply**, which is why the seller list matters.

### Seller-list math
```
Value of a seller lead = (% of seller leads you buy from) × (pieces per buy) × (profit per piece)
```
Example (assumption): 1 in 8 seller leads turns into a buy, averaging 2 usable pieces at $110 profit, so each seller lead is worth **≈ $27**. That's why paying for seller leads can make sense even when paying for buyer leads doesn't.

### Ad math and break-even
The book says leads typically cost **$1–$3** in Brunson's market, and computes break-even by value per name: at $1/name/month and a $3 lead, you break even in month 3 **(book)**.

Your version (assumptions):
- Buyer lead from a local ad: **$2**. At $2.52/name/month, break-even comes in **month 1**. At the weak $0.55, it's **month 4**.
- **Front-end recovery (SLO thinking):** if 3% of new leads buy a $40-profit delivery or care kit on the thank-you page, that's $1.20 back per lead right away. That covers 60% of a $2 lead before any furniture sells.
- **Rule:** don't scale paid ads until you know your real profit per flip and your real list conversion rate from 4–8 weeks of organic list growth. Track by source (pickup card, Marketplace listing, ad), because buyers who already bought from you will be worth far more than ad leads.

## 8. Compliance and platform cautions

- **Email:** follow CAN-SPAM (physical address, working unsubscribe, honest subject lines). Follow GDPR/CASL if any contacts are outside the US.
- **SMS:** marketing texts in the US require **prior express written consent** under the TCPA. Use the unticked checkbox, keep proof of consent, honor STOP, and check your state's rules (some states are stricter). Don't add buyers to your text list just because they messaged you about a dresser.
- **Facebook Marketplace:** read Meta's current Commerce Policies and Marketplace rules before putting links in listings or steering buyers off-platform. Running a Facebook Page or Group is the low-risk bridge.
- **Business basics:** check whether your city or state requires a resale or sales-tax permit for regular reselling.
- **Affiliate links:** disclose them **(std)**.
- **Revenue figures** in the book, and the numbers above, are illustrations, not guarantees **(book)**.
