# Lead Funnels for a Chiropractor: Parts, Lead Magnet, and Hook

*Note: I'm answering from general knowledge of the ClickFunnels / Russell Brunson lead-funnel method that the Lead Funnels Swipe File is built on. I don't have the book's exact wording or page-by-page examples in front of me, so check the specifics against your copy.*

---

## 1. The parts of a lead funnel

In the ClickFunnels model, a lead funnel exists for one reason: to trade something valuable for a visitor's contact details, usually a name and email address, sometimes a phone number. That trade starts a relationship you can then build on by email. A typical lead funnel has these parts:

| # | Part | What it does | Chiropractor example |
|---|------|--------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Traffic source** | Gets the right people to the page | Local Facebook/Instagram ads, Google, a QR code at the front desk, partner gyms, workshops |
| 2 | **The hook** | The headline or angle that stops the scroll and creates curiosity | "The 3 Morning Mistakes That Make Back Pain Worse" |
| 3 | **Squeeze (opt-in) page** | A simple one-step page: headline, short promise, maybe an image of the magnet, and an email form. It has no navigation and no distractions. | "Get the free guide: enter your email" |
| 4 | **The lead magnet (bait)** | The free item they get for opting in. It should solve one specific problem quickly. | A PDF, checklist, or short video series |
| 5 | **Thank-you / delivery page** | Confirms the opt-in, delivers the magnet, and makes the **next offer**. This could be a low-cost tripwire or a "book your new-patient visit" call to action. | "Here's your guide. Want a $49 first-visit exam and adjustment? Book now." |
| 6 | **Email follow-up sequence** | Builds a relationship and turns leads into patients. Brunson recommends a "Soap Opera Sequence" of story-driven emails, followed by ongoing "Seinfeld"-style daily or weekly emails. | Day 1 deliver the guide, Day 2 your story, Day 3 a patient success story, Day 4 an objection breaker, Day 5 the offer with a deadline |

The core idea is **Hook, Story, Offer**. The hook gets attention. The story, told on the page and in the emails, builds trust. The offer is the magnet first and the paid next step after that.

## 2. What lead magnet to create

A good lead magnet is:
- **Specific.** It solves one narrow problem, not "everything about spinal health."
- **Fast to use.** Someone can read or watch it in under 10 minutes and get a quick win.
- **Valuable on its own.** It is not just a disguised sales pitch.
- **Connected to your paid offer.** Using it naturally leads to "I should get checked by a chiropractor."

### Recommended primary magnet
**"The 7-Minute Morning Back Pain Relief Routine" (a free PDF plus a short follow-along video)**
- It shows simple stretches and posture resets that people can do at home.
- It includes a "red flags" section on when stretching isn't enough and you need an exam. That section bridges naturally to your offer.
- Put your new-patient special on the last page and on the thank-you page.

### Other strong options
1. **Self-assessment quiz.** "What's Really Causing Your Back Pain? Take the 60-Second Quiz." Quizzes convert very well, and each answer segments the lead by condition (low back, neck, headaches, sciatica) so you can send tailored emails.
2. **Checklist or cheat sheet.** "The Desk Worker's Posture Checklist: 10 Fixes to Stop Neck & Shoulder Pain."
3. **Condition-specific report.** "Sciatica Relief Guide: 5 Things to Try Before Drugs or Surgery."
4. **Free video mini-course or challenge.** "5-Day Pain-Free Posture Challenge," with one short video per day delivered by email. This builds habit and trust.
5. **Offer-as-magnet.** "Free Posture & Spinal Screening" or "Claim a $X New-Patient Exam." This converts at a lower rate than an educational magnet, but the leads are much hotter.

**Tip:** Start with **one** niche avatar, such as desk workers with neck pain, new moms, runners, or people with sciatica. The narrower the magnet, the higher the opt-in rate.

## 3. What hook to use

Your hook is the headline and angle on the ad and the squeeze page. Proven hook styles from the swipe-file approach include:

- **Mistake hook:** "The 3 Sleeping Positions That Are Secretly Wrecking Your Spine"
- **Curiosity / secret hook:** "The 7-Minute Morning Routine Our Patients Use to Wake Up Without Back Pain"
- **Specific-result hook:** "How to Ease Low Back Pain in 7 Days Without Pills or Expensive Gadgets"
- **Callout hook (name the avatar):** "Desk Workers: Is Your Chair Causing Your Headaches?"
- **Quiz hook:** "Take the 60-Second Back Pain Quiz and Find Out What's Really Causing It"
- **Myth-buster hook:** "Why 'Just Rest It' Is the Worst Advice for Back Pain"

### Recommended pairing to start
- **Hook:** *"Wake Up Without Back Pain: The Free 7-Minute Morning Routine (No Pills, No Equipment)"*
- **Lead magnet:** the 7-Minute Morning Back Pain Relief Routine (PDF plus video)
- **Thank-you page offer:** a limited new-patient exam special with a "book now" calendar link
- **Follow-up:** a 5-email Soap Opera Sequence that tells your story, shares patient wins, handles the "does chiropractic actually work / is it safe / is it expensive?" objections, and ends with the deadline on the special

---

## 4. Quick build checklist
1. Pick one avatar and one pain point.
2. Write 3–5 hook variations and split-test them on the squeeze page.
3. Create the magnet. A simple Canva PDF and a phone-recorded video are fine.
4. Build the squeeze page with a headline, 3 bullet benefits, an image of the magnet, and an email form (plus an optional phone field).
5. Build the thank-you page with delivery, a short intro video from you, and the new-patient offer.
6. Write the 5-email follow-up sequence.
7. Drive local traffic, track the opt-in rate (aim for 30%+ on a good squeeze page) and the bookings, then iterate.

**Compliance note:** Keep claims educational and realistic. Avoid promising cures. Include a disclaimer that the content isn't a substitute for professional diagnosis, and follow your state board's and HIPAA/privacy rules for patient testimonials and data.
