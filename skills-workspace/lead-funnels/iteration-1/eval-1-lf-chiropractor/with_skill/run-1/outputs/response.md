# A Lead Funnel for Your Chiropractic Practice

*Based on Russell Brunson's* Lead Funnels Swipe File *(ClickFunnels, about 2019). Tools and prices have changed since it came out, but the strategy still works. You can also build this in any funnel or email tool, not only ClickFunnels.*

The book's core idea: **"The money is in the list."** Build the list first, then build the relationship. For a chiropractor, the list is the group of local people with pain who will think of you first when they're ready to book.

---

## Part 1: The five parts of a lead funnel

| # | Part | What it does | What it looks like for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lead magnet** | Gives real value in exchange for contact details | A short guide, checklist or video on one specific pain (back, neck, sciatica, desk posture) |
| 2 | **Landing page** | Trades the magnet for an email address | Three things only: **an intriguing headline, an image of the lead magnet, and an opt-in form.** The book says a good page converts **20–30%** |
| 3 | **Thank-you page** | Delivers what you promised, then offers one next step | Put the download link right on the page, then add **one** offer. No bait-and-switch |
| 4 | **Sales page** | Sells a low-cost first offer that pays back your ad spend (a "self-liquidating offer", or SLO) and turns leads into *customers* | A paid intro offer (see below). You can skip this at first and send people to booking |
| 5 | **Follow-up funnel** | Builds the relationship through automated email and SMS plus broadcasts | A short welcome sequence, then regular tips. Score contacts by **RFMS**: recency, frequency, monetary, social |

Brunson's rule: **"Done is better than perfect."** Spend 5–10 minutes building the landing page, get to 80%, launch, then test and tweak.

## Part 2: What lead magnet to create

The book's main rules for lead magnets:

1. **Focus on pain.** Myron Golden's tip: most people move *away from pain*. Business owners wrongly assume people move *toward pleasure*. Your patients are a perfect fit for this. They come to you because something hurts.
2. **Keep it simple.** Brendon Burchard's best-known magnet is a one-page planner (example #103). A one-page checklist is enough.
3. **Make several and test them.** You can't know in advance which one will hit. The same magnet can also carry different titles.

### My recommended primary lead magnet

**"The 7 Biggest Mistakes Back-Pain Sufferers Make (That Keep the Pain Coming Back)"**: a 2–4 page PDF, or a 10-minute video, plus a one-page "Desk & Sleep Posture Checklist."

Why this one:
- It uses the book's proven **pain-point formula**: *"The (#) Biggest Mistakes (audience) Make (when doing X)."* Myron Golden's "12 Biggest Mistakes Network Marketers Make" is the book's model. It led into a webinar on *fixing* those mistakes. Your version leads into an exam on fixing *their* mistakes.
- It's fast to make. Use one of the book's nine quick formats, such as an **iPhone how-to video** (#8) or a short **text guide** (#1–3). Even better, record a short **workshop** (#9), like a 30-minute "Back Pain Q&A" at your clinic. Its first segment becomes the magnet.
- Each "mistake" naturally points to something you treat, so the magnet *pre-sells* the visit without feeling salesy.

Example mistakes to cover (keep them general and educational):
- sitting for hours without a break;
- sleeping in a position that strains the neck;
- relying only on painkillers;
- the wrong kind of stretching;
- lifting kids or groceries the wrong way;
- "waiting it out" for months;
- ignoring numbness or tingling.

### Other magnets to test (one niche each)

| Audience | Magnet idea | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Desk workers | "The 5-Minute Desk Reset: 4 Stretches for Tech Neck" | iPhone video + 1-page PDF |
| Sciatica sufferers | "Sciatica Survival Guide: What to Do (and Avoid) in the First 72 Hours" | PDF guide |

The last row follows the "free evaluation" model in the book. KlientBoost's "Get Your Free Facebook Ads Evaluation ($500 value)" (#11) asks for **name + email + phone**, and the thank-you page shows client testimonials. Local service funnels like Roto-Rooter (#54, "Call Now / Schedule Service Online") and the tree service (#74) also go straight to booking. Use this for people who are ready now. Use the PDF or video for people who aren't yet.

## Part 3: Hooks (headlines) to test

The book says every magnet needs **curiosity, a "hook."** Write 3–5 hooks and test them on the same magnet:

1. **"The 7 Biggest Mistakes Back-Pain Sufferers Make (And How to Stop Making Them)"** (the pain formula)
2. **"Why Your Back Pain Keeps Coming Back (It's Probably Not What You Think)"** (curiosity)
3. **"The 2-Minute 'Pillow Test' That Reveals Why You Wake Up With a Stiff Neck"** (modeled on David DeAngelo's "The Kiss Test," #1: one named, intriguing test)
4. **"Free Download: The 5-Minute Desk Reset Used by Office Workers in [Your Town]"** (modeled on Burchard's "1-Page Productivity Planner Used by CEOs…", #103, plus local proof)
5. **"Before You Book Another Massage or Refill Your Painkillers… Read This"** (pattern interrupt)

Add your town name to the headline or subhead. Local relevance lifts opt-ins for a clinic.

## Part 4: The full funnel, step by step

**Landing page**
- Headline: hook #1.
- Image: a mock-up of the guide's cover with your photo.
- Form: **first name + email** only. The book's top pages keep fields minimal. Add a mobile number field only if you'll send SMS, and only with clear consent.
- Optional subhead: "From Dr. [Name], [Town] chiropractor for 12 years."

**Thank-you page**
- "Here's your guide" and a **download button right on the page**. Deliver first, because that builds trust.
- **One** next step. Choose one:
  - **Self-liquidating offer:** "New-Patient Back Pain Assessment: exam + posture scan + first adjustment for $[low intro price]" (check your state board's and insurers' rules on discounted or free services). Or sell a low-price digital product, such as a "$7 7-Day Back Reset Video Program" in the style of the $7 offers in examples #4, #5 and #7.
  - **Beginner option:** if you don't have an offer ready, send them to your YouTube channel or podcast instead, as the book suggests.

**Follow-up sequence (email, plus SMS if they opted in)**
- **Day 0:** Delivery email with the guide link and a short intro to you.
- **Day 1:** Story. A patient (with permission, and no protected details) who was making mistake #3.
- **Day 3:** Quick win. A one-minute video of a stretch.
- **Day 5:** Offer. "Book your assessment. Here are this week's open times."
- **Day 7:** Answer an objection (FAQ: "Does it hurt? Do you take my insurance?") and repeat the offer.
- **After that:** a weekly tip email, plus a reminder of the offer about once a month.

**Publish daily (free traffic)**
The book says to publish every day in one medium you can stand: a blog, a podcast or a vlog. A chiropractor could post a 60-second "Posture Tip of the Day" video and repurpose it as a short blog post. Every piece ends with "Get the free guide → [link]." **Document the journey**: you only need to be a step or two ahead of your audience. Also, guest on local podcasts and community blogs, and offer the guide as your call to action.

## Part 5: The math (book's numbers, as estimates)

- **Lead cost:** Brunson cites **$1–$3 per lead** in his market. Local health leads often cost more, so track your own.
- **Self-liquidating example:** $300 of ads at $3 per lead gives 100 leads. If 5 buy a $59 intro assessment, that's $295 back, so your leads are almost free. Every patient who continues care is profit.
- **Break-even day:** if only 3% buy at first, follow-up emails often bring more buyers by day 3–7. The day your sales cover your ad spend is your break-even day.
- **List value:** the book's rule of thumb is about **$1 per name per month** even if you do it poorly, and $2–$50 with a strong relationship. For a clinic, a single new patient can be worth hundreds of dollars across a care plan, so even a 500-person local list is a real asset. These figures are illustrations, not guarantees.

## Important cautions for a health practice

- **Health claims:** keep content educational. Avoid promises like "cure" or "permanent relief," and follow FTC rules and your state chiropractic board's advertising rules. Use testimonials only with written consent and typical-results disclaimers where required.
- **Privacy:** don't collect detailed health information on the opt-in form. Keep the form to name and email. If you later collect symptom details, check whether HIPAA applies to how you store and send that information.
- **Email and SMS law:** get clear consent, include an unsubscribe link, and follow CAN-SPAM. For texts, get express written consent under the TCPA.

**Your first move this week:** write the "7 Biggest Mistakes" guide (or record it as a 10-minute iPhone video), build the three-element landing page, and add one thank-you-page offer. Launch it and point your social posts at it. Then test a second hook.
