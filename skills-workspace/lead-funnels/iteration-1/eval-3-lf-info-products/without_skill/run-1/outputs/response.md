# Fast Ways to Create a Lead Magnet or Info Product (No Book Required)

**A caveat first:** I'm answering from general knowledge of Russell Brunson's *Lead Funnels* material and the direct-response tradition it draws on. I don't have the Swipe File in front of me, so I can't quote its exact list or page order. The ideas below are the shortcuts that material and its sources keep coming back to. They all rest on one point: **your lead magnet doesn't need to be long. It needs to be specific and solve one problem quickly.** A one-page checklist often converts better than a 200-page book.

## 1. Interview an expert (or several)
- Get on a call with someone who has the result your audience wants. Ask the questions your audience would ask, and record it.
- The recording becomes the product. You can also have it transcribed into a PDF, or break it into a short series.
- Interviewing several experts gives you an "expert roundup" or a mini interview series. You become the go-to source without having to be the expert.

## 2. Record yourself teaching, then transcribe
- Run a live webinar, workshop, Q&A or training call, or just talk into your phone about what you know.
- Send the audio to a transcription service, tidy up the text, and you have a report or mini e-book.
- Talking is much faster than writing. An hour of talking gives you roughly 8,000 to 10,000 words.

## 3. Short "one problem, one fix" formats
These can be made in an afternoon:
- **Cheat sheets / checklists**: the steps you already follow, written down.
- **Swipe files / templates**: scripts, emails, ads or headlines people can copy.
- **Resource or toolkit lists**: "The 7 tools I use to…"
- **Quick-start guides / mini reports**: 3 to 10 pages on one outcome.
- **Case studies**: walk through one result, yours or a client's, step by step.
- **Quizzes / assessments**: they collect leads and tell people what they need next.

## 4. Repurpose what you already have
- Go back through old blog posts, emails, social posts, podcast episodes and client presentations. Pick out the best and package it as a "best of" guide or a short video series.
- Turn your answers to frequently asked customer questions into an FAQ report.

## 5. Curate rather than create
- Gather the best existing material on a topic, such as articles, videos, tools and quotes, into one organized guide with your commentary.
- What you're adding is the selection and the organization, not new writing.

## 6. Use other people's content legally
- **License or buy rights** to an existing product (resell or private-label rights, often called PLR) and rebrand or adapt it.
- **Hire a ghostwriter or freelancer** to turn your outline or recordings into a finished piece.
- **Partner** with someone who already has a product, and use it as your offer or bonus.

## 7. Video-first lead magnets
- A short video series (for example 3 videos of 10 minutes each), a screen-share walkthrough, or a recorded demo.
- Plain, simple footage is fine if the content is useful, and it takes far less time than writing.

## How to choose
1. Pick **one specific problem** your ideal customer has right before they'd need your paid offer.
2. Choose the **fastest format** above that solves it: usually a checklist, cheat sheet, interview or recorded training.
3. Give it a **strong, specific title** that promises a clear result. The title sells the opt-in more than the length does.
4. Make sure it **leads naturally into your core offer**. The lead magnet should create the need for whatever you sell next.

**Bottom line:** in the time it takes to outline a book, you can interview someone, record yourself teaching, or turn your existing process into a checklist. To check this against the exact list in the Lead Funnels Swipe File, look at its lead magnet or info product ideas section.
