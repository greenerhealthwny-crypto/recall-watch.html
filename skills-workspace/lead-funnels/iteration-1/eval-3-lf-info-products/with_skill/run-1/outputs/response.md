# Fast ways to make a lead magnet or info product, without writing a book

Good news: the *Lead Funnels Swipe File* (Russell Brunson / ClickFunnels, about 2019) says you don't need to write anything from scratch. It lists **nine fast ways to create info products**, grouped by medium. Each one can work as a lead magnet, as a product, or as both.

## The nine shortcuts

### Text (with almost no writing of your own)
1. **Crowd-sourced books.** Ask other people to contribute the chapters, then compile them. *Chicken Soup for the Soul* worked this way. So did Brunson's *30 Days*, where 30 people each wrote a chapter.
2. **Example books.** Collect real examples instead of writing theory. The book mentions a book of 108 split tests, a monthly funnel-hack newsletter and *The 74 Funnel Swipe File*. The *Lead Funnels Swipe File* is itself 114 examples.
3. **Public-domain books.** Repackage works whose copyright has run out, such as *Think and Grow Rich*. You can add your own intro, notes or workbook.

### Audio
4. **Audiobooks.** Record a book. The book's example is someone who recorded the public-domain *Think and Grow Rich* and sold it as a $97 CD course.
5. **Interview recordings.** Interview experts and package the recordings. The book cites Vince James and *12-Month Millionaire*. The experts supply the content and you supply the questions.
6. **Hard-to-find audio compilations.** Curate the best existing material on a topic, such as a set of podcast episodes, into one collection.

### Video
7. **Screen captures.** Record your screen while you show how you do something.
8. **iPhone how-tos.** Film quick tutorials on your phone. No studio needed.
9. **Workshops.** Run a live workshop once. The **first hour becomes the lead magnet** and the **whole workshop becomes the product**.

## The key move: make one thing and split it

The book's main tip is to build the full product first, then:
- give away its **best piece (or the first chapter)** as the lead magnet;
- sell the **whole thing on the thank-you page**.

That gives you a lead magnet and a front-end offer from a single effort. If you price the full product low (for example $7 to $37), it can work as a **self-liquidating offer** that helps pay back your ad costs.

## Keep it small

You don't need a big asset. The book stresses that lead magnets should be **simple**. Brendon Burchard's one-page "1-Page Productivity Planner" is its model (swipe example #103). It also says to:
- **make several and test them**, because you can't predict which one will hit;
- give each one a **curiosity hook** that speaks to a **pain**. For example: "The 7 Biggest Mistakes [your audience] Make When [doing X]";
- remember that **"done is better than perfect"**. Get to 80% and launch.

## A quick plan for this week

1. **Pick the medium you can stand.** If you'd rather talk than write, choose interviews, a workshop or iPhone videos.
2. **Record once.** For example, run a 2–3 hour workshop or do five 30-minute expert interviews.
3. **Split it.** Use the first hour or best interview as the free magnet, and sell the full set on the thank-you page.
4. **Write 3–5 hooks**, including one "# Biggest Mistakes" hook, and test them on a simple landing page: headline, image of the magnet and opt-in form.
5. **Repurpose.** You can turn the recordings into a transcript, which gives you a text version as well.

## Cautions

- **Public domain:** the book says US works published "before 1923" are public domain. That cutoff moves forward every January 1. Check the status of the specific work, and make sure you aren't copying a newer edition, translation or trademark that is still protected.
- **Interviews and crowd-sourced content:** get written permission from contributors to package and sell their material.
- The book is from about 2019 and promotes ClickFunnels. The methods work with any funnel or email tool. Its revenue figures are examples, not guarantees.
