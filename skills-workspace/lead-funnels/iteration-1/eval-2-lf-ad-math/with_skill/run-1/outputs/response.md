# Paying for leads with ads: the Lead Funnels Swipe File math at $2 a lead

The book's main idea is that paid traffic should pay for itself. You buy leads with ads. Then you make a first offer right after the opt-in, on the thank-you page. That offer is priced to earn back the ad spend. If it does, the leads cost you nothing and you keep the list. The list is where the long-term money is ("the money is in the list").

All the book's figures are examples from about 2019, not guarantees. In Brunson's market at the time, leads typically cost **$1–$3**, so your $2 sits in the middle of that range.

---

## 1. The self-liquidating offer (SLO)

An **SLO** is a low-priced front-end product. It sits on the sales page or thank-you page, right after someone opts in for your lead magnet. Its job is not profit. Its job is to **liquidate (pay back) the cost of the ads**. It also turns some leads into *customers*, and a customer list is worth more than a list of leads.

**The book's example (about $1 per lead):**
- $100 in ads brings in 100 leads.
- 10% of them (10 people) buy a $10 product.
- 10 × $10 = $100 back. The ads are paid for, so the 100 leads were effectively free.

### The break-even formula

> **Break-even happens when (% of leads who buy) × (SLO price) = cost per lead**

The same thing, solved for conversion:

> **Required conversion = cost per lead ÷ SLO price**

### The same math at your $2 a lead

At $2 a lead, **$100 in ads gets you 50 leads, not 100**. You need to earn **$2 back from each lead**. So the book's own example (10% buying a $10 product, which is $1 per lead) recovers only **half** your spend at your cost per lead.

| SLO price | % of leads who must buy to break even at $2/lead | Buyers needed per 100 leads ($200 of ads) |
|---|---|---|
| $7 | 28.6% | about 29 |
| $10 | 20% | 20 |
| $20 | 10% | 10 |
| $37 | 5.4% | about 6 |
| $47 | 4.3% | about 5 |

Your two levers are **price** and **conversion**. If a $7 or $10 offer won't reach 20–29% of buyers, raise the price, make the offer more compelling, or lower your lead cost. You can also let follow-up close the gap over time (next section).

---

## 2. Break-even day

Not everyone buys on the thank-you page. The book's point is that **your follow-up funnel keeps selling the SLO after the opt-in**. So the real question is not whether you break even on day 0. It's **which day you break even**.

**The book's example:** only 5% buy at first. Follow-up emails bring in another 5% by day 3, for 10% in total, so you break even on **day 3**.

**The same idea at $2 a lead, with a $20 SLO:**

| Day | Cumulative % who bought | Revenue per lead | vs. $2 lead cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (thank-you page) | 5% | $1.00 | −$1.00 |
| Day 3 (after follow-up emails) | 10% | $2.00 | **Break-even** |
| Day 7+ | more buyers | over $2.00 | Profit, and the leads are paid for |

This is why the fifth part of the funnel, the **follow-up funnel** (automated email, SMS and Messenger messages plus broadcasts), matters so much for ad math. Each extra sale it brings in moves your break-even day earlier.

---

## 3. Break-even by lead value (the list math)

The third way the book frames it ignores the SLO. It looks only at what a name on your list is worth over time.

- **Done poorly:** about **$1 per name per month**. So 1,000 names ≈ $1,000 a month, and 100,000 names ≈ $1.2M a year.
- **With a strong relationship:** **$2–$50 per name per month**.

**The book's example:** at $1 a name per month and a $3 lead cost, you break even in **month 3**.

**At your $2 a lead:**
- At $1 a name per month, you break even in **month 2**.
- At $2 or more a name per month (a good relationship), you break even in **month 1** or sooner.

Put the two together. If an SLO pays back the ads on day 0–3, all the later value per name per month is profit on leads that cost you nothing.

## Putting it together: a quick plan for $2 leads

1. **Know your number.** Cost per lead = ad spend ÷ opt-ins. Keep landing-page conversion high: the book says a good page converts about **20–30%** of visitors (the Marketing Secrets Blackbook page hit 55.63%). Better opt-in rates mean cheaper leads from the same clicks.
2. **Price the SLO against your cost per lead.** Required conversion = $2 ÷ price. Pick a price and an offer you think can actually reach that rate.
3. **Put the SLO on the thank-you page**, right after you deliver the lead magnet. No bait-and-switch: deliver what you promised first.
4. **Write follow-up emails that keep offering the SLO** (for example, day 0 delivery, day 2 story, day 4 offer) to pull your break-even day earlier.
5. **Track three numbers:** cost per lead, SLO conversion by day, and revenue per name per month.

### Swipe-file examples to model

Here are some paid next steps from the book's 114 funnels:
- **#4 and #5 (DigitalMarketer-style template magnets):** opt in for a swipe file or template library, then a **$7** offer ("Social Selling System", "$10 a Day Traffic Plan").
- **#21 (Copyblogger):** newsletter opt-in, then a **$7** "Content Confidence Checklist".
- **#41 (numerology report):** a free personalized report, then the "Complete Numerology Report" for **$37**.
- **#67 ("Download the First 2 Chapters, Free!"):** free chapters, then the whole book "free, just pay s&h". This is the book's "give away the best piece, sell the whole thing" tactic.

Note the trade-off. A **$7** SLO at $2 a lead needs about 29% of leads to buy just to break even. A **$37** offer like #41 needs only about 5%.

## Caveats

- **These are the book's illustrative numbers from about 2019.** Ad costs, platforms and conversion rates have changed. Test with a small budget and use your own data.
- **"Break-even" here means revenue vs. ad spend only** *(general business practice, not from the book)*. Payment-processing fees, refunds, fulfillment or shipping (for example, on a free-plus-shipping book), and software costs all mean you need a bit more than the break-even math above to truly recover your costs.
- **Follow email and SMS law** (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, TCPA): get consent, include unsubscribe options, and disclose any affiliate links.
