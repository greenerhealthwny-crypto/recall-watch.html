# Ranking Markets with Hormozi's "Starving Crowd" Criteria

In *$100M Offers*, Hormozi says the market matters more than the offer or the persuasion skill. A great offer to the wrong market fails; a decent offer to a starving crowd wins. He scores a market on four indicators:

1. **Massive pain** - they must have the problem, and badly. Not just want it; need it.
2. **Purchasing power** - they must be able to afford it.
3. **Easy to target** - you can find and reach them as a group (associations, publications, groups, lists).
4. **Growing** - the market is expanding, not shrinking (a tailwind).

He suggests scoring each 1-10. All four matter; a zero on any one, especially purchasing power, can sink the market.

---

## Scorecard

| Market | Pain | Purchasing power | Easy to target | Growing | Total (/40) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(b) Dentists wanting more patients** | 8 | 10 | 10 | 7 | **35** |
| **(a) Busy moms wanting to lose weight** | 8 | 6 | 7 | 7 | **28** |
| **(c) Teenagers wanting to learn guitar** | 3 | 2 | 6 | 4 | **15** |

### Ranking
1. **Dentists wanting more patients** (best)
2. **Busy moms wanting to lose weight** (good, but crowded and harder to monetize)
3. **Teenagers wanting to learn guitar** (weak; avoid unless you sell to parents)

---

## (b) Dentists wanting more patients - #1

- **Pain (8):** Practices have high fixed costs: staff, equipment, rent, loan payments for school and the practice. Empty chairs cost real money every day. New-patient flow directly decides whether the practice is profitable. Pain is financial, measurable and urgent.
- **Purchasing power (10):** Dentists are high earners, and the service is a business expense with a clear ROI. One new patient can be worth thousands over their lifetime, so paying $2,000-$10,000+ for coaching is easy to justify.
- **Easy to target (10):** Licensed, listed and organized. State and national dental associations, dental trade publications, conferences, Facebook groups, LinkedIn titles, Google Maps listings, and purchasable lists. You can name and reach nearly every one.
- **Growing (7):** Dental demand rises with an aging population, cosmetic demand (implants, aligners, whitening), and more dentists going independent or competing with DSOs (corporate chains) that push private practices to market better.

**Watch-outs:** This is a known niche, so marketing agencies already pitch dentists heavily. You win by being specific (for example, "implant patients for solo practices" or "new practices in their first 3 years") and by tying your offer to a hard result: "X new patients in 90 days or you don't pay."

**Verdict:** This is the classic starving crowd: painful problem, lots of money, easy to find. Hormozi himself points to selling business-growth services to businesses with clear ROI as the easiest route.

---

## (a) Busy moms wanting to lose weight - #2

- **Pain (8):** Real and emotional: health, energy, confidence, body image after pregnancy, being a role model for kids. Weight loss is one of the "big three" markets (health, wealth, relationships), so the need is evergreen.
- **Purchasing power (6):** Mixed. Many households have money, but moms often put the family first and feel guilty spending on themselves. Spending may need a partner's buy-in. Price sensitivity is higher than for a business owner with ROI.
- **Easy to target (7):** Easy to reach on Facebook/Instagram, mom groups, schools, and local communities. But "busy moms" is broad; ad platforms make targeting easy, so competition for their attention is fierce.
- **Growing (7):** Steady demand, boosted by new trends (GLP-1 drugs, at-home and online fitness). The GLP-1 trend is a threat as well as a tailwind: some buyers will choose the drug instead of coaching, and others will want help keeping muscle and keeping the weight off.

**Watch-outs:** Extremely crowded and full of past failures, so skepticism is high ("I've tried everything"). Perceived likelihood of success is low, so you need strong proof, a guarantee, and a niche angle (for example, "moms of under-5s who have 20 minutes a day" or "post-partum moms in year one").

**Verdict:** A viable market and a big one. You'll need a sharper niche and a stronger offer to stand out, and lower average prices mean you need more volume.

---

## (c) Teenagers wanting to learn guitar - #3

- **Pain (3):** It's a want, not a need. Nothing bad happens if they don't learn. Motivation is often short-lived.
- **Purchasing power (2):** Teens have little money of their own; parents pay, which means you're actually selling to a different buyer with different reasons. Hormozi is clear: if they can't pay, it doesn't matter how much they want it.
- **Easy to target (6):** Reachable via schools, music shops, YouTube/TikTok, but advertising to minors is restricted and you still have to convince the parent.
- **Growing (4):** Interest in guitar is fairly flat. Free YouTube lessons and cheap apps (Yousician, Fender Play) set price expectations near zero.

**Watch-outs:** Low prices, high churn, free substitutes everywhere.

**Verdict:** The weakest starving crowd. If you love this space, reframe it: sell to **parents** ("give your kid a skill and confidence, off screens") or to **adults** who always wanted to play and now have money and time. That changes purchasing power and pain dramatically.

---

## Bottom line

**Choose dentists.** They score highest on the two things that matter most for a new offer: **purchasing power** and **ease of targeting**, with real, money-driven pain. You can charge premium prices, find every prospect, and prove ROI in dollars.

Busy moms is a solid second choice if you are passionate about fitness, but expect more competition and lower prices. Teenage guitar is a hobby market with weak buying power; avoid it unless you reposition toward parents or adults.

**Next step:** Niche down inside the winner. "Dentists" becomes, for example, "solo general dentists in their first 5 years who want 30 new patients a month," then build the Grand Slam Offer for that exact person.
