# A Grand Slam Offer for Garage Cleanouts and Junk Hauling ($100M Offers)

Your problem right now is the one Hormozi calls **the commodity problem**. "$300 a load" is a commodity offer. The buyer can line you up against every truck on Craigslist and pick the cheapest one, and that is a race to the bottom. A **Grand Slam Offer** is "an offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available". You stop selling *a load of junk hauled* and sell a **finished result** in a **category of one**. The truck, the crew and the dump fees can stay much the same. Only the offer changes.

Hormozi's order of importance is **Starving Crowd > Offer Strength > Persuasion**, so the market comes first.

---

## Step 0: Pick the starving crowd and narrow the avatar

"Everyone with junk" is not an avatar. Score the obvious segments against the four indicators: massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing.

| Segment | Pain | Purchasing power | Easy to target | Growing | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Homeowners about to **list or sell** a house | Very high: a cluttered garage hurts the sale price and holds up the listing date | High: they're sitting on home equity | Very easy: through realtors, stagers and new listings | Steady | **Primary avatar** |
| Adult children **downsizing a parent** or clearing an estate | Very high: emotional, overwhelming, often working from out of town | High | Easy: through estate attorneys, senior-move managers and assisted-living facilities | Growing (an aging population) | **Strong second avatar / referral channel** |
| General homeowners who "want the garage back" | Moderate: they *want* it but don't *need* it | Mixed | Broad | Flat | Secondary |
| Renters / students | Low | Low | Easy | Flat | Avoid (the resume-for-the-unemployed trap) |

**Avatar:** a homeowner in your service area who is selling, moving or downsizing in the next 60 days. Their garage (or garage plus attic and basement) is full. They have a deadline and no time, crew or truck. "The pain is the pitch." The deadline is what makes the pain massive.

Tell prospects outside this avatar politely that you're not the right fit, or route them to a separate basic "load" price. "Riches are in the niches."

## Step 1: The dream outcome

In their own words:

> "My garage is empty, clean and photo-ready, and my house can go on the market on time, for more money, without me lifting a box or spending a weekend on it."

The status piece is a home that shows well. The buyer's agent says "wow", the neighbours see a clean garage, and they get relief from a chore they've avoided for years.

## Step 4: Delivery vehicles (the "cheat codes")

**Level of personal attention**
- One-to-one: an owner-led walk-through and a personal crew lead for the day.
- Small group / one-to-many: a recorded "How to Prep Your Garage for a Sale" video and a checklist sent to every booked client and every partner realtor.

**Effort expected from the customer (DIY / DWY / DFY)**
- DIY: a free "Keep/Donate/Sell" sorting checklist and sticker kit for people who want to pre-sort.
- DWY: a 15-minute video call where you tag the "keep" items together on their phone photos.
- **DFY (the core):** the crew sorts, hauls, donates, sweeps, degreases and photographs everything. The customer just unlocks the door.

**Medium, if live:** in person on the day, plus text updates with photos as you go (before, mid-job, after). Phone for the walk-through.

**Recorded format:** a 3-minute "what to expect" video, a written Everything-Goes list, and a one-page Stay-Clear zone plan.

**Response speed**
- Photo quote returned within **1 business hour**.
- Job booked within **72 hours**, or on a specific listing-date deadline.
- Same-day text replies on job day.

**The 10x / one-tenth test**
- *If they paid 10x ($15,000)?* A full house cleanout, an estate-sale coordination partner, a deep clean, installed shelving, a professional stager, a painter for garage walls, and a white-glove move-out to the closing date. Pull the cheap, high-value pieces down into the core offer: photos, a zone plan, a degrease, a coordinated donation.
- *At one-tenth (about $150)?* A curbside pickup where they've already sorted, plus the DIY checklist. Keep this as a **downsell** for the non-avatar so you don't lose them. Don't lead with it.

## Step 5: Trim and stack

Keep the items that are **low cost / high value** and **high cost / high value**. Cut the low-value ones.

| Item | Cost to you | Value to the customer | Keep? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Locked-Price Photo Quote | Very low | High (likelihood) | Keep |
| Listing-Date Lock | Low (scheduling) | Very high (time) | Keep |
| Full DFY sort + haul | High | Very high | Keep (the core) |
| Second-Life Donation Run + receipt | Low | High (outcome) | Keep |
| Sweep, Degrease & Detail | Low–Medium | High (outcome) | Keep |
| Listing-Ready Photos | Very low | High | Keep |
| Text photo updates (Hands-Off Day) | Very low | High (effort) | Keep |
| Floor & wall protection | Low | Medium (likelihood) | Keep |
| Stay-Clear Zone Setup (plan + basic hooks) | Low | Medium–High | Keep |
| 6-month re-check visit | Low (a 20-minute stop that also generates upsells) | Medium | Keep |
| Installed custom shelving | High | Medium (not everyone wants it) | **Cut from core.** Offer as an upsell |
| Painting garage walls | High | Low–Medium | **Cut** |
| Estate-sale coordination | High | High, but only for the estate avatar | Make it a separate tier or partner bonus |
| Branded fridge magnet | Low | Low | **Cut** |

Remember the **sales-to-fulfillment continuum**: "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction." Over-deliver on the first 20–30 jobs to collect testimonials and before/after photos. Then trim what isn't moving the needle.

### Bonuses (each named, priced and aimed at a specific doubt)

Following Hormozi's bonus rules, each one answers a reason the buyer thinks they can't succeed.

1. **"The 7-Day Pre-Listing Garage Checklist"** ($47): "I don't know what buyers look for." *A tool beats more training.*
2. **"Family Keepsake Protocol"** ($97): "I'm scared you'll throw out something irreplaceable." Photos, papers and jewelry go into a sealed keepsake bin, and you get a call before anything questionable is hauled.
3. **"Agent Fast-Track"** ($100): "My realtor needs this yesterday." Photos and a completion note go straight to the agent.
4. **Partner bonus:** a discount from a local stager, cleaner or mover. It costs you nothing, adds value and builds referrals (Hormozi's "get other businesses to provide bonuses").

The total bonus value should **eclipse** the core. In practice, grow these as you add partner bonuses.

## Pricing

Hormozi: "99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow." You're not competing with $300 a load any more. You're competing with *a delayed listing, a lower sale price and three lost weekends*.

- **Core "Listing-Ready Garage Reset": $1,497** (one standard garage, up to a stated volume). That's well below the $2,450 stack, so the price-to-value gap is wide.
  - Compare it with the real stakes. On a home sale, even a small hit to the sale price, or a week's delay in carrying costs, typically dwarfs $1,500. The customer still "gets a deal".
- **Whole-Home Reset (garage + attic + basement/shed): $2,997.**
- **Estate & Downsizing Concierge: from $4,500**, including estate-sale or consignment coordination with a partner.
- **Downsell (non-avatar, already sorted): "Curbside Load" at market rate (about $300–$400).** Never lead with it.

Why charge more? **The virtuous cycle of price.** The margin pays for a better crew, insurance, photo gear and paid ads to realtors. That produces better results, which justifies the price. "Profit is oxygen."

*Tip from the book:* "Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile." Test $1,497, $1,997 and $997 on real calls and track close rates.

**Money model:** take a deposit at booking (for example, 30%) to lock the Listing-Date slot. Collect the balance on completion. Offer realtors a referral fee or a pre-paid "listing credit" so they bring clients to you.

## Guarantee (reversing risk)

Hormozi's formula: **"If you do not get X result in Y time period, we will Z."**

Stack two guarantees:

1. **Locked-Price Guarantee (unconditional on price):**
   > "The price we quote from your photos is the price you pay. If we try to charge you a penny more on the day, the job is free."
   *Condition (stated up front):* this applies to the items and volume shown in your photos. Added items get quoted in writing *before* we touch them.

2. **Listing-Date Guarantee (conditional, "better than money back"):**
   > "If your garage isn't empty, clean and photo-ready by the date we lock in, you don't pay a cent for the reset, **and** we'll pay $250 toward your cleaning or staging bill."
   *Conditions (the key actions):* you send complete photos, give access on the scheduled day, and flag hazardous items in advance.

3. **"Leave-It-Better" Promise:** "If we damage anything, we repair it or pay for it, and we're insured to back that up."

**Do the math** (Hormozi's example): 100 sales with 5% refunds is 95 net, but 130 sales with 10% refunds is 117 net, about 23% more. A strong guarantee usually pays for itself. "Hit your guarantee hard."

*Ethics and compliance:* only promise what your crew capacity can honour. Put the terms in writing, honour every claim promptly, and check your state's consumer-protection, contractor and waste-disposal rules (especially for hazardous materials).

## Scarcity and urgency (real ones only)

- **Scarcity (real capacity):** "We take **[N] Listing-Ready Resets per week**, because each one gets a full crew for a full day." If you truly run one crew, say so: "Tell them your real limits and let psychology do the rest."
- **Rolling urgency:** "Book by Friday and we can lock you into next week's Listing-Date slots."
- **Seasonal urgency:** a **Spring Listing Season** push (Feb–May is peak listing season in many markets) and a **"Before the Holidays" cleanout** in Nov–Dec.
- **Bonus scarcity:** the partner staging discount or the Agent Fast-Track bonus "for the first 10 bookings this month", but only if that's true.

Never invent fake deadlines or fake limits. Hormozi insists scarcity and urgency be real.

## How to launch it (next steps)

1. **Go where the avatar gathers:** realtors, stagers, estate attorneys, senior-move managers and new-listing lists. Offer realtors the Agent Fast-Track and a referral fee.
2. **Do 5–10 jobs at a launch price** in exchange for before/after photos and video testimonials. That raises **perceived likelihood**, the Value Equation driver most junk haulers ignore.
3. **Lead every ad and quote with the dream outcome and the guarantee**, not the truck size.
4. **Track:** quote-to-close rate, average ticket and refund/guarantee claims. Adjust the price up until the close rate starts to fall meaningfully.
5. **Keep iterating.** "If your offer doesn't work… It means your offer sucks", not you. Try many offers.
