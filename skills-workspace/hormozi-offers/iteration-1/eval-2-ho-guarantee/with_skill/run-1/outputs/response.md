# Guarantees in *$100M Offers*, applied to a high-cost done-for-you marketing service

In *$100M Offers* (Chapter 15), a guarantee is the enhancer that **raises demand by reversing risk**. The buyer stops carrying the risk that it won't work, and you carry it. Below are the four types Hormozi describes, how he says to word one, and his answer on refunds, each applied to a premium done-for-you (DFY) marketing agency.

---

## 1. The four types of guarantee

| Type | What it is | When Hormozi says to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Unconditional** | A no-questions-asked refund. In effect it is a paid trial, and it is the strongest kind. | Your fulfillment cost is low and you're confident in the results. |
| **Conditional** | A guarantee with terms. It works best as a **"better than money back"** promise (double or triple their money back, or you keep working until they get the result), tied to the **key actions** that produce success. | High-cost services. The conditions push customers to do the things that get them results. |
| **Anti-guarantee** | "All sales are final", backed by a **credible reason why**. The usual reasons are that you hand over proprietary material or do heavy customization. | Consumables, information that loses its value once given, and custom high-ticket work. |
| **Implied (performance)** | Revenue share, profit share, pay per result, triggers, ratchets. | Only where outcomes can be measured openly and you have the client's trust or control over the result. |

You can also **stack** them. Hormozi's example is an unconditional 30-day guarantee plus a conditional 90-day "triple your money back" guarantee.

### Which one fits a high-cost DFY marketing service?
- **Conditional is your default.** Your costs to fulfill are high: ad spend management, creative, staff time. A no-strings refund would let clients take that work and leave. A conditional guarantee protects you and also gets clients to do their part: approve creative quickly, give you account access, follow up with leads fast.
- **Implied/performance is a strong option** if you can track results openly, for example booked calls through a CRM you control or attributed revenue. Pay-per-appointment or a bonus once a threshold is hit (a "trigger") fits agencies well.
- **Anti-guarantee** suits the parts of the work that are heavily custom, such as brand strategy or a custom build. You might say: "Because we build your entire funnel and creative from scratch for your business, setup fees are non-refundable."
- **Unconditional** is risky at your price and cost level. If you use it, keep it to a short window, for example the first 14 days, before your big costs land.

## 2. How to word it

**Hormozi's formula:**

> **"If you do not get [X result] in [Y time period], we will [Z]."**

The "or what" is the key part. A guarantee with no consequence sounds weak.

- **Weak:** "We will get you 20 clients guaranteed."
- **Strong:** "You will get 20 clients in your first 30 days, or we give you your money back plus your advertising dollars spent with us."

That strong example comes from the book, and it's already written for a marketing service.

### Templates for your agency

**Conditional, "better than money back":**
> "If you don't get at least **[30 qualified booked calls]** in your first **[90 days]**, we'll **keep running your campaigns at no charge until you do**, *provided you* approve creative within 48 hours, respond to leads within 5 minutes, and attend our weekly check-in."

**Conditional, refund plus ad spend (Hormozi's own pattern):**
> "Get **[X new customers]** in your first **[60 days]**, or we refund **every dollar of our fee plus the ad spend you ran through us**, as long as you followed the launch checklist."

**Stacked:**
> "**30-day 'love it or leave it' guarantee:** if you're not happy with the strategy and launch in the first 30 days, we refund your setup fee. **Plus our 90-day results guarantee:** hit [X] or we work for free until you do."

**Implied/performance:**
> "We charge a reduced retainer plus **$[N] per qualified appointment** shown on your calendar. If we don't produce, you don't pay the performance fee."

### Wording rules from the book
1. **Be specific about X and Y.** Use a number and a deadline, not "results" or "growth".
2. **Make Z vivid and costly to you.** "Better than money back" beats a plain refund.
3. **Tie the conditions to the key actions** that predict success, so they read as part of the plan and not as fine print.
4. **"Hit your guarantee hard, even if you don't have one."** State it boldly and give a reason why: "We only take clients we know we can help, so we put our fee on the line."
5. **Give it a name.** Hormozi says to name every part of the stack, for example "The 30-Calls-or-Free Guarantee".

## 3. Will a strong guarantee lose you money on refunds?

**Hormozi's answer: almost always no, if you do the math instead of reacting emotionally.** His example:

| | Sales | Refund rate | Net sales |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak or no guarantee | 100 | 5% | **95** |
| Strong guarantee | 130 | 10% | **117** |

Refunds doubled, yet you end up with **about 23% more net customers**. A strong guarantee lifts conversions more than it raises refunds. "Don't be emotional, just do the math." (His numbers are illustrative. He says the book's figures are theoretical.)

### Run your own version
Plug in your own numbers:
- **Net revenue = (sales x price) - (refunds x refund amount)**
- At a high price, also count the **fulfillment costs you can't get back** on refunded clients, such as staff hours and setup. That's why a conditional guarantee usually beats an unconditional one for DFY. Clients who do the key actions rarely fail, and clients who skip them don't qualify for the guarantee.
- Choose the remedy with the cost to you in mind. "We keep working until you hit it" often costs far less than a cash refund, because your marginal cost of delivery is lower than the price.

### The real risk Hormozi warns about
The bigger danger isn't refunds. It's **attracting bad customers**. People who buy *only* because of a guarantee can be poor clients. You protect yourself by:
- tying conditions to the work they must do;
- qualifying clients before they buy, and taking only those you know you can help (that also makes a strong guarantee safer); and
- adding real scarcity, such as "we take on [N] new clients per month", so you can be selective.

---

## Ethics and compliance
- Make only promises you can keep, and honor them. Hormozi insists the enhancers be real, "without being phony".
- Put the conditions in writing in your contract. Keep them clear and reasonable, not hidden traps.
- Guarantees and refund terms have to comply with consumer-protection and advertising law, such as FTC rules on outcome claims in the US. Have a lawyer review the final wording, especially any revenue or earnings claims.
