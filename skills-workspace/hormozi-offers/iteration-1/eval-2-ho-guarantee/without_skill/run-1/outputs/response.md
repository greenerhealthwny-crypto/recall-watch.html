# Guarantees in $100M Offers, Applied to a High-Ticket Done-For-You Marketing Service

*Answered from memory of the book. Treat exact phrasings as close paraphrases, not quotes.*

## Why guarantees matter (Hormozi's framing)

In *$100M Offers*, the guarantee is one of the main levers for **reducing perceived risk**. Risk lowers "perceived likelihood of achievement" in his Value Equation:

> Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)

Every purchase has risk, and by default the buyer carries it. A guarantee moves that risk onto you, the seller. Hormozi's main claim is that a strong guarantee **raises conversions by more than it raises refunds**, so the business makes more money overall. He also says the **wording** of the guarantee matters a great deal: a vague "satisfaction guaranteed" does almost nothing.

## The four types of guarantees

### 1. Unconditional guarantee
"No questions asked" money back, usually within a set window such as 30 days.
- Strongest pull and lowest friction for the buyer.
- Best for lower-ticket offers, or offers where delivery costs you little.
- Highest refund exposure, because the buyer can take the result and still ask for the money back.
- **Variants:** a full refund, a refund plus a bonus they keep, or a refund of more than they paid ("double your money back").

### 2. Conditional guarantee
The buyer qualifies for the guarantee by **doing specific things**: showing up, implementing, completing the work. Hormozi especially likes this type for services.
- It lets you promise something bigger, because the client's actions drive results.
- The conditions should be the actions that actually produce success, so the guarantee also gets clients to do the work.
- **Common forms:**
  - **Money back (or more)** if they did X, Y and Z and didn't hit the outcome.
  - **Service guarantee:** "We keep working with you for free until you hit the result." Hormozi favours this for services because your cost is extra labour, not cash leaving the business.
  - **Outsized guarantees**, such as paying the client's costs or adding extra money if you fail.
  - Guarantees tied to a clear **KPI** within a **time frame**.

### 3. Anti-guarantee
"All sales are final", **with an explicit reason** that adds to the value. Example: "Because we give you our proprietary playbooks and accounts setup on day one, which can't be returned, all sales are final."
- Fits offers where what the client gets can't be taken back, such as information, custom builds, or ad spend already used.
- The reasoning has to be stated. It shows confidence and filters for committed buyers.

### 4. Implied guarantees
Performance-based pricing in place of a stated promise:
- **Pay-per-performance or revenue share:** a percentage of revenue or profit, or a fee per result such as per lead or appointment.
- **Triggers:** you get paid only when certain milestones are hit.
- Common in agency models. The client pays only when you deliver, so the risk reversal is built into the deal.

### Stacking guarantees
Hormozi also describes **stacking** guarantees, meaning you combine more than one. For example:
- an unconditional guarantee for the first 30 days ("try us risk-free"), plus
- a conditional outcome guarantee for the longer term ("hit X in 90 days or we work free until you do").

Stacked guarantees can add up to more than the sum of their parts when each one targets a different fear.

## How to word a guarantee

His formula, close to verbatim:

> **"If you do not get [X result] in [Y time period], we will [Z]."**

Principles:
1. **Be specific.** Name a measurable outcome, a deadline, and exactly what you'll do.
2. **Name the fear you're removing.** Base it on the objections clients actually raise on sales calls. For DFY marketing those are usually "what if it doesn't work?" and "what if I'm locked in paying for nothing?"
3. **Give it a name** so it sticks, such as "The Pipeline-or-We-Work-Free Guarantee".
4. **Make it bold.** A timid guarantee does little. Hormozi says the more painful the guarantee is for you if you fail, the more it persuades.
5. **Spell out the conditions** in a conditional guarantee. List the client's required actions, and make them the actions that drive results.

### Example wordings for a high-cost DFY marketing service

**Conditional service guarantee (lowest cash risk, and the type Hormozi recommends for services):**
> "If we don't generate at least 40 qualified sales appointments for you in the first 90 days, we'll keep running your campaigns at no management fee until we do. You only need to: approve creative within 48 hours, maintain the agreed ad budget, and have your team respond to leads within 15 minutes."

**Conditional money-back:**
> "Hit fewer than [X qualified leads] in 120 days while following our onboarding and lead-handling process, and we'll refund every dollar of your management fees."

**Stacked:**
> "**30-Day Fit Guarantee:** If after our onboarding and strategy sprint you don't believe the plan will work, tell us within 30 days and we'll refund your setup fee, and you keep the full strategy and creative.
> **90-Day Results Guarantee:** If we don't hit [X] by day 90 (and you've met the conditions below), we work for free until we do."

**Implied (performance pricing):**
> "Lower retainer + $[Y] per qualified appointment booked" or "[N]% of attributable new revenue."

**Anti-guarantee (for the custom-build part):**
> "Because we build your funnel, ad accounts, and creative assets custom from day one, and you keep them, setup fees are non-refundable."

## Will a strong guarantee lose me money from refunds?

**Usually not. Hormozi's answer is that it typically makes you more money, as long as your delivery is actually good.** His reasoning:

1. **Run the maths on conversions vs. refunds.** If a bold guarantee lifts close rate from, say, 20% to 30%, refunds can roughly double and you still come out ahead. Compare net revenue after refunds, not just the refund rate.
   - Example: 100 calls at $10k. At a 20% close rate with 5% refunds, net is about $190k. At a 30% close rate with 10% refunds, net is about $270k.
2. **Most people don't claim guarantees**, even when they could. Many of those who do would have been unhappy anyway, and might have charged back or left bad reviews.
3. **Conditional guarantees protect you.** Clients who complete the required actions almost always get results, so few qualify for a payout. Those who don't do the actions don't qualify. Write the conditions fairly, though, not as a trap. Hormozi warns against guarantees built to be impossible to claim, because they ruin trust and reputation.
4. **Service guarantees cost labour, not cash.** "We keep working until you hit it" means your downside is extra fulfilment time, often at a low marginal cost, and it frequently turns into a long-term client.
5. **A guarantee exposes delivery problems.** If refunds are high, the problem is the product or the client fit, not the guarantee. Hormozi treats the guarantee as a forcing function to improve fulfilment and to qualify clients harder.

### Specific risks for a high-cost DFY service, and how to manage them

- **Results you don't fully control** (the client's sales team, offer, pricing, follow-up). Guarantee something upstream that you do control, such as qualified leads, booked appointments, or cost per lead, rather than closed revenue. If you do guarantee revenue, make lead handling a condition.
- **Large cash refunds on big retainers.** Prefer a "work free until achieved" or fee-credit guarantee over cash back. Or refund the management fee only, never the ad spend.
- **Bad-fit clients.** Use qualification criteria before the sale, such as minimum budget, a proven offer, and sales capacity. A strong guarantee plus strict qualification is a strong combination.
- **Gaming the guarantee.** Use tracked, objective conditions (attendance, approvals, response-time SLAs) and agree up front how the metric will be measured.
- **Test before rolling out.** Run the new guarantee on a group of sales calls and track close rate, refund or claim rate, and net cash per call. Keep it if net cash rises.

## Bottom line

Use a **named, specific, conditional service guarantee** based on a metric you control, such as booked qualified appointments within 90 days. Make its conditions the behaviours that make clients succeed. If you want extra pull, **stack** a short "fit" guarantee on top, and consider an **implied/performance** pricing element. Done this way, the guarantee should raise your close rate by more than it costs you in refunds or extra work. If it doesn't, fix delivery and client qualification.
