# Naming and Ethical Scarcity/Urgency for Your 6-Week New-Mom Weight-Loss Program

*(Using the frameworks from Alex Hormozi's* $100M Offers*: the M-A-G-I-C naming formula, and the chapters on scarcity, urgency, bonuses and guarantees.)*

---

## Part 1: Naming the Offer with M-A-G-I-C

Hormozi's naming formula has five parts. You usually use 3 to 5 of them in one name:

| Letter | Meaning | What it looks like for you |
|---|---|---|
| **M** – Make a Magnetic reason why | A reason for the offer to exist right now (a launch, a season, "free", "challenge", "new year") | "Fall Kickoff", "Founding Cohort", "Back-to-Me", "Holiday-Proof" |
| **A** – Announce your Avatar | Call out exactly who it is for, so the right person thinks "that's me" | "New Moms", "Postpartum Moms", "Busy Moms", "Mamas" |
| **G** – Give them a Goal | The dream outcome, stated as a result they want | "Lose the Baby Weight", "Feel Like Yourself Again", "Get Your Body Back", "Fit Into Your Jeans" |
| **I** – Indicate a time Interval | How long it takes. Your 6 weeks is a strong asset here | "6-Week", "42-Day" |
| **C** – Complete with a Container word | A word that tells them it's a structured system | Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Program, Reset, Method, Accelerator, Sprint, System |

### Name options

**Lead recommendations**

1. **The 6-Week Postpartum Reset** — avatar + time + container. Short, clear, safe.
2. **New Mom 6-Week Body Comeback** — avatar + goal + time.
3. **Back-to-Me: The 6-Week New Mom Reset** — magnetic reason + time + avatar + container. "Back to me" names the emotion most new moms actually feel: not just weight, but losing themselves.
4. **The 42-Day Mama Momentum Challenge** — "42 days" sounds more specific and fresh than "6 weeks", and it lines up with the traditional 6-week postpartum window.

**More options to test**

- The New Mom Shred-the-Stress 6-Week Program
- Fit Mama 6-Week Blueprint
- The Nap-Time Fit Method: 6 Weeks for New Moms (the "nap-time" hook signals it fits a mom's schedule)
- Founding Mamas 6-Week Bootcamp (good for the first cohort)
- The Feel-Like-Me-Again 6-Week Sprint
- Holiday-to-New-Year Mama Reset (a seasonal version; see urgency below)

### Naming tips from the book

- **Rotate the name, not the offer.** Hormozi notes that names "fatigue" in ads. Keep the core program and swap the magnetic reason (season, holiday, cohort number) each run.
- **Test in ads or posts.** Run 2 or 3 names as headlines and keep the one with the best click and reply rate.
- **Be careful with the goal word.** "Lose the baby weight" converts well but can trigger platform ad rules (Meta restricts before/after and body-shaming language) and can feel like pressure to some moms. "Feel like yourself again", "get strong", or "get your energy back" are softer alternatives. You can use the weight goal on the sales page, where you have room for context.
- **Avoid numbers you can't promise.** "Lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks" is both an FTC risk and medically inappropriate for many postpartum women (especially those breastfeeding). Put the goal in the name, not a specific pound figure.

**My pick:** *Back-to-Me: The 6-Week New Mom Reset* as the evergreen brand, with a rotating magnetic tag for each launch (for example, "Founding Cohort", "January", "Spring").

## Part 2: Ethical Scarcity

Hormozi defines scarcity as limiting **how much** people can buy (supply). His rule is that it must be real. Fake scarcity ("only 3 spots left!" forever) destroys trust, and with moms, who talk to each other constantly in groups and forums, it will be found out.

Coaching has natural, honest limits. Use them:

### 1. Limited seats per cohort (real capacity)
You can only give good feedback, check-ins and form reviews to so many women. Do the math, then publish it.
> "I personally review every check-in, so each cohort is capped at 25 moms. When it's full, the next cohort opens in [month]."

Show the real count ("18 of 25 spots taken") and **actually close** when you hit the number.

### 2. Limited bonuses (scarce add-ons)
Keep the core program available, but limit the extras:
- First 10 to join get a **1:1 kickoff call** with you (30 minutes, real time cost).
- First 15 get a **printed meal-plan and grocery kit** mailed to them.
- Founding cohort only: **lifetime access to the recipe vault** or a **free seat in the next cohort** if they want to repeat it.

### 3. Founding-member pricing
The first cohort is genuinely different: you need testimonials and feedback. That's an honest reason for a lower price.
> "This is the first cohort. In exchange for feedback and an honest review, founding moms get it at $X. The price goes up to $Y for the next round."

Then **raise the price** for round two, so the claim stays true.

### 4. Limited 1:1 tier
Offer a premium tier with weekly 1:1 calls and cap it at a small, real number (for example 5). This creates scarcity and also gives you a price anchor that makes the group tier look like great value.

## Part 3: Ethical Urgency

Hormozi defines urgency as limiting **when** people can buy (time). Four main types, all of which can be honest:

### 1. Cohort-based (rolling) start dates
This is the cleanest one for a 6-week program. Everyone starts together, so enrollment truly closes.
> "Enrollment closes Sunday at midnight. We start together Monday, [date]. The next cohort starts [date]."

A group start is a real benefit (community, accountability), not a trick.

### 2. Seasonal urgency
Tie each cohort to a real moment:
- **"Before the holidays"** — have energy and confidence for family gatherings and photos.
- **New Year cohort** — ride the January motivation.
- **"Before summer"** or **"before baby's first birthday"** — a personal deadline many moms already care about.

Given today's date (late November), a **"Holiday-Proof" December cohort** or a **January New-Year cohort** with doors closing in late December are natural fits.

### 3. Price or bonus deadlines
> "Early-bird price until Friday. After that the regular price applies."
> "Join by Wednesday to get the bonus 1:1 kickoff call."

Again, actually end it. Do not reopen "by popular demand" every time.

### 4. Exploding opportunity (use sparingly)
For leads who finished a free challenge or a consult call:
> "Because you completed the free 5-day challenge, you can join at the challenge price for the next 72 hours."

This is fair because it rewards action, but don't pair it with high-pressure sales calls aimed at sleep-deprived new moms.

### Urgency based on the cost of waiting (the most honest kind)
Remind them, without guilt, what waiting costs:
> "Every week you wait is another week of feeling tired and not like yourself. Six weeks from now you'll be six weeks older either way. The question is whether you'll have started."

## Part 4: Ethics Guardrails Specific to New Moms

This audience is vulnerable (sleep deprivation, hormonal changes, body-image pressure, possible postpartum depression). Keep the offer clean:

1. **Medical clearance.** Require or strongly recommend clearance from their OB/midwife (typically after the 6-week postpartum checkup), and screen for diastasis recti, C-section recovery, and pelvic floor issues. Say so on the sales page. It also builds trust.
2. **Breastfeeding-safe nutrition.** No aggressive calorie deficits. Say plainly that the plan protects milk supply.
3. **No shame-based messaging.** Avoid "get rid of that mom pouch" or "bounce back" pressure. Sell energy, strength, confidence and feeling like yourself, with weight loss as one outcome.
4. **Honest results claims.** Use real testimonials with "results vary" context. Don't promise a specific number of pounds.
5. **Real scarcity only.** If you say 25 spots, close at 25. If you say the price goes up, raise it. Moms share screenshots.
6. **Easy, fair refunds.** Pair the urgency with a strong guarantee so the pressure to decide is offset by low risk. Example (conditional guarantee, a Hormozi favorite):
   > "Do the workouts, log your meals 5 days a week, and attend the weekly calls. If you don't feel stronger and see progress in 6 weeks, I'll coach you for another 6 weeks free, or refund you in full."
7. **Mental-health safety net.** Include a note pointing to postpartum depression resources (for example, Postpartum Support International) and refer out when needed.

## Putting It Together: Sample Launch Copy

> **Back-to-Me: The 6-Week New Mom Reset — January Founding Cohort**
>
> For moms 3 to 24 months postpartum who are cleared to exercise and want their energy, strength and confidence back, without crash diets or 2-hour workouts.
>
> - 20-minute nap-time workouts, safe for core and pelvic floor recovery
> - Breastfeeding-friendly meal plan and grocery lists
> - Weekly group coaching calls plus daily check-ins with me
> - A private community of moms starting with you
>
> **Only 25 spots**, because I personally review every check-in. (14 left.)
> **First 10 moms** get a free 1:1 kickoff call.
> **Founding price of $X ends December 28.** Doors close December 31 and we start together January 5.
>
> **Guarantee:** Show up and do the work. If you don't see progress in 6 weeks, I'll coach you for the next 6 weeks free, or refund you.

### Quick checklist
- [ ] Pick one evergreen name and a rotating magnetic tag per cohort
- [ ] Set a real seat cap based on your coaching capacity
- [ ] Choose 1 or 2 limited bonuses that cost you real time or money
- [ ] Set real dates: enrollment close, start, next cohort
- [ ] Raise the price after the founding cohort (and actually do it)
- [ ] Add a guarantee to offset the urgency
- [ ] Add medical clearance, breastfeeding-safety and mental-health notes
