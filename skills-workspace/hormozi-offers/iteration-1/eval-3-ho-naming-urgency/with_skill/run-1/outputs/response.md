# Naming + Ethical Scarcity & Urgency for Your 6-Week New-Mom Weight-Loss Program

This uses the "Enhancing the Offer" section of Hormozi's *$100M Offers* (naming, scarcity, urgency). His point is that these enhancers change demand **without changing the core product**. They only work long-term if they're real. In his words, the scarcity has to be "without being phony."

One flag before the names: **weight loss is one of the most heavily regulated claim categories**, and postpartum bodies add a health layer on top. That affects how you name it (see the "I" in M-A-G-I-C below).

---

## Part 1: Naming with M-A-G-I-C

Hormozi's formula uses **3 to 5** of these five parts, in whatever order sounds punchy:

| Letter | Component | Options for your program |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Magnetic reason why (why now) | "New Year", "Spring", "Fresh Start", "Founding", "Back-to-Me" |
| **A** | Announce your avatar | "New Mom", "Mama", "Postpartum", "Busy Mom", "Mom-of-a-Newborn" |
| **G** | Give them a goal | "Feel Like Me Again", "Strong", "Energized", "Jeans-Ready", "Fit", "Confident" |
| **I** | Indicate a time interval | "6-Week" (OK as program *length*, see note) |
| **C** | Container word | Program, Reset, Challenge, Blueprint, Method, Bootcamp, Accelerator |

### The compliance note on "I" and "G"
The book warns: avoid an outcome-plus-time claim where compliance forbids it, and use an **action goal** instead. For weight loss this matters a lot:
- **Avoid:** "Lose 20 lbs in 6 Weeks", "Drop the Baby Weight in 6 Weeks". This is a specific results promise with a timeframe. In the US the FTC treats weight-loss claims strictly. Ad platforms (Meta, Google) restrict before/after and "lose X lbs" claims. And it sets a bad expectation for postpartum bodies, especially breastfeeding moms.
- **Fine:** "6-Week" as the **length of the program** ("The 6-Week Program"), paired with a goal that is about actions, energy, strength or habits ("Feel Like Yourself Again", "Strong Mama", "Build Your Mom Routine").

### Name options (strongest first)

1. **The 6-Week Strong Mama Reset**: I + A + G + C. Short, alliterative-ish, and "reset" signals a fresh start without a weight promise.
2. **Back-to-Me: The 6-Week New Mom Fit Method**: M + A + G + I + C (all five). "Back-to-Me" names the real emotional dream outcome, feeling like herself again.
3. **The Mama Momentum 6-Week Program**: A + G + I + C. Uses alliteration, which Hormozi says helps.
4. **Fresh Start Mama: 6 Weeks to Feeling Like You Again**: M + A + I + G. "Feeling" is an experience goal, not a pounds claim.
5. **The New Year New Mom 6-Week Challenge** (seasonal version): M + A + I + C. Use it for a January cohort, then rename for spring ("Spring Into Strong Mama") to re-stimulate demand. Hormozi notes that renaming an old offer brings fresh demand.

**Tip:** test 2–3 names in ads or to your email list and keep the winner. Hormozi says a good name can bring "2x, 3x, or 10x the response rate." Also make sure the name **repels** the wrong people. "New Mom" / "Postpartum" filters out general dieters, which is what you want.

### Name your bonuses the same way
Use the same formula on each item in the stack so each one feels like its own asset:
- "The 15-Minute Nap-Time Workout Library"
- "The One-Handed Meal Plan (Feeds You While You Feed Baby)"
- "The Mama Sleep-Debt Survival Guide"
- "The Grocery-Pickup Shopping Lists"

Hormozi's rule: **tools and checklists beat more training**, because they take the buyer less time and effort. New moms have almost no time, so this matters even more here.

## Part 2: Ethical Scarcity (limits on *how many*)

Scarcity works by **reducing supply**. The book lists three types. Here they are applied to your program:

### 1. Limited seats per cohort (use this as your main lever)
Coaching really is capacity-limited. Work out your real number. For example: "I can personally review check-ins and answer voice messages for **20 moms per cohort**." Then say it plainly.
- Script: *"Each cohort is capped at 20 moms so I can personally review every check-in. When 20 spots fill, enrollment closes until the next group."*
- Hormozi: tell prospects your real limits "and let psychology do the rest."
- **Ethics test:** if spot #21 signs up, you **really** turn her away or move her to the next cohort. Show a live, honest count ("14 of 20 spots taken") only if it's accurate.

### 2. Limited bonuses
Cap a high-touch bonus that costs you real time:
- *"The first 10 moms to enroll get a 1:1 45-minute Kickoff Call with me to build their personal plan around their baby's schedule."*
This is real because your 1:1 time is limited.

### 3. "Never available again" (use sparingly)
- A **Founding Mama** price or bonus for your first-ever cohort. *"This is the only time this program will run at this price. Founding members get it, plus lifetime access to future updates."*
- Only say "never again" if you will truly never offer it again. Breaking this once destroys trust with an audience that talks to each other a lot (mom groups and the like).

### Extreme scarcity (premium tier)
Hormozi's pro tip: a very small, very high-priced tier of close access to you. For example, **"VIP Mama: 3 spots"**, with weekly 1:1 video calls plus daily text support. It adds income, and it makes the main program look like a bargain next to it.

## Part 3: Ethical Urgency (limits on *when*)

Urgency works by **raising demand by lowering the threshold to act**. The book gives four ethical methods:

### 1. Cohort-based rolling urgency (your best fit)
A 6-week program is naturally cohort-based. Start groups on fixed dates (for example, the first Monday of every other month).
- Script: *"If you join by Thursday, you'll start with the March cohort on Monday, March 2nd. The next group doesn't start until May."*
- The less often you start, the stronger the urgency. Monthly works; every 6–8 weeks is stronger.
- It's 100% true, and the group start is a real benefit (community and accountability), not just a sales trick.

### 2. Rolling seasonal urgency
Moms already think in seasons:
- **January**: "New Year, New Mom" cohort.
- **Spring**: "Feel strong before summer."
- **Back-to-school / fall**: "Your turn now that the routine's back."
- **Pre-holiday**: "Have energy for the holidays."
Tie each cohort's name and kickoff to the season.

### 3. Promotional / pricing urgency
A real discount or bonus that ends on a real date:
- *"Early-bird: enroll by Sunday at midnight and get $100 off plus the Meal Plan bonus. After that it's full price."*
- **Ethics test:** at midnight the price actually goes up. No secret "extensions" and no countdown timers that reset when the page reloads (that's a fake deadline, and in some places a deceptive-practice problem).

### 4. Exploding opportunity
For warm leads after a discovery call or a free workshop:
- *"Because you came to the workshop, this workshop-only bonus is yours if you enroll within 48 hours."*
Keep it real and time-boxed, then let it go.

**Why real deadlines are worth it:** Hormozi notes that the last 4 hours of a launch's last day can bring **up to 50–60% of sales**. People who were going to buy anyway act when there's a true cutoff. You don't need to fake anything to get that effect.

> *For moms 3–12 months postpartum who want their energy, strength and confidence back, built around nap times and a baby on your hip.*

## Ethics & Safety Checklist (new-mom specific)

- [ ] **Every limit is real.** The seat cap matches your real coaching capacity, and you enforce it.
- [ ] **Every deadline is real.** Prices change and doors close when you say they will. No resetting timers.
- [ ] **No pounds-in-weeks promises** in the name or ads. Talk about actions, energy, strength and habits. Any client results you share should be typical, or clearly labeled as not typical.
- [ ] **Medical clearance.** Ask moms to confirm they've been cleared for exercise by their doctor or midwife (often at the ~6-week postpartum check, later after a C-section or complications). Say this up front.
- [ ] **Breastfeeding-safe nutrition.** No crash calorie targets. Breastfeeding raises calorie needs, and aggressive deficits can affect milk supply.
- [ ] **Mental-health awareness.** Avoid shame-based "bounce back" messaging. Postpartum depression and body-image struggles are common, so have a referral note ready and keep the tone supportive. The urgency should be "your next group starts Monday", **never** "you're running out of time to fix your body."
- [ ] **Guarantee and refund terms** (if you add one) are honored and follow your local consumer-protection rules.
