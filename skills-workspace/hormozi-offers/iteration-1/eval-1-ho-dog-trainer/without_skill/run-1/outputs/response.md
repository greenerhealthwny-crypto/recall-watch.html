# Building a Grand Slam Offer for Your Dog Training Business

*Using the framework from Alex Hormozi's **$100M Offers***

---

## The Core Problem: You're a Commodity

Right now you sell "a dog training session for $40." A prospect can line that up next to the $30 trainer down the street and compare them like for like. When products look the same, the only thing left to compare is price, and that's a race to the bottom.

Hormozi's answer is to stop selling something people can compare. Build an offer so good that people would feel stupid saying no, and so different that no competitor has anything like it. That's a **Grand Slam Offer**. Once there's nothing to compare it to, you're no longer competing on price. You're a "category of one."

Below is the process step by step, applied to your business.

---

## Step 0: Pick the Right Market (the "Starving Crowd")

Hormozi says market matters more than offer strength or persuasion skill. A good market has four traits:

1. **Massive pain.** They need a fix, they don't just want one.
2. **Purchasing power.** They can afford a premium.
3. **Easy to target.** You can find them and reach them.
4. **Growing.**

"Dog owners" is too broad. Niche down to a group with an urgent, expensive, emotional problem. Some options:

| Niche | Why the pain is high |
|---|---|
| **Owners of reactive or aggressive dogs** (lunging, biting, barking at people or dogs) | Liability, fear, embarrassment, risk of rehoming or euthanasia |
| **New puppy owners in their first 90 days** | Overwhelm, chewing, potty accidents, sleepless nights |
| **Busy professionals with "out of control" adolescent dogs** | No time, the dog is wrecking the house, guilt |
| **Families with a new baby on the way** | Safety fear: "Will the dog be okay with the baby?" |
| **Rescue and adoption owners with anxious dogs** | Separation anxiety, destruction, neighbor complaints |

For the rest of this walkthrough I'll use **reactive or aggressive dogs**. The pain is huge, the owners are desperate, and many trainers won't take these cases. Pick whichever niche fits your skills and your local market best.

> Hormozi's point: an offer that's fine for "everyone" is great for no one. The narrower your target, the more you can charge, because the offer feels made for that person.

---

## Step 1: Charge What It's Worth (Why a Higher Price Helps)

Hormozi argues that raising prices starts a **virtuous cycle**:

- A higher price brings in **more committed clients**. They do the homework, so they **get better results**.
- Better margins let you **spend more on delivery**: more time, better tools, more support. That improves results further.
- Better results bring **testimonials, referrals, and word of mouth**.
- You can **afford to spend more to get a customer**, so you outspend the cheap competition on marketing.

At $40 a session you can't afford to over-deliver, can't afford ads, and have to work huge hours. The aim isn't $50. Hormozi pushes for prices **10x or more** above the market, backed by value that makes the price feel like a steal.

---

## Step 2: The Value Equation

Everything you add to the offer should improve one of these four levers:

```
            Dream Outcome  x  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  ---------------------------------------------------------
                 Time Delay  x  Effort & Sacrifice
```

- **Dream Outcome ↑**: what they really want.
- **Perceived Likelihood ↑**: how sure they are it will work *for them*.
- **Time Delay ↓**: how fast they see results.
- **Effort & Sacrifice ↓**: how easy it is for them.

The top two make the offer worth more. The bottom two are where the most leverage usually sits. Cheap trainers sell "an hour of my time." You'll sell a fast, low-effort, near-certain path to the dream outcome.

For a reactive-dog owner:

- **Dream outcome:** "I can walk my dog calmly around the neighborhood, have guests over, and stop feeling anxious and embarrassed. My dog is safe and I keep him."
- **Perceived likelihood:** They've probably tried YouTube, maybe another trainer, and failed. They doubt it can work.
- **Time delay:** They want relief *now*, not in six months.
- **Effort & sacrifice:** They're tired, busy, and scared of doing it wrong.

---

## Step 3: List Every Problem (the Obstacles)

Write down every obstacle between your client and the dream outcome. A useful method is to take each step of their journey and ask what goes wrong before, during, and after. Then put each problem in terms of the four value levers.

**Reactive-dog owner problems:**

1. "My dog is different. Nothing works on him." *(likelihood)*
2. "I don't know what triggers him or why." *(likelihood)*
3. "I don't have time to train every day." *(effort)*
4. "I forget what to do in the moment when he's lunging." *(effort, likelihood)*
5. "It'll take forever to see a change." *(time)*
6. "Walks are stressful and I avoid them." *(effort)*
7. "I don't have the right equipment." *(effort)*
8. "My partner and kids don't train him the same way." *(likelihood)*
9. "He's fine with the trainer but not with me." *(likelihood)*
10. "What if he regresses after training ends?" *(likelihood)*
11. "I can't practice around other dogs safely." *(effort, likelihood)*
12. "I'm afraid of a bite incident or a lawsuit." *(dream outcome, urgency)*
13. "Having guests over is a nightmare." *(dream outcome)*
14. "Scheduling sessions is a hassle." *(effort)*
15. "I've already wasted money on training that didn't work." *(likelihood)*

---

## Step 4: Turn Each Problem into a Solution

Flip every problem into a "how to" solution. Don't worry about cost yet. Just list everything you *could* do.

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| "My dog is different" | Custom **Behavior Assessment & Trigger Map** for their dog |
| "Don't know the triggers" | Written **Reactivity Profile** showing thresholds and triggers |
| "No time to train" | **10-Minute-a-Day Training Plan**, short daily drills |
| "Forget what to do in the moment" | Laminated **"Emergency Calm" pocket card** and phone lock-screen cheat sheet |
| "Takes forever" | A **"First Calm Walk in 14 Days"** milestone protocol |
| "Walks are stressful" | **Guided real-world walks** with you, on location |
| "Wrong equipment" | **Starter Gear Kit**: fitted harness, long line, treat pouch, high-value treats |
| "Family inconsistent" | **Family Training Session** plus a shared cue sheet for everyone |
| "Fine with trainer, not me" | Sessions coach **the owner**, not just the dog, with video feedback |
| "Will regress" | **90-day Maintenance Support** with check-ins and refresher sessions |
| "Can't practice around dogs" | **Controlled "neutral dog" practice sessions** using your calm demo dogs |
| "Bite / liability fear" | **Safety & Management Plan**, plus muzzle conditioning if needed |
| "Guests are a nightmare" | **"Doorbell & Guest Protocol"** module and a mock-visitor session |
| "Scheduling hassle" | Pre-booked session calendar and text-to-reschedule |
| "Wasted money before" | A strong **results guarantee** (see Step 7) |

---

## Step 5: Pick Delivery Vehicles (How You Provide Each Solution)

For each solution, ask how you could deliver it. Hormozi suggests looking at a few dimensions:

- **Level of effort for the client:** Do-It-Yourself (DIY), Done-With-You (DWY), Done-For-You (DFY). More DFY means more value and a higher price.
- **1-on-1 vs. small group vs. one-to-many.**
- **Live vs. recorded.**
- **Format:** in-home, on location, video call, text support, app, printed materials.
- **Speed and convenience:** 24/7 text access, same-week start.

Some options for dog training:

- **DFY:** Board-and-train or "day training," where you train the dog without the owner, then do a handoff session.
- **DWY:** 1-on-1 in-home and on-location coaching.
- **DIY:** A video library of drills ("Reactivity Academy"), worksheets, cheat sheets.
- **Support:** Unlimited text and video-review support. The owner films a walk and you send feedback.
- **Group:** A monthly "Reactive Rover" group class or structured pack walk for graduates. Cheap to run, very valuable to clients.

---

## Step 6: Trim & Stack

Now go through the list and sort each item:

- **Cut** anything that's **high cost and low value**.
- **Keep** anything that's **low cost and high value**. These are your best items: video libraries, cheat sheets, text support, group sessions.
- Keep **high cost and high value** items selectively for premium tiers.

Then **stack** what's left into one package.

### Example Grand Slam Offer

> ## The "Calm Walk Guarantee" Program
> ### *Turn Your Reactive Dog into a Calm Walking Partner in 8 Weeks, or I Keep Training You for Free*
>
> **Core program:**
> - In-depth **Behavior Assessment & Trigger Map** (90 min, in-home). *Value: $250*
> - **8 private coaching sessions**, in-home and on real-world walks. *Value: $1,200*
> - **2 controlled "neutral dog" practice sessions** with my trained demo dog. *Value: $300*
> - **Family Training Session** so everyone uses the same cues. *Value: $150*
> - **Unlimited text and video-review support for 8 weeks**: send me a clip, get feedback within 24 hrs. *Value: $500*
>
> **Bonuses:**
> - **Bonus 1: "Emergency Calm" Pocket Card & Lock-Screen Guide.** Know exactly what to do mid-lunge. *Value: $49*
> - **Bonus 2: Starter Gear Kit** (fitted harness, long line, treat pouch, first bag of high-value treats). *Value: $120*
> - **Bonus 3: Reactivity Academy video library.** 30+ short drills you can do in 10 minutes a day. *Value: $297*
> - **Bonus 4: Doorbell & Guest Protocol.** Have friends over again without chaos. *Value: $150*
> - **Bonus 5: 90-Day Maintenance Plan** with monthly check-ins, plus free entry to the monthly graduate pack walk. *Value: $300*
>
> **Total value: $3,300+**
> **Your investment: $1,497** (or 3 payments of $549)

You've gone from selling **one $40 hour** to selling a **$1,500 outcome**. The client is far more likely to get the result, because the program covers every obstacle they listed. Many of the high-value pieces (videos, cards, group walks, text support) cost you very little to deliver at scale.

## Step 7: Enhance the Offer

Hormozi uses five enhancers to make the offer even more compelling.

### 1. Scarcity (limited supply)
Be honest about real limits:
> "I take on only **6 new reactive-dog clients per month**, because these cases need my personal attention."

### 2. Urgency (limited time)
Use cohorts, seasonal start dates, or bonus deadlines:
> "Enroll before the 1st and get the Gear Kit and Guest Protocol free. After that, they're $270 extra."
Or use the client's own urgency: "Spring walking season starts in 3 weeks."

### 3. Bonuses
- Each bonus should **address a specific objection or obstacle**.
- **Name each one** and **give it a value**.
- Bonuses presented separately feel more valuable than one big bundle.
- Bonuses can come from partners: a local groomer's discount, a vet's free behavior consult, a pet store gift card. That costs you nothing and builds your referral network.

### 4. Guarantees
The guarantee removes risk. That's the biggest fear for someone who's already "wasted money on training before." Hormozi describes several types:

- **Unconditional:** "Not happy in 30 days? Full refund, no questions."
- **Conditional (often the strongest):** "Attend all sessions, do your 10 minutes a day, and send me 2 walk videos a week. If your dog isn't walking calmly past other dogs at [defined distance] by week 8, **I'll keep training you for free until he is.**"
- **Anti-guarantee:** "All sales final, because I give you everything up front." Works for some exclusive offers.
- **Implied / performance-based:** "Pay $500 now, and the remaining $1,000 only when we hit the milestone."

For dog training, a **conditional "keep training you until you get the result" guarantee** fits well. It shows confidence, the conditions make clients do the work (so results go up), and your cost is extra time with a client who's engaged.

> Note: You can't guarantee a living animal's behavior with certainty. Set a **specific, measurable, reasonable** milestone, and make clear the guarantee depends on the owner following the plan. Check that your wording fits your local consumer-protection rules.

### 5. Naming: the MAGIC formula
Hormozi's naming formula uses some or all of these:

- **M**ake a magnetic reason why ("Spring Kickoff," "New Year," "Grand Opening")
- **A**nnounce your avatar ("Reactive Dog," "New Puppy," "Busy Owner")
- **G**ive them a goal ("Calm Walks," "Bite-Free," "Potty Trained")
- **I**ndicate a time interval ("in 8 Weeks," "in 21 Days")
- **C**omplete with a container word ("Program," "Bootcamp," "Blueprint," "System," "Challenge")

**Examples:**
- "The 8-Week Calm Walk Program for Reactive Dogs"
- "21-Day Puppy Foundations Bootcamp"
- "Spring Reactive Rover Rescue Challenge"
- "The Baby-Ready Dog Blueprint: 6 Weeks to a Safe, Calm Home"

Change the name every so often, by season or cohort, to keep your marketing fresh without changing what you deliver.

---

## Step 8: Build a Simple Offer Ladder (Optional but Powerful)

You don't have to drop low-ticket work entirely. Hormozi-style businesses often use tiers:

| Tier | Offer | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Entry / lead magnet | Free "Reactivity Quiz" or a $47 assessment call | Free–$47 |
| Core Grand Slam Offer | 8-Week Calm Walk Program | $1,497 |
| Premium (DFY) | 2-week Board & Train plus the full program | $3,500–$5,000 |
| Continuity | Graduate Club: monthly pack walk, monthly check-in, video library | $49–$99/mo |

The continuity tier gives you recurring revenue and keeps results from slipping, which in turn makes your guarantee safer.

---

## Step 9: Pricing Psychology Recap

- **Anchor to the value, not to competitors.** Show the $3,300 value stack before you show the $1,497 price.
- **Compare to the cost of the problem.** A bite incident, vet bills from a dog fight, a lawsuit, a ruined couch, the heartbreak of rehoming. Next to those, $1,497 is small.
- **Offer payment plans** to make it easier to say yes.
- **Never discount the price. Add bonuses instead.** Discounting cheapens the offer.
- **Expect some people to say no.** If nobody says it's too expensive, you're still charging too little.

## Your Action Plan

1. **Choose one niche** with high pain and the ability to pay (e.g., reactive dogs).
2. **Write the dream outcome** in your client's own words.
3. **List 15–25 problems** they face before, during, and after training.
4. **Turn each into a named solution.**
5. **Choose delivery vehicles**, favoring low-cost, high-value items (video, cards, text support, group sessions).
6. **Trim and stack** into a single program with bonuses.
7. **Add scarcity, urgency, a strong guarantee, and a MAGIC name.**
8. **Price it at 10x+ your current session rate**, anchored to the value stack.
9. **Test it** with your next 10 prospects. Track close rate and results, then refine.
10. **Collect before/after videos** of calm walks. They're your best proof and will make the next sale easier.

---

### The Big Shift

> Stop selling **dog training sessions**. Start selling **a guaranteed, fast, low-effort transformation**: a calm dog, a relaxed owner, and a household back to normal.

Your competitors will keep fighting over $30–$40 sessions. You'll be selling something they don't offer, at a price that lets you deliver your best work.
