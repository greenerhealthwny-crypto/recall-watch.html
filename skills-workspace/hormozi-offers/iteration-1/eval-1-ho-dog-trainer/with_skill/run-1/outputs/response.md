# Building a Grand Slam Offer for Your Dog Training Business

## The problem: you're selling a commodity

"$40 per session" is a commodity offer. Lots of trainers can sell an hour of dog training, so buyers compare on price, and the cheapest trainer wins. Hormozi calls that "a race to the bottom."

A **Grand Slam Offer** is "an offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available." Once nobody can line your offer up against the $30 trainer down the road, you're selling in a **category of one**, and price stops being the deciding factor.

Your training can stay largely the same. What changes is **what you sell**: a result, not hours.

> Stop selling "a session." Sell "a dog that walks calmly on a loose leash past other dogs in 6 weeks."

Below are the steps in the order the book teaches them, with a worked example for a dog trainer. The prices and numbers are illustrations to adapt, not benchmarks. Hormozi says himself that "the numbers in this book are theoretical."

---

## Step 0: Pick a starving crowd and niche down

Hormozi's order of importance is **market > offer > persuasion**. "Dog owners" is too broad. Choose a group with all four of these indicators:

| Indicator | What to look for in dog owners |
|---|---|
| **Massive pain** | They *desperately need* help, not just want it. "The pain is the pitch." |
| **Purchasing power** | They can afford premium help |
| **Easy to target** | They gather somewhere you can reach |
| **Growing** | The segment is expanding |

**Candidate niches, ranked by pain:**

1. **Reactive or leash-aggressive dogs.** Owners are embarrassed, stressed, walk at 5 a.m. to avoid other dogs, and may face a landlord or neighbor problem. Very high pain.
2. **New puppy owners (8–16 weeks).** They're overwhelmed by biting, house-training and sleepless nights. They have a strong "do it right from the start" motive, and a new puppy cohort arrives every month.
3. **Busy professionals with a "doodle" or other large, energetic adolescent dog.** They have money and no time, and the dog is pulling them down the street.
4. **Rescue owners with fear or anxiety cases.**

**Example pick:** *busy professional owners of reactive dogs within 20 miles.* This group:
- has high pain;
- has money;
- is easy to find through vets, groomers, local Facebook groups, rescues and apartment complexes;
- is growing, since pandemic-era dogs are now reactive adults.

Hormozi notes you can charge up to 100× more for the same thing sold to a specific avatar. "Riches are in the niches." Commit to the niche, and politely turn away or refer out work that doesn't fit.

## Step 1: Define the dream outcome

Write it in the owner's words, including the status and emotional payoff:

> "I can walk my dog around the neighborhood, past other dogs and people, without dread, lunging or embarrassment. I can have friends over, and I feel like a good dog owner again."

Notice that this says nothing about "sit," "heel" or "sessions." It's about their life.

---

## Step 2: List every problem in the way (before, during and after)

Go through the owner's whole journey. For each obstacle, note which Value Equation driver it attacks:
- **Outcome:** "it isn't worth it."
- **Likelihood:** "it won't work for my dog."
- **Effort:** "it's too hard."
- **Time:** "it takes too long."

| # | Problem | Driver |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I've tried a trainer before and it didn't stick." | Likelihood |
| 2 | "My dog is fine in class, then terrible on our real walk." | Likelihood |
| 3 | "I don't have time to practice every day." | Effort / Time |
| 4 | "I forget what to do in the moment when another dog appears." | Effort |
| 5 | "I don't know which tools to buy: harness, leash, treats, muzzle?" | Effort |
| 6 | "It'll take months before anything changes." | Time |
| 7 | "My partner or kids handle the dog differently and undo the work." | Likelihood |
| 8 | "I panic when a setback happens and don't know if it's working." | Likelihood |
| 9 | "Getting to a training facility is a hassle." | Effort |
| 10 | "What happens after training ends? Will it fall apart?" | Likelihood |
| 11 | "Is this even worth hundreds of dollars?" | Outcome |
| 12 | "I can't get help between appointments when something goes wrong." | Time / Effort |

---

## Step 3: Turn each problem into a named solution

Reverse each problem with "how to…":

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Didn't stick before | How to make results last: a structured 6-week protocol with checkpoints |
| Fine in class, bad on walks | How to train where the problem happens: real-world sessions on *your* route |
| No time to practice | How to train in 10 minutes a day: daily micro-drills built into normal walks |
| Forget what to do | How to know exactly what to do: a "Trigger Playbook" pocket card and a phone checklist |
| Gear confusion | How to have the right tools on day 1: a starter kit |
| Takes months | How to see a win in week 1: a fast "decompression and distance" win first |
| Family inconsistency | How to get everyone on the same page: a family handling session plus a cheat sheet |
| Setbacks | How to troubleshoot instantly: video review and text support |
| Travel hassle | How to skip the commute: in-home and on-route sessions |
| Falls apart after | How to keep results: graduation walks and a refresher plan |
| Not worth it | How to make it a no-brainer: a guarantee and proof |
| No help between sessions | How to get help when you need it: a response-time promise |

## Step 4: Create delivery vehicles with the "cheat codes"

This is the step Hormozi calls the most important. Brainstorm widely:

1. **Level of personal attention**
   - one-on-one in home or on route;
   - small group "reactive dog walks" with 3–4 dogs at controlled distance;
   - one-to-many recorded video lessons.
2. **Effort expected of the owner**
   - **DIY:** a video library and checklists.
   - **DWY (done with you):** coached sessions and video review.
   - **DFY (done for you):** board-and-train, or "day training," where you train the dog while they're at work and then hand over.
3. **Live medium:** in person, Zoom check-ins, text or WhatsApp.
4. **Recorded format:** short phone-friendly videos and a one-page PDF per week.
5. **Response speed:** "Send me a video of any walk, and I'll reply with feedback within 24 hours on weekdays."
6. **The 10x / one-tenth test**
   - *If they paid you $10,000, what would you do?* Train the dog daily for them (day training), walk their route with them, coach the whole family, provide all gear, and give unlimited text support for 3 months.
   - *How would you still get results at one-tenth of the price?* A self-paced video course, a group class and a private community.

   Both answers become raw material for tiers, bonuses and downsells.

## Step 5: Trim and stack

Remove anything that is **high cost and low value**, then anything that is **low cost and low value**. Keep:
- **Low cost, high value:** checklists, the Trigger Playbook, recorded videos, text support with set hours, a family cheat sheet.
- **High cost, high value:** one-on-one on-route sessions, day-training add-ons.

Watch the **sales-to-fulfillment continuum**. Unlimited 24/7 texting sells easily but will burn you out. Bounded support ("weekday replies within 24 hours") is easy enough to fulfill. "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction." Over-deliver with your first clients, collect testimonials, then trim.

### The stacked offer (example)

**"Calm Walks in 6 Weeks: The Reactive Dog Reset Program"**

| Component | What it is | Stated value |
|---|---|---|
| **Core:** 6 private coaching sessions | In-home and on *your* walking route, not a training floor | $480 at your old rate (6 × $80 market rate for in-home) |
| **Bonus 1:** "Trigger Playbook" | A pocket card and phone checklist so you know exactly what to do when a dog appears | $97 |
| **Bonus 2:** "10-Minute-a-Day" micro-drill video library | A short drill for each week that fits into normal walks | $197 |
| **Bonus 3:** Walk Review support | Send videos of real walks; feedback within 24 hours on weekdays for 6 weeks | $300 |
| **Bonus 4:** Day-1 Starter Kit | A front-clip harness, a long line, a treat pouch and a high-value treat list | $85 |
| **Bonus 5:** "Whole Family on the Same Page" session | One session for everyone who walks the dog, plus a fridge cheat sheet | $120 |
| **Bonus 6:** Graduation Group Walks | Two controlled small-group walks to practice around other dogs | $100 |
| **Bonus 7:** "Keep It Calm" 90-day maintenance plan | A follow-up check-in at 30, 60 and 90 days | $150 |
| **Total stated value** | | **≈ $1,529** |
| **Your price** | | **$997** (or 2 × $525) |

A few notes on the stack:
- **"Tools and checklists beat more training."** The Playbook and the starter kit cost you little, but they cut the owner's effort, so they're worth more to the owner.
- **Each bonus answers a specific doubt** from Step 2: "I'll forget," "no time," "my family undoes it," "it'll fall apart afterwards."
- **The bonuses together should be worth more than the core.** They are here: about $1,049 against $480.
- **Base every stated value on something real**, such as your own à la carte prices or what each item would actually cost bought separately. Don't inflate.

That's six sessions for $997, versus 6 × $40 = $240 before. That's **about 4× the revenue for similar contact hours**, and the owner gets far more value than they did from loose sessions.

## Step 6: Run the Value Equation over the offer

> **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

| Driver | How the offer moves it |
|---|---|
| ↑ **Dream outcome** | It's framed as "calm walks and pride in your dog," not "6 lessons" |
| ↑ **Likelihood** | Before-and-after videos of past reactive dogs, testimonials, a guarantee, real-route training, and family alignment |
| ↓ **Time delay** | A week-1 quick win ("fast beats free"): show visible progress by the second session |
| ↓ **Effort** | In-home visits, 10-minute drills, a starter kit, a pocket playbook, and video review in place of guesswork |

Hormozi's point is that the biggest gains are usually on the **bottom** of the equation: make it faster and easier. Bigger promises are "easy, and it's lazy."

## Step 7: Enhance the offer

### Scarcity (real only)
You're one person with a limited number of slots. Say so: *"I take on 6 new reactive-dog clients per month so each one gets my full attention."* Use your true capacity. Never invent a limit.

### Urgency (real only)
- **Cohort-based rolling urgency:** start new clients on the 1st and 15th. "Sign up this week and you're in the cohort starting on the 15th; otherwise it's the 1st."
- **Seasonal urgency:** "Get calm before spring walking season" or "before the holidays, when guests visit."
- **Bonus urgency:** Graduation Group Walks are included for people who enroll by a real date.

### Guarantee
Use Hormozi's formula: **"If you don't get X in Y time, we'll Z."**

Behavior depends on the owner doing the work, so a **conditional** guarantee tied to the key actions fits best:

> **"Calm Walk Guarantee:** Do your 10-minute daily drills and send me at least one walk video a week. If your dog isn't noticeably calmer on walks (able to pass another dog at a distance we agree on at the start) by the end of week 6, I'll keep coaching you for free until they are."

Why this works:
- It's "better than money back": they get the result, not just a refund.
- It rewards the behavior that actually produces results.
- It filters out people who want a magic fix.

You could also stack a short **unconditional** guarantee in front of it: "If you're not happy after the first session, I'll refund it in full."

Hormozi's math says a strong guarantee usually adds more sales than it costs in refunds. Do that math on your own numbers.

### Name it with M-A-G-I-C
Use three to five of these components:
- **M**agnetic reason why: "Spring," "New Year"
- **A**nnounce the avatar: "Reactive Dog," "Busy Owner," "New Puppy"
- **G**ive them a goal: "Calm Walks," "Polite Puppy"
- **I**ndicate a time interval: "in 6 Weeks"
- **C**ontainer word: Reset, Program, Bootcamp, Blueprint

Examples:
- *Calm Walks in 6 Weeks: Reactive Dog Reset*
- *The 6-Week Leash-Calm Bootcamp for Busy Owners*
- *Perfect Puppy Start: 30-Day Puppy Blueprint* (if you choose the puppy niche)

Name each bonus with the same formula.

## Step 8: Add a price ladder (optional)

The 10x / one-tenth test gives you natural tiers:

| Tier | What's in it | Example price |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-guided** | Video library, Playbook, starter kit, one Zoom review | $197 |
| **Core: Calm Walks Reset** | The stack above | $997 |
| **VIP / done-for-you** | Adds day training while they're at work, extra sessions, and priority text support. Use real scarcity: "3 spots per month" | $2,500+ |

The low tier catches price-sensitive buyers *without* competing with $30 trainers. The VIP tier makes the core look reasonable. A few clients will happily buy the top tier.

## Why charging more helps your clients too

Hormozi's **virtuous cycle of price**: when people pay $997 instead of $40, they:
- show up;
- do the drills;
- take it seriously.

That gets better results, which brings better testimonials, which justifies the price. You also have margin for gear, follow-ups and advertising. At $40 a session, you can't afford to give that level of care. "Profit is oxygen."

## Your next five actions

1. **Choose one niche** (for example, reactive dogs or new puppies) and write the dream outcome in their words.
2. **Interview 3–5 past clients** about what nearly stopped them from buying and what went wrong after. Add their answers to the problems list.
3. **Build the stack:** create the Playbook, the micro-drill videos and the starter kit list this week. They're cheap to make.
4. **Collect proof:** before-and-after walk videos (with permission) and short testimonials.
5. **Pitch the new offer to your next 10 leads.** Stop quoting per session. Track conversions, and adjust the stack, name or guarantee. "If your offer doesn't work, it doesn't mean you suck. It means your offer sucks." Iterate.

---

## Honest guardrails

- **Keep scarcity and urgency real.** No fake countdown timers or invented "only 2 spots left." Hormozi insists on this too.
- **Word guarantees and claims carefully.** Behavior can't be guaranteed in an absolute sense.
  - Tie promises to measurable, agreed criteria and to the owner's participation.
  - Honor every guarantee you make.
  - Make sure your refund and guarantee terms meet local consumer-protection rules. In the US that includes FTC rules on advertising claims.
- **Use real testimonials only**, and get permission before using client videos.
- **Know your limits.** Serious aggression, bite history or severe anxiety may need a veterinary behaviorist or a vet check for pain or medical causes. Build a referral partner into your process, and exclude those cases from the guarantee or adapt it for them.
- **Make your stated values defensible.** Base them on prices you actually charge, or on what the items really cost separately.

The core idea: **stop competing on price by making price irrelevant.** Sell a specific outcome, to a specific owner, with every obstacle removed, and back it with a guarantee. You'll no longer be compared to the $30 trainer, because they aren't selling the same thing.
