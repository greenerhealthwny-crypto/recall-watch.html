# A Quiet Strength Study of Philippians 4:6–7 (Anxiety)

This study uses the three-step method from the edition's "A Life of Quiet Strength" feature: **Observation, Interpretation, Application**. Tony Dungy calls the Bible "God's playbook". His advice is to study it *before* the game, not after you've fallen behind. That fits anxiety well. The best time to learn this play is before the next anxious moment comes.

> **Before you start:** open your Bible to **Philippians 4** (page 169 in the Quiet Strength edition), or use an authorized online NLT, and read **all of chapter 4**. I give only brief phrases below because the NLT text is copyrighted. Reading the passage in context is part of the method anyway.

---

## Step 1: Observation (what does the passage actually say?)

The edition asks you to restate the passage in your own words, or to underline key phrases. Read verses 6–7 slowly two or three times, then notice the following.

**The structure.** The passage gives one command to stop, one command to start, and then a promise.

| Part | What it says (short phrases and my paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| **Stop** | "Don't worry about anything." The command covers *anything*, not just small things. |
| **Start** | Pray about *everything* instead. Tell God what you need. |
| **With** | Thank him for all he has done. Thanksgiving goes *with* the request, not after the answer. |
| **Promise** | You will experience God's peace, a peace beyond understanding. |
| **What the peace does** | It will **guard** your hearts and minds. |
| **Where** | "in Christ Jesus". The guarding happens inside a relationship. |

**Things to underline:**
- "anything" and "everything". These matching words leave nothing out.
- "instead". Prayer *replaces* worry. It doesn't just sit beside it.
- "guard". This is a protection word.
- "hearts and minds". Both feelings and thoughts are covered.

**Observe the surroundings (context):**
- **4:4.** "Always be full of joy in the Lord." The Verse Finder uses this as its key verse for **Joy**.
- **4:5.** The Lord is near. This is the reason given just before "don't worry".
- **4:8.** Fix your thoughts on what is true, honorable and right. The Verse Finder lists this under **Purity**.
- **4:9.** Put into practice what you've learned, and the God of peace will be with you.
- **4:11–13.** Paul has learned to be content in any circumstance. The Verse Finder lists 4:11–13 under **Simplicity**.

**Restate it in your own words.** Try writing one sentence, for example:
*"Instead of turning my worries over and over, I turn them into specific requests to God, with thanks for what he's already done, and he guards my heart and mind with a peace I can't explain."*

## Step 2: Interpretation (what did the author mean?)

The edition says to ask *why* the passage was written. It suggests using the book summaries in its Overview, and a commentary or Bible dictionary if you need one.

**Who, when, what kind of writing.** According to the edition's Overview of Philippians:
- **Author:** Paul.
- **Date:** around AD 61. These are the edition's traditional views, and scholars debate some details.
- **Genre:** a letter.
- **Summary:** it is about "the mind-set, attitude and outlook a believer needs to experience the joy of the Lord in a troubled world."

That summary is the key to this passage. Philippians 4:6–7 is not a promise of a trouble-free life. It is about the **mind-set** a believer can have *in* a troubled world.

**Things to think through:**
1. **Paul's own situation.** Paul wrote Philippians while in custody (see Philippians 1:12–14). He wasn't writing "don't worry" from a comfortable chair. That gives his words weight. He had reasons to be anxious and was practicing what he taught.
2. **"Don't worry" is not "don't care."** In the same letter Paul says he is concerned for the churches and for Timothy's welfare (Philippians 2:20). The command is not about switching off concern. It is about what you *do* with concern: you bring it to God, rather than carrying it alone and going round in circles.
3. **Thanksgiving matters.** Thanks reminds the anxious person of God's past faithfulness. That fits the Verse Finder's practice **"Thankfulness must be cultivated"**: choose to be thankful in all circumstances, and God will give you peace.
4. **Peace as a guard.** The picture is of a guard posted at a gate. God's peace stands watch over the heart (emotions) and the mind (thoughts). The promise is that God will guard you. It is not that circumstances will change right away.
5. **"Beyond understanding".** This peace doesn't depend on having worked out a solution. You can have it before the problem is solved.

**Check your interpretation.** Does it fit 4:4–9 and the letter's theme of joy in a troubled world? Yes. Verses 6–7 (pray instead of worry) lead straight into verse 8 (what to think about) and verse 9 (what to practice). Taken together: **pray, think, practice → peace.**

*Optional deeper study:* look up "anxiety" or "peace" in a Bible dictionary, or read a commentary on Philippians 4. The NLT is a readable, meaning-based translation. For close word study you could compare a more literal translation (ESV, NASB or NKJV) to see how "guard" and "hearts and minds" are rendered.

## Step 3: Application (so what?)

The edition's question is: **"So what? What difference does this passage make in my life?"** It cites 2 Timothy 3:16: scripture is useful to teach, correct and train. Here is a practical playbook for this passage.

**1. Name the worry (be specific).** Write down what you're anxious about right now. Vague worry is hard to pray about.

**2. Turn each worry into a request.** "Tell God what you need." Example: *worry*: "What if I can't pay this bill?" → *request*: "Lord, I need provision for this bill, and wisdom on what to do next."

**3. Attach a thank-you to each request.** List two or three specific ways God has been faithful before. This is the "with thanksgiving" part, and it is the Verse Finder practice **Thankfulness must be cultivated**.

**4. Re-aim your thinking (4:8).** When the worry comes back, don't just try to push it away. Replace it. Ask what is *true* here, as opposed to what you fear might happen.

**5. Do the next faithful thing (4:9).** Peace often comes as you take the next responsible step. Make the call, have the conversation, set the budget.

**6. Repeat.** Dungy's rhythm is to read daily and pray daily. Worry tends to come back, so the prayer can come back too. Hand it over each time.

**A one-week practice plan:**

| Day | Read | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Philippians 4:4–9 | Write your own-words paraphrase of 4:6–7 |
| 2 | Philippians 4:6–7 | Make a worry → request → thanks list |
| 3 | 1 John 4:18; Psalm 27:1 | Name one fear and pray through it |
| 4 | Psalm 46:1–3 | Write down one way God has been your refuge |
| 5 | Romans 8:38–39 | Read it aloud when anxiety rises |
| 6 | Philippians 4:8 | Write three things that are true about your situation |
| 7 | Philippians 4:11–13 | Reflect: where is God teaching you contentment? |

**Memory phrase:** "Pray about everything." Say it whenever you notice you're worrying.

**Practices that fit this passage:**

- **Thankfulness must be cultivated.** Choose to be thankful in all circumstances, and God will give you peace. References: **1 Thessalonians 5:18**; Colossians 4:2; Ephesians 5:20; 2 Corinthians 1:3–4 (the God of all comfort).
- **Simplicity must be sought.** The less we depend on worldly things, the more we can focus on God. References: **Matthew 6:33**; Philippians 4:11–13; Hebrews 13:5 ("I will never fail you").

## A gentle word

This passage invites you to bring anxiety to God. It does not say you should never need help. If anxiety is heavy or constant, or affects your sleep, work or relationships, please talk with a doctor, a counselor or a pastor. Getting that help is one of the "next faithful things" from Step 3. If you are ever in crisis or having thoughts of harming yourself, in the US you can **call or text 988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) at any time.
