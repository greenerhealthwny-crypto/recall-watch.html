# Seven-Day Devotional: Work, Wealth and Integrity for a New Side Business

*Built on the Quiet Strength New Testament with Psalms and Proverbs (NLT)*

Starting a business brings two pressures at once. **Money** pushes you to worry, rush or cut corners. **Integrity** asks whether you'll do it right when nobody's checking. Tony Dungy calls the Bible "God's playbook". You study a playbook *before* the game, so this week is a good time to settle your plays before the pressure gets heavy.

**How each day works.** The edition's "A Life of Quiet Strength" teaches a three-step method, and each day follows it:
1. **Observe.** What does the passage say? Write it in your own words.
2. **Interpret.** What did the author mean, and why was it written?
3. **Apply.** So what? What changes in my business this week?

**Each day includes:**
- one Proverbs reading and one New Testament reading;
- the Quiet Strength Verse Finder's principle statement where one applies;
- reflection questions, a business action step, and a short prayer.

Please read each passage in full in your Bible or an authorized online NLT. Proverbs starts on page 301 of this edition. I quote only short phrases here because the NLT text is copyrighted.

> **Source note.** References marked **(VF)** come from the edition's Verse Finder entries on *Work, Wealth, Integrity, Responsibility, Simplicity* and related topics. Unmarked Proverbs are well-known passages I've added on the same themes. They aren't from the Verse Finder listing. Some Verse Finder print was faint, so check each reference before you memorize or share it.

## Day 1. Who is the real boss? Commit the work to God

**Verse Finder practice:** *Work must be done with God in mind.* Do your best at work, because God is ultimately your true boss.

- **Proverbs 16:3.** Commit your actions to the Lord, and your plans will succeed.
- **Ephesians 6:5–7 (VF).** Work with sincerity, as if you were serving the Lord rather than people.
- **1 Corinthians 15:58 (VF).** Work for the Lord is never useless.

**Observe.** Ephesians says to work wholeheartedly "as though you were working for the Lord" (my paraphrase), whether or not anyone is watching.

**Interpret.** Paul wrote this to workers who often had little power or recognition. His point is that whoever your earthly boss is, your real accountability is to God. As a business owner, *you* are now the boss. These verses remind you that you still answer to Someone.

**Apply.**
- Write a one-paragraph "mission" for your business that includes how it serves people and honors God.
- Ask yourself: if God were my client, what would I change about how I work?

**Prayer.** *Lord, this business is yours before it is mine. Help me work with sincerity, as for you.*

---

## Day 2. Integrity: honest scales and a secure path

**Verse Finder practice:** *Integrity must be protected.* Living with integrity requires personal sacrifice, but the eternal rewards outweigh what we give up.

- **Proverbs 10:9 (VF).** People with integrity walk safely, but crooked paths are exposed.
- **Proverbs 11:1.** The Lord detests dishonest scales but delights in accurate weights.
- **Titus 2:7 (VF).** Be an example of good works and integrity in everything you do.
- **Psalm 119:1–2 (VF).** Joyful are people of integrity.

**Observe.** Proverbs 10:9 contrasts two paths. One is *safe*. The other will be *exposed*.

**Interpret.** The "scales" in Proverbs 11:1 were the business tools of the day. Merchants could cheat with weights that looked fair. So integrity in Proverbs is not abstract. It is about pricing, measuring, invoicing and delivering what you promised.

**Apply.** Do an "honest scales" check of your business:
- Is your **pricing** clear, with no hidden fees?
- Does your **marketing** promise only what you deliver?
- Do you keep honest **records** and report income properly on your taxes?
- Are you clear with your **day-job employer**? Check any contract terms on side work, conflicts of interest, and the use of company time or equipment.

Pick the one area where you feel most tempted to blur the line, and fix it this week.

**Prayer.** *Lord, make my scales honest even when no one would catch me. I'd rather walk safely than get ahead crookedly.*

## Day 3. Wealth: a tool, not a master

**Verse Finder practice:** *Wealth must be used wisely.* Thinking wealth can make you happy is foolish. Only a right relationship with God brings fulfillment. Use all the resources God provides wisely and unselfishly.

- **Proverbs 30:8–9.** Give me neither poverty nor riches, just enough. This is Agur's prayer. The edition's Overview lists Agur as one of the authors of Proverbs.
- **Matthew 6:19–21, 24 (VF).** Store treasure in heaven. You can't serve both God and money.
- **Luke 12:21 (VF).** The man who was rich toward himself but not toward God.

**Observe.** Jesus says no one can serve *two masters* (Matthew 6:24).

**Interpret.** Jesus isn't condemning money. He is warning about what it can turn into: a master that sets your priorities and your peace. A side business is often started for good reasons, such as debt, family or freedom. But it can slowly start to rule your time, mood and identity.

**Apply.**
- Write down *why* you want this income, and what "enough" looks like for you.
- Decide now on a generosity plan, for example a percentage of profit you'll give. Deciding before the money arrives is easier than deciding after.

**Prayer.** *Lord, money can serve me, but it will not rule me. Show me what "enough" is.*

## Day 4. Worry about money: seek first

**Verse Finder practice:** *Simplicity must be sought.* The less we depend on worldly things, the more we can focus on God.

- **Proverbs 3:5–6.** Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and he will show you which path to take.
- **Matthew 6:33 (VF).** Seek the Kingdom of God above all else, and he will give you everything you need. Read **6:25–34** for the full passage on not worrying.
- **Philippians 4:11–13 (VF).** Paul learned contentment in plenty and in want.
- **Hebrews 13:5 (VF).** Don't love money; be satisfied with what you have, for God says he will never fail you.

**Observe.** Matthew 6 repeats "don't worry" several times and gives a reason each time.

**Interpret.** Jesus' answer to money worry is not "you'll get rich". It is about *order*: seek God first, and trust him for what you need. Paul's "I can do everything" (Philippians 4:13) is about being content in *any* circumstance, including lean months. It isn't a promise of business success.

**Apply.**
- List your top three money worries about the business. Turn each one into a prayer request (Philippians 4:6–7 goes well with this).
- Then take one practical step on each, such as a budget, a savings buffer, or limiting how much you risk.

Trusting God and planning carefully work together.

**Prayer.** *Lord, I put you first. Replace my worry with trust and my panic with a plan.*

## Day 5. Diligence and planning: steady work over quick money

- **Proverbs 21:5.** Good planning and hard work lead to prosperity; hasty shortcuts lead to poverty.
- **Proverbs 13:11.** Wealth from get-rich-quick schemes quickly disappears; wealth from hard work grows over time.
- **Proverbs 14:23.** Work brings profit, but mere talk leads to poverty.
- **Luke 14:28–30.** Count the cost before you build a tower.

**Observe.** Proverbs keeps contrasting *steady and planned* with *hasty and quick*.

**Interpret.** Proverbs is wisdom literature: practical wisdom for every area of life, as the edition's Overview describes it. It shows what is generally true, not guaranteed formulas. Its message for a new business owner is patience. Build slowly and honestly, and be wary of shortcuts that promise fast money.

**Apply.**
- Write a simple 90-day plan: three goals, the hours you can realistically give each week, and costs you'll spend only if a certain milestone is met.
- Be wary of any opportunity that promises fast returns with little work.

**Prayer.** *Lord, give me patience to build slowly and wisely, and the discipline to do today's work.*

## Day 6. Faithful in small things; wise counsel

**Verse Finder practice:** *Responsibility must be accepted.* If you are faithful in small things, God will trust you with greater responsibilities.

- **Proverbs 15:22.** Plans go wrong for lack of advice; many advisers bring success.
- **Luke 16:10 (VF).** Faithful in little, faithful in much. Dishonest in little, dishonest in much.
- **Matthew 25:21, 23 (VF).** "Well done, my good and faithful servant." Read the whole parable of the talents, Matthew 25:14–30.

**Observe.** Luke 16:10 links small honesty and big honesty. They grow together.

**Interpret.** The parable of the talents praises the servants who *put resources to work* faithfully, not the one who buried his out of fear. That speaks to both worry and stewardship. God expects you to use what you've been given, carefully and responsibly.

**Apply.**
- Pick one "small thing" to be faithful in this week, such as answering every customer on time, keeping receipts, or meeting a deadline.
- Find advisers. That might be a mentor who runs a business, a tax professional or accountant for the side income, and a trusted friend who will ask you hard questions about integrity.

**Prayer.** *Lord, make me faithful in the small things, and humble enough to ask for advice.*

## Day 7. A good name and the long view

- **Proverbs 22:1.** Choose a good reputation over great riches.
- **Proverbs 2:6–8 (VF, 2:6–7).** The Lord grants wisdom and is a shield to those who walk with integrity.
- **1 Timothy 6:6–10, 17–19 (VF, 6:6–7 and 6:17–19).** Godliness with contentment is great wealth. The love of money is a root of evil. The rich should be generous and ready to share.
- **Colossians 3:23–24.** Work willingly at whatever you do, as for the Lord.

**Observe.** 1 Timothy 6 doesn't tell the rich to stop having money. It tells them to be *generous* and to put their hope in God.

**Interpret.** In the edition's Overview, 1 Timothy is about conduct for the church and its leaders, and "applies to anyone wanting to be used by God." A business is a platform: your reputation becomes your testimony. The edition's Verse Finder also says, under **Leadership**, to ask God to develop honesty, kindness, patience, purity and integrity in you (Titus 2:7–8).

**Apply.** Write your "Business Playbook". This is a short list of non-negotiables to look at whenever a decision gets hard. For example:
1. I will tell the truth in pricing, marketing and records.
2. I won't let this business crowd out God, family or my current job commitments.
3. I'll give generously from what I earn.
4. I'll do excellent work as for the Lord, and leave the results to him.

**Prayer.** *Lord, I want a good name more than great riches. Let this business reflect you.*

## Going further

- **Keep reading Proverbs.** It has 31 chapters, so reading one a day takes about a month. Watch for verses on work, money, words and honesty.
- **Related Verse Finder practices:**
  - *Humility must be pursued.*
  - *Service must be practiced.*
  - *Self-control must be pursued.*
  - *Thankfulness must be cultivated.*
  - *Hypocrisy must be avoided:* don't pretend to be what you are not (Luke 12:1–2).
- **Study with others.** The edition encourages joining a Bible study. Even one friend who reads these verses with you and checks in on your business integrity is valuable.

**A practical note.** These devotions are about character and trust. They aren't financial, tax or legal advice. For things like business structure, taxes on side income, and employment-contract questions, talk to a qualified professional. Proverbs 15:22 would agree.
