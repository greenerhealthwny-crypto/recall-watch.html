# Faithful in Business: A 7-Day Devotional on Work, Wealth, and Integrity

*For anyone starting a side business who feels the pull between money worries and wanting to do things right.*

Starting something new is exciting and scary. You may be asking: *Will this make enough money? Will I cut corners when it gets tight? Will this take over my life?* Scripture speaks directly to all of these. Each day below pairs a passage from **Proverbs** with one from the **New Testament**. It includes a short reflection, questions, a practical step for your business, and a prayer.

**How to use it:** Set aside 15-20 minutes, ideally before you work on the business. Keep a notebook or a note on your phone for the "Business Step" each day.

---

## Day 1: Commit the Work to God

**Proverbs 16:3** — "Commit to the Lord whatever you do, and he will establish your plans."
**Also read:** Proverbs 16:1-9

**Colossians 3:23-24** — "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters… It is the Lord Christ you are serving."

### Reflection
Before this business is a source of income, it can be an act of worship. "Commit" in Proverbs 16:3 is literally "roll onto." You roll the weight of your plans onto God. That doesn't mean you stop planning. Proverbs 16:9 says, "In their hearts humans plan their course, but the Lord establishes their steps." You plan; God directs.

Colossians 3 was first written to slaves, people with almost no control over their work. If *they* could work "as for the Lord," so can you, whether you're filling orders at midnight or answering a cranky customer email.

### Reflect
- Why am I starting this business? Write down your real motives, the good ones and the mixed ones.
- What would it look like to see this business as service to God, not just a way to make money?

### Business Step
Write a one-paragraph "purpose statement" for your business. Include whom you serve and how it honors God. Keep it where you'll see it.

### Prayer
*Lord, I roll this business onto You. Establish my plans where they're good, and change them where they're not. Let me work for You first. Amen.*

---

## Day 2: Diligence and Honest Hard Work

**Proverbs 10:4** — "Lazy hands make for poverty, but diligent hands bring wealth."
**Proverbs 21:5** — "The plans of the diligent lead to profit as surely as haste leads to poverty."
**Also read:** Proverbs 6:6-11 (the ant)

**2 Thessalonians 3:10-13** — "…We hear that some among you are idle… Such people we command…to settle down and earn the food they eat… Never tire of doing what is good."

### Reflection
Proverbs praises diligence, but notice it contrasts diligence not only with laziness but with *haste* (21:5). Get-rich-quick thinking is just as dangerous as idleness. The ant in Proverbs 6 works steadily, stores up in season, and needs no boss to push it. That's a side-business picture: small, steady, consistent effort.

Paul worked as a tentmaker so he wouldn't be a burden (Acts 18:3; 2 Thessalonians 3:8). Work with your hands isn't less spiritual than ministry.

### Reflect
- Am I tempted more toward laziness or toward rushing?
- Where do I need steady, boring consistency instead of big bursts?

### Business Step
Set a realistic weekly schedule for the business. Block specific hours. Protect them, and also protect the time *outside* them (see Day 6).

### Prayer
*Father, make me diligent like the ant and patient instead of hasty. Help me be faithful in small daily tasks. Amen.*

---

## Day 3: Integrity When No One Is Watching

**Proverbs 11:1** — "The Lord detests dishonest scales, but accurate weights find favor with him."
**Proverbs 10:9** — "Whoever walks in integrity walks securely, but whoever takes crooked paths will be found out."
**Proverbs 11:3** — "The integrity of the upright guides them, but the unfaithful are destroyed by their duplicity."

**Luke 16:10** — "Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much."

### Reflection
In ancient markets, merchants could cheat with two sets of weights: one for buying, one for selling. God says He *detests* that. Modern versions look like overstated product claims, hidden fees, fudged hours, skipped taxes, "borrowing" someone's design, or fake reviews.

Jesus says integrity in small things predicts integrity in big things. A side business is "very little" right now. That makes it the perfect place to build honest habits before the stakes get higher.

Integrity also brings *security* (10:9). You don't have to remember which story you told whom.

### Reflect
- Where might I be tempted to shade the truth: pricing, marketing, taxes, or my employer's time and resources?
- Does my day job have rules about side businesses? Am I keeping them?

### Business Step
Do an "honest scales" check:
- Are your product descriptions and claims accurate?
- Are prices and fees clear up front?
- Are you tracking income for taxes?
- Are you working on the business only on your own time and equipment, not your employer's?

Fix one thing this week.

### Prayer
*Lord, give me accurate scales in every part of my business. Let me be trustworthy in little things so I can be trusted with more. Amen.*

## Day 4: The Right Place for Money

**Proverbs 30:8-9** — "Give me neither poverty nor riches, but give me only my daily bread. Otherwise, I may have too much and disown you… Or I may become poor and steal, and so dishonor the name of my God."
**Proverbs 23:4-5** — "Do not wear yourself out to get rich… Cast but a glance at riches, and they are gone, for they will surely sprout wings and fly off to the sky like an eagle."

**1 Timothy 6:6-10, 17-19** — "Godliness with contentment is great gain… The love of money is a root of all kinds of evil… Command those who are rich…not to be arrogant nor to put their hope in wealth…but to put their hope in God… to be rich in good deeds, and to be generous and willing to share."

### Reflection
Scripture doesn't call money evil. It calls the *love* of money a root of evil. Proverbs 30 gives a wise prayer: both too little and too much carry spiritual risks. Poverty can tempt you to steal; wealth can tempt you to forget God.

Wanting your business to succeed is fine. Proverbs often links diligence with profit. The question is whether money is a *tool* or a *master*. Jesus said, "You cannot serve both God and money" (Matthew 6:24).

### Reflect
- What number would feel like "enough" from this business? Have I ever defined it?
- If the business took off, what would I be tempted to do or become?

### Business Step
Write down a specific financial goal for the business and what it's *for* (debt payoff, family needs, savings, giving). Having a defined "enough" helps protect you from endless striving.

### Prayer
*God, give me my daily bread. Keep me from loving money, and let me hold whatever You give with an open hand. Amen.*

## Day 5: Trusting God With Money Worries

**Proverbs 3:5-6, 9-10** — "Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding… Honor the Lord with your wealth, with the firstfruits of all your crops; then your barns will be filled to overflowing."

**Matthew 6:25-34** — "Do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink… Look at the birds… your heavenly Father feeds them… Seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well. Therefore do not worry about tomorrow."

**Philippians 4:6-7, 19** — "Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God… And my God will meet all your needs."

### Reflection
New business owners worry: slow sales, unexpected costs, the fear of having wasted money. Jesus doesn't say these needs aren't real. He says your Father *knows* you need them (Matthew 6:32). Worry can't add an hour to your life, but it can steal joy and judgment.

Proverbs 3 pairs trust with action: honor God with your "firstfruits." Giving first, before you see how things turn out, is a practical way to declare that God, not the business, is your provider.

Trust isn't the same as being careless. Proverbs also says, "The prudent see danger and take refuge" (22:3). Plan wisely, then let go of the outcome.

### Reflect
- What specific money fear about this business keeps coming back?
- What would "seeking first the kingdom" look like this week in my business?

### Business Step
Turn worries into a plan and a prayer:
1. List your top three financial fears.
2. For each, write one prudent action (for example, a small emergency buffer, a cap on startup spending, or not quitting your day job too early).
3. Pray over each one and hand the outcome to God.

Also consider deciding now what portion of business profit you'll give.

### Prayer
*Father, You know what I need. I bring You my fears about money. Help me plan wisely and then trust You fully. Amen.*

## Day 6: Wise Counsel, Rest, and Boundaries

**Proverbs 15:22** — "Plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed."
**Proverbs 27:23-24** — "Be sure you know the condition of your flocks, give careful attention to your herds; for riches do not endure forever."
**Proverbs 22:7** — "The borrower is slave to the lender."

**Luke 14:28-30** — "Suppose one of you wants to build a tower. Won't you first sit down and estimate the cost…?"
**Mark 6:31** — "Come with me by yourselves to a quiet place and get some rest."

### Reflection
Wisdom in Proverbs is practical. Get advice. Know your numbers ("the condition of your flocks"). Be careful with debt. Jesus Himself uses a building project to teach counting the cost.

A side business can quietly eat up family time, sleep, church, and rest. Jesus pulled His tired disciples away to rest even when crowds were waiting. Sabbath (Exodus 20:8-10) is a weekly reminder that the world, and your business, doesn't depend on you working nonstop.

### Reflect
- Who could I ask for honest advice, such as a mentor, an experienced business owner, or an accountant?
- Do I really know my costs, margins, and cash flow?
- What is this business costing my family, health, or faith life?

### Business Step
- Schedule one conversation with a wise adviser this month.
- Set up a simple monthly money review: income, expenses, profit.
- Choose one weekly day or block that is fully off from the business.
- Talk with your spouse or family about boundaries, if that applies.

### Prayer
*Lord, give me humility to seek counsel, wisdom to count the cost, and faith to rest. Guard my family and my soul from being consumed by work. Amen.*

## Day 7: Generosity and a Lasting Legacy

**Proverbs 11:24-25** — "One person gives freely, yet gains even more; another withholds unduly, but comes to poverty. A generous person will prosper; whoever refreshes others will be refreshed."
**Proverbs 22:1** — "A good name is more desirable than great riches; to be esteemed is better than silver or gold."
**Proverbs 3:27** — "Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in your power to act."

**2 Corinthians 9:6-8** — "Whoever sows generously will also reap generously… God loves a cheerful giver. And God is able to bless you abundantly, so that in all things at all times, having all that you need, you will abound in every good work."
**Matthew 6:19-21** — "Store up for yourselves treasures in heaven… For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."

### Reflection
The end goal of a godly business isn't a bigger bank balance. It's a good name and an open hand. Proverbs 22:1 says reputation matters more than riches. Your business can bless customers through good products, suppliers and helpers through fair and *prompt* payment (Proverbs 3:27-28; James 5:4), and others through generosity.

Paul says God gives "so that" you can abound in good work. Blessing flows *through* you, not just *to* you. Where your treasure goes, your heart follows.

### Reflect
- What do I want people to say about how I did business?
- How could this business be generous right now, even in a small way?

### Business Step
Write a short "integrity and generosity commitment" for your business. For example:
- "We tell the truth in all marketing and pricing."
- "We pay people fairly and on time."
- "We give ___% of profit to ___."
- "We keep one day a week free for rest and family."

Sign and date it. Revisit it every three months.

### Prayer
*God, let this business build a good name that honors You. Make me a cheerful, generous giver. Let my treasure, and my heart, be with You. Amen.*
