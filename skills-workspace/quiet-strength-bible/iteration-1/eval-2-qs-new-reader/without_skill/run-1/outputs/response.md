# Getting Started with Your Quiet Strength New Testament

Congratulations on the gift. Starting the Bible for the first time can feel like a lot, so here is a simple way in.

## Where to start

1. **Start with a Gospel, usually the Gospel of John or Mark.**
   - The four Gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke and John) tell the story of Jesus. They are the center of the New Testament.
   - **Mark** is the shortest and moves fast. It's a good place to get the whole story quickly.
   - **John** is the one most often recommended to new readers. It was written so that readers "may continue to believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God" (John 20:31). It explains who Jesus is and why that matters.
2. **Then read Acts.** It continues Luke's story and shows how the early church began and spread.
3. **Then read some of the shorter letters**, for example Philippians, Ephesians, James and 1 John. They are practical and encouraging, and they're short enough to read in one sitting.
4. **Save Romans, Hebrews and Revelation for later.** They are rich, but they are easier to follow once you know the basic story.

If your edition also includes **Psalms and Proverbs** (many New Testament editions do), read a psalm or a few proverbs alongside your main reading. Proverbs has 31 chapters, so many people read one chapter a day to match the date.

## How to study it

**Keep it short and steady.** Ten to fifteen minutes a day does more than an occasional long session. One chapter a day is a great pace.

**Use a simple method.** One easy and popular approach is **S.O.A.P.**:
- **S**cripture: read the passage, and write down a verse that stands out to you.
- **O**bservation: ask what it says. Who is speaking, what is happening, and what words repeat?
- **A**pplication: ask what it means for your life today.
- **P**rayer: say a short, honest prayer in response.

**Ask three basic questions of any passage:**
1. What does this show me about God or Jesus?
2. What does it show me about people, and about me?
3. Is there something to believe, do, change or be thankful for?

**Use the extras in your Bible.** The *Quiet Strength* editions include devotionals and notes that connect the text to everyday life, character, family, leadership and handling pressure. Read the passage first and the notes second, so the Bible text comes first.

**Understand how it's laid out.** "John 3:16" means the book of John, chapter 3, verse 16. The table of contents lists page numbers, so use it freely.

**Don't go it alone.** A friend, a church small group or a men's Bible study makes the reading richer, and you'll have someone to ask when a passage is confusing. The person who gave you this Bible might like to read along with you.

**It's okay to have questions.** Some parts will be confusing at first. Write your questions in the margin or a notebook and keep reading. Many get answered as you go.

## A simple first-month plan

- **Days 1–21:** Gospel of John, one chapter a day
- **Days 22–28:** Philippians (4 chapters) and James (5 chapters), about one to two chapters a day
- **Optional, every day:** one psalm or the chapter of Proverbs that matches the date

After that, go on to Mark or Luke and then Acts, or follow the reading plan in your Bible if it has one.

Enjoy it. Read it slowly and honestly, and let it raise questions as well as answer them.
