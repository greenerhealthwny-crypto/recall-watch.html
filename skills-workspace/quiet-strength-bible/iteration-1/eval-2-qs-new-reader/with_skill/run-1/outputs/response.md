# Getting started with your Quiet Strength New Testament

That's a good gift to start with. The front pages of this edition (pages A1–A41, before Matthew) were written for someone in your position. Here is what they say about where to begin and how to study.

## Where the book says to start

The section **"A Life of Quiet Strength"** gives a clear starting point for readers new to the New Testament:

1. **Read Mark first (page 31).** It is the shortest and fastest-moving account of Jesus' life. It was written by John Mark, who probably drew on the apostle Peter's memories. The edition's overview says Mark covers "the life, ministry, miracles and words of Jesus" and stresses Jesus' servanthood. You can read it in a few sittings.
2. **Then read Acts (page 100).** Acts picks up where the Gospels end. It shows how Christianity spread, as the Holy Spirit changed ordinary people who "turned the world upside down."

If you want to keep going after that, a natural next step is **John** (page 79), which focuses on the *meaning* of Jesus' life, and then **Romans** (page 127). You can also read **a Psalm (page 235) and a Proverb (page 301)** each day. Proverbs has 31 chapters, so one a day takes you through it in a month.

**Tip:** Before you start a book, read its one-paragraph summary in the **"Overview of the New Testament Books"** in the front of your Bible. It tells you who wrote the book, roughly when, what kind of writing it is (story, letter, poetry), and what it is about. These author and date notes are the traditional views; scholars debate some of them.

## How the book says to study: three steps

"A Life of Quiet Strength" gives a simple three-step method you can use on any passage, whether that's a chapter or a few verses:

| Step | The question to ask | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Observation** | *What does the author actually say?* | Read slowly. Restate the passage in your own words, or underline a key phrase. |
| **2. Interpretation** | *What did the author mean when he wrote it?* | Ask why it was written. John 20:31 is an example: John says outright why he wrote his Gospel. Use the book summaries in the Overview, and a study Bible, commentary or Bible dictionary if you get stuck. |
| **3. Application** | *"So what?" What difference does this make in my life?* | Look for one thing to believe, change or do. 2 Timothy 3:16 says Scripture is useful to teach, correct and train us. |

**Extra tips from the book:**
- Underline key statements and mark verses that mean something to you.
- Join a Bible study group so you can share what you are learning and ask questions.

## Tony Dungy's "playbook" idea

Dungy's letter at the front calls **the Bible "God's playbook."** Football players get a playbook with everything they need to do their jobs, and they study it *before* the game, not after they fall behind. When a player struggles, coaches tell him to "get in his book." Dungy's advice is to **read the Bible and pray every day**. Prayer is like going to the head coach with questions about the plays you don't understand. He calls this "the best way to be successful in the game of life."

## Using the Verse Finder

The **Quiet Strength Verse Finder** (pages A11–A31) lists verses by life topic under three headings: the **Principles, Practices and Priorities** of a winning life. Topics include fear, anger, forgiveness, work, money and marriage. It helps most with step 3 (application). When something in your reading raises a topic, look that topic up there. One of its own entries fits a new reader: **"Study should be regular and disciplined"** (see Proverbs 2:3–5 and 2 Timothy 2:15).

## A simple first-month plan

- **Weeks 1–2:** Mark, about one chapter a day (16 chapters). Use the three steps on each chapter and write one sentence for each step.
- **Weeks 3–6:** Acts, one chapter a day (28 chapters).
- **Every day:** a short prayer before you read (for example, asking for understanding), plus one chapter of Proverbs if you want to add it.
- **Once a week:** pick one Verse Finder topic that came up in your reading and look up its verses.

Ten to fifteen minutes a day done regularly will help you more than a long session now and then.

## Two other front-matter pages worth knowing about

- **"How to Know Jesus Personally"** (pages A39–A40) sets out, from the edition's Christian perspective, what it means to begin a relationship with God. Read it whenever you're curious. There's no pressure.
- **"A Note to Readers"** explains that this is the **New Living Translation (NLT)**, which renders the original Hebrew and Greek into clear, everyday English. That makes it a good translation for a first-time reader. For close study later on, some people compare passages with a more word-for-word translation such as the ESV or NASB.
