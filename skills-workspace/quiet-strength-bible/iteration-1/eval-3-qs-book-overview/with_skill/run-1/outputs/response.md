# Hebrews, James and Galatians: from your Quiet Strength NLT overview pages

Here is what the "Overview of the New Testament Books" pages (A33–A38) in your edition say.

## Hebrews (begins on p. 190)

- **Author:** Uncertain. The overview names several possibilities: Paul, Barnabas, Apollos, Priscilla or Luke.
- **Date:** Around AD 68.
- **Genre:** Letter.
- **What it's about:** It was written to Jewish believers who were tempted to slip back into Judaism. Its message is that Jesus is superior and that he is the Messiah.

## James (begins on p. 200)

- **Author:** James, Jesus' half brother.
- **Date:** Around AD 49.
- **Genre:** Letter.
- **What it's about:** Faith with an emphasis on results. James teaches that real faith is practical and working: it shows up in how you live, not only in what you believe.

## Galatians (begins on p. 160)

- **Author:** Paul, around AD 49 (letter).
- **What it's about:** The overview calls Galatians foundational. Its theme is that Jesus' death on the cross was complete payment for our salvation, and nothing needs to be added to it.

## A note on dates and authorship

These are the edition's traditional views. Scholars debate some of them. The authorship of Hebrews has been uncertain since the early church. Dates for James and Galatians also vary: some place James earlier or later, and Galatians anywhere from the late 40s to the mid-50s AD.

## A study idea

Galatians and James pair well. Galatians stresses that salvation is by Christ's finished work alone. James stresses that genuine faith produces action. Using the "A Life of Quiet Strength" method (observation, interpretation, application), you could read the two letters back to back and ask how both truths fit together in your own life. Hebrews works well after them, to see why the overview says Jesus is presented as superior to everything that came before.
