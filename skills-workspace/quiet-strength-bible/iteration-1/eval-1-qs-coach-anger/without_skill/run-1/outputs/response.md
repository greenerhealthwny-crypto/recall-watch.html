# Anger and Leadership for a Coach: A Study Plan Using the Quiet Strength New Testament

Thank you for being honest about this. Seeing the problem and wanting to change it is where real change starts. Tony Dungy is a good guide for it. He is known as a coach who almost never yelled at his players. His view was that you can demand excellence and still treat people with respect, and that calm is a form of strength, not weakness.

**A note on the edition:** The Quiet Strength New Testament (NLT, Tyndale) pairs the New Living Translation with devotional and character material drawn from Dungy's life and coaching. I can't reliably quote its exact feature titles, page numbers or note wording from memory. Use the book's own index, topical guide or reading plan to find the notes on **anger, self-control, patience, leadership, character and integrity**, and read them next to the passages below. All the Scripture references below are standard New Testament passages, so you can find them in any edition.

---

## 1. Core Verses on Anger

| Passage | Key idea | Coaching application |
|---|---|---|
| **James 1:19–20** | "Be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to get angry. Human anger does not produce the righteousness God desires." | Your outburst won't produce the player you want. Listen first: ask "What did you see on that play?" before you correct. |
| **Ephesians 4:26–27** | "Don't sin by letting anger control you. Don't let the sun go down while you are still angry… it gives a foothold to the devil." | Feeling angry is not the sin. Being *controlled* by it is. Settle things the same day, and never let a kid go home with a bad blowup left unresolved. |
| **Ephesians 4:29–32** | Let no foul or abusive talk come out of your mouth, only what helps build others up. Get rid of bitterness, rage, anger, harsh words and slander. Be kind and forgiving. | Test every word you say at practice: does it build this player up or just let off my frustration? |
| **Colossians 3:8, 12–13** | Get rid of anger, rage and dirty language. Put on compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience. | Character is something you "put on" each day. Make it part of your pre-practice routine. |
| **Galatians 5:22–23** | The fruit of the Spirit includes patience, gentleness and **self-control**. | Self-control is fruit that grows over time, not something you force by willpower alone. Pray for it before practices and games. |
| **Matthew 5:21–22** | Jesus treats anger and contempt ("you idiot!") as serious, in the same family as murder. | Name-calling and belittling players is not "just coaching." It is a heart issue. |
| **1 Corinthians 13:4–5** | Love is patient and kind. It is not irritable and keeps no record of being wronged. | Don't hold a kid's past mistakes over him. Each practice is a fresh start. |
| **Romans 12:17–21** | Don't repay evil with evil. Overcome evil with good. | When a player disrespects you, answer with firm, calm discipline, not retaliation. |

## 2. Core Passages on Leadership

| Passage | Key idea | Coaching application |
|---|---|---|
| **Mark 10:42–45** | Gentile rulers lord it over people. "Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant." | Your authority exists for your players' good, not for your ego or your record. |
| **John 13:1–17** | Jesus washes the disciples' feet. | Servant leadership is hands-on: carry the water, stay late, know your players' lives. |
| **Ephesians 6:4** | "Do not provoke your children to anger by the way you treat them. Bring them up with the discipline and instruction that comes from the Lord." | This is written to fathers, but it fits you directly. Harsh coaching often makes teenagers angry, sullen or checked out. |
| **Colossians 3:21** | Don't aggravate your children, "or they will become discouraged." | Yelling often produces players who play scared, not players who play better. |
| **1 Timothy 3:2–3** and **Titus 1:7–8** | A leader must be self-controlled, gentle, "not quarrelsome," "not quick-tempered or violent." | In Scripture, controlling your temper is a basic qualification for leading people. |
| **2 Timothy 2:24–25** | The Lord's servant must be kind to everyone, able to teach, patient, and gently instruct those who oppose him. | Correct the kid who talks back by teaching gently, not by humiliating him. |
| **1 Peter 5:2–3** | Shepherd the flock willingly. Don't lord it over them; lead by your good example. | Your players will copy your demeanor in close games. Calm coaches build composed teams. |
| **Philippians 2:3–4** | In humility, value others above yourselves. | Ask yourself whether you are angry because the kid failed, or because *you* look bad. |
| **1 Corinthians 9:24–27** | Run to win. "I discipline my body like an athlete." | Paul uses sports language. Self-discipline starts with the coach before it reaches the team. |

## 3. Proverbs Worth Adding

The Quiet Strength edition is a New Testament, and many editions also include Psalms and Proverbs. If yours does, or if you have a full Bible on hand, add these:
- **Proverbs 15:1**: "A gentle answer deflects anger, but harsh words make tempers flare."
- **Proverbs 16:32**: Better to be patient than powerful, and to have self-control than to conquer a city.
- **Proverbs 29:11**: Fools vent their anger, but the wise quietly hold it back.
- **Proverbs 14:29**: People with understanding control their anger. A hot temper shows great foolishness.

## 4. Dungy-Style Principles to Draw Out

These themes run through Dungy's books (*Quiet Strength*, *Uncommon*, *The Mentor Leader*) and through how he coached:

1. **Calm is strength.** Dungy believed players hear you better when you don't yell. Intensity can show in preparation and high standards instead of volume.
2. **Character first, then winning.** Your job is to build young men, not only a winning record. Ask what kind of person each player is becoming under you.
3. **Mentor leadership.** Lead by investing in people, not by controlling them. Know each player's story; the kid who shuts down may have a hard home life.
4. **"Do what we do."** Dungy kept things simple and consistent. Players do best with a coach whose standards and moods are predictable.
5. **Your identity isn't the scoreboard.** A lot of coaching anger comes from tying your worth to results. Dungy often stressed that faith and family came before football. Coaching from security instead of fear lowers the temperature.
6. **Accountability without humiliation.** Discipline is firm, fair and done in private when possible. Consequences are calm and pre-set (laps, playing time) instead of improvised in rage.

## 5. A 4-Week Study Plan

- **Week 1: The heart behind anger.** James 1:19–27; Matthew 5:21–26; Mark 7:20–23. *Journal:* What triggers my outbursts? (Mistakes? Effort? Losing? Feeling disrespected?)
- **Week 2: Putting off and putting on.** Ephesians 4:17–32; Colossians 3:1–17; Galatians 5:16–26. *Practice:* Choose one "put on" trait each week, such as patience, and focus on it.
- **Week 3: The servant leader.** Mark 10:35–45; John 13:1–17; Philippians 2:1–11; 1 Peter 5:1–7. *Practice:* Do one specific act of service for your team.
- **Week 4: Leading the next generation.** Ephesians 6:4; Colossians 3:21; 1 Timothy 4:12–16; 2 Timothy 2:22–26; Titus 2:6–8. *Practice:* Have one-on-one check-ins with players.

Each week, read the matching Quiet Strength devotional or character notes on these topics. Ask: *How did Coach Dungy handle a moment like this, and what would that look like on my court?*

## 6. Practical Steps on the Court

- **Make things right.** Apologize to the team, and to any individual players you blew up at. Admitting your failure models exactly the character you want them to learn. (Matthew 5:23–24)
- **Create a pause routine.** When you feel the heat rising: deep breath, hands behind your back, count to five, *then* speak. Some coaches call a timeout for themselves.
- **Teach, don't vent.** Change "What are you DOING?!" to "Here's what I need on that rotation."
- **Use a memory verse.** Write James 1:19 on your clipboard or a card in your pocket.
- **Get an accountability partner.** Ask an assistant coach or a trusted friend to signal you when you're losing it, and to talk with you after games.
- **Pray before every practice and game.** Pray specifically for self-control and for each player by name.
- **Look under the anger.** If the anger feels out of proportion, look at what else is going on: stress, sleep, pressure at home or at work. A pastor or counselor can help with that too.

---

**Bottom line:** The New Testament doesn't ask you to stop being intense or to lower your standards. It asks you to be *self-controlled* (Galatians 5:23), to *serve* your players (Mark 10:45), and not to *exasperate* them (Ephesians 6:4). Dungy's career shows that a coach can be calm, demanding, kind and successful all at the same time. Start with James 1:19–20, own your mistakes in front of your team, and let your players see a man of quiet strength.
