# Headline Critique: "Quality Products At Great Prices - Check Us Out!"

## The verdict

This headline breaks almost every rule in Robert Bly's *The Copywriter's Handbook*. It could belong to any business that sells anything, so it gives the reader no reason to stop. Bly would call it a "blind" or generic headline: it names no product, promises no specific benefit and gives no reason to believe it.

## Critique using Bly's rules

### 1. The four jobs of a headline
Bly says a headline must (1) **get attention**, (2) **select the audience**, (3) **deliver a complete message** and (4) **draw the reader into the body copy**.

| Job | How this headline does | Why |
|-----|------------------------|-----|
| Get attention | Fails | "Quality" and "great prices" are the most overused phrases in advertising. Readers skip them. |
| Select the audience | Fails | No product, customer type or problem is named. A bride, a plumber and a gamer all get the same message. |
| Deliver a complete message | Fails | If the reader reads only this line, which most people do, they still don't know what you sell. |
| Draw the reader into the body | Weak | "Check Us Out!" is an order with no reason behind it. Nothing makes the reader curious. |

### 2. The 4 U's (Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful)
Scored 1 to 4:

| U | Score | Comment |
|---|:-----:|---------|
| Urgent | 1 | No deadline, no limited offer, no reason to act now. |
| Unique | 1 | Every competitor claims quality and good prices. Nothing here sets you apart. |
| Ultra-specific | 1 | "Products," "quality" and "great" are all abstract. There are no numbers, names or details. |
| Useful | 1 | The benefit is only implied ("you might save money") and never stated or shown. |
| **Total** | **4 / 16** | About as low as a headline can score. |

### 3. Specific rule breaks
- **Vague claims instead of specifics.** Bly keeps stressing that specific copy sells and general copy doesn't. "Great prices" proves nothing. "$29, half the big-box price" does.
- **Seller-focused, not reader-focused.** "Check **us** out" is about the business. Bly's advice, in line with Caples and Ogilvy, is to talk about the reader's self-interest ("you," "your").
- **Features and benefits are both missing.** There is no feature (what it is) and no benefit (what it does for me).
- **Two unsupported claims together.** "Quality" and "great prices" pull against each other in the reader's mind ("cheap means low quality"), and the headline offers no proof for either.
- **Weak, empty call to action.** A command headline can work, but only when the command carries a benefit ("Get...", "Stop...", "Discover..."). "Check us out" asks for effort and offers nothing.
- **Title Case and the exclamation mark.** Capitalizing every word makes it harder to read, and the "!" tries to add excitement the words don't have. Bly prefers sentence case and letting the content do the work.
- **No headline type.** It isn't a real direct, how-to, question, news, reason-why or testimonial headline. It is a slogan.

## Rewrites

The original names no product, so the rewrites below use a sample business, an **independent furniture store**, to show the method. Replace the specifics with your real product, price, proof and audience. Each version is labelled with its Bly headline type.

| # | Type | Rewrite | Why it's better |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Direct** | **Solid-Wood Dining Sets From $499: Half What the Big Stores Charge** | Names the product, gives a real price and a concrete comparison that backs up "great prices." |
| 2 | **How-to** | **How to Furnish Your Whole Living Room for Under $1,500, Without Particleboard** | A useful promise, an exact number, and it answers the "cheap means junk" worry. |
| 3 | **Question** | **Why Pay $2,000 for a Sofa That Sags in Two Years?** | Targets the reader's pain and draws them into the body copy to find the answer. |
| 4 | **Reason-why** | **5 Reasons Our 10-Year-Warranty Sofas Cost Less Than Mall Brands** | A specific number promises a list, and the warranty is proof of "quality." |
| 5 | **News** | **New: Factory-Direct Oak Furniture Now in [City], Save 40% Through Sunday** | News headlines get attention. Adds a location, a specific saving and a deadline. |
| 6 | **Testimonial** | **"We furnished our entire home for less than one designer sofa." — Maria T., [City]** | Social proof in the customer's own words. *(Use only a real customer quote.)* |
| 7 | **Command** | **Stop Overpaying for Furniture: See This Week's $299 Solid-Oak Specials** | Keeps the call to action but attaches a clear benefit and a specific offer. |

### Recommended rewrite
**"Solid-Wood Dining Sets From $499: Half What the Big Stores Charge"**

4 U's check: Urgent 2, Unique 3, Ultra-specific 4, Useful 4 = **13 / 16**, up from 4. To push urgency higher, add a deadline: *"...Half What the Big Stores Charge, This Week Only."*

## Template to apply to your own business

> **[Specific product] for [specific price or result]: [proof or comparison][, deadline if real]**

Before you write the headline, answer these four questions (Bly's starting point is knowing the product and the buyer):
1. What exactly do you sell, and who buys it?
2. What is the single most important benefit to that buyer?
3. What specific fact proves "quality"? (Material, warranty, years in business, ratings.)
4. What specific number proves "great prices"? (A dollar figure, a percentage saved, a competitor comparison.)

Put those answers into the headline in place of "quality" and "great."
