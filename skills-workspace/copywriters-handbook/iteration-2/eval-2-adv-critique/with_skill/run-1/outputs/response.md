# Critique: "Quality Products At Great Prices - Check Us Out!"

## Verdict

This headline fails almost every test in Bly's *Copywriter's Handbook*. It could sit on any store in the world, so it sells nothing. It's built from two of the most worn-out generalities in advertising ("quality" and "great prices") plus a vague command. It doesn't say who it's for, what is sold, or why anyone should act.

---

## 1. The four functions of a headline

| Function (Bly) | Does it do it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Get attention** | No | "Quality" and "great prices" are claims every competitor makes. Readers have learned to skip past them. Nothing new, specific or surprising. |
| **Select the audience** | No | No reader is named or implied. A plumber, a bride and a teenager would all read it the same way, so it calls to none of them. |
| **Deliver a complete message** | No | We don't learn what the products are, how good, or how cheap. The only message is "we sell stuff." |
| **Draw the reader into the body copy** | Weakly | "Check us out!" asks for a click but gives no reason or curiosity to act on it. |

**Score: 0 of 4 functions fully met.**

## 2. The 4 U's (Masterson's formula, used by Bly)

Score 1 to 4. Bly's rule: a headline should score 3 or 4 on at least three of the four, or rewrite it.

| U | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Urgent** | 1 | No reason to act now: no deadline, no limited offer, no time element. "Check us out!" is a command with no clock on it. |
| **Unique** | 1 | Every retailer claims quality and good prices. Nothing sets this business apart. |
| **Ultra-specific** | 1 | No product, number, percentage, price, brand, place or detail. This is its worst failing. |
| **Useful** | 1 to 2 | "Great prices" hints at saving money, but it's too vague to count as a real benefit. |

**Total: about 4 to 5 out of 16. Zero U's at 3 or higher. Clear fail; rewrite.**

## 3. Other Bly rules it breaks

- **Generalities instead of specifics.** Bly's clarity tip "Be specific" says facts, figures and details sell and generalities don't. "Quality" and "great" are the two words he'd cut first. *Show* the quality (material, warranty, rating) and *state* the price or discount.
- **No selling proposition, no USP.** Bly's headline method is to ask who the customer is, what the key features are, and why they'll buy, then put the answer to the last question in the headline. There's no answer here. Rosser Reeves's USP test asks for a specific proposition the competition doesn't offer. "Quality at great prices" is the opposite, since everyone claims it.
- **Features vs. benefits.** There are no features (what *is* the quality?) and no concrete benefit (what does the buyer *get*: lasts 10 years, costs 30% less, ships tomorrow?).
- **Weak command headline.** Command headlines work in Bly's list when the command carries the benefit or a reason. "Check us out" has neither. It's also the language of a casual flyer, not a selling proposition.
- **Doesn't name the offer.** Bly's technique list includes "highlight your offer," "state the price" and "stress savings, discounts or value." This headline mentions value but gives no offer or price.
- **Not you-oriented.** The focus is "us" ("Check *us* out"). Bly's first clarity tip is to put the reader first.
- **Style.** All-Caps-On-Every-Word, a dash and an exclamation mark make it look like an ad and read as hype. Exclamation marks don't add urgency; a real reason to act does. *(Bly's e-mail chapter advises using all-caps sparingly. Title case on a headline is normal; the exclamation mark is the bigger problem.)*

## 4. What would fix it

I don't know what this business sells, its real prices, or its offer. A good rewrite **needs those facts**, and inventing them would break the other rule: claims must be true and substantiated (FTC truth-in-advertising). So below are **templates with [placeholders]**, each based on a named Bly headline type, and then one fully worked **illustrative** example clearly marked as invented.

### Rewrites (fill the brackets with real facts)

1. **Direct (state the selling proposition plainly, state the price):**
   > **[Product category] from [real price], with a [X]-Year Warranty**
   *Specific, useful; add a time element for urgency.*

2. **Reason-why (numbers, reasons-why):**
   > **[5] Reasons [Target customer] Buy Their [Product] From [Store], Not [Big-box competitor]**
   *Selects the audience, sets up the body copy, unique.*

3. **News + time element (give news, add a time element, stress savings):**
   > **This Week Only: [Real %] Off [Specific product line], Ends [Date]**
   *Urgent and specific. Use it only if the sale and deadline are real.*

4. **How-to (promise useful information, help the reader achieve a goal):**
   > **How to Get [Specific benefit, e.g., "a Kitchen That Looks Custom"] for Under $[real figure]**
   *Useful and specific. Pulls the reader into the copy.*

5. **Command, done right (command plus benefit):**
   > **Compare Our [Product] Side by Side with [Brand], and Keep the Difference: [Real $] on Average**
   *Keeps a call to action but gives the reason.*

6. **Testimonial (the customer sells):**
   > **"[Real customer quote about quality or savings]" – [Name, town]**
   *Use real, permitted testimonials only.*

### Illustrative example (invented business, for demonstration only)

Suppose the business sells refurbished office chairs, with a real 5-year warranty and prices about 60% under new:

> **Refurbished Designer Mesh Office Chairs from $389, Backed by a 5-Year Warranty. This Month's Stock Ships in 48 Hours**

4 U's: Urgent 3 (this month's stock, 48 hours) · Unique 3 (refurbished designer chairs plus a long warranty) · Ultra-specific 4 (price, warranty, ship time) · Useful 4 (saves money and the warranty lowers risk). **Passes: 4 of 4 at 3 or higher.** Every number here is invented for the example. Swap in your own verified facts.

## 5. Process going forward (Bly's workflow)

1. Answer: Who is the customer? What are the key features? Why will they buy?
2. List features, then translate each into a benefit ("so what?").
3. Find the USP, or the under-publicized benefit competitors don't mention.
4. Write 5 to 10 headlines using the techniques above. Score each on the 4 U's. Keep those with three or more U's at 3 or higher.
5. Make the body copy follow through: attention, show a need, satisfy it, prove it, ask for specific action (not just "check us out," but "Call [number] / Shop the sale at [URL] before [date]").
