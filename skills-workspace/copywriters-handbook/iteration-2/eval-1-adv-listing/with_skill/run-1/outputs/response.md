# Facebook Marketplace Listing: Used Solid-Oak Dining Table ($180)

You gave me three facts: **used**, **solid oak**, **dining table**, **$180**. Bly's main rule for selling copy is to be specific, because facts and details sell and generalities don't. That rule only works if the details are true. So I have **not made up** dimensions, seating, age, brand, condition or pickup terms. Wherever the listing needs one, I put a **[bracketed placeholder]** for you to fill in from the real table. Delete any line you can't back up. Truthful, substantiated claims are the baseline (the FTC standard for advertising applies even to a classified listing).

Context note: the *Handbook* (3rd ed., 2005) predates Facebook Marketplace. The principles still apply (headline, benefits, specifics, a clear call to action), but the platform tactics are my adaptation, not Bly's.

---

## Step 1: Bly's three headline questions

1. **Who is my customer?** Local buyers furnishing a home on a budget: first apartments, young families, people who want real wood but not new-furniture prices. Some resellers or refinishers too.
2. **What are the important features?** Solid oak (not veneer or particleboard), used, $180, [size / seats], [condition], [extras such as leaves or chairs].
3. **Why will they buy (the key selling proposition)?** *A real solid-wood table that lasts, for a fraction of what new solid oak costs.* That proposition goes in the headline.

**BFD check** (Masterson's Beliefs, Feelings, Desires, as Bly reports it):
- *Beliefs:* "Cheap furniture is particleboard that falls apart." "Used furniture might be wobbly or damaged."
- *Feelings:* Wary of bad listings and wasted trips. Frustrated by new-furniture prices.
- *Desires:* A sturdy table that looks good and holds up to family life, bought quickly and cheaply.

**Copy motivators in play** (from Bly's list of 22): save money, be secure (it won't fall apart), comfortable, happy (family dinners), convenience (local pickup), greed (a bargain).

---

## Step 2: Features to benefits

Ask "so what?" of each feature until it becomes a reason to buy.

| Feature | So what? Benefit to the buyer |
|---|---|
| Solid oak (not veneer or particleboard) | It's sturdy and holds screws, so it won't sag, wobble or peel like flat-pack furniture. It can last for decades. |
| Solid wood throughout | If it gets scratched, you can sand and refinish it instead of throwing it away. Veneer can't take that. |
| Oak's grain and colour | A warm, classic look that fits farmhouse, traditional or modern rooms. |
| $180, used | You get real hardwood for much less than a new one would cost. *(Only quote a "new costs $X" comparison if you check a real retail price for a similar table.)* |
| Heavy, dense hardwood | It stays put. It doesn't slide or rock when kids lean on it or you're carving a roast. |
| [Seats X / measures L x W x H] | You know before you drive over whether it fits your room and your family. Fewer wasted trips. |
| [Has leaf / extends to X] | Room for holiday guests without owning a second table. |
| [Condition: e.g., light surface wear, no structural damage] | No surprises at pickup. Honest condition notes build trust (Bly's "prove it" step). |
| Local pickup | Take it home today. No shipping wait, no delivery fee. |

**USP:** You can't claim a feature no other seller has, so use Reeves's fallback as Bly gives it: **stress the benefit most sellers don't mention.** Most used-table listings just say "solid wood." Few explain that **solid oak can be refinished, so you can buy it now and make it look new later.** That is the under-publicized benefit to lead with.

## Step 3: Three headlines, typed and scored on the 4 U's

Scale: 1 (weak) to 4 (strong). Bly's test (borrowed from Michael Masterson): a headline should score **3 or 4 on at least three of the four U's**, or you rewrite it.

### Headline A: Direct (techniques: state the selling proposition plainly, state the price, be specific)
> **Solid Oak Dining Table, Seats [6], $180**

| Urgent | Unique | Ultra-specific | Useful | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 4 | 3 | No (2 of 4) |

The clearest headline, and it scans well in a Marketplace feed, where price and item show first. Urgency is weak. Two honest ways to raise it: add "Pickup This Weekend," or use "First Reasonable Offer" only if that's really your policy. Revised:
> **Solid Oak Dining Table, Seats [6], $180, Pickup This Weekend in [Neighbourhood]**

Revised score: U3 / Un2 / Sp4 / Us3, which passes (3 of 4). Note that the urgency only holds if you really can do weekend pickup.

### Headline B: Reason-why (techniques: use numbers, reasons-why, stress savings and value)
> **3 Reasons This $180 Solid Oak Table Beats Anything Flat-Pack**

| Urgent | Unique | Ultra-specific | Useful | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | Yes (3 of 4) |

The "3 reasons" go straight into the body copy (sturdy, refinishable, price). It names the buyer's fear (particleboard) as the comparison, which follows Bly's "address the reader's concern."

### Headline C: How-to / useful information (techniques: promise useful information, help the reader achieve a goal, address the reader's concern)
> **How to Get a Real Hardwood Dining Table for $180 (One You Can Refinish Instead of Replace)**

| Urgent | Unique | Ultra-specific | Useful | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 3 | 4 | Yes (3 of 4) |

This one leads with the USP (refinishable). It's long for a Marketplace title field, so use it as the **first line of the description**, with Headline A as the title.

**What I avoided, per Bly:** puns ("Oak-ay Deal!", "Table This Offer"), because wordplay doesn't sell. Blind headlines that make no sense alone. Negatives like "No Damage!" (say what it *is*: "Sturdy, Solid Oak"). Fake scarcity or invented "retail value" figures.

**Recommendation:** Title = Headline A (revised). First line of description = Headline C or B.

## Step 4: The listing (written in Bly's motivating sequence)

**Title:**
Solid Oak Dining Table, Seats [6], $180, Pickup This Weekend in [Neighbourhood]

**Description:**

> **How to get a real hardwood dining table for $180, one you can refinish instead of replace.**
>
> *(1. Attention: lead with the strongest benefit.)*
>
> Tired of tables that wobble, peel or sag after a year? Most budget furniture is particleboard with a thin veneer. Once it's scratched or chipped, it's done.
>
> *(2. Show a need.)*
>
> This is **solid oak**, all the way through. That means:
> - **It's built to last.** Heavy and stable. It won't rock when the kids lean on it.
> - **It can look new again.** Scratches sand out. Refinish it whenever you like instead of buying another table.
> - **It fits any style.** Warm oak grain works in farmhouse, traditional or modern rooms.
>
> *(3. Satisfy the need and position the table as the answer.)*
>
> **The details** (so you know before you drive over):
> - Size: [L x W x H]. Seats [X] [extends to Y with leaf, if true]
> - Condition: [honest description, e.g., "light surface scratches on top, legs and joints solid, no wobble"]
> - [Chairs included / not included]
> - [Smoke-free / pet-free home, only if true]
> - Photos show [top, legs, underside, any wear up close]
>
> *(4. Prove it: specifics and honest photos of flaws are the proof. Showing wear up close builds trust.)*
>
> **$180.** Pickup in [neighbourhood / cross streets]. It's heavy, so bring a helper and a vehicle that fits [L] inches. [Cash / e-transfer / other] at pickup.
>
> **Message me with a day and time that works and it's yours.** [Available this weekend.]
>
> *(5. Ask for action: tell the reader exactly what to do, and make it easy.)*

## Step 5: Clarity check (Bly's 11 tips)

- **You-orientation:** "you," "your room," "your family" throughout. ✔
- **Organized selling points:** follows the motivating sequence. ✔
- **Short sections, short sentences, simple words, no jargon:** bullets, plain words. ("Veneer" is the one technical word, and it's explained by contrast.) ✔
- **Specific:** depends on you filling every placeholder with real numbers. This is the most important step. ✔ once filled
- **Straight to the point, conversational:** ✔

## Before you post

1. Replace every [placeholder] with verified facts, or delete the line.
2. Don't add a "retails for $___" claim unless you've checked a real comparable price.
3. Only use urgency ("this weekend") you will honour.
4. Photograph flaws. Bly's "prove it" step and the buyer's trust both depend on it.
5. Optional: test two titles (A vs. B) by reposting or editing after a few days and see which gets more messages. Bly's advice to keep and test headlines applies here.
