# Facebook Marketplace Listing: Used Solid-Oak Dining Table ($180)

This follows Robert Bly's approach in *The Copywriter's Handbook*: a headline that does the selling, features turned into benefits, the motivating sequence (attention, need, satisfaction, proof, action), and a specific call to action.

> Placeholders in [brackets] are details only you know (size, seats, location, flaws). Fill them in honestly. Bly says specific, true details sell, and made-up ones lose the buyer's trust once they see the table.

## 5. How this follows Bly's method

- **Attention:** the headline names the product, the key difference ("not veneer") and the price.
- **Need:** the opening question names a real annoyance (wobbly, peeling furniture).
- **Satisfaction:** features are paired with benefits, so the buyer pictures using the table.
- **Proof:** stated dimensions, an honest condition note with a photo reference, and a reason for the low price. Admitting a small flaw makes the rest of the listing more believable.
- **Action:** one simple next step ("Message me...") and a reason to act now ("first person with a pickup time," "this weekend").

**Photo tips (these do much of the selling on Marketplace):** shoot in daylight, take one wide shot with chairs or a place setting so buyers can picture it at home, one close-up of the oak grain, one of the underside or joinery to prove it's solid wood, and one honest photo of the worst flaw.
