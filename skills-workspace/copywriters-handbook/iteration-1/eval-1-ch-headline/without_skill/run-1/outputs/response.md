# Headline Critique and Rewrite (Bob Bly, *The Copywriter's Handbook*)

**Original:** "Quality Bookkeeping Solutions For Your Business"

## 1. Critique against Bly's four headline functions

In *The Copywriter's Handbook*, Bly says a headline has four jobs: **get attention, select the audience, deliver a complete message, and draw the reader into the body copy.** Here is how the original does on each one.

| Function | How the original does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Gets attention** | Weak | Nothing in it is new or specific, and it makes no promise. "Quality" and "solutions" are the kind of filler words Bly warns against, because every competitor claims them. |
| **Selects the audience** | Weak | "Your business" covers every business, so it speaks to nobody in particular. A busy owner of a 10-person contracting firm won't recognize themselves in it. |
| **Delivers a complete message** | Weak | It names a category (bookkeeping) but gives no benefit. Readers don't buy bookkeeping. They buy saved time, fewer tax worries, cash-flow clarity, and no scramble at audit time. |
| **Draws the reader into the copy** | Weak | It raises no question and offers no curiosity, news or specific reward for reading on. |

Other problems Bly would point out:

- **It names features or a category, not benefits.** Bly's basic rule is to sell what the product *does for the reader*.
- **It isn't specific.** Bly keeps repeating that specifics (numbers, time frames, dollar figures, named audiences) are more believable and more persuasive than general claims.
- **It's corporate "brag-and-boast" copy.** It's about the seller, not the reader's problem.
- **"Solutions" is jargon.** Bly prefers plain, conversational language.
- **Title Case For Every Word** reads like a label, not a message. It's a minor point, but it adds to the impersonal tone.

## 2. The method: how to rebuild it

Bly's process, applied step by step:

1. **List features, then turn them into benefits.** Monthly reconciliation turns into "always know your real cash position." Tax-ready books turn into "no April panic." Outsourcing turns into "get your evenings back."
2. **Pick the strongest benefit for a specific audience.** For small-business owners, the strongest pain is usually time lost to admin and fear of tax or IRS problems.
3. **Make it specific.** Add numbers, time frames, prices or guarantees wherever you can honestly back them up.
4. **Choose a proven headline type.** Bly catalogs proven formats: direct, indirect, news, how-to, question, command, reason-why and testimonial. Each one below is labeled with its type.
5. **Check it against the four functions** and test it by asking whether a competitor could run the exact same headline. If they could, it isn't specific enough.
6. **Write several and test them.** Bly advises writing many headlines and then A/B testing the best few. The headline matters most because far more people read it than read the body copy.

## 3. Rewrites (by Bly headline type)

**Direct benefit:**
> Get Your Books Done Every Month, Without Spending Another Weekend on Receipts

**How-to:**
> How Small-Business Owners Are Getting Back 10 Hours a Month by Handing Off Their Bookkeeping

**Question (it must be one the reader cares about and wants answered):**
> Do You Know Exactly How Much Cash Your Business Has Right Now?

**Command:**
> Stop Dreading Tax Season: Get Clean, CPA-Ready Books for a Flat $299 a Month

**Reason-why:**
> 7 Reasons Contractors Switch to [Firm Name] for Their Bookkeeping

**News:**
> New: Monthly Bookkeeping for Restaurants, With Your Financial Statements on Your Desk by the 5th

**Testimonial:**
> "I Used to Spend Every Sunday on QuickBooks. Now I Spend It With My Kids."

**Guarantee/offer:**
> Tax-Ready Books in 30 Days, or Your First Month Is Free

## 4. Recommended pick

> **"Stop Dreading Tax Season: Get Clean, CPA-Ready Books for a Flat $299 a Month"**

Here is how it scores on the four functions:

- **Attention:** "Stop dreading tax season" names a pain the reader already feels.
- **Selects the audience:** Business owners who handle their own books, or handle them badly, know at once that it's about them.
- **Complete message:** It states the benefit (CPA-ready books), the outcome (less stress at tax time) and the price.
- **Draws the reader in:** A flat, known price raises the question "what's included?", which the body copy then answers.

To narrow the audience further, add a niche, for example "...for Dental Practices." Bly notes that the more specific the audience, the stronger the response, even though fewer people are reached.

**Caveat:** Use only numbers, prices and guarantees the business can honestly deliver. The figures above are placeholders. Bly also insists that specifics must be truthful and provable.
