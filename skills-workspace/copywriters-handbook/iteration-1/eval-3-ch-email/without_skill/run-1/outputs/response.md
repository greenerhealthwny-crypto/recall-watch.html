# Structuring a Promotional E-mail (per Bob Bly, *The Copywriter's Handbook*, 3rd ed.)

*Note: this answer comes from memory of Bly's guidance on online and e-mail copy in the 3rd edition (2005), not from the text in front of me. Treat the specific numbers as Bly's rules of thumb, and check the book if you need exact wording.*

Bly treats a promotional e-mail as a short direct-mail letter that has to work inside an inbox. Its main job is usually to **get the click**, which takes the reader to a landing page that does the rest of the selling. Build it top to bottom like this.

## 1. "From" line
- Say plainly who the message is from: your name, your company, or a name the reader knows.
- The reader decides whether to open, or delete it as spam, based on whether they recognize the sender. Use the same sender name every time so readers come to trust it.

## 2. Subject line (it works like a headline or a teaser on an envelope)
- Make it specific and give the reader a reason to open: a benefit, news, curiosity, or the offer itself.
- Keep it short so it isn't cut off in the inbox.
- Avoid hype and words that spam filters catch, such as all caps, lots of exclamation points, and "FREE!!!" Aim for something that sounds like a note from a colleague, not an ad.

## 3. Opening paragraph (the lead)
- Treat the first sentence or two as a second headline. Many readers see only the preview pane, so the strongest benefit or the offer goes first.
- Get to the point at once and skip the warm-up. A short, punchy lead works best.
- **Put the first response link high up**, in or right after the first paragraph, so readers who are ready to act can do so.

## 4. Body copy
- Use the usual direct-response sequence: get attention, show the need or problem, present the product as the answer, prove it (facts, specifics, testimonials), then ask for action.
- Write the way you talk, one person to another. Use "you," keep it friendly, and leave out the corporate puffery.
- **Keep it short and easy to scan:** short paragraphs of 1–3 sentences, short sentences, and subheads or bullets for features and benefits.
- Bly also advises keeping lines to roughly 55–65 characters with hard returns so the text doesn't wrap badly in different e-mail programs.
- If the product needs long copy, keep the e-mail brief and send readers to a landing page or microsite for the full sales story.

## 5. The offer and call to action
- Make a clear offer, ideally free or low-risk: a free report, trial, sample, consultation, or a discount with a deadline.
- Tell the reader exactly what to do ("Click here to download your free guide").
- **Repeat the link several times:** near the top, in the middle, and at the close. Many readers never reach the bottom.
- Give a reason to act now, such as a deadline, limited quantity, or a bonus for fast responders.

## 6. Close and P.S.
- Restate the main benefit and the offer, then give the link once more.
- A P.S. gets read often, as it does in letters. Use it to restate the offer, the deadline, or a guarantee.

## 7. Signature and housekeeping
- Sign as a real person, with a name, title, and company.
- Include contact details and a clear, easy **unsubscribe/opt-out** link. Bly stresses permission-based ("opt-in") e-mail only: mail people who asked for it and honor removal requests.
- Personalizing with the recipient's name and referring to how they joined your list ("You're receiving this because you subscribed to...") helps both credibility and deliverability.

## Quick skeleton

```
From:    [Recognizable sender name]
Subject: [Specific benefit / news / offer, short, no spammy hype]

[Lead: 1–2 sentences stating the key benefit or offer]
[Link: Click here to ...]

[Body: problem -> solution -> proof, short paragraphs, bullets]

[Offer + reason to act now]
[Link: Click here to ...]

[Close: restate benefit, final link]

[Signature: real name, title, company]
P.S. [Restate offer / deadline / guarantee]

[Unsubscribe link + why they're receiving this]
```

**Bottom line:** a recognizable sender, a subject line that earns the open, the payoff in the first lines, short and conversational copy, one clear offer with the link repeated, and respect for permission and opt-out.
