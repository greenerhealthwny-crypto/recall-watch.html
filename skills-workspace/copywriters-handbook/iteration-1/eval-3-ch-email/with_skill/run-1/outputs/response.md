# Structuring a promotional e-mail (Bly, *The Copywriter's Handbook*, 3rd ed., Ch. 12)

Bly's Chapter 12, "E-mail marketing," gives a clear order for a promotional e-mail. Here it is from top to bottom, with the selling principles from the rest of the book that support each part.

## The structure, top to bottom

### 1. FROM line
Write it with care. On a **house list** (your own subscribers), the FROM line should name you or your company, someone the reader knows. On a **rented list**, it usually names the list owner, because the reader has a relationship with them, not with you.

### 2. SUBJECT line
This is the e-mail's headline, so it should do what Bly says every headline does: get attention, pick out the right reader, and give them a reason to open. Two points:
- Be careful with **"free"** and similar trigger words. Spam filters catch them.
- Test it against the **4 U's** (from Michael Masterson): urgent, unique, ultra-specific, useful. Score each from 1 to 4. If it doesn't get a 3 or 4 on at least three of them, rewrite it.

### 3. Opening: a killer headline or benefit
Open the body with your strongest benefit or a headline. Don't warm up first. This is step 1 of Bly's motivating sequence (**get attention**), and he says to do it with the main benefit, not with a clever line or a pun.

### 4. First paragraph: a mini-version of the whole e-mail
This is the most typically "Bly" rule for e-mail. Your first paragraph should **sum up the whole message, including the offer and a response link**. Many readers never scroll down. The ones who stop here should still know what you're offering and be able to click to get it.

### 5. Expanded copy: features, benefits, proof
After that, give the full argument for readers who keep going. Follow the rest of the motivating sequence:
- **Show a need.** Name the problem the reader has.
- **Satisfy the need.** Present your product as the answer, and turn each feature into a benefit (ask "so what?" after every feature).
- **Prove it.** Use testimonials, results, track record and guarantees.

### 6. Close: repeat the offer and the response link
Restate the offer and **ask for action** (step 5 of the sequence). Tell the reader exactly what to do and make it easy. Put the response link here again. Bly notes that **several response links** in one e-mail help, so readers can click wherever they decide.

### 7. Opt-out statement
Include one at the bottom. (Today the law also requires it. In the U.S., CAN-SPAM requires a working unsubscribe link and your physical postal address.)

## Format and tone

- **Layout:** use wide margins and go easy on ALL CAPS.
- **Length:** short is generally better. Get the key points across fast. Bly also allows long-copy e-mails when the sale needs them (see below).
- **Tone:** be helpful, informative and educational, not hard-sell. His phrase is that "information is the gold in cyberspace."

## Pick the format for the job

Bly sorts online formats into **long-copy, teaser and lead-generation e-mails**, which can be **short or long** and point to either a **short landing page** or a **microsite** (a long-copy landing page):
- A **teaser / short e-mail** gets the click and lets a landing page or microsite do the selling. This is like his direct-mail rule, "the letter sells, the brochure tells," except here the page does most of the work.
- A **long-copy e-mail** carries the full sales argument itself and links to a short order or transaction page.

Pick the pairing before you write. The e-mail's job (get the click, or make the sale) decides how much copy it needs.

## A skeleton you can fill in

```
FROM:     [You / your company — or the list owner if rented]
SUBJECT:  [Specific, useful, urgent benefit — no spammy "FREE!!!"]

[Headline: your single strongest benefit]

[Paragraph 1: The whole pitch in 2–3 sentences — the benefit, the offer,
 and a link: "Click here to ...". A reader who stops here can still act.]

[Need: the problem the reader recognizes]
[Solution: how the product solves it — features turned into benefits]
[Proof: testimonial, result, guarantee]

[Close: restate the offer + deadline/reason to act now + link again]

[P.S. optional — one more benefit or the deadline, plus link]

[Opt-out / unsubscribe statement, sender address]
```

(The P.S. is a common direct-mail habit, not one of Bly's Chapter 12 rules.)

## Caveats

- The 3rd edition came out in **2005**. The structure still holds, but its technical advice predates smartphones and today's inbox filtering. Bly's deliverability points (valid reverse DNS, a limited send rate, valid HTML, watching bounces by ISP) still apply. Today also check how the e-mail looks on mobile, keep the key message and the first link above the fold on a phone, and follow current sender-authentication rules (SPF, DKIM, DMARC).
- Only make claims you can back up. Advertising rules and CAN-SPAM apply to e-mail.
