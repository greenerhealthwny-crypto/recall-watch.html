# Botrytis in the heads and wet lower fruits: what to change

Your symptoms point to **cold, wet surfaces**: the plant heads at night and the lower fruits in the morning. The high pipe temperature and the screen gap are both well meant. In this setup, though, they probably cause the problem instead of fixing it.

## What is probably happening

1. **Cold air dropping through the 5% gap onto the heads.** With the screen 95% closed, the air above it is cold (often 5–10 °C colder than the crop). A gap that size lets a steady stream of cold, heavy air fall straight onto the heads under the gap. The heads cool below the dew point of the warm, humid air around them. Moisture condenses on them, and Botrytis follows. In the heads you typically see it in lines or patches that match where the gap is.
2. **A 45 °C minimum pipe with a nearly closed screen sets up a strong convection loop.** Warm air rises fast from the pipes at the bottom, picks up moisture, meets the cold gap or screen, cools, and comes back down. You burn a lot of energy, but the heads (top) and the lower fruits (bottom) are the coldest parts of the crop, while the air in between is warm. Air RH of about 90% looks fine on paper, but the surfaces at the top and bottom are at or below the dew point.
3. **Morning condensation on the fruit.** Fruits have a lot of thermal mass. When the air warms (setpoint rise, screen opening, sunrise) faster than the fruit can follow, the fruit surface stays below the dew point of the air and gets wet. Lower fruits in the shade of the canopy warm up last.
4. **90% RH at night is simply too high in March.** That is a humidity deficit of only about 1.5–2 g/m³, or a VPD around 0.2 kPa. The crop barely transpires, the heads get soft, and any cold spot tips into condensation.

## What to change

### Night screen and gap
- **Do not run a fixed 5% gap all night.** Close the screen fully, or nearly so, and open the gap **in small steps (for example 1–2%, maximum about 3%)**, controlled on humidity deficit, not on a fixed setting.
- Open the gap **slowly** (ramp over 15–30 minutes) and keep the heating responding, so you don't create a cold-air waterfall.
- If you have a stacked or double screen, or gap control per section, use it so the gap opens above the paths and not directly above the heads.
- Better still: **remove moisture with windows above the closed screen** (the screen is permeable) or with **forced ventilation / an air treatment unit** (outside air blown in through hoses under the crop) if you have one. That takes out moisture without dropping cold air on the heads.

### Heating
- **Lower the minimum pipe from 45 °C to around 35–40 °C**, and use the pipe actively when you need moisture removal (for example together with a gap opening), instead of a high fixed minimum all night.
- If you have a **growing pipe (heating pipe in the head area)**, use it at night (for example 35–45 °C). This is the most direct way to keep the heads a few tenths of a degree above air temperature, which keeps them dry and transpiring. A warm head is a dry head.
- Aim for a **small vertical temperature difference** (< 1–1.5 °C between top and bottom of the crop). Measure it with a second sensor box at head height, or with an IR or leaf-temperature sensor.

### Humidity target
- Steer on **humidity deficit / VPD**, not RH. At night aim for at least **2.5–3 g/m³ HD (about 0.3–0.4 kPa VPD, roughly 83–87% RH)**. Above about 92% RH, take action.
- Use the extra air movement from a slightly open screen or vents to keep air moving. Vertical fans (if installed) help a lot in breaking stratification at a low energy cost.

### Morning transition (against wet fruit)
- **Pre-heat slowly**: start the temperature rise 1.5–2 hours before sunrise, at **no more than about 1 °C per hour**, so fruit temperature can follow air temperature.
- Raise the pipe a little **before** you raise the air setpoint, so the crop warms by radiation from below and from the growing pipe.
- **Open the screen in steps** in the morning (for example 2–5% steps with pauses), and don't open it all at once at sunrise. A sudden opening brings in cold air and makes the dew point jump around the cold fruit.
- Keep an eye on fruit temperature compared with the air dew point. The fruit surface should stay at least about 1 °C above the dew point.

### Water and plant management
- **Avoid high root pressure at night**: stop irrigating earlier in the afternoon (1–3 h before sunset, depending on weather) and don't start too early in the morning. Excess water in the plant at night gives guttation drops and soft heads, which Botrytis loves.
- Watch the EC. A slightly higher drip/slab EC in this period gives firmer, less susceptible tissue.

### Hygiene (fight what is already there)
- Remove infected heads, stems and leaves daily, bag them and take them out of the greenhouse. Spores spread fast in humid air.
- **Deleaf and harvest in the morning to midday**, with clean, flat cuts close to the stem, so wounds dry out before night. Avoid making wounds late in the day.
- Treat stem lesions early with a registered paste or product, and consider preventive biologicals (for example *Bacillus* or *Trichoderma*-based products) in line with your crop advisor and local registrations.

## In short

| Now | Change to |
|---|---|
| Screen 95% closed + fixed 5% gap all night | Screen (almost) closed, gap 1–3% opened in small steps on humidity deficit; remove moisture through vents above the screen or forced ventilation |
| Fixed 45 °C minimum pipe | Minimum pipe about 35–40 °C, used actively; growing pipe on to keep heads warm |
| Night RH about 90% | HD at least 2.5–3 g/m³ (about 83–87% RH) |
| Fast morning warm-up / screen opening | Pre-dawn rise of about 1 °C/h, screen opened in steps |
| Late irrigation | Stop earlier in the afternoon to limit root pressure |

The core idea: **don't just make the air warm and the RH acceptable. Keep the plant surfaces (heads and fruits) warmer than the dew point, with as little cold air falling on the crop as possible.** Measure the temperature at head height and at the lower trusses for a week after the changes, and fine-tune from there.
