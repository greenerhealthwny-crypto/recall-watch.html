# Noon wilting in cucumbers on sunny days

**Short answer:** Don't use whitewash yet. A movable shade screen is a reasonable short-term tool for the hottest hours. But your symptoms point to a water-balance problem: the plant loses water faster than the roots can supply it. Shading only hides that by cutting light, and light is what drives your yield. Fix the climate strategy and the root-zone supply first. Use the screen only when the sun is at its strongest.

## What is happening

- **Wilting at midday** means transpiration demand is greater than water uptake and transport. The plant protects itself by closing its stomata.
- **Leaves that feel warm** confirm this. A leaf that is transpiring freely is usually 1 to 3 °C *cooler* than the air. A leaf at or above air temperature has partly closed its stomata and has lost its evaporative cooling.
- **RH around 50%** at roughly 26 to 28 °C gives a vapour pressure deficit (VPD) of about 1.7 to 1.9 kPa. Cucumbers do best at about 0.8 to 1.2 kPa. The air is pulling water out of the leaves too fast.
- **Opening the vents wide at 24 °C** causes most of this. Sunny summer air outside is dry, so venting hard exhausts your humidity, raises VPD sharply, and throws away the CO2 you are paying for. High CO2 also narrows the stomata a little. If you dose CO2 while venting hard, you get very little CO2 benefit and lower humidity.

## What to do, in order of priority

### 1. Change how you ventilate
- Raise the venting setpoint. With CO2 enrichment, cucumbers handle 26 to 28 °C during the day in high light. A 24 °C vent temperature is a low-light or no-CO2 setting.
- Open the vents **gradually and in proportion to need** instead of wide open at once. Limit the opening rate per minute, and favour the leeward side.
- Add a **humidity-based limit** on vent opening, or use a VPD target of about 1.0 to 1.2 kPa, so the vents don't dry out the house.
- Once the vents are well open, lower the CO2 setpoint, for example to about 400 to 500 ppm. Keep high dosing for periods when the vents are nearly closed.

### 2. Add humidity at midday
- If you have high-pressure fogging or misting, use it to hold RH at about 70 to 80% (VPD below about 1.2 kPa) from late morning to mid-afternoon. Fog also cools the air by evaporation, so you can keep the vents more closed.
- Without fog, wetting the paths or floor helps a little. Overhead sprinkling helps a bit more, but watch for disease.

### 3. Make sure the roots can keep up
- **Irrigation timing:** start watering early enough that the substrate is fully rewetted before light builds. Then irrigate according to accumulated radiation, with frequent small doses that keep pace with the sun. Aim for some drain by late morning, about 20 to 30% over the day on sunny days.
- **EC:** if the slab or substrate EC is high, for example above 3.5 to 4 mS/cm in the root zone, lower the dripper EC in summer. A high EC makes it harder for the roots to take up water.
- **Root temperature and health:** check that the root zone isn't too cold early in the day or overheating in the afternoon. Pull a plant or two and look at the roots. Brown, sparse roots, or Pythium, will cause midday wilt no matter how good your climate is.
- **Plant load:** a heavy fruit load and a big leaf area raise demand. Keep the leaf area balanced, but don't strip too many leaves at once.

### 4. Use shade only when the sun is strongest
- A **movable screen** is much better than whitewash. Close it partway, about 30 to 50%, only above a high radiation threshold, for example about 700 to 800 W/m² outside, or when leaf temperature rises clearly above air temperature. Open it again as soon as conditions ease.
- **Whitewash** cuts light all day, every day, including mornings, afternoons and cloudy days when the crop needs every bit of light. That costs you production for weeks. Use it only if the problem stays after the steps above *and* you have no screen. Use a light coat or a removable product, and remove it as autumn approaches.

## How to check that it's working

- Measure leaf temperature with an infrared thermometer at about 11:00, 13:00 and 15:00. You want the leaves 1 to 3 °C below air temperature during sunny hours.
- Log RH or VPD, vent position, CO2 and irrigation or drain data together, and compare a wilting day with a recovered day.
- Watch the head of the plant. Heads should stay turgid, and tendrils should stay fresh and curled rather than limp.

**Bottom line:** keep the light, and stop drying out the greenhouse. Vent less aggressively with a higher setpoint, add humidity at midday, match irrigation to radiation, and check root health. Then use the movable screen only to cut the strongest midday sun. Whitewash is a last resort.
