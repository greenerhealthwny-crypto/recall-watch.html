Eight months of consistent work with little to show for it is frustrating. The good news is that your setup points to a few likely causes, and each one can be fixed. Here's what I'd change, starting with the biggest factor.

## 1. You're probably doing too much, and not hard enough

Five days a week at about 15 sets per session comes to roughly **75 glute sets a week**. That's far more than most people can recover from or make productive. With that much volume, a few things tend to go wrong:

- **Effort per set drops.** You pace yourself, and most sets end well short of failure. Those sets add fatigue but don't do much for growth.
- **Recovery never catches up.** Glutes need time to repair and grow between hard sessions. Training them hard every weekday leaves very little room for that.
- **Loads stall.** If your hip thrust or RDL weights haven't gone up much in months, that's a strong sign you're overreaching rather than progressing.

**The change:** train glutes **3 (at most 4) days a week**, with about **10–15 quality sets per session**. That's roughly **30–45 hard sets a week**, about half what you're doing now. Take most working sets to **1–3 reps short of failure**. On isolation moves like abduction and kickbacks, it's fine to go all the way to failure on the last set.

## 2. Track your lifts and add weight or reps over time

Muscle grows when you gradually ask it to do more. If you don't already keep a log, start one. For each main lift, aim to beat last time by adding a rep or a little weight. Keep using the same core exercises for 6–12 weeks before rotating them, so progress is measurable. Changing exercises constantly makes it hard to tell whether you're actually getting stronger.

## 3. Pick hip-dominant exercises so your thighs grow less

If your program has a lot of squats, lunges, leg press, split squats or step-ups, your quads (and adductors) are getting heavy work. That's very likely why your thighs are growing. The fix is to move most of your volume to exercises where the hips do the work and the knees stay fairly still:

**Prioritize these (they emphasize glutes and keep thigh involvement low):**
- Barbell hip thrusts and glute bridges (your main lift)
- Romanian deadlifts and good mornings (these also work the hamstrings, but don't build the front of the thigh much)
- 45° back extensions done glute-focused: round your upper back slightly, turn your feet out, and squeeze your glutes to come up
- Cable or machine kickbacks
- Hip abduction (machine, banded, or seated/lying). This targets the upper glutes and side glutes, which give the "shelf" and the rounder look from behind.
- Frog pumps, or high-rep banded bridges as a finisher

**Reduce or adjust these:**
- Cut back on heavy squats, leg press, lunges and step-ups, or remove them for a training block. You don't have to drop them forever.
- If you keep a squat pattern, favor versions that load the glutes more and the quads less: sit back more, use a wider stance, go a bit less deep if your knees travel far forward, or swap in box squats. Use moderate loads.
- On lunges and split squats, take a longer stride and lean your torso slightly forward. That shifts more of the work to the glutes.

## 4. Check whether the thigh growth is muscle or fat

Before you assume the squats are to blame, check whether your bodyweight has crept up. Many women store fat on the thighs and hips first. If the scale has risen, some of the "bigger thighs" may be fat rather than muscle, and that calls for a different fix: a small calorie adjustment rather than different exercises. Take tape measurements of your thighs, hips and waist plus progress photos every 3–4 weeks. Measurements tell you much more than how your jeans feel.

## 5. Eat enough to grow

You can't build much new muscle if you're eating too little.
- **Protein:** about **1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight per day** (about 0.7–1 g per pound), spread across 3–4 meals.
- **Calories:** if you're at a healthy body fat and want real glute growth, eat at **maintenance or a small surplus** (around +200–300 kcal/day). Dieting the whole time while hoping your glutes grow is one of the most common reasons for a plateau.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours. This is where recovery actually happens.

## 6. Learn to feel your glutes working

If you mostly feel your hamstrings, quads or lower back during hip thrusts and bridges, your glutes may not be doing their share of the work. Some cues that help:
- On hip thrusts, tuck your chin and keep your ribs down. Tilt your pelvis slightly backward (a "posterior pelvic tilt") at the top, and push through your heels or mid-foot.
- Pause for 1–2 seconds at the top of every rep and squeeze.
- Do a short activation warm-up before lifting, such as 2 sets of banded glute bridges and banded abduction.
- Lighten the load if you need to. A weight you can actually feel in your glutes beats a heavier one your back takes over.

## A sample week

**Day 1: Heavy hip extension**
- Barbell hip thrust: 4 × 6–10
- Romanian deadlift: 3 × 8–10
- Hip abduction machine: 3 × 15–20
- Cable kickback: 2 × 12–15 per side

**Day 2: Moderate / pump**
- Glute-focused 45° back extension: 3 × 12–20
- Single-leg hip thrust: 3 × 10–12 per side
- Banded or seated abduction: 3 × 20–30
- Frog pumps: 2 × 30 to near failure

**Day 3: Strength + variety**
- Barbell glute bridge or hip thrust (heavier, or with a pause at the top): 4 × 8–12
- Glute-biased squat variation (box squat or wide-stance squat, moderate weight) *or* a long-stride split squat: 3 × 8–10. Leave this out entirely if you want to minimize thigh growth.
- Cable pull-through or kettlebell swing: 3 × 12–15
- Lying or side-lying abduction: 2 × 15–20 per side

Rest 2–3 minutes between sets on the main lifts and 60–90 seconds on the accessories. Every 6–8 weeks, take an easier week where you cut sets and weight by about a third, then build back up.

## Be realistic about timing

Once these pieces are in place (recovery, real effort, steady weight increases, enough food), most people notice visible changes in **8–12 weeks**. After that, results keep building slowly. Genetics also affect how glutes grow and where your body stores fat, so judge your progress against your own measurements, not other people's photos.

**In short:** train glutes less often but harder, base your program on hip thrusts, RDLs, back extensions and abduction instead of squats and lunges, track your lifts so the numbers keep going up, and eat enough protein and calories to grow.
