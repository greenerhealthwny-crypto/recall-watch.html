Eight months of training five days a week is a lot of consistency, so that part isn't the issue. Here's what I think is going on and what I'd change. The approach comes from Bret Contreras's *Glute Lab*.

## What's probably holding you back

**1. You're doing far more volume than you can recover from.** Five days × 15 sets is about **75 glute sets a week**. Contreras's range is **10–40 weekly glute sets**, depending on the person, and most people do best somewhere in the middle. He gives an example close to yours: a lifter doing 4 sessions × 15 sets (60 sets a week) cut back to 2 × 12 (24 sets a week). With less fatigue, the strength and muscle she had been building could finally show. Past a certain point, extra sets just add fatigue. The first set of an exercise gives most of the stimulus, and each set after it adds less.

**2. You may be getting less effort per set than you think.** This is the most common mistake Contreras sees: "too much volume, too little effort." When you have 15 sets to get through five days in a row, you naturally pace yourself, and many sets end with 4–6 reps still in the tank. Growth comes mainly from the last ~5 reps before failure (the "stimulating reps"). You can have high frequency, high volume or high effort, but you can't have all three at once.

**3. Your exercise choices are probably growing your thighs.** If your 15 sets lean on squats, lunges, step-ups, split squats, leg press or heavy RDLs, those are vertically loaded, quad- and hamstring-heavy lifts. They work the lower glutes in a stretched position, but they also build a lot of leg. Glute-dominant exercises (hip thrusts, bridges, abduction) put most of the tension on the glutes.

**4. You're probably not progressing.** If your hip thrust weight has been about the same for months, the stimulus hasn't changed either. Mechanical tension that increases over time is the main driver of growth.

**5. Recovery and food matter more than they seem.** Five hard days, poor sleep or not eating enough can stall even a perfect program.

## What to change

### Train glutes 3 days a week, not 5
Contreras considers three glute days the sweet spot for most people. In his rough estimate, three days get you about 98% of possible gains, and a 4th or 5th day helps or hurts depending on recovery. If you love being in the gym, use days 4–5 for upper body or a short, easy band "pump" session (see below), not another hard glute day.

### Cut to about 12–15 hard sets per session (~36–45 a week), and make every set count
- **Main lift:** take it to failure or 1 rep shy.
- **Accessories:** stop 2–3 reps shy of failure.
- **Band and abduction work:** go for burn and pump, not true failure.
- Rest 2–3 minutes on the main lift. If you can chat between sets of hip thrusts, you aren't working hard enough.

### Swap leg-dominant lifts for glute-dominant ones
To grow your glutes without growing your legs, Contreras says to **cut back on** squats, lunges, step-ups, split squats, leg press, hack squats, leg extensions and heavy hinges. **Emphasize** instead:
- **Barbell glute bridges** (he calls these the "money" lift for this goal) and hip thrusts
- Feet-elevated hip thrusts, frog pumps
- Abduction: seated machine or knee-banded seated abduction, side-lying abduction, lateral band walks
- Hip external rotation (cable or band)
- Lighter hinges that hit the glutes more than the hamstrings: cable pull-throughs, reverse hypers, kettlebell swings, American deadlifts (shorter range)
- Low-load activation work to warm up

You don't need to drop squats or lunges completely. One lighter, higher-rep set a week keeps the movement pattern without driving leg growth.

### Get the glutes, not the quads, doing the work on thrusts
If you feel thrusts mostly in your thighs:
- Shoulder blades on the bench edge. A bench of about 14 in suits most people; lower it if you're short.
- Move your feet a bit farther out, so your shins are roughly vertical at the top.
- Push through your heels, knees out. A band around your knees helps.
- Ribs down, chin tucked, slight posterior pelvic tilt at the top, and squeeze for 1 second.
- If you still feel it mostly in your quads, try the **American hip thrust** (bench edge at mid-back instead of under the shoulder blades).

## A sample week

| Day | Exercise | Sets × reps | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon: heavy** | Barbell hip thrust | 4 × 6–8 | Last 2 sets 0–1 RIR |
| | Cable pull-through | 3 × 12–15 | 2 RIR |
| | Seated hip abduction machine (lean forward) | 3 × 15–20 | 1–2 RIR |
| | Side-lying hip abduction | 2 × 20/leg | Burn, not failure |
| **Wed: medium** | Barbell glute bridge | 3 × 10–12 | 1 RIR |
| | Single-leg or B-stance hip thrust | 3 × 10–12/leg | 1–2 RIR |
| | 45° back extension (rounded upper back, glute focus) | 3 × 15–20 | 2 RIR |
| | Cable standing hip external rotation | 2 × 15/side | 2 RIR |
| **Fri: light / pump** | Feet-elevated hip thrust (pause 2 s at top) | 3 × 15–20 | 1–2 RIR |
| | Reverse hyper or kettlebell swing | 3 × 15–20 | 2 RIR |
| | Frog pumps | 2 × 30–50 | Close to failure |
| | 3-minute band burnout (kickbacks → band walks → banded seated abduction → banded bridge hold) | 1 | Pump, not failure |

That's about 12–13 sets per session and about 38 a week. It also follows Contreras's **Rule of Thirds** across the week:
- A mix of heavy, medium and light rep ranges.
- A mix of horizontal (thrusts, bridges), hinge, and lateral or rotary (abduction) work.
- Some sets taken to failure, some a few reps shy, some nowhere near failure.

**Optional days 4–5:** upper body, walking, or a 10-minute low-damage band session (banded bridges, clams, band walks) kept well short of failure. Take at least one full rest day.

## How to progress
- **Start with an easy week.** You're carrying eight months of fatigue. Run the program at about 60–70% effort with no sets to failure. Strength often jumps after this.
- **Use 4-week cycles:**
  - Week 1: easy
  - Week 2: normal, still no failure
  - Week 3: to failure with a little left in the tank
  - Week 4: go for personal bests (PRs)

  Then start the next cycle.
- **Log every set** and try to beat your load or total reps over time. Before adding weight, improve your form and range of motion, then add reps, then add load.
- **Rotate the program every 4–6 weeks.** For example, run a hip-thrust-focused month, then a bridge or single-leg month.

## About the thighs
Two things are worth knowing:
- **Part of what you're seeing may be fat, not muscle.** Contreras notes that when people say their legs are getting bigger, it's often leg fat rather than leg muscle, and you can't spot-reduce fat. Body-fat changes from diet show up on your thighs as well.
- **Bigger glutes make thighs look smaller.** Growing the glutes changes the proportions of your whole lower body.

## Food, sleep and recovery
- **Protein:** about 1 g per lb of lean body mass a day, or roughly 0.8 g per lb of body weight, split across 3–4 meals.
- **Calories:** your glutes are unlikely to grow if you're also in a long calorie deficit. Eat around maintenance, or slightly above it if you're lean. A rough estimate of maintenance is body weight (lb) × 14–15. Eating at maintenance while getting much stronger can grow your glutes while your waist stays the same or shrinks.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours.
- **Cardio:** keep it moderate (walking is best). Lots of hard cardio competes with recovery.

## What to expect
Progress comes in waves, and it's slower after the first few months. Give this plan **at least 12 weeks** (three cycles) before you judge it. Track it in four ways: hip thrust and bridge numbers, monthly progress photos in the same lighting, a hip/glute tape measurement, and body weight. If your thrust is clearly climbing, your glutes are almost certainly growing.

*This is training education, not medical advice. If any of these exercises causes sharp or lasting pain, stop doing it and see a physical therapist.*
