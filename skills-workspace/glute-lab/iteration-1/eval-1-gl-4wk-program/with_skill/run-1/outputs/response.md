# Your 4-Week Glute Program (Mon / Wed / Fri, ~60 min)

This is built on Bret Contreras's *Glute Lab* principles:

- **Three glute days a week.** In his experience, that's the sweet spot for most people.
- **A different focus each day.** Monday is the hip thrust, Wednesday the squat, Friday the hinge.
- **The Rule of Thirds:**
  - **Direction of force:** about a third of your glute work is horizontal (hip thrusts, back extensions), a third vertical (squats, lunges, deadlifts) and a third side-to-side or rotational (abduction).
  - **Weight:** a mix of heavy, medium and light sets.
  - **Effort:** some sets close to failure, some moderate, some easy.
- **A 4-week ramp.** Week 1 is easy and week 4 is where you go for personal records.

Your numbers (hip thrust 185×8, squat 135×5) put you at roughly a 225–235 lb hip thrust max and a 150–155 lb squat max. The loads below are based on those estimates. Treat them as starting points and adjust to how the bar moves.

---

## How the 4 weeks progress

"RIR" means reps in reserve: how many more good reps you could have done when you stopped the set.

| Week | Feel | Main lifts | Accessories |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1: Groove** | About 60–70% effort. Learn the exercises and find your weights. | 3–4 RIR, no failure | 3 RIR |
| **2: Build** | Normal training | 2 RIR | 2 RIR |
| **3: Push** | Hard, with a little left in the tank | 1 RIR | 1–2 RIR |
| **4: Peak** | Go for personal records (PRs) | Last set as many reps as possible (AMRAP) with good form | 1 RIR |

**Rest:** 2–3 minutes on the first lift of each day, about 90 seconds on accessories, and 30–60 seconds on the pump and abduction work.

**Fitting it into an hour:** do the upper-body pair (exercise E) as a superset. On Mon and Wed you can also slot it into your rest periods during exercises B–D.

---

## Warm-up (5–7 min, every session)
- Glute bridge: 1×20 with a 1-second squeeze at the top
- Banded lateral walk: 1×10 steps each way
- Bodyweight squat: 1×10
- Then 2–3 lighter warm-up sets of your first lift (for example, on hip thrusts: empty bar ×10, 95×6, 135×4)

---

## Monday: Hip Thrust Focus (horizontal, heavy)

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Barbell hip thrust** | 4×6 | W1: 165 · W2: 175 · W3: 185 · W4: 195 (last set AMRAP) |
| B | Goblet squat (heels slightly elevated is fine) | 3×10–12 | Go deep. Add 5–10 lb when you get 12 reps at the target RIR. |
| C | 45° back extension, glute-style (rounded upper back, feet turned out) | 3×15–20 | Hold a plate or dumbbell once 20 reps is easy |
| D | Seated hip abduction machine | 3×20 | Change torso angle each set: leaning back, upright, leaning forward. Don't go to failure. |
| E | Lat pulldown + dumbbell bench press (superset) | 2–3×10 each | Keeps your upper body progressing |

**Glute sets: about 13**

## Wednesday: Squat Focus (vertical, heavy)

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Barbell back squat** | 4×5 | W1: 115 · W2: 125 · W3: 135 · W4: 140–145 (last set AMRAP) |
| B | Dumbbell Bulgarian split squat | 3×8 per leg | Start with your weaker leg. Lean your torso forward slightly to bias the glutes. |
| C | Single-leg or B-stance hip thrust | 3×12 per leg | Bodyweight or a light dumbbell. Squeeze 1 second at the top. |
| D | Side-lying hip abduction (band or ankle weight) | 2×20 per leg | Slow and controlled. You should feel a burn in your upper glute. |
| E | Seated cable row + dumbbell shoulder press (superset) | 2–3×10 each | |

**Glute sets: about 12**

## Friday: Hinge + Pump Focus (vertical stretch, then light horizontal)

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Romanian deadlift (barbell)** | 3×8 | Start around 95–115 in W1 and add 10 lb a week while your back stays flat. Push hips back and stop when your hamstrings are fully stretched. |
| B | Knee-banded barbell hip thrust | 3×15 | About 125–135. Keep a steady rhythm without resting between reps. Push your knees out against the band. |
| C | Dumbbell walking lunge | 2×20 total steps | Take longer strides to target the glutes more. |
| D | **3-minute band burnout** | 1 round, no rest | 60 s band kickbacks (30 s per leg) → 30 s lateral band walk → 30 s seated band abduction → 30 s banded bridge hold → 30 s glute squeeze in a plank |
| E | Assisted chin-up or pulldown + push-ups (superset) | 2–3 sets each | |

**Glute sets: about 9, plus the burnout**

**Weekly total:** about 34 glute sets. That's within the book's 10–40 range for dedicated glute training. If you're still sore when the next session comes around, cut one set from each accessory before you cut the main lifts.

## Progression rules
1. **Log everything:** weight, reps and how many reps you had left. Each week, try to beat last week's total on the same lift (sets × reps × load), whether by adding load, reps or better form.
2. **Main lifts (A):** follow the weekly loads above. If week 2 felt like 3+ RIR, add 5–10 lb from then on. If it felt like 0–1, repeat that weight.
3. **Accessories:** use double progression. Stay in the rep range, and once you hit the top of it on every set at the target RIR, add a little weight next time.
4. **Form before load.** Lock out every hip thrust, squat to at least parallel and keep the same depth on every rep. A rep with shortened range doesn't count as progress.

## Hip thrust checklist (your most important lift)
- Bench edge just below your shoulder blades. Use a bar pad.
- Feet about shoulder-width, toes slightly out. At the top your shins should be close to vertical.
- Look forward and tuck your chin as you rise. Keep your ribs down, and finish with a slight tuck of the pelvis (posterior tilt). Squeeze for 1 second at the top.
- Push through your heels and keep your knees out.
- **Feel it in your quads?** Move your feet farther out, add a knee band or use a lower bench. **Feel it in your hamstrings?** Bring your feet closer. **Low back?** You're likely arching at lockout, so keep your ribs down and tuck slightly at the top.

## Swaps if something hurts or you don't feel your glutes
- Back squat bothers your back or hips → goblet squat, leg press or hack squat
- RDL bothers your back → 45° back extension or cable pull-through
- Knee irritation from lunges or split squats → reverse lunges or step-ups
- No abduction machine → knee-banded seated abduction (sitting on a bench)

Train *around* pain, never through it. Sharp or lingering pain is a reason to see a physical therapist, not to push harder.

## After week 4
- **Week 5:** start a new 4-week cycle with another easy week, using your new numbers as the baseline.
- **Rotate your focus.** Strength is easy to keep and hard to gain, so Contreras suggests specializing in one or two lifts per month.
  - **Next block:** for example, a squat focus. Squat twice a week and keep hip thrusts at maintenance volume.
  - **The block after:** for example, a hip thrust focus, using the hip thrust example from the book where each day uses different reps:

| Day | Hip thrust | Approx. % of max | Then |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 3×10 | ~75% | 1 AMRAP set |
| Wed | 4×6 | ~85% | 1 AMRAP set |
| Fri | 2×15 | ~65% | 1 AMRAP set |

- Keep the same exercise categories but change the variations every 3–6 weeks (Contreras calls this "same but different").

## Recovery basics
- **Protein:** about 0.8 g per lb of body weight per day, spread over 3–4 meals. That's about 110 g a day at 140 lb.
- **Calories:** you don't need to bulk to grow your glutes. Eating at maintenance and getting stronger for months works well. If you're dieting, holding your strength steady counts as progress.
- **Sleep and rest:** 7–9 hours of sleep and at least one full rest day a week.
- **Cardio:** walking or easy cycling is fine. Do it after lifting or on other days, and keep it moderate so it doesn't cut into recovery.

Strength gains come in waves, and some weeks will feel flat. Judge your progress by four things together: strength, body weight, measurements and the mirror. Most of all, praise yourself for the effort you put in.

*This is training education, not medical advice. If you have an injury, a medical condition, or are pregnant or postpartum, check with your doctor or a physical therapist first.*
