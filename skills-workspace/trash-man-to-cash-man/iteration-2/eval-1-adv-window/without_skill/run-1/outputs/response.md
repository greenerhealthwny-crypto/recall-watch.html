# From H.A.J. to O.A.S.: A $0 Facebook Marketplace Roadmap

*From general knowledge of Myron Golden's teaching. I'm working from memory and not from the book, so check the exact labels and quadrant names against* From the Trash Man to the Cash Man.

## The Window of Opportunity (as I understand it)

Golden draws a "window" that sorts income earners by **how** they earn. They either trade their own time, or they own something that produces without them:

| Pane | Stands for | How money is earned | The trap / the lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H.A.J.** | Have A Job | You trade time for money for someone else's system. | Your income is capped by your hours and your boss decides your value. |
| **O.A.J.** | Own A Job (self-employed) | You trade time for money for yourself. | You have more freedom, but if you stop working the money stops. |
| **O.A.S.** | Own A System | People and processes produce income while you manage the system. | This is the real business. Your value comes from solving problems at scale. |
| **Investor / money works** | Money and assets make money | Your capital works for you. | This is where the profits from O.A.S. end up. |

Golden's core idea is that you move through the window by changing **what you're paid for**. You stop selling your time and start selling solved problems. Then you stop doing the solving yourself and own the process that does it. He also teaches that you get rich by serving many people and that the person who adds the most value earns the most.

You asked for **H.A.J. to O.A.S.** Here is a staged plan that starts from $0 and a Facebook Marketplace account. **Keep the job the whole way through.** It pays for the transition.

---

## Stage 0: Mindset and setup (Week 1, $0)

- **Treat the job as your investor.** Your paycheck covers your living costs, so every Marketplace dollar can be put back into the business.
- **Set a time budget:** 10–15 hours a week, for example 2 weeknight evenings plus Saturday morning.
- **Pick a lane** you can source for free or cheaply and that sells reliably on Marketplace:
  - Free furniture (curbside, the "Free" section on Marketplace, Craigslist free, Buy Nothing groups)
  - Appliances, tools and outdoor gear
  - Moving clean-outs ("I'll haul it away for free")
- **Set up your account:** a real profile photo, a short bio ("Local reseller – quality used furniture, fast pickup"), and a separate notes or spreadsheet file to track every item's cost, sale price and hours.

## Stage 1: H.A.J. + side hustle, flipping the "trash" ($0 → ~$1,000)

This is the "trash man" stage. You find value other people throw away.

1. **Source for $0.** Check the Marketplace "Free" listings, curb alerts and Buy Nothing groups every day. Respond within minutes, because speed wins.
2. **Add value.** Clean it, tighten it, touch up the paint, take good photos in daylight, and write a clear title that names the brand, size and condition.
3. **Price to move.** Look at sold and comparable listings and price slightly below them. You get paid for turnover more than for margin.
4. **Reinvest everything.** The first profits go to tools you'll use again: cleaning supplies, a drill, paint, moving straps and, if needed, a trailer or truck rental.

**Milestone:** about 10–20 flips and roughly $500–$1,000 of reinvested capital. You now know what sells, how fast, and for how much.

## Stage 2: O.A.J., you're a business but you are the business (~$1,000 → steady $1–3k/month)

You've become self-employed on the side. The money is real, but it depends on your hours.

- **Start buying, not only taking free items.** Use capital to buy underpriced items (estate sales, garage sales, "must go today" listings) when you know the resale value.
- **Add paid services** that come out of the same activity:
  - Haul-away or junk removal: you get paid to take the items you would resell anyway. That's two income streams from one trip.
  - Delivery for buyers, which adds $25–$75 per sale.
  - Furniture refinishing or assembly.
- **Get a list of buyers.** Note repeat buyers, ask them what they want, and create a Facebook group or text list for "first look" drops.
- **Track your hourly profit.** If your Marketplace hour earns more than your job hour, you've reached the decision point. That doesn't mean quitting yet. It means building the system.

**Warning sign:** you're tired, you're turning down jobs, and income stops when you stop. That is exactly the O.A.J. ceiling Golden warns about.

## Stage 3: Build the system (the bridge from O.A.J. to O.A.S.)

Turn what you do into something someone else can repeat.

1. **Document every step** as a checklist: sourcing, pickup, cleaning, photography, listing templates, pricing rules, messaging scripts and delivery.
2. **Hand off the lowest-value tasks first.** Pay a helper per job for pickups and hauling (for example, $20–$40 per pickup). Keep the high-value decisions: buying and pricing.
3. **Standardize your listings.** Use template titles and descriptions, consistent photo angles and canned replies ("Yes, it's available. Pickup at ___, cash or Zelle").
4. **Separate the money.** Open a business bank account. Consider registering a simple LLC or DBA and check your local resale or hauling permits and sales-tax rules.
5. **Set up a space.** A storage unit or garage bay lets you buy in volume, such as whole estate lots.

## Stage 4: O.A.S., owning the system (people + process + profit)

You own a resale or haul-away operation. Others run it and you manage it.

- **Team:** 1–2 pickup and haul crew, a listing and messaging person (this can be a remote virtual assistant who manages the Marketplace inbox), and you as the buyer, manager and money person.
- **Multiple channels:** Marketplace plus OfferUp, Craigslist, eBay for small high-value items, and consignment.
- **Recurring problem-solving:** partnerships with apartment complexes, realtors, property managers and estate-sale companies for clean-outs. These customers pay you to take inventory away.
- **Your job becomes:** checking numbers each week, training people, making deals and doing quality control.
- **Leaving H.A.J.:** leave only when the system's *net profit* (after paying your team) covers your living expenses for several months in a row and you have a cash reserve of at least 3–6 months.

## Beyond O.A.S.: putting money to work

Golden's final step is moving system profits into assets, such as equipment you rent out, real estate, or other businesses, so your money earns money.

## Key principles to carry the whole way

- **Solve problems for money.** People with unwanted stuff have a problem and people who need affordable furniture have a problem. You solve both.
- **Add value first and income follows.**
- **Don't quit the job early.** Quit when the system replaces your income and doesn't depend on you.
- **Reinvest before you spend** in the early stages.
- **Stay legal and safe:** meet in public places, use contactless payment, avoid recalled or unsafe items such as cribs and car seats, and check local permit and tax rules.

*These figures are illustrations, not guarantees. Results depend on your market, effort and execution.*
