# From H.A.J. to O.A.S. Starting With $0 and a Facebook Marketplace Account

This plan follows Myron Golden's *From The Trash Man To The Cash Man* (2007). One thing up front: **the book came out before Facebook Marketplace existed (2016), so it says nothing about Marketplace.** Everything below about Marketplace is my application of Golden's framework, not something he wrote. Book ideas are marked **(book)**. My suggestions are marked **(applied)**.

---

## 1. The Window of Opportunity (book)

Golden says there are four ways to make money. He calls them the four panes of the Window of Opportunity:

| Pane | Meaning | Golden's ranking | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H.A.J.** | Have A Job | One of the two worst | Your income stops when you stop working |
| **O.A.J.** | Own A Job (self-employment) | One of the two worst | Your income still stops when you stop working |
| **S.M.S.** | Sow Money Seeds (investing) | Second best | Your money works for you |
| **O.A.S.** | Own A System (a business that runs without you) | Best | The system makes money whether you are there or not |

Golden ranks the panes. He does not lay out a required order, H.A.J. then O.A.J. then S.M.S. then O.A.S. The order below is a practical route for someone starting at $0. **(applied)**

**Where a Marketplace account fits:** buying and reselling items yourself (sourcing, listing, messaging buyers, meeting for pickups) is **O.A.J.** If you stop, the money stops. Treat it as a stepping stone and a way to fund your cans, not as the goal. **(applied, using the book's definitions)**

## 2. Your financial house today (book framework)

Golden's financial house has four windows: **Income, Outgo, WRL (Wealth Reducing Liabilities), and IPA (Income Producing Assets).**

- **Poor:** every dollar of income goes straight out the Outgo window.
- **Middle class:** income goes to wealth reducing liabilities (car payments, consumer debt, keeping up with the Joneses).
- **Rich:** income buys income producing assets, and over time their cash flow covers the bills.

Golden's point: *the difference is not how much you make. It is what you do with money after you make it.* **(book)**

With $0 saved, your house probably looks like the "poor" or "middle class" picture: job income in, everything out. Phase 1 is about opening the IPA window, even if only a little goes through it at first.

## 3. The path, phase by phase

### Phase 0: Keep the H.A.J. and fix the plumbing (now)
- **Keep your job.** Golden calls it "paralyzed by a paycheck" (practical trap #5) when you let a job stop you from building. He does not say to quit before you have something to replace it. Use the job as a base. **(book trap; applied advice)**
- **Set up the six cans (chapter 13) on your paycheck right away (book):**
  1. I can tithe: 10% of gross
  2. I can finish free: 10%, **never spent**, only used to buy income producing assets or businesses
  3. I can educate myself: 10%
  4. I can save for what I want: about 10% *(the book's text gives the total for cans 4 and 5 together as the remaining 20%; a 10/10 split is my reading)*
  5. I can have fun: about 10%, and it **must be spent** every month
  6. I can pay my bills: 50%
  If 50% doesn't cover your bills yet, start with smaller amounts in cans 1–5 and work up to the book's numbers. The habit matters more than the percentage at first. **(applied)**
- **Start self-education.** Golden's first step in chapter 16 is to invest in your mind (traps: "don't invest in their greatest asset" and "entertain themselves more than educate themselves"). Can #3 pays for books, courses and seminars. **(book)**

### Phase 1: O.A.J. on Marketplace, starting from $0 (months 0–6)
Ways to start with no money **(applied)**:
- **Sell what you already own** that you don't use. That is your first inventory, and it costs nothing.
- **Free-to-flip:** pick up "free" or curb-side items (furniture, appliances, tools), clean or fix them, and relist. Fitting, given Golden's own start as a trash collector.
- **Consignment:** sell items for friends or neighbours and keep a cut, so you need no money up front.
- **Reinvest profits.** Don't spend them. Run every Marketplace dollar through the six cans like any other income.

Golden ideas to bring in **(book)**:
- **You are in the marketing business** (chapter 9). You aren't really "in furniture flipping." You are in the business of marketing. Learn to take good photos, write strong titles and descriptions, price well and reply fast. That skill carries into every later phase.
- **Leverage technology** (chapter 7). Use the internet to research prices (sold listings), market and sell.
- **No competition** (chapter 11). Other sellers aren't fighting you over a fixed pie. Wealth is created. Swap tips with other resellers and send buyers to each other.
- **Product over service** (chapter 6). You are already selling products, which is good. But at this stage each sale still takes your time.

**Watch out:** trap #6 "do everything themselves." Note every task you do (sourcing, cleaning, photos, listing, messaging, delivery). Phase 3 is about handing those off.

### Phase 2: Start the S.M.S. pane (begins as soon as can #2 has money)
- Can #2, **"I can finish free,"** is your seed money. Golden says it is **never spent**. It only buys income producing assets or businesses, and you may spend what those assets *earn*. **(book)**
- Practical first seeds, sized to your can **(applied)**: buy equipment that raises your output (a trailer, a better tool set, a cleaning machine), buy inventory in bulk lots, or put money into broad investments you understand. Do your own research. This is not personal investment advice.
- Watch trap #13, **"don't use good debt; bad debt uses them."** Debt for an asset that pays for itself can be good debt. Debt for lifestyle items is a WRL. **(book)**

### Phase 3: Build toward O.A.S. (months 6–24)
Golden's **Hands-Free Money business plan (book):** build a home business that makes **$10,000 a month within two years** and takes no more than "one finger's worth of effort" to run. **Then start a different business that does the same, and repeat.**

Ways to turn a Marketplace hustle into a system **(applied)**:
1. **Write down the process.** Make a checklist for each step: source, inspect, clean, photograph, list, message, hand off.
2. **Outsource one step at a time.** Pay a helper per pickup or delivery, or per item cleaned. Use templates or tools for listings and replies. That fixes trap #6.
3. **Move from flipping one-offs to repeatable products.** A product business can sell 1 or 100,000 units in the same time (chapter 6). Look for items you can buy in volume, private-label goods, or digital products like guides and templates about reselling. A one-of-a-kind flip doesn't scale.
4. **Add sales channels** beyond Marketplace (your own site, other marketplaces), so one platform's rules don't control your income.
5. **The test:** if you take two weeks off, does money still come in? If yes, you are getting close to O.A.S. If no, you still own a job.

### Phase 4: The V.M. milestone and repeat
- Golden's **Virtual Millionaire (V.M.)** is someone whose income gives a million-dollar lifestyle, for example business income equal to what $1 million would pay at 6% (about $60,000 a year, or $5,000 a month), without having a million in the bank. **(book)** Your first system hitting $5,000 a month hands-free makes you a "V.M." by that measure. Hitting $10,000 a month is the Hands-Free plan's two-year target.
- Then follow the plan: **start the next, different business.** Keep running all income through the cans. The finish-free can keeps buying IPAs until their cash flow covers your Outgo. That is the "rich" financial house. **(book)**
- **Leaving the H.A.J.** Golden doesn't give an exact rule for this. A careful approach is to wait until hands-free income steadily covers can #6 (your bills) for several months. **(applied)**

## 4. Mindset checks from the trap lists (book)
Watch these traps most closely on this path:
- **#7 Value security more than freedom.** The job feels safe. The system is freedom.
- **#4 Value consumption more than production.** Using Marketplace to *buy* stuff instead of sell it.
- **#11 Renter's mentality vs owner's mentality.** Think like the owner of a system, not a gig worker.
- **#5 Don't trust or like people with money.** Chapter 12, "Since you can't beat 'em, join 'em," answers this one. Don't resent successful sellers. Study them. "You won't become what you despise."
- Practical trap **#9, no financial mentor or coach.** Chapter 10 says to find a financial guide who has already found their fortune. Take advice from people who already have the result you want, not from broke friends.

## 5. Quick-reference roadmap

| Phase | Pane | Main action | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | H.A.J. | Six cans on your paycheck; self-education | Cans set up and funded every payday |
| 1 | O.A.J. | $0-start Marketplace flipping; learn marketing | Steady monthly profit; tasks written down |
| 2 | S.M.S. | Can #2 buys income producing assets | First asset paying you cash flow |
| 3 | O.A.S. | Systematize, outsource, move to products | Income continues with "one finger's" effort |
| 4 | O.A.S. ×2 | Hit $10k a month in about 2 years; start business #2 | Asset cash flow covers your Outgo |

## Caveats
- **Marketplace is not in the book.** It came out in 2007. The mapping above applies the book's principles. They are not Golden's instructions.
- The book promotes Golden's own businesses, network marketing and programs. Its income claims carry a "results vary" disclaimer. **$10,000 a month in two years is a target, not a promise.** Research any "opportunity" pitched to you carefully.
- Golden's six cans include a tithe because of his Christian faith. If that isn't your belief, the book allows giving to charity, or you can adapt it.
- Follow Marketplace's rules, local resale and sales-tax laws, and report reselling income for taxes.
- This is educational, not individual financial, tax or investment advice.
