# Splitting $500 with Myron Golden's Six Money Cans

*A note up front: I'm working from general knowledge, not the book. Golden teaches a "can" or bucket system where every dollar gets a job, starting with giving, but I can't confirm his exact can names or percentages from memory. The structure below follows his core principles: give first, pay yourself, save, invest in assets, and grow your skills. The percentages are a reasonable example, so adjust them to match the book if your copy lists different ones.*

## The split

| # | Can | % | Amount from $500 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Giving / Tithe** | 10% | **$50** |
| 2 | **Savings (pay yourself / emergency)** | 10% | **$50** |
| 3 | **Investing (assets that produce income)** | 10% | **$50** |
| 4 | **Education (self-development)** | 10% | **$50** |
| 5 | **Business reinvestment / Opportunity** | 10% | **$50** |
| 6 | **Living (necessities + some enjoyment)** | 50% | **$250** |
| | **Total** | **100%** | **$500** |

## What each can is for

### 1. Giving / Tithe: $50
Golden puts giving first. In his teaching, generosity comes before everything else, and a person who can't give $50 out of $500 won't give $5,000 out of $50,000. Give it to your church or a cause you believe in **before** you spend anything else. That way the habit isn't left to whatever money is left over.

### 2. Savings (pay yourself): $50
This money keeps you safe. It goes into a separate savings account you don't touch, and its first job is an emergency fund, so one surprise bill doesn't push you back into debt. Pay yourself before you pay your wants.

### 3. Investing: $50
This can buys **assets**, meaning things that put money in your pocket. Golden draws a sharp line between assets and liabilities. This can is never spent. It's only put to work, for example in index funds or a brokerage account, and later in income-producing property or businesses. The money in it should earn money.

### 4. Education: $50
Golden often says that your income won't grow beyond your skills and knowledge. Spend this on books, courses, coaching or events that make you more valuable, especially in sales, marketing and offers. On a side-sales income, one good skill can be worth far more than $50.

### 5. Business reinvestment / Opportunity: $50
Your side sales are a small business, so feed them. Use this can for inventory, supplies, better photos, ads or tools that let you make the next $500 faster. This is how a side hustle grows from "Have A Job" toward "Own A System." If you have no current business needs, let it build up for a good deal when one comes along.

### 6. Living: $250
This covers your necessities: groceries, gas, phone and bills. If your job already pays for your living costs, put part of this can toward debt payoff and use a small slice (for example $25) for guilt-free fun so the system feels sustainable.

## How to put it into practice

1. **Split it as soon as you're paid.** Each time a sale comes in, divide it right away. Don't wait until the end of the month.
2. **Keep the cans apart physically.** Use separate bank accounts or sub-accounts (many banks allow free "buckets"), or literal envelopes or cans for cash.
3. **Never borrow between cans,** especially from Investing.
4. **Scale the percentages, not the habit.** As income grows, keep giving and investing at least at these levels and let the living share shrink. For example, 10/15/15/10/10/40.

## If your side income is extra money (your job covers living)

A more aggressive wealth-building split for $500 could be:

- Giving 10% → $50
- Savings 15% → $75
- Investing 25% → $125
- Education 15% → $75
- Business reinvestment 25% → $125
- Living / fun 10% → $50

*This is general educational information, not personal financial advice.*
