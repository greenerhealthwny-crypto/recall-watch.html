# How Myron Golden Says to Split Your Paycheck ("The Cans")

**A caveat first:** I'm answering from memory, without the book in front of me. I'm confident about Myron Golden's overall philosophy in *From the Trash Man to the Cash Man*. I'm **not** confident about the exact number of "cans," their names, or the percentages he gives. Check the details below against the book before you build a budget on them.

## The core idea

Golden's money teaching rests on biblical stewardship and on the "pay yourself first" principle, which you also find in books like *The Richest Man in Babylon*. The point of splitting your paycheck into separate containers ("cans," jars or accounts) is this:

1. **Every dollar gets a job before you spend any of it.** You don't spend first and save what's left. You divide the money first and spend only from the spending portion.
2. **Some money must go toward owning things, not just consuming.** Golden's whole book is about moving from trading time for money (the "trash man") to owning assets and businesses (the "cash man"). The cans are how an ordinary paycheck starts paying for that move.
3. **Keeping the money physically or mentally separate keeps you disciplined.** Once money is in the "investing" can, it isn't available for impulse purchases.

## The typical can structure in his teaching (verify the details)

As best I can reconstruct it, the paycheck is divided roughly like this:

| Can | Purpose | Rough share |
|---|---|---|
| **Giving / Tithe** | Returning the first portion to God or giving generously. Golden treats this as the first step, not an afterthought. | ~10% |
| **Saving / Investing ("pay yourself")** | Money you keep and grow. You never spend it on lifestyle. It goes into assets, skills and business-building that produce income. | ~10% or more |
| **Living / Spending** | Bills, necessities and lifestyle. You live on what's left after the first cans are filled. | the remainder (~80%) |

Some versions of this teaching split the saving portion further, for example into:
- an **emergency or reserve** can,
- an **investment / wealth-building** can that you never touch for spending, and
- a **business or education** can for skills and ventures that raise your earning power.

I can't confirm which of these sub-cans (if any) Golden names specifically, or the exact percentages he uses.

## How to apply the spirit of it

- **Do it the moment you get paid.** Move the money into the cans before you touch anything else.
- **Start small if you have to.** The habit matters more than the percentage. Even 1–2% per can builds the discipline, and you raise the share over time.
- **Live on the remainder.** If your expenses don't fit, the answer is to cut costs or increase income, not to raid the giving or investing cans.
- **Point the investing can at ownership.** In Golden's framework, the goal is to eventually own income-producing assets or a business. That's what moves you up from laborer toward owner.

## Bottom line

Golden's message is: **give first, pay yourself second, live on the rest, and use the "pay yourself" money to buy assets, not liabilities.** For the exact can names and percentages, check the relevant chapter of *From the Trash Man to the Cash Man*. I don't want to state specific figures as his when I can't verify them.
