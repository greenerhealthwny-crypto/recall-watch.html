# Splitting your paycheck: Myron Golden's six cans

In Chapter 13 of *From The Trash Man To The Cash Man* (2007), titled "You can if your cans say you can," Myron Golden says to buy **six paint cans**, give each one a label that starts with "I can...", and split **every paycheck** among them before you spend anything. The labels are meant to work on two levels. They split up the money, and they also tell you what you *can* do with it.

## The six cans

| # | Can | Share | What it's for |
|---|-----|-------|---------------|
| 1 | **"I can tithe"** | **10% of gross** | Giving to God's work through your church, or to charity if that fits your beliefs. |
| 2 | **"I can finish free"** | **10%** | Never spent. It only buys **income producing assets** or businesses. You may spend the *profits* those assets earn, but not the money itself. |
| 3 | **"I can educate myself"** | **10%** | Seminars, books and audio programs, so you keep investing in your mind. |
| 4 | **"I can save for what I want"** | ~10%* | Saving for big items such as vacations, down payments, furniture and emergencies. |
| 5 | **"I can have fun"** | ~10%* | This can **has to be spent every month** on something you enjoy. |
| 6 | **"I can pay my bills"** | **50%** | Rent or mortgage, utilities, food and other living costs. |

*\*The text I checked gives the percentages for cans 1, 2, 3 and 6 (10/10/10/50). That leaves 20% for cans 4 and 5, which most likely means 10% each. Treat that split as a likely reading, not a quote from the book.*

## Why the cans matter in Golden's system

- **Can 2 is the engine.** Golden's "financial house" has four windows: Income, Outgo, Wealth Reducing Liabilities, and Income Producing Assets. Poor people send everything out the Outgo window. Middle-class people buy liabilities. Rich people buy income producing assets. The "finish free" can makes you fill that last window on purpose, until the cash flow from your assets covers your expenses. Golden says the difference between rich and poor isn't how much you make. It's what you do with money after you make it.
- **Can 3 fits his central theme of self-education.** His list of practical money traps includes "don't invest in their greatest asset (their mind)" and "entertain themselves more than educate themselves."
- **Can 5 is required.** Making you spend the fun money every month keeps the plan livable, so you don't quit out of feeling deprived.
- **Living on 50%** forces the "good money management practices" whose absence Golden counts as a money trap.

## Example: a $4,000 paycheck

| Can | Amount |
|-----|--------|
| I can tithe (10% of gross) | $400 |
| I can finish free | $400 |
| I can educate myself | $400 |
| I can save for what I want | $400* |
| I can have fun | $400* |
| I can pay my bills | $2,000 |

*\*Assumes the 10% / 10% reading for cans 4 and 5.*

## How to start
1. Set up six "cans". These can be real jars, envelopes or separate bank sub-accounts.
2. Each payday, split the money **before** you spend any of it.
3. Use the finish-free money only for assets or businesses that produce income. In Golden's words, that means "Sow Money Seeds" (investing) and work toward "Own A System" (a business that runs without you).
4. If you can't live on 50% right now, start with smaller percentages for the other cans and raise them over time. Keep the habit going even when the amounts are small.

## Caveats
- The tithing can comes from Golden's Christian faith. If that isn't your belief, he suggests charity instead, or you can adapt it as you see fit.
- The book also promotes the author's own businesses and programs. Do your own research before putting "finish free" money into any opportunity.
- This is a summary of the book's framework, not personal financial, tax or investment advice.
