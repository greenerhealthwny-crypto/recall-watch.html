# Myron Golden's Money Traps and Hands-Free Money Business Plan

*From The Trash Man To The Cash Man: How Anyone Can Get Rich Starting From Anywhere* (2007) has two chapters on money traps. Chapter 14 covers **eleven mental money traps** and chapter 15 covers what Golden titles **thirteen practical money traps**. Chapter 5 sets out the **Hands-Free Money business plan**.

## The 11 mental money traps (chapter 14)

These are about mindset. Golden phrases each one as something "poor people..." do:

1. **Mistake negativism for skepticism.** They write off ideas instead of checking them.
2. **Are too myopic about money.** They think about the next paycheck, not long-term wealth.
3. **Don't desire or like money.**
4. **Value consumption more than production.**
5. **Don't trust or like people with money.**
6. **Are technophobic.** They avoid the technology that gives a business leverage.
7. **Value security more than freedom.**
8. **Value formal education over self-education.**
9. **Have no financial education.**
10. **Don't understand and use the power of principles.**
11. **Have a renter's mentality rather than an owner's mentality.**

## The practical money traps (chapter 15)

These are about behavior. The chapter is titled "thirteen," but the copy I worked from goes straight from #11 to #13, so it gives only 12 traps:

1. **Don't protect their minds** from negative input.
2. **Don't invest in their greatest asset**, which is their mind.
3. **Have no financial assets**, only wealth-reducing liabilities.
4. **Entertain themselves more than they educate themselves.**
5. **Are paralyzed by a paycheck.** The steady check keeps them from building anything of their own.
6. **Do everything themselves** instead of outsourcing.
7. **Don't have good money management practices.** Golden's fix is the six "cans" (see below).
8. **Operate on a cash basis.**
9. **Don't have a financial mentor or coach.**
10. **Don't set a good financial example for their children.**
11. **Don't teach their children how to become financially free.**
12. *(No #12 found. It may have been lost in the scan or skipped in the book's numbering.)*
13. **Don't use good debt. Instead, bad debt uses them.**

## The Hands-Free Money business plan (chapter 5)

**The goal:** build a home-based business that makes **$10,000 a month within two years** and needs no more than **"one finger's worth of effort"** to keep running.

**Then repeat it:** once the first business reaches that point, start a **second, different business** and bring it to the same level. Keep going, one hands-free business after another.

**Why it matters:** the plan puts Golden's "Window of Opportunity" into action. He names four ways to make money:

| Way to make money | What it means | Golden's rating |
|---|---|---|
| H.A.J. (Have A Job) | Employee | Worst: income stops when you stop |
| O.A.J. (Own A Job) | Self-employed | Also worst, for the same reason |
| S.M.S. (Sow Money Seeds) | Investing | Second best |
| **O.A.S. (Own A System)** | **A business that runs without you** | **Best** |

The Hands-Free plan is how you get to **Own A System**. Golden bases it on Scripture, citing 1 Thessalonians 4 (work quietly with your own hands) and Ecclesiastes 5:3 (dreams come from a multitude of business).

**How the plan fits with the rest of the book:**
- **Pick a product business over a service business** (ch. 6). A service business sells your hours. A product business can sell 1 unit or 100,000 units in the same amount of time.
- **Use technology for leverage** (ch. 7). Use the internet to market, research and sell.
- **Aim to be a V.M., a "Virtual Millionaire"** (ch. 8). This means having enough business income to live a million-dollar lifestyle, for example income equal to what $1 million would pay at 6%, without needing a million in the bank.
- **Remember that you're in the marketing business** (ch. 9), whatever your product is.
- **Fund it with the "I can finish free" can** (ch. 13). Ten percent of every paycheck goes only toward income-producing assets or businesses. You can spend what they earn, but never the 10% itself. The other cans are: tithe 10%, educate yourself 10%, save for what you want, have fun, and pay bills 50%.

## How to use this

- **Check yourself against both lists.** The mental traps (like valuing security over freedom, or having a renter's mentality) often explain the practical ones (like being paralyzed by a paycheck, or having no assets).
- **Name your current pane in the window.** If all your income comes from H.A.J. or O.A.J., the first step is a part-time business you could build toward $10,000 a month over two years, preferably product-based and leveraged with technology.
- **Start the six cans now.** The "finish free" can is what pays for the move from a job to owning a system.

## Caveats

- Golden also promotes his own programs and network-marketing opportunities in the book, and his income claims come with a "results vary" disclaimer. Check any business opportunity carefully before you invest.
- The faith and tithing framing reflects the author's personal beliefs.
- This summary comes from a scanned (OCR) copy, so some details may be imperfect, especially the missing practical trap #12.
- This is not personalized financial, tax or investment advice.
