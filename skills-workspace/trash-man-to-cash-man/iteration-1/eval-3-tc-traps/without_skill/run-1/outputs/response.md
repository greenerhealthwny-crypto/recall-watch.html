# Myron Golden, *From The Trash Man To The Cash Man*: money traps and the hands-free business

**A note on accuracy first.** I'm answering from memory, without the book in front of me. I'm sure of the book's overall message and of Golden's well-known frameworks. I'm less sure of the exact chapter names, how many traps he lists, and his precise wording. Check the specific labels below against the text before you quote or teach them.

## The book's core idea

Golden spent years working as a garbage man. He moved from that to running seminars and businesses, and he says the move came from changing how he thought about money, not from luck or education. Here is his central argument, which draws heavily on Bible principles:

- Poor people, middle-class people and rich people follow different rules with money.
- Most people are caught in traps that keep them trading time for dollars.
- The way out is to stop being a consumer and an employee. Instead, become a **producer and owner** of income-producing assets, above all a business that doesn't need you there to make money.

## The money traps

Golden describes thinking habits and behaviours that keep people broke or stuck in the middle class. As best I recall, the main traps are these:

1. **The Job (Employee) Trap.** You rely on a single paycheck you don't control. Your income is limited by the hours you can work and by what your employer decides you're worth. Golden's line is that a J-O-B keeps you "Just Over Broke."
2. **The Time-for-Money Trap.** Your income stops when you stop working. You can't scale it, and illness, age or a layoff can wipe it out.
3. **The Debt / Consumer-Credit Trap.** You borrow to buy things that lose value, such as cars, gadgets and lifestyle purchases. That makes you a servant to the lender ("the borrower is servant to the lender," Proverbs 22:7). Interest moves your future income to someone else.
4. **The Consumption (Liabilities-as-Assets) Trap.** You spend raises and windfalls on a bigger lifestyle instead of buying assets. You treat your house and car as "investments" even though they take money out of your pocket.
5. **The Tax Trap.** As a W-2 employee you're taxed first and live on what's left. A business owner can legally spend on business purposes before tax. Golden uses this to show the employee structure itself costs you money.
6. **The Mindset (Poverty-Thinking) Trap.** This means scarcity thinking, seeing money as evil, envying rich people, and believing you lack the education or background to succeed. Golden treats this as the root trap that holds all the others in place. He separates "the love of money," which is the problem, from money itself, which is a tool.

The book may group or name these differently, for example as a smaller set of named traps. The themes, though (jobs, time-for-money, debt, consumption, taxes and poverty thinking), are the ones Golden keeps returning to.

## The hands-free business plan

Golden's answer is to build or own a business that makes income **without your constant presence**. A summary of the plan:

1. **Change your identity from worker to owner.** Your goal is to own systems that do the work, not to do the work yourself. An owner gets paid on what the system produces, not on hours.
2. **Start with what you have, while you're still employed.** Keep your job at first and start a business part-time. Use skills, knowledge or a product you can sell. Grow it until the business income can replace your paycheck.
3. **Solve a problem people will pay for.** Golden argues that wealth is the reward for solving problems for other people. The bigger the problem you solve, or the more people you solve it for, the more you earn.
4. **Learn sales and marketing.** He treats the ability to sell as the highest-paid skill in the marketplace. Every business depends on bringing in customers.
5. **Systematize everything.** Document how the work gets done so other people, automation or tools can deliver the product or service the same way every time. If the business needs you every day, you've built yourself a job, not a business.
6. **Leverage other people's time, money and effort.** Hire, delegate, outsource, and use partners or joint ventures. This breaks the link between your hours and your income.
7. **Create recurring and residual income.** Favour products and models that pay again and again, such as information products, subscriptions, licensing and repeat customers, over one-off transactions.
8. **Reinvest profits into more assets.** Use the business's cash flow to buy or build more income-producing assets, such as more businesses, real estate and investments, instead of spending it on lifestyle.
9. **Give and steward faithfully.** In Golden's faith-based framework, generosity and good stewardship are part of the plan, not an afterthought.

### In short
Get out of the traps that tie your income to your time and your debts. Then build a problem-solving business, systematize it, staff it, and point its cash flow at buying more assets, so that eventually it runs "hands-free" and you become the cash man instead of the trash man.
