# Myron Golden's Window of Opportunity and the Financial House

These two ideas come from Myron Golden's *From The Trash Man To The Cash Man: How Anyone Can Get Rich Starting From Anywhere* (2007). The Window of Opportunity is chapter 3 and the financial house is chapter 4 ("What kind of house do you live in?"). Golden is a former trash collector. The first idea is about **how you earn** money. The second is about **what you do with it after you earn it**.

---

## 1. The Window of Opportunity: four ways to make money

Golden pictures a window with four panes. Each pane is one way to make money, and they run from worst to best.

| Pane | Short name | What it is | Golden's verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H.A.J.** | Have A Job | You work for someone else and trade your time for a paycheck. | One of the two **worst** ways |
| **O.A.J.** | Own A Job | Self-employment, where you are the business (a solo plumber, consultant, or shop owner who has to be there). | Also one of the two **worst** ways |
| **S.M.S.** | Sow Money Seeds | Investing: putting money into things that produce more money. | **Second best** |
| **O.A.S.** | Own A System | A business that runs without you doing the work. | **The best** way |

**Why the first two are the worst:** with a job, or with self-employment that depends on you, **the income stops when you stop**. If you get sick, retire, or take time off, the money stops. Owning a job gives you more control than having one, but you are still trading time for money.

**Why the last two are better:** money seeds and systems keep producing whether or not you are working. Sowing money seeds lets your money work for you. Owning a system goes further, because it is a business with processes, people, products and technology that brings in income without your daily effort.

**How this ties into the rest of the book:**
- His **Hands-Free Money business plan** is about moving to the O.A.S. pane. You build a home business that makes **$10,000 a month within two years** and takes no more than "one finger's worth of effort" to run. Then you start a different business and do it again.
- He prefers **product businesses over service businesses**. A service business sells your time, which puts you back in the Own A Job pane. A product business can sell 1 unit or 100,000 in the same time.
- His **"I can finish free" can** (10% of every paycheck) can be spent only on income producing assets or businesses. That is how a paycheck from the first two panes pays for a move into the last two.

## 2. The financial house: poor, middle class and rich

Golden's second picture is a house with four windows. Money moves through these windows:

1. **Income**: money coming in.
2. **Outgo**: money going out for living expenses.
3. **WRL (Wealth Reducing Liabilities)**: things you buy that take money from you over time, usually with debt. Examples are a car loan, an oversized house payment, credit card purchases and toys.
4. **IPA (Income Producing Assets)**: things that put money into your pocket. Examples are a rental building, a business, or investments that pay you.

What makes someone poor, middle class or rich is **which windows their money flows through**.

### The poor man's house
Income comes in one window and **every dime goes straight out the Outgo window**. Nothing is left over, nothing is built, and the next paycheck has to cover the next round of bills. There are no assets, only survival.

```
Income  ──►  Outgo
```

### The middle-class house
Income comes in, pays the bills, and the rest goes into **wealth reducing liabilities**. The middle class buys the bigger house, the newer car and the nicer things, often on credit, to **keep up with the Joneses**. It looks like prosperity, but each liability adds payments that pull more money out. Many middle-class people **live in debt**, and their raises go into bigger liabilities.

```
Income  ──►  Outgo
        ──►  Wealth Reducing Liabilities (debt payments)  ──►  more Outgo
```

### The rich man's house
Income comes in, and the rich send part of it through the **Income Producing Assets** window first, buying something like a rental building or a business. Those assets produce **cash flow**, which comes back in as more income. Over time **the assets' income covers the living expenses**, and the person no longer depends on a job. Liabilities, if they buy them at all, are paid for out of the assets' income rather than the paycheck.

```
Income  ──►  Income Producing Assets  ──►  cash flow ──► back to Income
        ──►  Outgo (eventually covered by asset income)
```

### The main point
In Golden's words, the difference is **not how much you make but what you do with money after you make it**. A high earner can live in the poor man's house or the middle-class house. A modest earner who keeps buying income producing assets can move into the rich man's house.

---

## 3. How the two ideas fit together

- The **Window of Opportunity** is about **where income comes from**: job, self-employment, investments, or systems.
- The **financial house** is about **where income goes**: outgo, liabilities, or assets.

Money from the H.A.J. or O.A.J. panes is the starting fuel. If you put it into the IPA window, through investing (S.M.S.) or by building a business (O.A.S.), you gradually move your income into the better panes and your house becomes the rich man's house.

## 4. Applying it to yourself

1. **Find your pane.** List each source of income and label it H.A.J., O.A.J., S.M.S. or O.A.S. Most people find that all of it comes from the first two.
2. **Draw your house.** For one month, sort where every dollar went: Outgo, WRL (debt payments, depreciating purchases), or IPA. One test is to ask whether a thing puts money into your pocket or takes it out.
3. **Divert money to the IPA window.** Golden's six-can system sets aside 10% ("I can finish free") only for income producing assets, along with a tithe (10%), self-education (10%), savings, fun, and 50% for bills.
4. **Stop adding liabilities.** Golden's practical money traps include having "no financial assets, only wealth-reducing liabilities" and letting "bad debt use them" instead of using good debt.
5. **Plan your move to a system.** Look for a product-based business you could leverage with technology so it works without you.

---

### Caveats
- The book promotes the author's own businesses, network marketing and programs, and its income claims include a disclaimer that results vary. Check any business opportunity carefully before putting money into it.
- The labels "poor," "middle class" and "rich" are Golden's teaching shorthand for money habits. They are not economic definitions.
- This is a summary of the book's ideas, not personal financial, tax or investment advice.
