# Dan Koe's Formula, as a Worksheet You Can Fill In Today

This follows **"The Formula"** from Part II (*Reinvent Yourself*) of Dan Koe's *The Art of Focus* (KOE Press, 2023). The sequence is Koe's: **anti-vision, then vision, then purpose, then a big goal broken into yearly, monthly, weekly and daily goals.** The book does not give a numbered question list, so the prompts below are my working prompts built on how he describes each step. Where I add my own suggestions (time boxes, formats), I say so.

**Why bother, in Koe's framing:** most people are "non-player characters" who follow an accepted web of expectations and drift. A game gives you clarity through one big goal and a descending series of smaller goals. In his words, *"a plan creates clarity, and clarity creates action."* Nobody else can plan your life for you. This exercise is how you write your own game.

**Set-up (my suggestion):** about 60 to 90 minutes, pen and paper or a blank document, no phone. Write fast. Don't edit. Honesty matters more than polish here.

---

## Step 1: Anti-vision (about 20 to 30 minutes)

Koe asks you to describe, in detail, **the life you refuse to live**: how you don't want to look, feel and act, the money that would be too little, the places you'd hate to be in. Keep going **until you feel uncomfortable**. Then use that discomfort as fuel. The point isn't to wallow. It's to make the cost of drifting feel real.

Answer each of these in 3 to 5 sentences, in the present tense, as if it's five or ten years from now and nothing changed:

1. **Body and appearance:** If I keep my current habits, how do I look and move? How do I feel in my body when I wake up?
2. **Mind and emotion:** What is my default mood? What do I worry about at 2 a.m.? What do I numb myself with?
3. **Behaviour:** How do I spend a normal Tuesday evening? What do I keep saying I'll "start next month"?
4. **Money:** What income feels insultingly small to me, given what I'm capable of? What can't I afford, and how does that feel?
5. **Work:** What job or role would it crush me to still be doing? Who am I reporting to? What am I making for someone else?
6. **Environment and people:** Where do I live, and who is around me? Which conversations do I dread?
7. **The cost:** What do the people who matter to me miss out on because I stayed this way?
8. **The sentence I'd hate to hear:** Finish this line: "At the end of my life, I regret that I never ______."

**Check:** if you don't feel at least a little sick reading it back, it's too vague. Add specifics: numbers, places, names, scenes.

---

## Step 2: Vision (about 20 to 30 minutes)

Now flip it. Using the anti-vision as raw material, write the future you **do** want. Koe says it should be **big, not limited by your current beliefs, and emotionally compelling**. If it feels "realistic", it's probably too small.

1. **Inverse of the anti-vision:** For each of the eight answers above, write the opposite. What does my body, mood, Tuesday evening, income, work, environment and legacy look like at my best?
2. **An ideal day:** Walk through one ordinary day, from waking to sleep, in that future. Where am I? What do I do in the first two hours? Who do I talk to?
3. **Work I'd do unpaid:** What problems would I happily work on even if no one paid me? (This links to what Koe calls specific knowledge in Part III: the curiosity school trained out of you.)
4. **Who I've become:** What three words would people who know me well use to describe me then?
5. **The unreasonable version:** If I knew I couldn't fail, what would I attempt that I'm currently too embarrassed to say out loud?

**"Dance between the two."** Koe's point is that you keep both documents and move between them. The anti-vision pushes you away from what you fear, and the vision pulls you toward what you want. Together they help you make better small decisions each day. *My suggestion:* keep a one-line summary of each where you'll see it daily (phone lock screen, notebook cover).

---

## Step 3: Purpose and the path of mastery (about 10 to 15 minutes)

Koe turns the vision into a **purpose**, then into a **big goal**, the "final boss" of your game. Purpose here is less a grand calling and more a direction you can keep walking.

1. **The theme:** Reading my vision, what single thread runs through it? (For example: "freedom through building things", "health so I can be present for my family", "teaching what I've learned".)
2. **Purpose statement:** Finish this line: "I am becoming someone who ______ so that ______."
3. **The big goal:** What one concrete outcome, about 3 to 10 years out, would prove I'm living the vision? Make it something you could point to as done or not done.
4. **The skill path:** What skills would the person who reached that goal have mastered? List 2 or 3.

---

## Step 4: Goals from yearly to daily (about 15 to 20 minutes)

This is where Koe's game metaphor becomes practical. You break the big goal into **yearly, monthly, weekly and daily goals**. Each level gives you clarity, **frames your attention, and repels distractions**. Anything that doesn't serve the current level is noise.

Fill in this cascade, top to bottom:

| Level | Prompt | Your answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Big goal** (3 to 10 years) | From Step 3 | |
| **Yearly** | What must be true 12 months from now for me to be clearly on track? (1 to 3 outcomes) | |
| **Monthly** | What milestone this month moves the yearly goal forward the most? | |
| **Weekly** | What 2 or 3 deliverables would make this week a win? | |
| **Daily** | What is the one task I'll do tomorrow, in my best-energy hours, before anything else? | |

Then two more prompts:

- **Today's move:** What can I do in the next 24 hours, even for 30 minutes, that proves the vision is real? Do it today.
- **Distraction list:** Which three things will I say "no" to this week because they don't serve this cascade?

**Tie-in to energy (Part III, "fill / empty / use"):** Koe suggests designing your days around how you **fill** your mind (inputs), **empty** it (reflection, rest), and **use** it (focused output). Schedule the daily goal for your highest-energy hours, and treat the whole setup as a lifelong experiment, not a fixed plan.

---

## Keeping it alive

- **Self-discipline, in Koe's definition,** is when the desire to become your highest self outweighs the desire for cheap pleasures. The anti-vision and vision are how you make that desire strong enough.
- **Review rhythm (my suggestion):** re-read both documents each week when you set weekly goals. Rewrite the vision whenever it stops moving you. Each year, redo the whole exercise.
- **Expect your "level of mind" to rise.** Koe argues that every real problem asks you to widen your perspective and let go of fixed expectations. Your vision will likely change as you grow, and that's part of the process.

### A note on sources
The structure (anti-vision, vision, purpose, then yearly to daily goals), the "dance between the two" idea, the NPC and game framing, and the definition of self-discipline come from Koe's *The Art of Focus*. The specific numbered questions, time boxes and table are my own working version of his method, not a list printed in the book. The book also leans philosophical and spiritual in places (Wilber, Tolle, Watts). Take what's useful for your own outlook.
