# Dan Koe's Focus Formula: A Working Session You Can Do Today

In *The Art of Focus*, Dan Koe treats life like a well-designed video game. A game holds your attention because it has a clear **win condition**, a real **lose condition**, a **mission**, smaller **quests**, and **rules** that limit you. When those parts are missing from your life, your attention leaks into whatever is loudest: scrolling, other people's goals, and busywork.

The formula, in order:

1. **Anti-vision**: what's at stake. The life you refuse to live.
2. **Vision**: the win condition. The life you're building.
3. **Purpose**: the "why" that ties the two together and points your energy.
4. **Goals**: the mission, broken down from yearly to monthly to weekly to daily.
5. **Constraints**: the rules that protect your focus.

Set aside 60 to 90 minutes. Write by hand or in one document. Don't edit as you go. Honesty matters more than polish.

---

## Step 1: Anti-Vision (the stakes)

Koe argues that most people aren't moved by a nice vision alone. They change when the pain of staying the same becomes vivid. The anti-vision makes that pain concrete so you can't ignore it.

**Prompts (answer in full sentences):**

1. Picture an average Tuesday five years from now if nothing changes. When do you wake up? How do you feel? What do you do for work? Who's around you?
2. Now ten years. What does your body look like? Your bank account? Your relationships?
3. What do you complain about over and over without acting on it?
4. What would you be embarrassed to admit to a younger version of yourself?
5. Which people in your life are living a version of this you don't want? What habits got them there?
6. What do you do daily that, if it continued for a decade, would quietly ruin you?
7. Finish this sentence five times: "I refuse to become someone who..."

**Then compress it:** write one paragraph (5 to 7 sentences) that sums up your anti-vision. Read it out loud. If it doesn't sting a little, you're being too polite. Go darker and more specific.

---

## Step 2: Vision (the win condition)

The vision is the opposite pole. It doesn't have to be perfect or permanent. Koe stresses that your vision will change as you grow. It only has to be clear enough to pull you forward now.

**Prompts:**

1. Describe your ideal average Tuesday three to five years from now, hour by hour. Not a vacation day: a normal day you'd love to repeat.
2. What work are you doing? What problems are you solving, and for whom?
3. How much money do you need for that life, really? Put a number on it.
4. What does your health look like? Your energy? Your mind?
5. Who are you spending time with? What are those relationships like?
6. What skills have you mastered by then?
7. What do you own, and what have you deliberately chosen not to own?
8. Finish this sentence five times: "I am becoming someone who..."

**Then compress it:** one paragraph, present tense, as if it's already true. Keep it next to your anti-vision. The space between the two is where your focus comes from: one pushes you, one pulls you.

---

## Step 3: Purpose (the direction)

Koe treats purpose as something you **build through action**, not something you discover sitting still. You don't wait to "find your passion." You pick a direction that moves you away from the anti-vision and toward the vision, and you refine it as you learn. A useful purpose is often simply: *escaping the anti-vision and moving toward the vision, in a way that helps others do the same.*

**Prompts:**

1. Look at both paragraphs. What's the core problem you're solving in your own life? (For example, "I feel trapped by work I didn't choose.")
2. Who else has that same problem? You, two or three years ago, is often the best answer.
3. What topics do you learn about for fun, even when no one is paying you?
4. What have people always asked you for help with?
5. If you spent the next year getting very good at one thing that serves both the vision and other people, what would it be?
6. Write a single purpose statement in this shape: "I'm building **[the life in my vision]** by **[the skill or work]**, and helping **[people like my past self]** do the same."

Don't agonize. A rough purpose you act on beats a perfect one you're still thinking about.

---

## Step 4: Goals, from Yearly to Daily (the mission and quests)

This is where the game gets playable. Every level should serve the one above it. If a task doesn't connect upward, question it.

### 4a. The 1-Year Goal (the mission)

Pick **one** main goal for the next 12 months: the one that, if you hit it, makes everything else easier or unnecessary. Koe favors a single focus over a scattered list.

**Prompts:**

1. If only one thing changed in the next year, which change would get you closest to the vision?
2. Make it measurable. What number or result proves you did it? (Revenue, body weight, a published book, a launched product, a skill level.)
3. Why does this matter? Link it back to one line from your anti-vision and one from your vision.
4. What would you have to give up to hit it?

**Write:** "By [date one year from now], I will [specific, measurable result]."

### 4b. The 1-Month Goal (the boss fight)

Break the year into a project you can finish in about 30 days. It should be a real milestone, not just "work on it."

**Prompts:**

1. What's the single biggest bottleneck between you and the 1-year goal right now?
2. What project, finished in 30 days, would remove or weaken that bottleneck?
3. How will you know on day 30 that you won?

**Write:** "By [date], I will have [finished project or result]."

### 4c. The Weekly Goal (the quest)

**Prompts:**

1. Which part of the monthly project, finished this week, moves it furthest?
2. What two or three outputs would make this week a clear win? (Things you can point to, not vague effort.)
3. What could derail this week, and how will you prevent it?

**Write:** "This week, I will finish [1 to 3 concrete outputs]."

### 4d. Daily Priorities (the levers)

Koe's point here is that the day is where focus actually happens. Don't plan ten tasks. Pick the **one to three levers** that move the weekly goal, and do the most important one first, before you react to messages or feeds.

**Prompts (answer every morning, or tonight for tomorrow):**

1. What one task, if I finish it today, makes today a win?
2. What are the other two (at most) that support it?
3. When exactly will I do the main one? Block 90 minutes to 4 hours of deep work for it.
4. What will I *not* do today?

**Tonight's review (5 minutes):**
- Did I do the main lever? If not, what got in the way?
- Did today move me toward the vision or the anti-vision?
- What's tomorrow's lever?

---

## Step 5: Constraints (the rules of the game)

Games are fun because of their rules. Without limits, attention spreads thin. Koe frames constraints as freeing: they remove options that drain you, so the important work becomes the easiest thing to do.

**Prompts:**

1. What are your top three sources of distraction? Write one rule for each. (For example, "No phone in the bedroom," "No social apps before noon.")
2. What will you say no to for the next year so you can say yes to the main goal?
3. What's your non-negotiable deep-work block each day, and where does it happen?
4. What are your minimum standards for sleep, movement, and food, so your energy holds up?
5. Who could hold you to these rules?

---

## Put It on One Page

When you're done, collect everything onto one sheet you'll see every day:

| Layer | Your answer |
|---|---|
| Anti-vision | *(one paragraph)* |
| Vision | *(one paragraph)* |
| Purpose | *(one sentence)* |
| 1-year goal | *(one measurable result)* |
| 1-month goal | *(one project)* |
| This week | *(1 to 3 outputs)* |
| Today's lever | *(one task)* |
| Constraints | *(3 to 5 rules)* |

## How to Keep It Alive

- **Daily:** read the vision and anti-vision in the morning, pick the lever, and do it first.
- **Weekly:** review the week, set next week's outputs, and check that they still serve the month.
- **Monthly:** decide whether you won the boss fight, then set the next one.
- **Quarterly or when stuck:** rewrite the vision and anti-vision. As you grow, your view of what's possible grows, and the vision should expand with it. Koe expects this. A vision that never changes usually means you stopped learning.

The point of all this isn't the document. It's that when you sit down tomorrow, you already know what matters, and anything else is easier to ignore.
