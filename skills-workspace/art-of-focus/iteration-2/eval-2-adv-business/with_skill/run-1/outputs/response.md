# Thrift Furniture Flipping as a Dan Koe One-Person Business

In Part III of *The Art of Focus* (KOE Press, 2023), Dan Koe makes a simple argument. **"The most profitable niche is you"**: the overlap of your skills, interests and experience. You start as **one person**, not a startup. You turn a personal **project** into a public **product**. And you stack a few skills (**message, medium, and results-oriented skills**) so you can capture attention and solve real problems.

You already have the two raw ingredients he cares about: a practical skill (flipping furniture) and a pull toward **talking about it** (the message). Here's how his framework maps onto that. The specific business ideas below are my applications of his ideas, not examples from the book.

---

## 1. Start from "you are the niche", not "niche down"

Koe warns against "niching down" into work you'd hate just because it looks profitable. Instead, solve problems **you've already solved for yourself**.

Your niche isn't "furniture". It's **you**: someone who finds cheap pieces, sees what they could be, fixes them, and sells them for a profit, and who enjoys explaining how. Ask yourself:

- What did I struggle with when I started? (Spotting good pieces, pricing, refinishing, photos, selling, hauling, finding space?)
- What other interests overlap with this? (Design, sustainability, side income, DIY, small spaces, history of styles?)
- Who was I two years ago? That person is your first audience.

Koe calls this **specialized generalism**: get obsessed with one corner of reality and stack skills around it. A furniture flipper who can also teach, write and sell is much rarer than one who only flips.

---

## 2. Treat the flipping as a public project ("project to product")

Koe's line: *"Everyone is an entrepreneur; some just choose to get paid for the problems they solve in their personal life."* He argues you become an entrepreneur when you **take your projects public**.

Practically, every flip is already a project with a story:
- **Before:** where you found it, what you paid, what was wrong with it.
- **Process:** the decisions, mistakes and techniques.
- **After:** the finished piece, what it sold for, what you'd do differently.

Document flips instead of just doing them. That record turns into your content, your proof, and later your products. Koe also stresses **learning by doing** and becoming a builder. Each flip makes you better at the craft and at explaining it.

---

## 3. Build the skill stack

### The message
Koe says a message must be **relatable, understandable or actionable**, and you have to learn to capture attention. For you, that might look like:
- **Relatable:** "I paid $15 for this dresser and almost threw it out."
- **Understandable:** why one finish works and another peels; how to tell solid wood from veneer.
- **Actionable:** "5 things I check in 30 seconds at a thrift store."

### The medium
*"In business, distribution equals freedom."* Pick one or two places to publish regularly. Furniture is visual, so short video and photo platforms are a natural fit (my suggestion). A newsletter or written posts let you go deeper and keep your audience over time. The point is to publish where people can find you, rather than relying only on marketplace listings.

### Results-oriented skills
Koe names **writing, speaking, marketing and selling**. He says the message and medium are evergreen, while these skills change with the landscape. For a flipper, that means:
- writing listings and captions that sell,
- explaining your process clearly on camera,
- understanding what buyers and followers want,
- getting comfortable with selling, both pieces and later your own products.

### Creative problem-solving
This is the core of flipping already: seeing what a broken, dated piece could become.

---

## 4. Turn it into a one-person business (the vessel)

Koe's model is **one person, low overhead, with income built on what you know and share**, not hiring a team or chasing a giant company. A possible ladder for you (my application, not a blueprint from the book):

1. **Flips (cash today):** keep selling pieces. This funds everything and gives you a steady stream of stories.
2. **Audience (attention):** post every flip's before, process and after. Teach one lesson per post.
3. **Small digital products (knowledge):** e.g. a sourcing checklist, a pricing guide, a beginner's refinishing guide, or a "first flip" walkthrough. These solve problems you've already solved for yourself.
4. **Higher-touch offers:** a course or cohort for new flippers, 1:1 coaching, or paid workshops. Some flippers also do commissions or styling for clients.
5. **Partnerships:** once there's an audience, brands (paints, tools, hardware) may want to work with you.

Physical flipping has natural limits: your time, space, and how far you can haul things. Teaching what you know can scale past those limits. That's the leverage Koe points to when he talks about the internet giving individuals unlimited reach.

---

## 5. Use value creation as your compass

Koe describes a chain: **information → awareness of opportunity → valuable ideas → behaviour change that ripples outward.** Your content works if someone watches it and then *does* something: buys their first $20 chair, saves a piece from landfill, or makes their first $200 side profit. Aim each post at that kind of change, not just at views.

---

## 6. Design your days around focus

From the same book, **fill / empty / use**:
- **Fill:** study styles, prices, refinishing methods and other creators.
- **Empty:** walks, reflection, time away from screens, so ideas can come.
- **Use:** do your most focused work (filming, writing, product building) in your **highest-energy hours**, and save sourcing and sanding for other times.

Treat the whole thing as an experiment, as Koe suggests. Try formats, watch what lands, and adjust.

---

## A 30-day starting plan (my suggestion)

- **Week 1:** Write a short anti-vision and vision for this business (Koe's formula from Part II). List 10 problems you've solved as a flipper.
- **Week 2:** Pick one platform. Post 3 flips in a before, process, after format with one lesson each.
- **Week 3:** Post every second or third day. Note which questions people ask most.
- **Week 4:** Turn the most-asked question into a free guide or a cheap product. Offer it, and see who bites.

---

## Caveats
- Koe reports his own revenue rising over about five years, from roughly $10K to $3.5M. Those are his results, not typical or guaranteed. Most one-person businesses grow slowly.
- Furniture flipping has real costs and risks (materials, tools, transport, storage, safety with finishes and lead paint on old pieces), and local rules on resale businesses and taxes vary. Check these for where you live.
- The book's ideas are a philosophy as much as a playbook. The specific products and steps above are my application of Koe's framework to your situation.
