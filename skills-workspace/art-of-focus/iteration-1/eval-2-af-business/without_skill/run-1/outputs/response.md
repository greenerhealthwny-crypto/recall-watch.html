# Turning Your Interests into a One-Person Business: Dan Koe's Approach in *The Art of Focus*

*Note: This is written from general knowledge of Dan Koe's book and the wider body of his work (newsletter, YouTube, *The Art of Focus*, 2024). The ideas below are paraphrased, not quoted, and the chapter structure may not match the book exactly.*

## The core premise: you are the niche

Koe's main move is to reject the usual advice to "pick a niche" based on market research. He says you should build around **yourself**: your interests, your curiosity, and the problems you have already solved. Many people can teach the same skill. Only you have your particular mix of interests, experiences and point of view. Your personal brand is the lens you show the world through, not a demographic you target.

He frames this as part of a larger argument. Traditional work and education make people narrow specialists who do other people's goals. In a "new renaissance" economy, a self-directed generalist with a clear vision and the leverage of the internet can make a living from their own development.

## Step 1: Follow your curiosity and pursue self-improvement

- Start with what you already think about, read about and practice: fitness, writing, philosophy, design, business, and so on.
- See your interests as a portfolio, not a distraction. You don't have to choose just one. The overlap of several interests (for example, fitness + philosophy + productivity) is what makes you distinct.
- Work on improving yourself in those areas. The work you put in becomes your raw material.

## Step 2: Solve your own problems first

A central idea: **you are building for a past version of yourself.** The problems you have worked through, and the goals you have reached or are working toward, are your best "market research." People one or two steps behind you want what you have figured out. You don't need to be the world's top expert. You need to be a little ahead and willing to share the process.

## Step 3: Learn in public through content

- Write about what you are learning and the problems you are solving. Koe puts writing first because it clarifies your thinking and builds your skills, and you can repurpose it for every platform.
- Use social media, especially one main platform to begin with (X/Twitter is his usual example), as a free way to share ideas and attract people who resonate with them.
- Make content on a schedule. Treat posts as experiments to see which ideas get engagement. Expand the winners into threads, newsletters, videos and longer pieces.
- He recommends a focused daily routine: a few hours of deep work spent on reading, writing and building, rather than busy work.

## Step 4: Build an audience, then an offer

- Content builds attention and trust. A newsletter or email list turns casual followers into an audience you own.
- Your content shows you which problems people care about most. Turn your best-performing ideas and your own solutions into a **product**: a digital course, a cohort, coaching, a template, a paid newsletter or a community.
- Koe favors **digital products** because they have near-zero marginal cost and can scale without employees. That is what keeps the business "one-person."
- Start with a single offer. Polish it, then add more (low-ticket to high-ticket) only once the first one works.

## Step 5: Use leverage and stay small on purpose

- **Leverage**: code and media (content, software, digital products) let one person reach millions without hiring.
- **Systems over hustle**: repeatable routines for content creation, repurposing and selling, so the business fits around your life.
- **Freedom as the goal**: the point is not to build a large company. The point is to get time, location and creative freedom while doing work that is an extension of who you are.

## Step 6: Build a vision, not just a business

This is where the book's "focus" theme comes in. Koe argues that most people are distracted because they don't have a compelling vision for their lives. When you know what you want to become, the business follows from your personal growth. Each thing you learn becomes content, each solved problem becomes a product, and focus comes on its own because the work is meaningful to you. He calls this approaching your work as your "life's work," a creative project that grows as you grow.

## The loop in one line

**Interests → self-improvement → solving your own problems → writing and sharing what you learn → audience → product that sells your solution to people a step behind you → repeat as you keep growing.**

## Practical takeaways

1. List 3 to 5 interests you already follow and spot where they overlap.
2. Write down problems you have solved (or are solving) in those areas.
3. Pick one platform and publish daily or near-daily about what you are learning.
4. Start an email list early.
5. Watch which topics get the most traction, then build one simple digital product around that problem.
6. Protect a daily deep-work block for reading, writing and building, and cut out the rest.
