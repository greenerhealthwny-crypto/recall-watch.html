# Turning Your Interests into a One-Person Business: Dan Koe's Path in *The Art of Focus*

Part III of *The Art of Focus* (KOE Press, 2023), "Create Your Ideal Future", is about this question. Koe's view is that **you shouldn't hunt for a niche outside yourself. You become the niche.** You build skills around what you're already curious about, work on it in public, and package what you learn into products. Below is his path in order, then a short plan you can use.

---

## 1. Start with specific knowledge: get your curiosity back

Koe borrows the idea of **specific knowledge** from Naval Ravikant. It's the knowledge that comes from your own curiosity, the childlike kind school tends to train out of you. Your interests aren't a distraction from business. They're the raw material for it.

He pairs this with two shifts:
- **Career creation:** design a career instead of picking one off a menu.
- **Understanding the internet:** for the first time, one person can reach an unlimited audience.

## 2. Build a skill stack: "specialized generalism"

Don't niche down into one narrow skill. Koe says to **become obsessed with one crevice of reality** (an area you care about) and **stack several skills to solve burning problems in it.** The stack has four parts:

| Layer | What it means | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| **The message** | Ideas that are relatable, understandable or actionable. You have to learn to capture attention. | Evergreen |
| **The medium** | Where you publish. "In business, distribution equals freedom." | Evergreen |
| **Results-oriented skills** | Writing, speaking, marketing, selling | Change with the landscape |
| **Creative problem-solving** | Joining the pieces in new ways to solve real problems | Ongoing |

The point is that what you say and where you say it are the foundation. The tactical skills on top get updated as platforms and markets shift.

## 3. Turn a project into a product

Koe thinks you learn by **doing**. You learn how to learn, and you become a builder. His key line:

> "Everyone is an entrepreneur; some just choose to get paid for the problems they solve in their personal life."

The move he describes:
1. Pick a **personal project** tied to your interests: a problem you're solving for yourself.
2. **Take it public.** Document it, write about it, share what works. In Koe's framing, this is the moment you become an entrepreneur.
3. **Package the result** as a product (a guide, course, service, newsletter or tool) for people a few steps behind you.

He also says **"You are the ultimate project."** Your own growth is the thing being built and shared.

## 4. The vessel: the one-person business

### Start as one person
Don't try to start a billion-dollar company. Start as one person with low overhead and full control. Koe gives his own revenue path over about five years as roughly **$10K → $100K → $150K → $800K → $3.5M** (rounded). Those are his numbers, not typical or guaranteed results. Take them as an illustration, not a forecast.

### You are the niche
This is the core of his answer:

> "The most profitable niche is you."

Your niche is where **your skills, interests and experience overlap.** Nobody else has that exact mix, so there's no real competition for it. Don't force yourself into a profitable niche you'd hate. **Solve problems you've already solved for yourself.** Your own experience is your credibility and your content.

### Value creation
Koe sees the business as a chain of value: **information → awareness of opportunity → valuable ideas → behavior change**, and that change spreads outward. If your content and products actually change how people think or act, the business grows.

## 5. The groundwork: focus and direction

Koe ties the business back to the book's main idea, that **focus is the catalyst for the good life.** Two tools from earlier chapters support it:

- **The Formula (Part II):** write an **anti-vision** (the life you refuse to live) and a **vision** (the life you want). Turn your purpose into a big goal, then break it into **yearly, monthly, weekly and daily goals.** "A plan creates clarity, and clarity creates action." Your business becomes the vehicle for that vision.
- **Fill / empty / use (lifestyle design):** set up a daily system for how you **fill** your mind (reading, learning), **empty** it (reflection, rest, writing things down), and **use** it (creative output). Put your narrow-focused work, like writing and building, in your **highest-energy hours.**

---

## Putting it into practice: a Koe-style starter plan

1. **List your overlap.** Write three columns: *skills*, *interests*, *experiences (problems you've solved for yourself)*. Circle where they meet. That's your "you are the niche" area.
2. **Pick one crevice and one burning problem.** Be specific about the problem you solved and who still has it.
3. **Choose one medium** and publish on it regularly. Practise writing ideas that are relatable, understandable or actionable.
4. **Run a public project.** Work on the problem in the open for 30 to 90 days and share what you learn.
5. **Learn the results-oriented skills** as you go: writing, then marketing and selling.
6. **Package it.** Turn what you've learned into a first small product or service, see what helps people, and improve it.
7. **Protect your focus.** Use anti-vision and vision for direction, daily goals for clarity, and peak-energy hours for deep work.

---

### A note on framing
This is **Koe's perspective**, and parts of the book are philosophical (it draws on Ken Wilber, Eckhart Tolle, Alan Watts and Csikszentmihalyi). His revenue figures are his own results, not a benchmark. The method itself (build around your interests, work in public, turn what you've solved into products) is something you can test at small scale before relying on it for income.
