# Dan Koe's *The Art of Focus*: pillars, "level of mind," and "fill, empty, use"

**A note on confidence:** this answer comes from general knowledge only. I don't have the book's text in front of me. I'm fairly sure about the book's broad themes. I'm much less sure about the exact names Koe gives his "3 pillars." Check those names against the table of contents before you quote them.

---

## 1. The three pillars

*The Art of Focus* (2024) argues that most people's problem isn't distraction. Their problem is that they have nothing compelling to focus *on*. The book keeps coming back to three connected ideas, and these are the usual way to summarize its "pillars":

| Pillar | What it means in Koe's framing |
|---|---|
| **Purpose / Vision (the direction)** | You need a clear picture of the life you want, plus an "anti-vision" of the life you refuse to live. A goal you chose and care about pulls your attention without effort. Without one, attention goes to whatever stimulates it most: feeds, games, other people's goals. |
| **Mind / Self-development (the lens)** | How you see your problems decides what you can do about them. This is where "level of mind" comes in (see below). You grow by changing your identity and perspective, not by adding more productivity tricks. |
| **Creation / Work (the vehicle)** | Focus is shown through making things: writing, building, a one-person business. Creative work that is also useful to others is how you learn, earn and become who you want to be. Koe's "fill, empty, use" loop belongs here. |

In short: **know where you're going (vision), raise how you think (mind), and turn it into output (creation).** Focus is what comes out when all three line up. It isn't something you force with willpower.

---

## 2. "Level of mind"

"Level of mind" (Koe also says "level of consciousness" or "stage of development") is his version of **developmental psychology models**. He draws on Ken Wilber's Integral Theory, Spiral Dynamics, and Susanne Cook-Greuter's and Robert Kegan's ego-development stages.

The core ideas:

- **Your mind develops in stages.** Each stage is a more complex way of making sense of yourself and the world. A rough sequence looks like this:
  - impulsive or self-protective, driven by immediate wants and survival
  - conformist, driven by fitting in and other people's rules
  - individualist or achiever, driven by personal goals and success
  - strategic or systems-level, where you see the frameworks themselves and can choose or build them
- **Problems are solved from the level above where they were created.** This is a riff on the famous Einstein line. If you stay at the same level of mind, the same problems keep coming back in new forms.
- **What looks like lack of discipline is often a level-of-mind problem.** Someone stuck in a conformist frame can't stay focused on an unconventional goal, because their attention is still tuned to approval.
- **You move up a level by facing friction and taking in new perspectives.** That means hard goals, reflection, journaling and exposure to bigger ideas. Each new level makes the old way of thinking an object you can look at, instead of a lens you look through.

Practically, Koe uses this to argue that the most important thing to work on is how you see things. A higher level of mind shows you options that were invisible before, and focus gets easier because the goal makes sense from where you now stand.

## 3. "Fill, empty, use"

"Fill, empty, use" is Koe's simple loop for **learning, thinking and creating**. It's the practical engine of the creation pillar:

1. **Fill.** Take in high-quality ideas on purpose: books, essays, conversations, courses. Choose them for the problem you're working on, not for passive entertainment. This feeds your mind raw material.
2. **Empty.** Get those ideas out of your head. Journal, take notes, write drafts and posts, and connect the new ideas to your own experience. Emptying is where you actually understand something, and where your own view takes shape. It also clears mental space so you aren't just piling up information.
3. **Use.** Apply what you've processed. Build something, publish it, act on it in your life or business, and teach it. Using an idea tests it against reality. That shows you the gaps, which tells you what to fill next.

Then the cycle repeats. Koe's point is that most people get stuck at **Fill**. They consume endlessly and feel productive, but never empty or use anything. That leads to scattered attention and no growth. Running the full loop turns consumption into skill, output and, over time, a higher level of mind. So this loop is also the everyday mechanism that links the three pillars together.

---

### One-line summary
**Pillars:** vision (direction), mind (perspective) and creation (output). **Level of mind:** your developmental stage of perception, which sets which problems and solutions you can even see. **Fill, empty, use:** consume with intent, process by writing, then apply and create, and repeat.
