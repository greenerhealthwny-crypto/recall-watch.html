# The 3 Pillars, Level of Mind, and Fill / Empty / Use

*Drawn from Dan Koe's* The Art of Focus *(KOE Press, 2023). The book's thesis is that **focus is the catalyst to the good life**. Koe calls focus "dimension-agnostic": the same skill powers spiritual practice, business, relationships, health and creative work.*

---

## 1. The 3 Pillars (Part I, "Find Meaning")

Koe presents the pillars as a **mental feedback loop**. Each one feeds the next, and the third feeds back into the first.

| Pillar | What it means in Koe's framing |
|---|---|
| **Focus** | The first pillar. Koe sees it as the common pattern among successful people. Focus always has a **frame**, which is your perspective. What you focus on, and through what lens, shapes what you can see and do. |
| **Energy** | "Energy flows where attention goes." Mental energy is made of **attention, intention and will**, and **focus is the vessel** that holds and directs it. If focus is scattered, energy leaks away. If focus is concentrated, energy compounds. |
| **Experience** | What is right in front of you. Koe argues you get the most enjoyment from life by living in **direct experience**, not by overthinking the past or the future. |

**How the loop works:** focus directs energy. Directed energy shapes your experience. Experience lived fully, rather than in your head, gives you clearer perception, and clearer perception sharpens your next act of focus. A distracted mind breaks the loop at the first step.

---

## 2. Level of Mind (one of the Part I principles)

Level of mind is one of roughly twenty principles Koe sets out early in the book. Others include systems thinking, self-experimentation, radical open-mindedness, and mastery over misery.

**The idea:** *every problem demands an increase in your level of mind*, meaning **how open your perspective is**. If a problem is stuck, the usual reason is that you are looking at it through the same frame that created it.

**How Koe says to raise it:**
1. **Let go of attachments** to your current expectations and beliefs about how things "should" be.
2. Then solve the problem through **self-education** (learning what you didn't know), **self-experimentation** (testing approaches on yourself), and **persistence** (iterating until it works).

This links directly to the first pillar. Focus has a frame, and raising your level of mind widens that frame, so the same focus can reach more solutions.

**A practical prompt:** when you are stuck, ask yourself: *"What belief am I holding that makes this look unsolvable? What would someone with a wider view try first?"*

## 3. Fill, Empty, Use (Part III, "Create Your Ideal Future": lifestyle design)

Koe treats **lifestyle design** as building a daily system around three ways you work with your mind:

- **Fill:** take in high-quality inputs: reading, learning, studying ideas in your field of interest.
- **Empty:** clear the mind with rest, reflection, walks, meditation, journaling, or getting thoughts onto paper so they stop cluttering your attention.
- **Use:** put the mind to work through **narrow-focused output**, such as writing, building, or creating. Koe's key guidance is to do this in your **highest-energy hours**.

He frames the whole system as a **lifelong experiment**. You keep adjusting the balance and timing to fit how you actually work best.

**A sample day built on this idea** (this is an illustration, not a schedule from the book):
- **Morning (peak energy): Use.** 2–3 hours of deep, single-task creative output.
- **Midday: Empty.** A walk with no input, a brief journal entry, or meditation.
- **Afternoon or evening: Fill.** Reading, courses, notes on ideas you'll use tomorrow.
- **Weekly:** review what worked and change one variable. That is the self-experimentation principle in action.

---

## How the three ideas fit together

- The **3 pillars** describe *how attention works*: focus directs energy, and energy shapes experience.
- **Level of mind** describes *how to grow the frame* your focus works within, so you can solve bigger problems.
- **Fill / empty / use** is the *daily system* that keeps the loop healthy. It gives the mind good inputs, gives it space to clear, and protects your best hours for focused output.

---

*Note: these are Dan Koe's perspectives. Much of the book is philosophical and draws on thinkers such as Csikszentmihalyi, Eckhart Tolle, Ken Wilber, Naval Ravikant and Alan Watts. Adapt the ideas to your own outlook.*
