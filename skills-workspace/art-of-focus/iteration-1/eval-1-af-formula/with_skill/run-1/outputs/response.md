# Dan Koe's "Formula" for Reinventing Yourself (*The Art of Focus*)

The formula is the closing chapter of **Part II, "Reinvent Yourself"**, in *The Art of Focus* (KOE Press, 2023). Part II goes The Self → The Game → The Lens → The Formula. The formula is how Koe turns the earlier ideas into something you can actually do.

## Why it exists: the game and the NPC problem

Koe's starting point is that most people live as **"non-player characters"**. They follow a web of expectations they accepted from parents, school and society without choosing it. If you do nothing, you become what he calls a mental slave: someone else owns your *attention*, even if they don't own your labor.

His fix comes from games. A game gives you clarity because it has **one big goal and a series of smaller goals that lead to it**. In his words, "a plan creates clarity, and clarity creates action." Nobody can write that plan for you. You have to learn the game, take it apart, and play it in your own way.

The formula is that plan. It has three steps.

---

## Step 1: Anti-vision (the life you refuse to live)

**What it is:** a detailed written picture of the future you do *not* want.

**How to do it:** keep writing until you feel uncomfortable. That discomfort is the point. Cover things like:
- How you don't want to **look**: your body, your health, how you carry yourself.
- How you don't want to **feel**: drained, anxious, stuck, resentful, bored.
- How you don't want to **act**: your habits, how you react, how you spend your days.
- **Money:** what "too little" means to you, in real numbers and real situations.
- **Environments** you'd hate to be stuck in: the job, the city, the people.

Make it concrete, for example: *"It's 10 years from now. I'm still in the same job, still saying 'next year', scrolling at 1 a.m...."*

**Why it works:** the anti-vision makes energy. Once you feel that discomfort, you **turn it into fuel** and point it at changing your life, rather than ignoring it or numbing it.

## Step 2: Vision (the life you want)

**What it is:** written from the anti-vision, a picture of the future you actually want.

**Rules Koe gives:**
- **Make it big.** Don't let what you currently believe is possible shrink it.
- **Make it emotionally compelling.** It should pull you forward, not just sound sensible.
- Again, cover how you want to look, feel and act, the work you do, your money, your surroundings and your relationships.

**"Dance between the two":** keep both documents in mind. The anti-vision pushes you away from what you fear and the vision pulls you toward what you want. When a daily choice comes up (open the app or open the draft? stay out or sleep?), ask which future that choice builds. That's how the two lead to **better daily decisions**.

## Step 3: Purpose and the path of mastery (goals from big to daily)

**What it is:** turning the vision into a purpose, then into a chain of goals you can act on today.

1. **Purpose → one big goal.** Boil the vision down to the main thing you're working toward. This is the "final boss" of your game.
2. **Yearly goal:** what has to be true by the end of this year to move toward it?
3. **Monthly goals:** the milestones that add up to the yearly goal.
4. **Weekly goals:** the projects or outputs that finish each monthly milestone.
5. **Daily goals:** the one to three focused actions that move this week forward.

**Why it works:** each level gives you clarity, and clarity **frames your attention**. Once you know what today is for, distractions are easy to spot and easy to turn down. This ties back to Koe's 3 pillars: *focus* is the vessel, *energy flows where attention goes*, and the goals decide where your attention goes.

---

## The engine underneath: self-discipline, redefined

Koe doesn't treat discipline as willpower. To him, **self-discipline is when your desire to become your highest self outweighs your desire for cheap pleasures.** The anti-vision and vision exist to make that desire strong and vivid enough to win. If discipline feels impossible, the usual cause is a weak or vague vision, not a lack of grit.

## A practical way to run it (about 60 to 90 minutes)

| Step | Prompt to write on | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Anti-vision | "If nothing changes, what does my life look like in 5 to 10 years? How do I look, feel, act? What do I earn? Where am I?" Keep going until it stings. | 20 to 30 min |
| 2. Vision | "If I weren't limited by my current beliefs, what life would I build? How do I look, feel, act? What do I create? Who's around me?" | 20 to 30 min |
| 3a. Purpose / big goal | "What single goal, if I reached it, would make the vision real?" | 10 min |
| 3b. Year → month → week → day | Break the big goal down level by level. End with **tomorrow's** 1 to 3 actions. | 15 to 20 min |

Then:
- **Reread both visions** often (Koe's "dance between the two"), especially when you're tempted to drift.
- **Treat it as an experiment.** Koe stresses self-experimentation and iteration. Rewrite the vision and goals as you learn. A new problem usually calls for a higher "level of mind", meaning a more open view, not more of the same effort.
- **Protect the daily goals.** Do them in your highest-energy hours. That's Koe's later "fill / empty / use" idea: fill your mind with good inputs, empty it to think clearly, and use it for focused output.

## Where it leads next

In Part III, Koe connects reinvention to work. Your purpose and goals often point to a personal **project**. Taking that project public turns it into a **product**, and that can grow into a **one-person business** built on the idea that "the most profitable niche is you". If your vision includes work or money, that's the natural next chapter to explore.
