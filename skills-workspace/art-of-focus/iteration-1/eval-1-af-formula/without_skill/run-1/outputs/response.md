# Dan Koe's "Formula" for Reinventing Yourself (The Art of Focus)

**A caveat first.** I'm working from memory here, not from the book's text. Dan Koe goes over the same reinvention ideas in *The Art of Focus* (2024), in his essays ("How to fix your entire life in 1 day", "How to reinvent yourself") and in his videos. The exact wording, the order and the chapter placement in the book may differ from what follows. Read this as a faithful summary of his framework, not as a quote.

---

## The core idea

Koe's view is that you don't reinvent yourself by trying harder. You change **what you focus on**, and your behavior follows from that. People stay stuck because they have no clear direction. Without one, their attention drifts to whatever is easy, loud or comfortable. Reinvention means deliberately building a new direction and then setting up your days so your attention keeps going back to it.

The rough formula:

> **Anti-vision + Vision → Mission (1-year goal) → Project (1-month goal) → Daily levers → Constraints → Identity shift**

You could also put it as: *dissatisfaction gives you energy, vision gives you direction, goals give you structure, and constraints protect your focus.*

---

## Step by step

### 1. Get honest about where you are (awareness)
Start by facing your current life squarely. What do you do on an ordinary day? What do you complain about but tolerate? Which beliefs about yourself keep you where you are? Koe stresses that most people never really look. Until the pain of staying the same is felt, nothing changes.

**Exercise:** Write out a normal day hour by hour. Then ask: "If I keep doing exactly this for 5–10 years, where do I end up?"

### 2. Build your anti-vision
The **anti-vision** is a vivid picture of the life you refuse to live: the future you get if nothing changes. Make it specific and uncomfortable, covering health, money, relationships, regret and the person you'd become.

Why it matters: moving away from pain is a strong motivator. When motivation fades, the anti-vision acts as the "stick."

### 3. Build your vision
The **vision** is the life you actually want: how your ideal day looks, what work you do, how you feel, who you are. It doesn't need to be perfect or permanent. Koe says it should change as you grow. It just needs to be clear enough to pull you forward.

Anti-vision and vision together create **tension**. That tension is where the energy and direction come from. You're running from one future and toward another.

### 4. Turn the vision into a mission (the 1-year goal)
Shrink the vision into one concrete objective for roughly the next year, the thing that would move you most clearly toward the vision. Examples: "Build a one-person business making $X/month", "Get into the best shape of my life", "Write and publish a book."

The aim is **one primary focus**, not five competing ones.

### 5. Break it into a project (the 1-month goal)
Pick the project for the next month that moves the mission forward. Koe treats life as a series of projects. Projects are where you actually learn, because you pick up skills by solving real problems, not by consuming endless information.

### 6. Identify your daily levers
List the 2–4 high-leverage actions you can do every day that push the project forward: writing for an hour, a workout, outreach, deep work on the core skill. These are the non-negotiables. Everything else is secondary.

### 7. Set constraints (protect your focus)
Decide what you **won't** do: the distractions, habits, people, inputs and commitments that pull your attention away. Constraints aren't restriction for its own sake. They're how you spend your limited attention on purpose. Saying no is what makes the yes possible.

### 8. Let identity follow action (the identity shift)
In Koe's framing, identity isn't something you declare. It's the build-up of repeated action and the stories you tell yourself about it. When you do the levers day after day, you start to *see yourself* as the kind of person who does them. The old identity's habits stop fitting. That's the reinvention, and it comes **last**, as the result of focused action, not as the first step.

## The deeper layer: levels of mind and the "game"

Koe often wraps this in a broader idea:

- **Levels of psychological development.** People move through stages: conforming to society, then individualizing, then integrating. Reinvention usually means outgrowing the values and goals you absorbed without choosing them (a "default" life) and building your own.
- **Treat life like a game.** A good game has a clear goal (vision), an enemy or stakes (anti-vision), levels (mission and projects), daily quests (levers) and rules (constraints). When life is set up like that, focus becomes engaging rather than forced, and you get closer to a flow state.
- **Ideas and self-education.** He also stresses taking in high-quality ideas and learning on purpose through projects. A new perspective is often what makes a new direction visible at all.

---

## How to actually run it (a one-day reset)

1. **Morning:** Answer the awareness questions honestly in writing.
2. **During the day:** Set reminders to interrupt autopilot ("What am I avoiding right now?" "Is this moving me toward my vision or my anti-vision?").
3. **Evening:** Write your anti-vision and vision in detail.
4. Turn them into your **1-year mission**, **1-month project** and **daily levers**.
5. Write your **constraints**: what you'll cut or refuse.
6. Review these every week. Change the vision as you learn. Keep the levers going every day.

---

## The one-sentence version

**Get clear on the life you're escaping and the one you're building, compress that into one mission and a current project, do a few high-leverage actions every day while ruthlessly protecting your attention, and your identity will reshape itself around what you repeatedly do.**
