# How to Get Rich (Without Getting Lucky): Naval's Key Principles

*From* The Almanack of Naval Ravikant: A Guide to Wealth and Happiness. Eric Jorgenson compiled and edited it from Naval's tweets, tweetstorms, podcasts and interviews, and Tim Ferriss wrote the foreword. The wording below is Naval's as Jorgenson edited it. The material sits in Part I, "Building Wealth".

The short version, in Naval's words: **"Arm yourself with specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage."** Then play long-term games, own equity, get paid for your judgment and be patient. Each principle follows.

---

## 1. Seek wealth, not money or status
- **Wealth** is assets that earn while you sleep: a business, a product, code, media, investments.
- **Money** is how we transfer time and wealth.
- **Status** is your place in the social hierarchy.

Status is zero-sum and wealth creation is positive-sum. Naval warns that people playing status games attack people who are creating wealth. Ignore them.

## 2. You won't get rich renting out your time, so own equity
"You're not going to get rich renting out your time." A salary or an hourly rate is tied to your inputs, which puts a ceiling on it. You need **equity**, a piece of a business, so that your income can grow without you putting in more hours.

## 3. Build specific knowledge
Specific knowledge is "knowledge you cannot be trained for." If a course could teach it, someone else can be trained to do it, and they can replace you.
- You find it by following your **genuine curiosity and passion**, not whatever is hot right now.
- It often looks like play to you and work to other people.
- Figuring out what you can uniquely provide may take **the better part of a decade**.

## 4. Play long-term games with long-term people
"Pick an industry where you can play long-term games with long-term people." "Play iterated games. All the returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest." Trust, reputation and skill compound when you stay in the game with the same people.

## 5. Take on accountability
Take business risks under your own name. Being publicly accountable exposes you to failure, but it also brings credit, trust and upside. "Society will pay you for creating things it wants" that it doesn't yet know how to create.

## 6. Find a position of leverage
"Fortunes require leverage." There are three kinds:
- **Labor**: people working for you. This is the oldest kind and the hardest to manage.
- **Capital**: money. You need permission from whoever owns the money.
- **Products with no marginal cost of replication**: **code and media**.

"Code and media are permissionless leverage." Nobody has to approve them, and they work for you while you sleep. Naval calls this the leverage behind the newly rich.

## 7. Get paid for your judgment, not your hours
Once you have leverage, judgment matters most, because a good decision gets multiplied. "Earn with your mind, not your time."

## 8. Productize yourself
- **"Yourself"** brings your uniqueness, accountability and specific knowledge.
- **"Productize"** brings leverage.

Ask yourself two questions: *Is this authentic to me? Am I scaling it with labor, capital, code or media?*

**Escape competition through authenticity.** Competition comes from copying other people. Nobody can compete with you on being you.

## 9. Learn to sell, and learn to build
"Learn to sell. Learn to build. If you can do both, you will be unstoppable."

## 10. Value your time and focus on it
Set an **aspirational hourly rate**, well above what you earn now. Spend ruthlessly to protect your time at that rate, and turn down low-value tasks. "You will never be worth more than you think you're worth."

## 11. Make luck predictable: the four kinds
"Without getting lucky" really means changing which kind of luck you depend on:
1. **Blind luck.** Pure chance.
2. **Luck from hustle.** Enough energy and motion that luck can find you.
3. **Luck from being good at spotting luck.** You know a field well enough to notice a break that other people miss.
4. **Luck that finds you because of your unique character, brand and mindset.** Opportunities come to you because of who you are and what you're known for.

**The goal is the fourth kind.** Build specific knowledge, accountability, a reputation and leverage until luck becomes close to deterministic.

## 12. Be patient
"Impatience with actions, patience with results." Move fast on what you do, but give compounding the years it needs. Also, "Inspiration is perishable," so act on it immediately.

---

## Supporting principle: build judgment
The second half of the wealth section explains that leverage multiplies whatever judgment you bring:
- To think clearly, shed your identity and ego so you can see reality as it is.
- "If you can't decide, the answer is no."
- Collect mental models, such as compound interest, game theory and the principal-agent problem.
- "Read what you love until you love to read." Reading is the foundation of learning.

## How to apply this yourself
1. **Specific knowledge:** What feels like play to you but looks like work to other people?
2. **Leverage:** Which kind can you reach today? Code and media need no one's permission.
3. **Equity:** How do you move from renting out your time to owning part of what you build?
4. **Long-term game:** Which industry and which people could you stay with for a decade or more?
5. **Accountability:** What could you put your own name on?

---

*Caveats:* The book is a compilation that Jorgenson edited from many sources, so the quotes are edited versions of what Naval said. This is not financial advice. Naval's own path ran through startups and angel investing, which is unusual and not everyone can copy it.
