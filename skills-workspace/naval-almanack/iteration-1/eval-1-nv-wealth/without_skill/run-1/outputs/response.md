# How to Get Rich (Without Getting Lucky): Key Principles from *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*

The *Almanack* (compiled by Eric Jorgenson, 2020) draws heavily on Naval's 2018 tweetstorm "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)." The idea running through all of it is that wealth comes from **owning things that earn while you sleep, built with specific knowledge, leverage, and judgment, and compounded over a long time with good people.** Below are the main principles, grouped by theme.

---

## 1. Understand what wealth is

- **Wealth is not money or status.** "Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep." Money is how we transfer time and wealth. Status is your place in a social hierarchy.
- **Play positive-sum games, not zero-sum ones.** Wealth creation is positive-sum: everyone can win. Status games are zero-sum, because for one person to rise another must fall. Avoid people who play status games, since they attack wealth creators to raise their own standing.
- **Seek wealth, not money or status.** The goal is freedom: being able to spend your time as you choose.
- **Wealth comes from creating things society wants.** "Figure out what product you can provide and then figure out how to scale it." Society will pay you for giving it what it wants but doesn't yet know how to get.

## 2. Ownership: you won't get rich renting out your time

- "You're not going to get rich renting out your time." Hourly work has a ceiling, and the money stops when you stop.
- **You must own equity**, meaning a piece of a business, to gain financial freedom. Ownership gives you upside, accountability, and returns that don't depend on your hours.

## 3. Specific knowledge

- **Specific knowledge is knowledge that cannot be trained for.** If society can train you to do it, it can train someone else (or build a machine) to replace you.
- It is found by **following your genuine curiosity, passion, and talents**, not by chasing whatever is hot right now. It often looks like play to you and work to others.
- It is usually **highly technical or creative**, at the edge of knowledge, and is often learned through apprenticeship rather than school.
- **Build it by becoming the best at being you.** Combine skills into a unique mix (e.g., someone who is good at both science and selling) so that nobody can compete with you. "Escape competition through authenticity."
- **Learn to sell and learn to build.** If you can do both, you will be unstoppable.

## 4. Accountability

- **Embrace accountability and take business risks under your own name.** Society rewards responsibility, equity, and leverage.
- Putting your name on something exposes you to failure, but it also gives you credit, brand, and the trust that brings leverage.
- "The most accountable people have singular, public, and risky brands."

## 5. Leverage: the force multiplier of judgment

"Give me a lever long enough, and a place to stand, and I will move the earth." Naval describes three kinds of leverage:

1. **Labor:** other people working for you. This is the oldest and most contested kind. It is hard to manage and others must agree to follow you.
2. **Capital:** money to invest or deploy. This is powerful, but it takes permission and trust to get it.
3. **Products with no marginal cost of replication:** **code and media** (books, podcasts, videos, software). This is "permissionless leverage," the newest kind and the one that matters most for the new rich. It works for you while you sleep, and you need nobody's permission to use it.

- **Pick a business model with network effects or zero marginal cost** where possible.
- Leverage multiplies your judgment, so good judgment matters more than ever.

## 6. Judgment and intelligence

- "In an age of infinite leverage, judgment becomes the most important skill." A leveraged worker with good judgment can out-produce an unleveraged one by a factor of 1,000 or 10,000.
- **Judgment means knowing the long-term consequences of your actions.** Clear thinking, honesty with yourself, and reading widely all feed it.
- **Reading is a superpower.** Read what you love until you love to read. Learn the foundations: **microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics, mathematics, and computers**.

## 7. Get paid for outputs, not inputs

- **Earn with your mind, not your time.** The new economy rewards results and leverage rather than hours worked.
- **Inputs and outputs aren't linked** in creative or leveraged work. Become so good at something that you can negotiate pay based on output.
- **Work as hard as you can, but who you work with and what you work on matter more than how hard you work.** Direction matters more than speed.

## 8. Play long-term games with long-term people

- **All the returns in life come from compound interest**: in wealth, relationships, and knowledge.
- Pick partners and colleagues with **high intelligence, high energy, and high integrity**. Integrity is the one you can't do without.
- Long-term relationships and reputations compound. Trust lowers transaction costs over decades.
- **Be patient.** "Apply specific knowledge, with leverage, and eventually you will get what you deserve." It takes time, even when you are doing everything right.

## 9. Productize yourself

- Naval sums it up in two words: **"Productize yourself."** Find what is uniquely *you* (specific knowledge), take responsibility for it (accountability), and scale it (leverage: code, media, capital, labor).
- You should be **too busy to "do coffee"** while still keeping an uncluttered calendar. Value your time highly. Set an aspirational hourly rate (Naval suggests something like $5,000/hour) and outsource or skip any task worth less.

## 10. How luck fits in (without depending on it)

Naval describes four kinds of luck. The aim is to move from relying on the first to creating the fourth:

1. **Blind luck:** pure chance.
2. **Luck from hustling:** moving around and creating chances to bump into opportunity.
3. **Luck from preparation:** noticing opportunities others miss because you are skilled and aware.
4. **Luck from your unique character:** opportunities *find you*, because you've built a reputation and specific knowledge that make you the obvious person to call.

The goal is to become **the kind of person luck comes looking for**, so that "getting rich is not about luck" but about predictable, compounding effort.

## 11. Finding your path

- **Find work that feels like play to you but looks like work to others.** Nobody can compete with you there.
- Explore widely when you're young, then **go deep** once you find your thing.
- **Escape competition through authenticity.** If you're building something unique to you, there's no one to copy and no one to compete with.
- **Know your desires.** Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want. Pick one big desire and pursue it, not many at once.

## 12. Spend wisely, stay free

- **Avoid lifestyle inflation.** Wealth buys freedom only if you don't let your spending absorb it.
- The point of getting rich is to **become free**: free to stop playing money games and turn to what matters to you, such as health, family, peace of mind, and curiosity.
- Naval stresses that **happiness, health, and peace** are worth more than wealth, and that wealth should serve them, not replace them.

---

## The formula in one line

> **Specific knowledge + accountability + leverage (especially code and media) + good judgment, compounded over long-term games with high-integrity people = wealth that doesn't depend on luck.**

Or in Naval's own shortest form: **"Seek wealth, not money or status. ... Productize yourself."**
