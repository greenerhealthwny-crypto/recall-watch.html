# Naval on Luck and Decision-Making (per *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*)

*The Almanack* was compiled and edited by Eric Jorgenson from Naval's tweets, podcasts and interviews, so the wording below is Naval's as Jorgenson edited it. Luck comes up in Part I, *Building Wealth* ("How to Get Lucky"). Decision-making comes up in *Building Judgment* ("Learn the Skills of Decision-Making", next to "How to Think Clearly" and "Shed Your Identity to See Reality").

---

## Part 1: The four kinds of luck

Naval sorts luck into four kinds. Each one depends less on chance and more on who you are than the one before it.

| # | Kind of luck | How it works | How much you control it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Blind luck** | Pure fortune: something good just happens to you. | None |
| 2 | **Luck from hustle** | You stay active and energetic and keep "stirring the pot", so you run into more chances and luck has more ways to *find you*. | Some: it comes from effort |
| 3 | **Luck from spotting it** | You know a field so well that you notice a lucky break that other people walk past. | More: it comes from skill and knowledge |
| 4 | **Luck that finds you because of your character** | Your unique character, brand and mindset draw opportunities to you, so the luck comes looking for you. | The most: it comes from who you are |

**The goal is the fourth kind.** It ties into the rest of Naval's wealth ideas:
- **Specific knowledge.** You build it by following your own curiosity. That makes you the person who can spot luck (kind 3) and the person who is known for something (kind 4).
- **Accountability.** You take risks under your own name and build a reputation, which is how kind-4 luck finds you.
- **Long-term games with long-term people.** "All the returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest." A reputation compounds over time and keeps bringing in opportunities.
- **Escaping competition through authenticity.** "No one can compete with you on being you." Kind-4 luck can't be copied.

The book's larger claim is that you can get rich without relying on luck. You move from kinds 1 and 2, which you can't control, to kinds 3 and 4, which you can build.

**Patience matters too.** "Impatience with actions, patience with results." Luck that comes from character takes years to build up.

## Part 2: Making decisions

In Naval's view, judgment is what you get paid for once you have leverage. With leverage, "earn with your mind, not your time", and one good decision can be worth more than years of work. So he treats decision-making as a skill to learn. Its main parts:

### 1. Think clearly by dropping your identity and ego
To see reality as it is, you have to let go of identity and ego. When a belief is part of who you think you are, you defend it instead of checking it. Clear thinking comes first, and good decisions follow from it.

### 2. "If you can't decide, the answer is no."
This is his simplest rule. If you are still unsure after thinking it over, that doubt is your answer. It fits the big, hard-to-reverse choices best: jobs, partners, moves, investments.

### 3. Get the three big decisions right
Early in life the decisions that matter most are **where you live, who you're with, and what you do**. Put your best judgment there, because a lot of everything else follows from them.

### 4. Choose long-term people and long-term games
When you decide who to work with or what to commit to, prefer repeated games with people you can trust over time. Over many rounds, trust and compound interest pay off.

### 5. Collect mental models
Better decisions come from a toolkit of mental models. The Almanack covers **compound interest, game theory and the principal-agent problem**, among others. These help you see the structure of a situation, for example whose interests line up with yours, or what grows over time.

### 6. Build judgment by reading
"Read what you love until you love to read." Reading is the base of learning. Over time it gives you the models and the knowledge that good judgment depends on.

### 7. Act fast once you've decided
"Inspiration is perishable": when an idea grabs you, act on it right away. Be impatient with actions and patient with results. Decide carefully, then move fast.

---

## How the two connect

Both topics come down to the same thing. **Luck and good decisions both come from who you become.** Kind-4 luck finds you because of your character and reputation. Good decisions come from clear, ego-free thinking, mental models and years of reading. Each one builds on the other: good judgment helps you spot and take luck (kind 3), and taking it well over many years builds the reputation that brings kind-4 luck.

**In practice:**
- Ask yourself what your specific knowledge is, meaning what feels like play to you but looks like work to others. Build that up so you can spot luck in your field.
- Put your name on your work and play long-term games so your reputation can attract chances.
- When you're stuck on a big choice and still can't decide, take that as a no.
- Check where you live, who you're with and what you do before you tune the details.
