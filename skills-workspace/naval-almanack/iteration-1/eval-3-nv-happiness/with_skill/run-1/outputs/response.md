# Naval on Happiness (from *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*)

*The Almanack* was compiled and edited by Eric Jorgenson from Naval's tweets, podcasts and interviews, so the wording below is Naval's as Jorgenson edited it. Happiness is **Part II** of the book. It comes in three sections: **Learning Happiness**, **Saving Yourself** and **Philosophy**.

## 1. Happiness is a skill you can learn
- **"Happiness is a choice you make and a skill you develop."** Naval says you can train happiness the way you train fitness. It is not something fixed you're born with, and it isn't luck.
- He describes happiness as **the state when nothing is missing**. **Peace is happiness at rest.** Happiness takes presence and a quiet mind, not more stimulation.

## 2. Desire is the main thing that gets in the way
- **"Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want."** Each desire is a choice to be unhappy for a while.
- So choose your desires carefully, and **pursue one at a time**. Don't carry dozens of them at once.

## 3. Success won't make you happy, and envy makes you less happy
- Getting what you chase, whether money, status or achievement, does not earn you happiness. External wins don't fix internal states.
- **Envy is the enemy of happiness.** Comparing yourself to others means wanting someone else's whole life, not just the parts you like.

## 4. Habits build happiness
- In practice, happiness comes from daily habits more than from insight. Meditation gets the most attention in the book. Exercise and less screen time come up too.
- Naval's picture of a good life: **"A calm mind, a fit body, and a house full of love."** He sees wealth, health and happiness as the three big things in life.

## 5. Acceptance and the present moment
- Happiness comes from **accepting** reality instead of fighting it in your head.
- The book's philosophy chapters end with **"the present is all we have."** Living for a future reward means always putting life off. Naval puts it this way: **"Retirement is when you stop sacrificing today for an imaginary tomorrow."**

## 6. Saving yourself and philosophy
- **Saving Yourself** covers choosing to be yourself, care for yourself (health, mind), build, grow, and free yourself from what drives you.
- **Philosophy** covers the meanings of life, living by your values, "rational Buddhism" (taking the useful ideas of Buddhism such as presence, non-attachment and seeing through the ego, but holding them with a skeptic's mind), and the primacy of the present.

## The short version
Happiness is a skill, not a reward. Hold fewer desires, don't measure yourself against others, practice habits like meditation and exercise, accept what is, and live in the present. Peace is the goal: *a calm mind, a fit body, and a house full of love.*

---
*How to use this:* if you're unhappy, find the specific desire behind it and decide whether it's worth the unhappiness you've signed up for. Then pick one habit (meditation is Naval's top pick) and keep at it.

*Note:* the quotes are Naval's lines as Jorgenson edited them in the book, which he assembled from many sources.
