# A 3-Day Shared Dinner Plan for ADHD and Anxiety
*Based on Dr. Uma Naidoo's* This Is Your Brain on Food *(2020)*

> This is educational, not medical advice. Food is meant to work **alongside** treatment. Naidoo says diet can work "alongside your other treatments". Don't change any ADHD or anxiety medication because of this plan. Talk to your doctors before starting any supplement.

---

## 1. How the two cheat sheets fit together

Naidoo ends each chapter with a **cheat sheet** of foods to embrace and foods to avoid. Your two lists overlap a lot, and that makes shared meals easy.

**The shared base.** For ADHD she writes that "the Mediterranean eating pattern is a great overall diet to follow" (p. 112). The anxiety list points the same way: more fiber and fermented foods, less of the Western diet. So every dinner below starts from a Mediterranean base: olive oil, vegetables, beans, whole grains, fish or lean protein, nuts and herbs.

| | **Anxiety (you), p. 79–80** | **ADHD (your partner), p. 112–113** |
|---|---|---|
| **Foods to embrace** | High-fiber foods; aged, fermented and cultured foods; tryptophan **with carbohydrates**; vitamins D, B1, B6, A, C, E; magnesium, potassium, selenium; turmeric; lavender, passionflower, chamomile | Mediterranean pattern; a good breakfast; polyphenols (berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, coffee, green tea); vitamins C and B12; zinc, iron, potassium, magnesium; caffeine can help, under 400 mg a day |
| **Foods to avoid** | Western diet (red and fried meat, plus high-GI carbs such as white bread, white rice, potatoes and pasta); caffeine over 400 mg a day; alcohol over the limits; gluten *if* you have celiac disease or gluten sensitivity; artificial sweeteners (aspartame especially) | Gluten *if* celiac or sensitive; **A1 milk caseins**; sugar (she calls it "unfairly vilified" but still says to limit it); food colorings and additives |

**Where the lists overlap, so one meal serves you both:**
- **Magnesium and potassium** are on both lists: leafy greens, beans, nuts, seeds and bananas.
- **Vitamin C** is on both: peppers, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, berries and citrus.
- **Berries, onions and kale** count twice. They are fiber and prebiotics for you, and polyphenols for your partner.
- **Neither of you** needs refined sugar, fried food or ultra-processed food.

**Where the lists pull apart, and how this plan handles it:**
1. **Dairy.** Yogurt is a cultured food on your list, but A1 milk casein is on your partner's avoid list. For fermented dairy, use **goat's- or sheep's-milk yogurt or kefir, or A2 yogurt**, all of which Naidoo names (p. 113). Most of the fermented foods here are non-dairy anyway: miso, tempeh, kimchi, sauerkraut, pickled vegetables and apple-cider vinegar.
2. **Caffeine.** It can help ADHD, but it can worsen anxiety, and both lists cap it at 400 mg a day. The insomnia chapter adds no caffeine after 3 p.m. (p. 178–179). So **dinners are caffeine-free**. Your partner's coffee or green tea belongs earlier in the day.
3. **Carbohydrates.** Your list says to pair tryptophan with carbohydrates. Naidoo's own example is turkey with mashed potatoes. But potatoes, white rice and pasta are on your avoid list as high-GI carbs. This plan pairs tryptophan with **lower-GI whole grains** from her fiber list instead: brown rice, quinoa, pearl barley and buckwheat.
4. **Gluten.** Only avoid it if one of you has celiac disease or non-celiac gluten sensitivity. Naidoo makes that condition explicit on both lists. Every dinner below happens to be gluten-free or easy to make gluten-free. That's a convenience, not a requirement.
5. **Wheat bran.** The anxiety chapter warns that its phytic acid blocks zinc absorption. Zinc matters for ADHD, so leave out bran add-ins.

## 2. The 3-day dinner plan

### Day 1: Turmeric–black-pepper salmon, quinoa, roasted broccoli and Brussels sprouts, and kimchi

- **Main:** Salmon fillets rubbed with olive oil, **turmeric, a pinch of black pepper**, garlic and lemon. Bake them on a parchment-lined sheet pan, the setup Naidoo recommends in chapter 11.
- **Grain:** Quinoa tossed with parsley and lemon.
- **Vegetables:** Broccoli and Brussels sprouts roasted in olive oil, with sliced red onion.
- **Fermented side:** A few spoonfuls of kimchi or sauerkraut.
- **Dessert:** A bowl of mixed berries and cherries with a few walnuts.
- **After dinner:** Chamomile tea.

**What it does for each of you**

| Food | For your anxiety | For your partner's ADHD |
|---|---|---|
| Salmon | Vitamin D, and omega-3s, which are anti-inflammatory and run through Naidoo's whole book | B12 and potassium; omega-3 fish is a core part of the Mediterranean base she recommends for ADHD |
| Turmeric with black pepper | On your embrace list. The pepper helps absorption, which is why she pairs them | Anti-inflammatory polyphenol |
| Quinoa, broccoli, Brussels sprouts | High-fiber foods named on your list; magnesium, B vitamins | Magnesium, iron, vitamin C |
| Onion, berries, cherries | Prebiotic fiber feeds gut bacteria (the **gut-brain connection**) | Polyphenols named on the ADHD list |
| Kimchi | A fermented food that supports the microbiome | Dairy-free, so no A1 casein |
| Chamomile tea | On your herb list; Naidoo cites 1–3 cups a day | Caffeine-free wind-down |

### Day 2: Turkey and chickpea stew with eggplant and kale, over brown rice or pearl barley

- **Main:** Ground turkey browned in olive oil. Add a *sofrito* of onion, garlic, carrot and celery, then cubed **eggplant**, chickpeas, chopped **kale**, cumin and turmeric. Simmer it in broth.
- **Grain:** Brown rice or pearl barley. Barley contains gluten, so use brown rice if either of you is gluten-sensitive.
- **Topping:** A spoonful of goat's- or sheep's-milk yogurt with lemon and herbs, or tahini for a dairy-free option.
- **Side:** Quick-pickled cucumbers or carrots in apple-cider vinegar.
- **Dessert:** Sliced pear or apple with almond butter.

**What it does for each of you**

| Food | For your anxiety | For your partner's ADHD |
|---|---|---|
| Turkey and chickpeas **with** brown rice or barley | **Tryptophan with carbohydrates.** This is Naidoo's "tryptophan myth" point: tryptophan alone doesn't reach the brain well, but pairing it with carbs helps (p. 79) | Protein, zinc, iron, B12 |
| Eggplant, kale, onion | Fiber, vitamins A and C | Three of the polyphenol foods on the ADHD cheat sheet |
| Olive-oil *sofrito* | A healthy fat. Naidoo notes that cooking vegetables in olive oil moves their polyphenols into the dish (ch. 6) | The same benefit |
| Goat's or sheep's yogurt, or apple-cider-vinegar pickles | Cultured or fermented foods | Avoids A1 cow's-milk casein |
| Pear, apple | Both are named high-fiber foods | Potassium, vitamin C |

### Day 3: Miso-glazed tempeh stir-fry with red pepper, broccoli and spinach, over buckwheat or brown rice

- **Main:** Cubes of **tempeh** glazed with **miso**, ginger, garlic and a little rice vinegar, then stir-fried in a small amount of oil with red and yellow peppers, broccoli, carrots, spinach and scallions. Naidoo recommends tempeh and tofu as plant proteins that take on spice well.
- **Grain:** Buckwheat (soba labelled 100% buckwheat is gluten-free) or brown rice.
- **Garnish:** Toasted pumpkin seeds and sesame seeds.
- **Dessert:** A small square of dark chocolate with a few strawberries. The anxiety chapter mentions the flavonoids in dark chocolate. Dark chocolate has a little caffeine, so keep the portion small at night.
- **Drinks:** Plenty of water. Naidoo notes that dehydration can worsen anxiety.

**What it does for each of you**

| Food | For your anxiety | For your partner's ADHD |
|---|---|---|
| Tempeh and miso | Both are on your fermented-foods list. Tempeh is also lysine-rich, which the anxiety chapter mentions | Plant protein, iron, magnesium; dairy-free |
| Peppers, broccoli, spinach, carrots | Vitamins A, C and E, plus fiber | Vitamin C, iron and magnesium |
| Pumpkin seeds | Potassium and magnesium (the anxiety chapter names pumpkin seeds for potassium) | **Zinc** and magnesium |
| Buckwheat or brown rice | Low-GI fiber grains named on your list | Steady energy |
| Dark chocolate and berries | Flavonoids | Polyphenols |

**Glutamate note.** Miso, soy sauce, spinach and tomatoes are high in glutamates. Naidoo lists them as foods to avoid only for **PTSD and OCD**, not for anxiety or ADHD (p. 98, 157). They're fine here unless one of you also has one of those conditions.

## 3. Which nutrients target which condition: quick reference

| Nutrient or food group | Mainly for | Where it is in this plan |
|---|---|---|
| Fiber and prebiotics | **Anxiety** (also gut health in general) | Beans, chickpeas, whole grains, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, pears, apples, berries, onions |
| Fermented and cultured foods | **Anxiety** | Kimchi, sauerkraut, miso, tempeh, apple-cider-vinegar pickles, goat's or sheep's yogurt |
| Tryptophan with carbohydrates | **Anxiety** | Turkey and chickpeas with brown rice or barley (Day 2) |
| Vitamins D, B1, B6, A, E | **Anxiety** | Salmon (D), whole grains (B1, B6), carrots, kale and peppers (A), seeds and greens (E) |
| Selenium | **Anxiety** | Fish; Naidoo also suggests Brazil nuts as a snack |
| Herbs: chamomile, lavender, passionflower | **Anxiety** | Chamomile tea after dinner |
| Polyphenols | **ADHD** (also anti-inflammatory) | Berries, cherries, eggplant, onions, kale, dark chocolate |
| Vitamin B12 | **ADHD** | Salmon, turkey, eggs and dairy alternatives |
| Zinc and iron | **ADHD** | Pumpkin seeds, turkey, chickpeas, tempeh, spinach |
| A1-casein-free dairy | **ADHD** | Goat's, sheep's or A2 dairy, or none |
| Magnesium, potassium, vitamin C | **Both** | Greens, beans, seeds, peppers, citrus, berries |
| Turmeric with black pepper | **Anxiety** list, and anti-inflammatory in general | Day 1 salmon, Day 2 stew |
| Omega-3 fatty fish | The Mediterranean base for both. It is named directly on the depression, PTSD, sleep and bipolar cheat sheets, not on the anxiety or ADHD sheets | Day 1 salmon. Consider fish 2–3 times a week |

## 4. Beyond dinner

- **Breakfast matters for ADHD.** "Start the day right" is first on the ADHD embrace list. Naidoo gives a breakfast smoothie recipe on **p. 261**. One easy dairy-free option is berries, banana, spinach, nut butter and nut milk.
- **Caffeine:** your partner can have coffee or green tea in the morning, under 400 mg a day. You may want less. Both of you should avoid caffeine after 3 p.m.
- **Alcohol, from the anxiety chapter:** women should stay under 7 drinks a week and no more than 1 a day. Men should stay under 14 a week and no more than 2 a day. Naidoo adds, "By cutting back slowly, you will help lower anxiety."
- **Sweeteners:** skip diet sodas made with aspartame. Use stevia, sucralose or saccharin only in moderation.
- **Additives and colorings:** cooking from scratch, as in this plan, removes most of them. Naidoo saves strict approaches such as the **Feingold diet** or the **few-foods elimination diet** for cases where milder changes don't work. Those are best done with professional guidance.

## 5. Safety notes

- **Passionflower** (anxiety herb): avoid it if either of you takes blood thinners such as Coumadin or Plavix, or MAOI antidepressants.
- **Chamomile:** generally safe at 1–3 cups a day. Avoid it with blood thinners or before surgery, and check with a doctor if you're pregnant.
- **Lavender oil supplements:** check with your doctor first.
- **Gluten:** don't cut it out "just in case". If either of you suspects celiac disease or gluten sensitivity, get tested first. Testing generally needs gluten in your diet to be accurate *(general knowledge, not from the book)*.
- **ADHD medication:** don't use food as a reason to stop or change stimulant medication. Naidoo presents diet as an add-on.
- **Keep it balanced.** Naidoo warns about **orthorexia**, an unhealthy fixation on eating "correctly". This plan is a template, not a rulebook.

## 6. How to start

1. Cook these three dinners in your first week and repeat the rotation. Swap in sardines or mackerel for salmon, or lentils or black beans for chickpeas.
2. Each week, add one fermented food and one new colorful vegetable rather than overhauling everything at once.
3. Each of you can keep a simple log: anxiety level for you, and focus and restlessness for your partner. Look for trends over **weeks to months**. That's the timeline Naidoo describes for her patients. Her anxiety patient "Marisol" felt calmer after about 6 months of fiber, fermented foods and family meals.
