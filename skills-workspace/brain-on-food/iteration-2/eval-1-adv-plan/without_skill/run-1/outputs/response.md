# Shared 3-Day Dinner Plan for ADHD + Anxiety (based on Dr. Uma Naidoo's *This Is Your Brain on Food*)

> This is general nutrition guidance drawn from Dr. Naidoo's book, not medical treatment. Food can support ADHD and anxiety, but it doesn't replace medication, therapy or a clinician's care. If either of you takes prescription medicine (stimulants, SSRIs, benzodiazepines, MAOIs, etc.), check with the prescriber before big diet changes or supplements.

## Why one menu can serve you both

Naidoo's chapters on ADHD and anxiety point in largely the same direction, so you don't need two menus:

| Shared principle | ADHD angle | Anxiety angle |
|---|---|---|
| Cut refined sugar and refined carbs; favour low-glycaemic, high-fibre foods | Blood-sugar swings worsen inattention and impulsivity | Sugar spikes and crashes mimic or worsen anxious arousal; high-sugar diets link to more anxiety |
| Eat omega-3 fats (fatty fish, walnuts, flax, chia) | Low omega-3 levels are common in ADHD; EPA/DHA support attention | Omega-3s (especially EPA) are linked to lower anxiety and inflammation |
| Feed the gut (fibre, prebiotics, fermented foods) | Supports the gut–brain axis and dopamine-related signalling | Probiotic and fermented foods are a core of her anxiety advice via the gut–brain axis |
| Avoid ultra-processed food, artificial colours and additives | She highlights food dyes and preservatives as possible aggravators of hyperactivity | Processed food and poor-quality fats are linked to inflammation and worse mood |
| Get enough minerals: magnesium, zinc, iron | Low iron (ferritin), zinc and magnesium are linked to worse ADHD symptoms | Magnesium is calming; low magnesium links to anxiety |
| Limit caffeine and alcohol | Caffeine can be erratic and disturb sleep | Caffeine and alcohol both worsen anxiety; alcohol rebound raises it further |

Gluten note: Naidoo raises gluten mainly for people with coeliac disease or gluten sensitivity (where it can affect mood and attention). The plan below happens to be gluten-light, but you don't need to go strictly gluten-free unless one of you reacts to it.

## Day 1: Salmon, Greens and Quinoa

**Main:** Baked salmon with a turmeric-and-black-pepper glaze (olive oil, lemon, garlic, turmeric, pinch of black pepper).
**Sides:** Quinoa tossed with parsley and toasted pumpkin seeds; sautéed spinach with garlic.
**Starter / side:** Small bowl of plain yoghurt or kefir raita (cucumber, mint).
**Dessert:** A few squares of dark chocolate (70%+) with a handful of blueberries.

| Nutrient / food | Mainly targets | Why (Naidoo's reasoning) |
|---|---|---|
| Salmon (EPA/DHA omega-3) | Both | Attention and focus (ADHD); lowers anxiety and inflammation |
| Pumpkin seeds (zinc, magnesium, iron) | ADHD (zinc, iron), anxiety (magnesium) | Zinc and iron support dopamine function; magnesium calms the nervous system |
| Spinach (magnesium, folate, iron) | Both | Folate and magnesium for mood; iron for attention |
| Turmeric + black pepper (curcumin) | Anxiety mainly | Anti-inflammatory; she recommends pairing it with pepper to boost absorption |
| Yoghurt or kefir (probiotics) | Anxiety mainly | Gut–brain axis; fermented foods linked to lower social anxiety |
| Quinoa (complex carbs, protein) | Both | Steady blood sugar; keeps the meal low-glycaemic |
| Dark chocolate + blueberries (polyphenols, flavonoids) | Both | Antioxidants; small amounts of cocoa linked to better mood; berries support cognition |

## Day 2: Chickpea and Vegetable Curry with Brown Rice

**Main:** Chickpea, sweet potato and cauliflower curry in coconut milk with ginger, garlic, turmeric and cumin.
**Sides:** Brown rice (or cauliflower rice to keep it lower-glycaemic); side of kimchi or sauerkraut.
**Garnish:** Fresh coriander, squeeze of lime, toasted cashews.
**Drink:** Chamomile or another caffeine-free herbal tea after dinner.

| Nutrient / food | Mainly targets | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chickpeas (fibre, protein, folate, tryptophan) | Both | Prebiotic fibre for the gut; protein steadies attention; tryptophan is a serotonin building block |
| Sweet potato (complex carbs, vitamin B6) | Anxiety mainly | B6 helps make serotonin and GABA; slow-release carbs |
| Cauliflower and other crucifers (fibre) | Both | Feeds gut bacteria; low-glycaemic bulk |
| Cashews (magnesium, zinc) | Both | Magnesium calms (anxiety); zinc supports attention (ADHD) |
| Kimchi or sauerkraut (live cultures) | Anxiety mainly | Fermented foods are central to her anxiety plan |
| Ginger, turmeric, cumin (spices) | Anxiety mainly | Anti-inflammatory; she lists spices as brain-friendly |
| Chamomile | Anxiety | Mild calming effect; also keeps the evening caffeine-free for both |

## Day 3: Lean Turkey (or Lentil) Bowls with Roasted Vegetables

**Main:** Grilled turkey or chicken thigh (or lentils for a plant-based night), seasoned with rosemary and garlic.
**Sides:** Roasted broccoli, red pepper and Brussels sprouts with olive oil; a big salad of rocket, avocado, walnuts and a few slices of orange or berries.
**Dressing:** Extra-virgin olive oil, apple cider vinegar (unpasteurised for live cultures), Dijon mustard.
**Dessert:** Plain Greek yoghurt with ground flaxseed, cinnamon and berries.

| Nutrient / food | Mainly targets | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Turkey or chicken (protein, tryptophan, iron, zinc) | Both | Protein supports dopamine and focus (ADHD); tryptophan for serotonin (anxiety) |
| Lentils, if used (iron, folate, fibre) | Both | Non-heme iron plus vitamin C from the peppers and orange to aid absorption |
| Avocado (healthy fats, magnesium, B vitamins) | Both | Steady energy; magnesium and B6 for anxiety |
| Walnuts and flax (ALA omega-3) | Both | Plant omega-3s; she favours walnuts as a brain food |
| Broccoli, Brussels sprouts (fibre, folate, sulforaphane) | Both | Gut health and anti-inflammatory compounds |
| Red pepper, orange, berries (vitamin C, polyphenols) | Both | Antioxidants; vitamin C helps absorb iron (important for ADHD) |
| Olive oil (polyphenols, monounsaturated fats) | Both | Core Mediterranean-style fat she endorses for mood |
| Greek yoghurt (probiotics) | Anxiety mainly | Gut–brain support |
| Cinnamon | Both | Helps blunt blood-sugar spikes from dessert |

## Quick reference: nutrient to condition

**Mainly for ADHD (partner):**
- Omega-3s (EPA/DHA): salmon, sardines, walnuts, flax
- Iron: lean meat, lentils, spinach (with vitamin C)
- Zinc: pumpkin seeds, cashews, poultry
- Protein at meals: steadier attention and dopamine precursors
- Avoiding: artificial colours, preservatives, refined sugar

**Mainly for anxiety (you):**
- Probiotics and fermented foods: yoghurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut
- Prebiotic fibre: chickpeas, onions, garlic, vegetables
- Magnesium: leafy greens, nuts, seeds, legumes
- Tryptophan and B vitamins (B6, folate): poultry, chickpeas, sweet potato, greens
- Turmeric with black pepper; chamomile
- Avoiding: excess caffeine, alcohol, sugar, fried and processed foods

**Helps both:** omega-3s, magnesium, low-glycaemic whole foods, polyphenol-rich produce, olive oil, and cutting ultra-processed foods.

## Practical tips for a shared kitchen
- **Batch-cook** the quinoa, rice and roasted vegetables. ADHD-friendly cooking means fewer steps, so prep once and assemble.
- **Keep dinners consistent in timing.** Regular meals help both blood-sugar stability and anxiety.
- **No alcohol with dinner on these days**, or keep it minimal. Naidoo is clear that alcohol worsens anxiety and sleep.
- **Watch caffeine after lunch.** It affects anxiety directly and can disturb the sleep both of you need.
- **Labels:** skip products with artificial dyes (Red 40, Yellow 5 and similar) and lots of added sugar.
- **Supplements** (omega-3, iron, zinc, magnesium, vitamin D) only after testing and a clinician's advice. Iron in particular shouldn't be taken without a blood test.
