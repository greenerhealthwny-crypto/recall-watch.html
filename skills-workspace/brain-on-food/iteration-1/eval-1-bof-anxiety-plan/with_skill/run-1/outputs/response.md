# Eating for Anxiety and a Nervous Stomach: Dr. Uma Naidoo's Approach

I'm sorry you're dealing with this. Anxiety and gut trouble often show up together, and that link is central to *This Is Your Brain on Food*. Naidoo is a Harvard-trained nutritional psychiatrist and a trained chef. She says we already talk about the **gut-brain connection** in everyday phrases like "butterflies in your stomach" and "a pit in your stomach." Your gut and brain are wired together through the **vagus nerve**, the stress (HPA) system, inflammation, and chemicals made by your gut bacteria. Stress upsets the balance of gut bacteria, and an unbalanced gut can feed back into anxiety. Her anxiety chapter is built around two things: **fermented foods** and **fiber**.

Below is her anxiety "cheat sheet" (book pp. 79–80), with practical notes added.

---

## Foods to embrace

### 1. Fiber (food for your good gut bacteria)
- **Beans and grains:** beans, brown rice, oats, buckwheat, pearl barley, amaranth
- **Fruit:** berries, pears, apples, bananas
- **Vegetables:** broccoli, Brussels sprouts, carrots, artichokes
- **Nuts:** almonds, walnuts

*Nervous-stomach tip (general advice, not from the book):* if your stomach is touchy, add fiber **gradually** over a few weeks and drink more water as you do. A sudden jump can cause gas and bloating. Cooked vegetables, oats and bananas are usually gentler to start with than big raw salads or lots of beans.

*Book caveat:* Naidoo says to skip **wheat bran** specifically, because its phytic acid blocks zinc absorption. Get fiber from the foods above instead.

### 2. Aged, fermented and cultured foods (probiotics)
- Yogurt with live, active cultures
- Kombucha
- Miso
- Tempeh
- Apple-cider vinegar
- Pickled vegetables. Her depression list adds kefir, sauerkraut and kimchi.

Start with a small daily serving, such as a cup of yogurt or a spoonful of sauerkraut with dinner.

### 3. Tryptophan, paired with carbohydrates
- Turkey, other meats, chickpeas

This is her **"tryptophan myth"** point. Turkey alone won't calm you, because little tryptophan reaches the brain by itself. Eating it **with carbohydrates** helps it get there. Her examples:
- turkey with mashed potatoes
- a whole-grain, low-sugar cereal with milk
- peanut butter on whole-grain toast
- cheese with whole-wheat crackers

Hummus with whole-grain pita works on the same principle.

### 4. Vitamins and minerals
- **Vitamins:** D, B1, B6, A, C and E
- **Minerals:** magnesium, potassium, selenium
- **Food sources from the chapter:** Brazil nuts for selenium; pumpkin seeds and bananas for potassium; leafy greens, nuts and seeds for magnesium
- **Lysine-rich foods** may also help: lentils, black beans, quinoa, tempeh, seitan, lean beef and lamb.
- **Other calming compounds:** flavonoids from dark chocolate, and theanine from green tea.

### 5. Spices and herbs
- **Turmeric.** Use it with a pinch of black pepper, which Naidoo recommends throughout the book to help absorption.
- **Chamomile and passionflower tea:** 1–3 cups a day has evidence behind it.
- **Lavender.**

### 6. Water
Dehydration can make anxiety worse, so drink enough water through the day.

---

## Foods to cut back on

### 1. The "Western diet"
- **Bad fats:** red meat and fried foods
- **High-glycemic carbs:** white bread, white rice, potatoes, pasta, and anything made from refined flour

Swap white rice for brown rice or quinoa. Swap white bread for whole-grain bread. Bake or roast instead of frying.

### 2. Caffeine
Keep it **under 400 mg a day**, about 3–4 regular cups of brewed coffee (*general estimate*). Caffeine can also stimulate the gut, so if your stomach is sensitive you may feel better well below that limit. Energy drinks and large café coffees add up quickly.

### 3. Alcohol
Her limits:
- **Women:** under 7 drinks a week, and no more than 1 on any day
- **Men:** under 14 drinks a week, and no more than 2 on any day

In her words, "by cutting back slowly, you will help lower anxiety."

### 4. Artificial sweeteners
She calls **aspartame** "particularly harmful." Use saccharin, sucralose and stevia only in moderation and with caution. Check diet sodas, "sugar-free" gum and flavored yogurts.

### 5. Gluten, only if it applies to you
If you have **celiac disease or non-celiac gluten sensitivity**, avoid wheat products such as bread, pizza, pasta and many alcoholic drinks. Since you have stomach symptoms, it's worth asking your doctor about a **celiac test before** you cut out gluten. Cutting it first can make the test inaccurate (*general advice*). There's no need to drop gluten without a reason.

### 6. Added sugar (from her depression list, and relevant here too)
Limit baked goods, candy, soda, and anything sweetened with sugar or high-fructose corn syrup.

## What a day could look like

| Meal | Example |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | Steel-cut oats with banana, berries and walnuts, or plain live-culture yogurt with berries and a little honey |
| Snack | An apple or pear with almonds, plus chamomile tea |
| Lunch | Lentil or chickpea soup with carrots and a little turmeric and black pepper, plus a slice of whole-grain bread |
| Snack | Hummus with carrots, or a square of dark chocolate with pumpkin seeds |
| Dinner | Turkey or salmon with brown rice or quinoa, roasted broccoli or Brussels sprouts in olive oil, and a forkful of pickled vegetables or sauerkraut |
| Evening | Chamomile or passionflower tea (see the cautions below), and water through the day |

Naidoo's base for overall brain health is a **Mediterranean-style** pattern: vegetables, fruit, whole grains, beans, nuts, olive oil and fish. The anxiety foods fit easily on top of it. Her book has recipes from about p. 240 onward.

---

## How to make the changes

- **Go gradually.** Change one or two things a week, for example cutting coffee by one cup and adding a daily fermented food. This is easier on your stomach and easier to keep up.
- **Track how you feel.** Note anxiety levels and stomach symptoms for a few weeks. Her patients usually improved over **weeks to months**, not days. One patient in the anxiety chapter, "Marisol," felt noticeably calmer after about six months of adding fiber and fermented foods.
- **Don't get rigid.** Naidoo warns against **orthorexia**, an unhealthy fixation on eating "perfectly." If tracking food starts making you more anxious, ease off.

---

## Safety notes

- **Passionflower:** avoid it if you take **blood thinners** (such as Coumadin/warfarin or Plavix) or **MAOI antidepressants**.
- **Chamomile:** generally safe at 1–3 cups a day, but avoid it if you take blood thinners or have surgery coming up. Ask your doctor first if you're pregnant.
- **Lavender oil supplements:** check with your doctor first.
- **Medications:** if you take medication for anxiety or anything else, keep taking it as prescribed. Naidoo uses food **alongside** medication and therapy, not instead of them. Talk to your doctor before adding supplements.
- **See a doctor about your stomach** if symptoms persist, wake you at night, or come with weight loss, blood in your stool, trouble swallowing or ongoing vomiting. These need checking, not just a diet change (*general advice*).
- If anxiety ever comes with thoughts of harming yourself, get help right away. In the US, call or text **988**.

*This is educational information based on Dr. Naidoo's book, not personal medical advice.*
