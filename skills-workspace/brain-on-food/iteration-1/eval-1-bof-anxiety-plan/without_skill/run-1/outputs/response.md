# Eating for Anxiety and a Nervous Stomach: Guidance from *This Is Your Brain on Food*

In *This Is Your Brain on Food* (2020), Dr. Uma Naidoo, a Harvard-trained nutritional psychiatrist, builds her approach on the **gut-brain axis**. The gut and brain talk to each other constantly through the vagus nerve, the immune system and chemicals made by gut bacteria. Much of the body's serotonin is made in the gut. That is why anxiety and stomach trouble so often show up together. Her anxiety chapter aims to feed a healthy microbiome, lower inflammation and keep blood sugar steady.

> **Important:** This is educational information from the book, not medical advice. Constant anxiety and ongoing digestive symptoms deserve a proper check by a doctor. Food can help alongside therapy or medication, but it does not replace them. Talk to your clinician before taking supplements, especially if you take medication.

---

## Eat MORE of These

### 1. Fermented foods (probiotics)
They bring in helpful bacteria, which Naidoo links to lower anxiety.
- Plain yogurt with live, active cultures (unsweetened)
- Kefir
- Sauerkraut and kimchi (refrigerated, unpasteurized kinds)
- Miso and tempeh
- Kombucha (choose low-sugar brands)

*Tip for a nervous stomach:* start with a spoonful or two a day and build up slowly.

### 2. Fiber and prebiotic foods
Fiber feeds your good gut bacteria, which turn it into anti-inflammatory short-chain fatty acids.
- Beans, lentils and chickpeas
- Oats and other whole grains (in moderation; see carbs below)
- Onions, garlic, leeks and asparagus
- Slightly green bananas
- Jerusalem artichokes and chicory root
- Plenty of vegetables in a range of colors

*Caution:* many prebiotic foods are high in FODMAPs. If you have IBS-type symptoms (bloating, cramps), add them slowly. You might ask a dietitian about a low-FODMAP trial.

### 3. Omega-3 fatty acids
Naidoo highlights omega-3s as anti-inflammatory and helpful for anxiety.
- Fatty fish: wild salmon, sardines, mackerel, anchovies and herring (about 2 servings a week)
- Plant sources: walnuts, chia seeds and ground flaxseed

### 4. Magnesium-rich foods
Low magnesium is linked with anxiety in her discussion.
- Leafy greens such as spinach and Swiss chard
- Almonds, cashews, and pumpkin seeds
- Avocado
- Black beans and edamame
- Whole grains
- A little dark chocolate (70% or more, in moderation)

### 5. Tryptophan-containing foods (with complex carbs)
Tryptophan is the building block of serotonin.
- Eggs, turkey, chicken and fish
- Chickpeas, oats, nuts and seeds
- Pair these with a complex carb (such as oats or legumes), which helps tryptophan reach the brain.

### 6. Vitamin D
Naidoo ties low vitamin D to anxiety and mood problems.
- Fatty fish, egg yolks and mushrooms exposed to UV light
- Some sensible sun exposure
- Ask your doctor to check your vitamin D level before you supplement.

### 7. B vitamins
These support nervous system health and neurotransmitter production.
- Leafy greens, legumes, eggs and avocado
- Almonds
- Nutritional yeast and lean animal proteins (B12)

### 8. Healthy fats
- Extra-virgin olive oil as your main cooking and dressing oil
- Avocado, nuts and seeds

### 9. Antioxidant-rich foods
These fight the oxidative stress linked with anxiety.
- Berries (blueberries, strawberries and raspberries)
- Beans, artichokes, kale and spinach
- Brightly colored vegetables
- Green tea, in moderation because it has some caffeine

### 10. Calming herbs and spices
- **Turmeric** with a pinch of black pepper, which improves absorption of its active compound, curcumin
- **Chamomile tea**, which is calming and also gentle on the stomach
- **Lavender**, which she notes as soothing
- **Saffron**, which has some evidence for mood and anxiety
- Ginger and peppermint tea are also commonly used to settle the stomach

## Cut BACK on These

### 1. Added sugar and high-glycemic carbs
Blood sugar spikes and crashes can feel like, or set off, anxiety. Sugar also feeds less helpful gut microbes and drives inflammation.
- Soda, sweetened coffee drinks and fruit juice
- Candy, pastries, cookies and sweetened cereals
- White bread, white rice, regular pasta and potatoes in large portions
- Swap in whole, minimally processed grains and legumes, and eat carbs with protein, fat or fiber.

### 2. Artificial sweeteners
Naidoo flags aspartame in particular, which some studies associate with anxiety. Artificial sweeteners can also disturb gut bacteria.
- "Diet" sodas and sugar-free snacks
- Sugar alcohols such as sorbitol and xylitol can also upset a sensitive stomach.

### 3. Fried foods and unhealthy fats
- Deep-fried foods and fast food
- Trans fats and partially hydrogenated oils
- Too many omega-6-heavy refined vegetable oils compared with omega-3s

### 4. Highly processed foods (the "Western diet")
- Packaged snack foods and ready meals full of additives
- Processed meats (hot dogs, deli meats, bacon) with nitrates
- Naidoo contrasts this eating pattern with a whole-food, Mediterranean-style diet, which is linked with better mental health.

### 5. Caffeine
Caffeine can bring on jitteriness, a racing heart and anxiety-like symptoms. It can also irritate the gut.
- Naidoo suggests keeping caffeine moderate (roughly under 400 mg a day, about 3–4 cups of coffee). If you are prone to anxiety, cut back further.
- Taper slowly to avoid withdrawal headaches. Remember energy drinks, some teas and pre-workout products.

### 6. Alcohol
A drink may feel relaxing at first, but alcohol disrupts sleep, upsets neurotransmitter balance, irritates the gut and can cause rebound anxiety ("hangxiety").
- Cut it down a lot or avoid it.

### 7. Gluten (if you are sensitive)
Naidoo discusses gluten as a possible trigger for gut inflammation and anxiety in people with celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.
- Get tested for celiac disease *before* cutting out gluten, because removing it first can skew the result.
- If you are not sensitive to gluten, whole grains are still fine.

## A Sample Anxiety-Friendly Day

| Meal | Example |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | Plain yogurt or kefir with blueberries, chia seeds and walnuts; chamomile or green tea |
| Lunch | Big leafy green salad with chickpeas, avocado, pumpkin seeds and an olive oil dressing |
| Snack | A handful of almonds plus a square of dark chocolate |
| Dinner | Baked salmon with turmeric and black pepper, roasted vegetables and a small portion of quinoa; a side of sauerkraut |
| Evening | Chamomile, peppermint or ginger tea |

## Practical Tips for a Nervous Stomach
- **Go slowly.** Add fermented foods and fiber bit by bit to avoid gas and bloating.
- **Eat regular meals.** Skipping meals causes blood sugar dips that can make anxiety worse.
- **Drink enough water,** especially as you eat more fiber.
- **Keep a food and mood journal** for 2–4 weeks to spot your own trigger foods.
- **Eat mindfully.** Eating slowly and calmly helps the gut-brain connection.
- **Pair food with other habits.** Sleep, exercise, breathing exercises and therapy all work alongside diet.

If symptoms are severe or you notice red flags, see a doctor promptly. Red flags include weight loss, blood in your stool, symptoms that wake you at night, and panic attacks.
