# Eating for Bipolar Disorder: What Naidoo Says, and the Lithium Cautions

Your brother is right that diet can help, and Dr. Uma Naidoo agrees. Chapter 9 of *This Is Your Brain on Food* covers bipolar disorder and schizophrenia. She is clear, though, that these conditions need serious medical treatment, and that food "can work alongside medications". It does not replace them. So the aim is to build an eating pattern that supports his lithium treatment, not to swap one for the other.

> This is educational information, not medical advice. He shouldn't start, stop or change any medication, or begin a major diet change such as keto, without talking to his psychiatrist first.

---

## 1. Naidoo's cheat sheet for bipolar disorder (pp. 205–206)

### Foods to embrace
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** fatty fish such as salmon, mackerel, tuna, herring and sardines.
- **N-acetylcysteine (NAC):** either as a supplement, or through cysteine-rich foods such as meat, grains, eggs, ricotta, cottage cheese, yogurt, broccoli, red pepper and onion. The supplement dose is not the same as a food dose, so he should ask his doctor before taking NAC capsules.
- **Vitamin B9 (folate):** leafy greens, beans and lentils. The book has a folate-rich spinach dal recipe on p. 271.
- **Minerals:** magnesium and zinc (nuts, seeds, legumes, whole grains).

### Foods to avoid
- **The Western diet:** bad fats (red and fried meats) and high-glycemic carbs (white bread, white rice, potatoes, pasta, refined flour).
- **Caffeine:** keep it under 400 mg a day.
- **Sodium:** for people treated with lithium, keep sodium levels **constant** (see below).
- **Gluten:** only if he has celiac disease or gluten sensitivity.
- **Alcohol:** abstain completely or limit it heavily.
- **Complications with medicine:** grapefruit juice, and foods high in **tyramine** (aged cheese, aged or cured meats, fava beans, Marmite, sauerkraut, soy sauce, tap beer), can interfere with some medications prescribed for bipolar disorder.

### Whole-diet approach
Naidoo writes that "the keto diet has been shown to be a good whole-diet approach for patients with bipolar disorder." In her patient story, "Nancy" switched to keto. She cut back on coffee, avoided gluten, and ate more fish and olive oil. Her symptoms eased over about six weeks.

Naidoo also stresses that keto must be **medically supervised**, especially for people on psychiatric medicines. Early on, people often get "keto flu" (nausea, headache, fatigue, dizziness, insomnia, constipation), and there are long-term side effects to weigh too.

### One more point from the chapter
Naidoo notes that binge-eating disorder and night-eating syndrome are fairly common in people with bipolar disorder. If your brother struggles with either, a strict, restrictive diet may do more harm than good. He should raise this with his care team. She also warns against **orthorexia**, an unhealthy fixation on eating "perfectly".

## 2. What he needs to be careful about with lithium

### a) Keep sodium (salt) steady. This is the big one.
This caution comes from the book itself. The body handles lithium and sodium in similar ways through the kidneys. *(General medical knowledge:)* if his salt intake drops suddenly, his body holds on to more lithium, and blood levels can rise toward toxic levels. If his salt intake jumps, lithium levels can fall and the drug may work less well.

What this means in practice:
- Don't suddenly go "low-salt" or start eating a lot more salt. Small, steady changes are fine. Big swings are the risk.
- Watch out for diets that quietly change salt intake. Cutting out processed food, which is usually a good thing, can lower sodium a lot.

### b) Be very cautious with keto while on lithium *(general medical knowledge, consistent with Naidoo's call for supervision)*
In its first weeks, a ketogenic diet makes the body lose more water and sodium. Keto flu can also bring vomiting. Both can push lithium levels up. If he wants to try the approach Naidoo describes, his psychiatrist will likely want to check his lithium levels more often during the switch.

### c) Stay well hydrated *(general medical knowledge)*
Dehydration raises lithium levels. That includes hot weather, heavy exercise and sweating, and vomiting or diarrhea. He should drink fluids steadily, and call his doctor if he gets sick and can't keep fluids down.

### d) Keep caffeine steady, as well as moderate
Naidoo's limit is under 400 mg a day. *(General medical knowledge:)* caffeine can lower lithium levels slightly, so a sudden big change matters. Quitting coffee abruptly could raise his level. He should change his intake gradually and tell his doctor.

### e) Alcohol
Naidoo's advice for bipolar disorder is to abstain or limit heavily. *(General medical knowledge:)* alcohol also dehydrates, adds to lithium's sedating effects, and can destabilize mood.

### f) Grapefruit and tyramine: does this apply to lithium?
Naidoo lists these as a problem for *some* bipolar medications, not all of them. *(General medical knowledge:)*
- **Tyramine** is a serious danger mainly with **MAOI** antidepressants, where it can cause dangerous spikes in blood pressure. It isn't a typical lithium problem.
- **Grapefruit** affects drugs broken down by certain liver enzymes. Examples include carbamazepine, quetiapine and lurasidone. Lithium is cleared by the kidneys, so grapefruit isn't a known problem for lithium itself.

Many people with bipolar disorder take more than one medicine, though. He should ask his pharmacist whether grapefruit or tyramine matters for **his full medication list**.

### g) Supplements
NAC, magnesium, zinc and fish-oil capsules are all on Naidoo's "embrace" list, and she frames supplements as something to discuss with a doctor. He should check each one with his psychiatrist or pharmacist before starting. *(General medical knowledge:)* some over-the-counter products, such as ibuprofen and naproxen, can raise lithium levels too. That isn't about food, but he should know it.

## 3. A practical starting point

1. **Use a Mediterranean-style base**, Naidoo's default pattern: vegetables, beans, whole grains, olive oil, nuts, and fatty fish two or three times a week.
2. **Add the bipolar-specific foods:** folate-rich greens and legumes, magnesium and zinc sources, and cysteine-rich foods such as eggs, yogurt, broccoli and onion.
3. **Cut back on the "avoid" list gradually:** sugar, refined carbs, fried foods, and alcohol.
4. **Keep salt, fluids and caffeine consistent from day to day**, and tell his doctor before making big changes.
5. **Track mood, sleep and energy over weeks.** Naidoo's patients usually improved over weeks to months, not days.
6. **Keep the care team in the loop**, especially before trying keto or starting supplements.

**Warning signs to know** *(general medical knowledge):* signs of high lithium levels include worsening tremor, vomiting or diarrhea, unsteadiness, confusion, slurred speech and unusual drowsiness. These need urgent medical attention. If he has thoughts of suicide or signs of mania, crisis help comes first. In the US, call or text **988**.
