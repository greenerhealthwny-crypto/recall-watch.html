# Controlling *Listeria monocytogenes* in Cooked, Chilled, Sliced Deli Turkey

> **Scope note.** This is educational guidance drawn from food microbiology principles (Matthews, Kniel & Montville, *Food Microbiology: An Introduction*, 4th ed.) plus standard US regulatory reference points. Turkey breast is a poultry product, so in the US your plant falls under **USDA-FSIS**: mandatory HACCP (9 CFR 417), Sanitation SOPs (9 CFR 416), and the **Listeria Rule (9 CFR 430)** for post-lethality-exposed ready-to-eat (RTE) products. Numbers marked *(std)* are typical regulatory or industry values. **Before you finalize the plan, check each one against the current FSIS *Listeria* Compliance Guideline and the current FSIS Appendix A (lethality) and Appendix B (stabilization/cooling) guidance, and validate it for your own product and equipment.**

---

## 1. Why *Listeria* is *the* hazard for your product

Your process makes cooked turkey exactly the kind of food *Listeria* causes trouble in:

| Property of *L. monocytogenes* | Why it matters for sliced deli turkey |
|---|---|
| **Psychrotrophic.** Grows at refrigeration temperatures, slowly even near 0 °C (32 °F) *(std)*. | Refrigeration slows it but does not stop it. A few cells at packing can become many by the end of a 30–70-day shelf life. |
| **Killed by normal cooking.** It is a vegetative, non-sporeforming cell. | The cook step isn't the main problem. The main risk is **recontamination after cooking**: during chilling, peeling, slicing and packing. |
| **Salt-tolerant**, grows down to about a_w 0.92 and pH about 4.4 *(std)*. | Deli turkey (pH about 6–6.5, a_w about 0.97–0.98, modest salt) supports growth easily. |
| **Forms biofilms and establishes "harborage" in plants** *(book)*. | It persists for months or years in drains, slicers, conveyors, hollow rollers, cracked floors and condensate. The same strain keeps coming back. |
| **Fewer competitors in cooked product** *(book)*. | Cooking removes the background flora that might otherwise hold it back. Recontaminated cooked meat lets it grow with little competition. |
| **Severe illness in high-risk people.** Pregnancy (miscarriage, stillbirth), newborns, the elderly and immunocompromised people; incubation from days up to about 70 days *(book/std)*. | Deli meats are a classic vehicle. Your retail customers include these groups, and the CDC tells them to avoid deli meat unless it is heated until steaming hot. |
| **US regulatory status: zero tolerance in RTE foods** *(book)*. | Any detectable *L. monocytogenes* in product makes it adulterated, which means a hold, destruction or recall. |

**The key idea.** You can't "inspect safety in" by testing finished product *(book)*. Control comes from four things working together: a validated **lethality** step, **cooling** that keeps sporeformers from growing, a strong **sanitation and environmental monitoring** program that stops post-cook recontamination, and ideally a **post-lethality treatment** and/or a **growth inhibitor** as extra hurdles.

## 2. Process flow (verify by walking your line)

```
Receiving raw turkey breasts (chilled/frozen) + non-meat ingredients + packaging
  → Cold storage (≤ 40 °F / 4.4 °C)
  → Tempering/thawing (if frozen)
  → Injection / tumbling (brine: salt, phosphate, ± lactate/diacetate or vinegar-based inhibitor)
  → Forming / stuffing into casings or molds
  → COOK (smokehouse / water cook)                          ← CCP 1 (lethality)
  → CHILL / STABILIZATION (shower, brine chill, blast cooler) ← CCP 2 (cooling)
  ===== product is now RTE and EXPOSED from here on =====
  → Peel / unmold / remove casing
  → Hold in RTE cooler (logs)
  → SLICE (slicer, conveyors, interleaver, hand contact)
  → Package (vacuum / MAP)
  → [Post-packaging lethality: HPP or post-pack pasteurization] ← CCP 3 (if used)
  → Label (use-by date, safe-handling / "keep refrigerated")
  → Cold storage and shipping (≤ 40 °F)
```

Draw your own diagram, then **walk the line to confirm it**. Check rework routes, where people and carts cross from raw to RTE areas, where condensate drips, and where equipment was recently added *(book)*.

## 3. Hazard analysis (main hazards)

| Step | Hazard | Type | Reasonably likely to occur? | Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Receiving raw turkey | *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter*, *L. monocytogenes*, *C. perfringens* spores on raw poultry | Biological | **Yes**, raw poultry routinely carries them | Destroyed or controlled later at **cook (CCP 1)** and **cooling (CCP 2)**. Supplier specs and receiving temperature are prerequisite programs. |
| Receiving ingredients | Allergens (e.g. dairy, soy in some marinades); undeclared nitrite in "natural" cures | Chemical | Depends on formula | Supplier letters, label review, allergen program |
| Injection / tumbling | Cross-contamination from injector needles; growth if brine warms | Biological | Low, if product is kept cold | Sanitation SOPs, brine temperature, injector cleaning |
| **Cook** | **Survival** of *Salmonella* and *L. monocytogenes* | Biological | **Yes** | **CCP 1: lethality** |
| **Chill** | **Outgrowth** of *C. perfringens* and *B. cereus* spores that survive cooking; *C. botulinum* in uncured product | Biological | **Yes**, large logs cool slowly | **CCP 2: stabilization** |
| Peeling, holding, **slicing**, packing | **Recontamination with *L. monocytogenes*** from food-contact surfaces, the environment, people, condensate | Biological | **Yes**. This is the single biggest risk in your plant. | Listeria Rule controls: **post-lethality treatment (CCP 3)** and/or **antimicrobial agent**, plus **Sanitation SOPs and environmental monitoring** |
| Slicing / packing | *S. aureus* from handlers | Biological | Low with GMPs and cold product | Hygiene, gloves, temperature |
| Slicing / packing | Metal fragments from slicer blades | Physical | Plant-specific | Metal detection (often a CCP), or a blade-inspection prerequisite program |
| Storage and distribution | *Listeria* growth over shelf life if temperature is abused | Biological | Yes, if any cells are present | Cold chain ≤ 40 °F, a validated shelf life, growth inhibitors |
| Labeling | Undeclared allergens; wrong use-by date | Chemical / biological | Plant-specific | Label control program |

> **C. perfringens reminder:** cooking kills vegetative cells but leaves spores. Slow cooling of large cooked turkey logs is a textbook cause of *C. perfringens* outbreaks *(book)*. That makes cooling a CCP in its own right, alongside the cook.

## 4. The FSIS Listeria Rule: choose your Alternative

Under 9 CFR 430 every post-lethality-exposed RTE product must be produced under one of three Alternatives *(std)*:

| Alternative | What you use | FSIS verification sampling | Practical meaning for a small plant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alt 1** | A **post-lethality treatment (PLT)** that reduces *Lm*, **plus** an **antimicrobial agent or process (AMAP)** that limits growth over shelf life | Lowest | Strongest protection. Example: **HPP** after packaging, **plus** a lactate/diacetate or buffered-vinegar inhibitor in the formula. |
| **Alt 2** | **Either** a PLT **or** an AMAP, plus sanitation | Medium | 2a: PLT only. 2b: inhibitor only, and it needs a more intensive environmental testing program with hold-and-test. |
| **Alt 3** | Sanitation program only | **Highest**. Deli meat under Alt 3 attracts the most FSIS attention. | Not recommended for sliced deli meat. If you must use it, your environmental monitoring has to be intensive, with hold-and-test after positives on food-contact surfaces. |

**Recommendation:** aim for **Alternative 1**. Sliced deli meat is one of the highest-risk RTE categories because slicing spreads any contamination across many packages and the product is eaten without further cooking. Two independent hurdles, a kill step after packaging plus an inhibitor during shelf life, give you a safety margin that sanitation alone can't.

## 5. Critical control points

### CCP 1: Cooking (lethality)

- **Hazard controlled:** survival of *Salmonella* and *L. monocytogenes* (and other vegetative pathogens).
- **Critical limit (example):** cook to a validated internal temperature at the slowest-heating point of the thickest log. A common approach is FSIS Appendix A for poultry: **≥ 160 °F (71.1 °C) internal, held long enough to meet the required log reduction**, or an equivalent validated time–temperature combination *(std)*. Appendix A also requires **humidity** during the cook so the product surface doesn't dry out. A dry surface lets *Salmonella* survive by evaporative cooling. *(std)*
  - Why the numbers matter: thermal death is log-linear, and **temperature drives lethality far more than time** *(book)*. Product that hits only a few degrees below target gets far less kill than you'd expect.
- **Monitoring:** a calibrated probe in the coldest (thickest) piece of each batch or smokehouse load, plus continuous chart recording of oven temperature and humidity.
- **Corrective action:** if the limit isn't met, keep cooking, or hold the product and evaluate it. Find the cause (loading, oversized logs, oven fault). Don't release product until lethality is shown.
- **Verification:** daily thermometer calibration checks, record review before shipment, periodic heat-distribution studies of the oven, and validation with Appendix A or a scientific support document.

### CCP 2: Chilling / stabilization

- **Hazard controlled:** outgrowth of *C. perfringens* (and *B. cereus*, *C. botulinum*) spores that survive cooking.
- **Critical limit (example, FSIS Appendix B):** **130 °F → 80 °F (54.4 → 26.7 °C) in ≤ 1.5 h**, then **80 °F → 40 °F (26.7 → 4.4 °C) in ≤ 5 h** *(std)*. Some alternative options exist for cured products. Whatever you use, it has to be validated for **≤ 1 log growth of *C. perfringens*** with no growth of *C. botulinum* *(std)*.
- **Monitoring:** probe the slowest-cooling log (largest, centre of the rack) at defined times, or use a data logger.
- **Corrective action:** if cooling is out of limits, hold the lot and evaluate it. Options are a predictive model (e.g. ComBase / Perfringens Predictor), sampling, recooking, or destruction. Fix the cause (overloaded cooler, refrigeration failure, logs too large).
- **Verification:** record review, calibration, periodic re-validation when you change product size or equipment.

> **Listeria note at the chill step:** brine and shower chill water is a well-known recontamination route. Cooling systems have spread microbes and caused outbreaks *(book)*. Keep chill brine at the correct temperature and antimicrobial level, and change and sanitize it on a schedule (prerequisite program or Sanitation SOP). Product in **intact, sealed** casings is protected. Once you peel, it is exposed.

### CCP 3: Post-lethality treatment (if you use Alt 1 or 2a)

Pick one validated process applied **after final packaging**, so it kills anything picked up during slicing:

| PLT option | Typical parameters *(std)* | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **High pressure processing (HPP)** | About **600 MPa (87,000 psi) for about 3 min** at cold temperature | Kills vegetative cells, including *Listeria*, inside the sealed pack without cooking. Used for deli meats as a post-packaging *Listeria* step and a lower-nitrite option *(book)*. Needs flexible packaging *(book)*. Many small plants use **toll HPP** services instead of buying the equipment. |
| **Post-package pasteurization** | Hot water or steam, e.g. about 185–205 °F surface for a validated time | Works best on whole logs or chubs. Harder with shingled sliced packs. |
| **Pre-package surface pasteurization** (infrared, steam) | Validated time–temperature | Product can be recontaminated between treatment and packing. |

- **Critical limits:** the validated pressure, hold time and temperature (HPP), or water temperature and time (pasteurization). Claims must be backed by validation, usually a **≥ 1–2 log** reduction of *Lm* for FSIS purposes. Higher is better *(std)*.
- **Monitoring:** HPP cycle printout for every batch (pressure, time), or a continuous temperature and time record.
- **Corrective action:** re-run the cycle; hold and evaluate any product that missed it.
- **Verification:** record review, calibration, supporting validation study for your product and pack format.

### Optional CCP: Metal detection

If your hazard analysis finds slicer-blade or equipment fragments reasonably likely, make metal detection a CCP. The critical limit is test-wand sizes such as Fe 2.0 mm, non-Fe 2.5 mm and SS 3.0 mm *(std; set per your equipment)*, checked at defined intervals.

## 6. Antimicrobial agent or process (growth inhibitor)

These limit growth of any *Listeria* that gets in, over the product's shelf life:

- **Sodium or potassium lactate plus sodium diacetate**, or **buffered vinegar / cultured sugar–vinegar** blends, added to the brine. Typical use is at levels shown to allow **≤ 2 logs of *Lm* growth over the labeled shelf life** *(std)*. Organic acids work best in their undissociated form, so their effect depends on product pH *(book)*.
- **Bacteriophage preparations** sprayed on surfaces before packaging. They are approved processing aids for *Listeria* *(book)*.
- **Bacteriocins**, e.g. nisin, or pediocin-like (class IIa) bacteriocins, which are strongly anti-*Listeria*, sometimes delivered by protective cultures *(book)*.
- **Synergy with packaging and cold:** inhibitors plus MAP or vacuum plus ≤ 40 °F storage form a multi-hurdle system *(book)*.

You need a scientific basis, such as a challenge study or a validated predictive model (e.g. a lactate/diacetate calculator), for the inhibitor level, product formula and shelf life together. If you change the formula, for example lower salt or a "clean-label" cure, re-validate. Removing preservatives without a replacement can increase risk *(book)*.

## 7. Prerequisite programs that make or break *Listeria* control

Most *Listeria* failures in deli plants happen **here**, not at a CCP.

### 7.1 Hygienic zoning and plant design
- **Physically separate raw and RTE areas**: walls, separate staff, separate tools, colour-coded gear, foot traffic patterns and positive air pressure in the RTE room.
- RTE employees change smocks and gloves, wash hands, and use **foot baths or foamers** at entry. No raw-side tools, carts or pallets enter the RTE room.
- Keep the RTE room **cold and dry**. Control **condensation**, with no drips onto exposed product or food-contact surfaces.
- Floors and drains: no cracks or standing water. Drains flow away from RTE areas, and nobody clears drains with high-pressure hoses during production (it aerosolizes *Listeria*).

### 7.2 Sanitation (Sanitation SOPs, required by FSIS)
- **Clean, then sanitize.** Organic soil inactivates sanitizers *(book)*. The sequence is dry clean → pre-rinse → detergent → rinse → sanitize *(std)*.
- **Take the slicer fully apart** every day, including blades, guards, conveyor belts, rollers, and the bearings and hollow framework where harborage hides.
- **Rotate sanitizers** and include products that work against biofilms. Biofilm cells resist sanitizers mainly because they grow slowly *(book)*. Peracetic acid, quats (they leave residual activity on surfaces) and chlorine all have roles *(book)*.
- Do periodic **deep cleaning** with heat or foam of hard-to-reach equipment.
- Run **pre-operational inspection** before start-up.
- Review **equipment design** before you buy. Choose sanitary designs with no hollow tubes or hard-to-reach crevices, and keep maintenance tools dedicated to the RTE area.

### 7.3 Environmental monitoring program (EMP)

This is your early-warning system. Sample by zone:

| Zone | Examples | Target organism / test | Typical frequency (small plant, adjust by Alternative) *(std)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone 1: product-contact surfaces** in the RTE area | Slicer blades, conveyor belts, peeling tables, interleaver, gloves, packaging film path | *Listeria* spp. (or *Lm*) | At least weekly or per FSIS frequency for your Alternative. Alt 3 deli meat requires the most. |
| **Zone 2: near product contact** | Slicer frame, control panels, carts, scales | *Listeria* spp. | Weekly |
| **Zone 3: further from product** | Floors, drains, walls, cooler fans, condensate pans, forklift wheels | *Listeria* spp. | Weekly / biweekly |
| **Zone 4: outside the RTE room** | Raw area, locker rooms, hallways | *Listeria* spp. | Monthly |

- Sample **during production** (several hours in), not only after cleaning. That's when harborage shows itself.
- Test for ***Listeria* spp.** as an indicator. It is more sensitive than testing for *Lm* only and catches problems earlier.
- **Respond to every positive:**
  1. **Zone 1 positive:** intensified cleaning, re-sampling, and **hold-and-test** of the product lots made on that surface. Test those lots for *Lm* and **don't ship until results are negative**. Under Alt 2b and 3, consecutive Zone 1 positives trigger escalating actions defined in your plan *(std)*.
  2. **Zones 2–4 positives:** "seek and destroy." Do vector swabbing around the positive to find the harborage site, then fix it (repair, replace, redesign).
  3. **Trend the data.** Repeated positives in the same place mean a harborage site. Subtyping isolates (WGS) can show that a resident strain is persisting *(book: PulseNet/WGS)*.
- Keep **records** and review trends monthly.

### 7.4 People, GMPs and other prerequisites
- Training, hand washing, glove changes, illness exclusion, and dedicated RTE clothing *(book: GMPs)*.
- **Temperature control** of the RTE room and coolers at ≤ 40 °F (4.4 °C). Keep product cold during slicing.
- **Supplier control** for casings, packaging film and non-meat ingredients.
- **Shelf-life and labeling:** a use-by date supported by challenge or predictive data, "Keep refrigerated" labeling, and safe-handling statements.
- **Recall plan and lot coding** so you can trace one day's production.

## 9. Quick summary: your *Listeria* defense in depth

1. **Kill it**: validated cook (**CCP 1**) with humidity, measured at the cold spot.
2. **Cool fast**: Appendix-B-style stabilization (**CCP 2**) to stop sporeformers.
3. **Keep it out**: hygienic zoning, slicer sanitation, condensate and drain control, people flow.
4. **Find it early**: a zone-based environmental monitoring program with hold-and-test and seek-and-destroy.
5. **Kill it again after packing**: HPP or post-pack pasteurization (**CCP 3**).
6. **Stop it growing**: lactate/diacetate or vinegar-based inhibitors, ≤ 40 °F cold chain, validated shelf life.
7. **Prove it**: validation, verification, records, and annual reassessment.

Following **Alternative 1** (items 5 and 6 together) is the most robust choice for sliced deli turkey, and it also lowers FSIS verification sampling.
