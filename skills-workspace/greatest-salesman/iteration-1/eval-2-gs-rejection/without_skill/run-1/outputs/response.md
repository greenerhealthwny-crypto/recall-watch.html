# What Og Mandino's Scrolls Say About Rejection

In *The Greatest Salesman in the World* (1968), Og Mandino tells the story of Hafid, a camel boy who becomes the richest merchant of his time. Hafid's secret is ten scrolls he reads every day. A lot of the book is about the exact problem you describe: hearing "no" over and over and feeling it wear you down. Here is what the scrolls would tell you.

---

## 1. Treat every "no" as progress (Scroll III: *"I will persist until I succeed"*)

This is the scroll most directly about rejection. Its main idea is that a "no" doesn't mean you failed. It's a step toward the next "yes." Paraphrasing the scroll: each refusal moves you closer to acceptance, and each frown prepares you for the smile that comes next.

What it asks you to do:
- **Think of the work as many small blows against a big oak tree.** No single swing fells it, but each one counts.
- **Remove defeatist words from how you talk to yourself**, words like *quit, cannot, impossible, hopeless, failure*.
- **Make one more call.** When you want to stop for the day, make one more attempt, and try to end the day on an effort instead of a retreat.
- **See yourself as a lion, not a sheep.** You weren't born to wait for someone to push you forward.

**In practice:** Track your activity, not only your results. If you know your ratio, say 1 close per 20 calls, then every rejection is a known part of the process and not a judgment of you.

---

## 2. Run your emotions; don't let them run you (Scroll VI: *"Today I will be master of my emotions"*)

Mandino says moods rise and fall like tides, and a salesman who acts only when he feels good will fail. A well-known line from this scroll says the weak let their thoughts control their actions, while the strong make their actions control their thoughts.

The scroll lists specific actions to take against specific bad moods:
- If I feel **depressed**, I will sing.
- If I feel **sad**, I will laugh.
- If I feel **fear**, I will plunge ahead.
- If I feel **inferior**, I will wear new garments.
- If I feel **uncertain**, I will raise my voice.
- If I feel **incompetent**, I will remember past success.
- If I feel **insignificant**, I will remember my goals.

The scroll also says **your prospects have moods too.** A harsh "no" often reflects the buyer's bad day, not your worth. Don't take it personally, and come back another day.

---

## 3. Remember that this too shall pass (Scroll VII: *"I will laugh at the world"*)

Laughter keeps things in proportion. When a rejection stings, tell yourself *"This too shall pass."* Mandino applies the phrase to good fortune as well, so a big win shouldn't make you arrogant either. A bad week, a lost deal, a rude prospect: none of it is permanent. Don't take yourself so seriously that each "no" feels like the end of the world.

---

## 4. Know your own worth (Scroll IV: *"I am nature's greatest miracle"*)

Rejection can make you feel like the problem. This scroll reminds you that no one else in the world has your mix of abilities, voice and character. Use that uniqueness. Don't copy other people, and stop measuring your value by today's results.

---

## 5. Lead with love, even toward the people who reject you (Scroll II: *"I will greet this day with love in my heart"*)

Mandino calls love the greatest secret of success. In practice it is a shield: love "can melt hearts and disarm resistance." Bless the prospect who turns you down, and look for something to admire in them. That keeps you from getting bitter or defensive, and it makes you easier to buy from next time.

---

## 6. Live today as if it were your last (Scroll V)

Don't dwell on yesterday's rejections, and don't dread tomorrow's. Yesterday is gone and tomorrow isn't promised. Put full effort into today's calls and let the rest go.

---

## 7. Act now (Scroll IX: *"I will act now"*)

When you feel down, it's easy to procrastinate. You avoid the phone, over-prepare and wait until you "feel ready." Mandino's answer is to repeat *"I will act now"* and make the call before your feelings talk you out of it. Action is what beats the fear.

---

## 8. Keep raising your goals and your value (Scroll VIII: *"Today I will multiply my value a hundredfold"*)

Set goals for the day, week, month and year that stretch you a bit, and try to beat yesterday's performance. Every rejection is a chance to sharpen your pitch, product knowledge and listening.

---

## 9. Ask for guidance, not for things (Scroll X)

The last scroll is a prayer. It asks not for gold or sales but for **guidance**: the wisdom to find the way and the strength to keep going. Whatever your beliefs, it's about humility and help with the inner struggle, not the outcome.

---

## 10. Build the habit (Scroll I: *"Today I begin a new life"*)

Mandino says the difference between people who fail and people who succeed is mostly their **habits**. His method:
- **Read one scroll three times a day for 30 days** (on waking, after lunch and before bed, the last time aloud) before moving to the next scroll.
- That takes about ten months for all ten scrolls. The repetition rewires your automatic reactions, so persistence and control over your emotions become reflexes instead of willpower.

---

## A simple daily plan based on the scrolls

1. **Morning:** Read today's scroll aloud. Say *"I will persist until I succeed"* and *"I will act now."*
2. **Before calls:** Set an activity goal, such as a number of calls or meetings, and not only a sales target.
3. **After each "no":** Tell yourself *"Each no brings me closer to yes,"* write down one thing you learned, and move to the next call right away.
4. **When the mood drops:** Use the Scroll VI antidote. Put on music, laugh, stand up and raise your voice, or review a past win.
5. **End of day:** Make one more call. Then tell yourself *"This too shall pass"* and let the day go.

---

**A caring note:** Mandino's scrolls are a strong tool for resilience, but if feeling down turns into something heavier or lasts a long time, like trouble sleeping, losing interest in things or hopelessness, please talk to someone you trust or a mental health professional. Persistence matters, and so does taking care of yourself.
