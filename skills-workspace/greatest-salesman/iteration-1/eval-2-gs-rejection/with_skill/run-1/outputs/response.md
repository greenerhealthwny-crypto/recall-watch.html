# What Og Mandino's Scrolls Say About Rejection

Rejection is part of sales, and feeling down about it is normal. *The Greatest Salesman in the World* (Og Mandino, 1968) was written for exactly this. In the story, the young camel boy Hafid fails for days to sell a single robe in Bethlehem and rides home sure he has failed. Then his master Pathros gives him ten scrolls, and they make him the greatest salesman in the world.

Three scrolls speak most directly to you right now: **III** (rejection), **VI** (low mood) and **VII** (perspective).

## 1. Scroll III: "I will persist until I succeed." Treat each "no" as progress

This is the scroll for rejection.

- **The law of averages.** Every failure raises the odds that the next attempt succeeds. In Mandino's words, "Each nay I hear will bring me closer to the sound of yea." You have to fail often to succeed once, so a "no" is a step forward, not a verdict on you.
- **The oak and the blade.** Each day's work is one blow against a great oak. No single blow looks like it does anything, but together they bring the tree down.
- **Drop the defeat words.** The scroll tells you to stop using *quit, cannot, unable, impossible, hopeless*.
- **One more attempt.** When you're tired at the end of the day and want to go home, try once more. If that fails, try again.

**Do this:** set a daily minimum of calls or attempts. After you hit it and want to stop, make one more "persistence call."

## 2. Scroll VI: "Today I will be master of my emotions." Answer the mood with an action

Mandino says moods come and go in cycles, like tides and seasons. A dead flower holds the seed of the next bloom, so today's slump won't last. The key is not to let the mood decide what you do. Answer each feeling with an action:

- "If I feel depressed I will sing."
- "If I feel sad I will laugh."
- "If I feel fear I will plunge ahead."
- "If I feel incompetent I will remember past success."
- "If I feel insignificant I will remember my goals."

The scroll also covers the buyer's side. A prospect who's short with you today may just be in a bad mood. Make allowances, and try them again another day. Their "no" is often about their day, not about you.

**Do this:** write your own "If I feel X, I will Y" list on a card and keep it where you can see it. For example: "If I feel rejected, I will review my last win and dial the next number."

## 3. Scroll VII: "I will laugh at the world." Shrink the setback

- Four words bring things back into balance: **"This too shall pass."** They work for a bad week and for a great one.
- Laugh at yourself. Mandino says a man is most comical when he takes himself too seriously. Laughter brings failures down to their true size.

**Do this:** when a deal falls through, say "This too shall pass," then make the next call.

## Supporting scrolls, if they fit

- **Scroll IV, "I am nature's greatest miracle":** a rejection doesn't measure your worth. You are rare, and rarity has value. List three things that set you apart and build your pitch around them, not around copying others.
- **Scroll IX, "I will act now":** brooding after a "no" is the real danger. The cure is the next action, right away.
- **Scroll V, "I will live this day as if it is my last":** don't drag yesterday's rejections into today. Each day starts fresh.
- **Scroll II, love:** silently say "I love you" before each call and find one genuine thing to praise in the prospect. It changes how you come across, and it changes how a "no" feels.

## How to use them: Mandino's 30-day method

The book insists that reading a scroll once isn't enough. The words have to become habit (Scroll I).

1. Pick **one** scroll. Given what you describe, start with **Scroll III (persistence)**.
2. Read it **silently when you wake up**, **silently after lunch**, and **aloud just before bed**.
3. Keep going for **30 days** without skipping. Mandino warns that a missed day can't be retrieved.
4. On day 31, move to the next scroll. Scroll VI (emotions) and then VII (laughter) are good next picks.

If you don't have the book, write the scroll's key line on a card ("I will persist until I succeed") and read that three times a day, along with your own if/then list.

## An honest note

The scrolls are about mindset and motivation, not a sales-technique manual. If rejections stay high over many weeks, also look at the mechanics: who you're targeting, your opening, your offer and your follow-up. Persistence works best when you keep making the approach better too.

And if "feeling down" turns into something heavier that won't lift, such as trouble sleeping, losing interest in things, or feeling hopeless, please talk to someone you trust or a professional. That's about more than sales, and you deserve real support.

Hafid's whole story begins with what looked like a failure. As Scroll III puts it: **"I will persist until I succeed."**
