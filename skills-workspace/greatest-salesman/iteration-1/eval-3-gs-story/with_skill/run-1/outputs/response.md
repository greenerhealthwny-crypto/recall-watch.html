# The story of *The Greatest Salesman in the World* (Og Mandino, 1968)

The book is a short parable. The story is a frame around ten "scrolls" of success, and it has three parts: an old man looking back, a test he faced as a boy, and a handover at the end.

## Who is Hafid?

- When the book opens, **Hafid** is old and very rich, and lives in **Damascus**. People call him "the greatest salesman in the world."
- He gives away his fortune, and he tells his loyal bookkeeper, **Erasmus**, the secret of how he became so successful.
- Then the story goes back in time. Hafid started out as a lowly **camel boy** working for the merchant **Pathros of Palmyra**. He wanted more from life and asked Pathros to let him become a salesman.

## What happens with the robe?

1. **The test.** Pathros gives Hafid one fine **red robe** and sends him to **Bethlehem** to sell it. This shows whether Hafid can sell.
2. **The failure.** Hafid tries for days and makes no sale.
3. **The gift.** He finds a **newborn baby in a cave** with no warm covering. He wraps the baby in the robe, so he gives away the one thing he was supposed to sell.
4. **The star.** He rides back to Pathros sure that he has failed. Above him shines the **brightest star he has ever seen**, and it follows him.
5. **The sign.** Pathros sees the star over Hafid and knows it is the sign he has been waiting for. Hafid did not sell the robe, but he passed the real test. Pathros gives him a chest holding the **ten scrolls**, which teach the principles of success.

The robe comes back at the end of the story (see below).

## What are the scrolls?

Hafid follows the scrolls and becomes rich. Each scroll teaches one principle:

| Scroll | Principle |
|---|---|
| I | Form good habits ("Today I begin a new life"). This scroll also gives the reading method: read each scroll three times a day for 30 days. |
| II | Greet each day with love in your heart |
| III | Persist until you succeed |
| IV | You are nature's greatest miracle |
| V | Live each day as if it is your last |
| VI | Be master of your emotions |
| VII | Laugh at the world ("This too shall pass") |
| VIII | Multiply your value a hundredfold |
| IX | Act now |
| X | Pray for guidance (the salesman's prayer) |

## Who gets the scrolls in the end?

- In old age, Hafid keeps the scrolls and waits for the person they are meant for next.
- That person is **Paul (Saul of Tarsus)**. He arrives carrying a robe that came from **Jesus' crucifixion**.
- Hafid looks at the robe and finds the marks of **Tola** and **Pathros** on it. It is **the same red robe** he wrapped around the baby in the Bethlehem cave. That means the baby was Jesus.
- So Hafid knows Paul is the one he has been waiting for, and he gives the scrolls to him. The chain that Pathros started is passed on.

## A note on the ending and the themes

- The edition ends with a preview of the sequel, *Part II: The End of the Story*. In it Hafid, at sixty, decides to begin "a second life" of helping others.
- The story has strong Christian themes: the baby in Bethlehem, the star, Paul, and prayer. These are the book's own framing. The ideas in the scrolls (habits, persistence, controlling your moods, taking action) also work as plain advice for anyone.
