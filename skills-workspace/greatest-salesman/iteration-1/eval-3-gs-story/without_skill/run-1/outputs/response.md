# The Greatest Salesman in the World: Story Summary

*Og Mandino, 1968*

## The frame: an old man giving his fortune away

The book opens in Damascus. Hafid, by now very old, is known as the greatest salesman in the world. He tells his loyal bookkeeper Erasmus to sell off his trading empire and give the money to the poor, keeping only enough for himself and his wife to live on. Then he takes Erasmus to a hidden room in his warehouse, where a small chest sits under guard. It holds the secret of his success, and he has been waiting years for the one person meant to receive it. He then tells the story of how he got it.

## Who is Hafid?

Hafid starts as a poor camel boy in the caravan of **Pathros**, a rich and respected merchant. He is in love with **Lisha**, daughter of the wealthy Calneh, but he knows her father will never let her marry a camel boy. So he asks Pathros to make him a salesman, hoping to earn his fortune and be worthy of her.

## What happens with the robe?

Pathros tests him. He gives Hafid one fine **red robe** and sends him to sell it in the poor village of **Bethlehem**.

- For three days Hafid tries and fails to sell it, and he grows discouraged.
- On the last night he finds a young couple sheltering in a cave behind an inn. They have a newborn baby and nothing to keep him warm from the cold.
- Out of pity, Hafid wraps the infant in the robe and leaves it with them, even though he thinks this means he has failed his test.

As he rides back to the caravan in shame, a **bright star** seems to follow him and shine above his head. Pathros sees the star and understands it as the sign he had been told to wait for. Years before, he had been told that a young man with the star over him would be the next person to receive the scrolls. The child Hafid helped is, by implication, the infant Jesus.

## The scrolls

Pathros, now old and dying, gives Hafid a chest holding **ten scrolls**. These contain the principles of success that made Pathros rich. He sets conditions:

- Hafid must read the scrolls in order, one at a time. He reads each scroll **three times a day for thirty days** before going on to the next. That way each principle becomes a habit.
- He must give away half of whatever he earns to people in need.
- He must not share the scrolls with anyone until he receives a sign showing who should have them next.

Hafid follows the method, builds a huge trading empire based in Damascus, marries Lisha, and becomes the greatest salesman in the world. (The middle of the book is the text of the ten scrolls themselves. Two of them are "I will greet this day with love in my heart" and "I will persist until I succeed.")

## Who gets the scrolls in the end?

Years later, after Lisha has died and while Hafid lives quietly and waits, a stranger comes to see him: **Saul of Tarsus**, the man later called the Apostle **Paul**. Paul tells Hafid about his conversion on the road to Damascus. He is struggling to spread the message of Jesus, and a voice told him to seek out the greatest salesman in the world.

Paul shows Hafid a **red robe**. It belonged to Jesus, and Roman soldiers gambled for it at the crucifixion. Hafid recognizes the markings of Pathros's house on it. It is the same robe he once wrapped around the baby in Bethlehem. This is the sign he had waited for. Hafid gives the chest of scrolls to **Paul**, who will use their principles to "sell" the greatest message of all.
