# How *The Greatest Salesman in the World* Tells You to Read the Scrolls

Og Mandino's 1968 book is a parable. An old merchant named Hafid gives a young camel boy ten ancient scrolls. The first scroll ("The Scroll Marked I") is mostly a set of instructions for how to read the other nine, and how to read itself. Following those instructions is the whole method.

## The schedule

**1. One scroll at a time, for 30 days.**
You read a single scroll every day for thirty days before you go on to the next one. You don't skip around, read ahead, or move on early.

**2. Three readings every day.**
- **When you wake up:** read it silently.
- **After your midday meal:** read it silently again.
- **Before you go to sleep:** read it again, and this time **read it aloud**.

**3. Repeat every day for the 30 days.**
The next morning you start again with the same scroll. After thirty days you move on to Scroll II and follow the same routine.

**4. Go through all ten scrolls in order.**
10 scrolls × 30 days = **300 days**, about ten months. That's 90 readings per scroll and roughly 900 in total.

### The ten scrolls, in order

| Scroll | Theme |
|---|---|
| I | Today I begin a new life. I will form good habits and become their slave. (the method itself) |
| II | I will greet this day with love in my heart. |
| III | I will persist until I succeed. |
| IV | I am nature's greatest miracle. |
| V | I will live this day as if it is my last. |
| VI | Today I will be master of my emotions. |
| VII | I will laugh at the world. |
| VIII | Today I will multiply my value a hundredfold. |
| IX | I will act now. |
| X | A prayer for guidance. |

## Why it works (the book's reasoning)

**Failure comes from bad habits, not missing knowledge.** Scroll I says the only real difference between people who fail and people who succeed is their habits. Good intentions and wisdom don't help if your habits pull the other way.

**Only a habit can beat a habit.** You can't get rid of a bad habit by deciding to. You have to push it out with a new one. In the book's words, "only a habit can subdue another habit." The daily repetition is how you build the new habits.

**Repetition reaches the subconscious mind.** The scroll says that reading the words again and again lets them sink into the part of the mind that "never sleeps." Once they're there, they shape what you dream about and how you act. In time you respond to the world the way the scrolls describe without having to stop and think about it.

**Reading becomes doing.** The first stage is the habit of reading the scrolls. After a while, the ideas in them become your reflexes: you greet people with love, you persist, you act now. The book says you will "become the words."

**The 30-day stretch gives each idea time to take hold.** A whole month on one scroll keeps it in front of you long enough to become routine. It also has to last past the first few days, which the scroll warns are the hardest, when the old habits push back and you'll be tempted to quit. Reading three times a day means the message starts your day, interrupts its middle, and is the last thing in your head before sleep. Reading it aloud at night adds hearing and speaking to seeing, which strengthens the impression.

**Discipline is part of the lesson.** Doing the program is already practice in what the scrolls teach, especially persistence (Scroll III) and acting now (Scroll IX). If you can keep the reading schedule for 300 days, you've shown you can keep other commitments too.

## In short

Read one scroll three times a day: silently when you wake, silently after lunch, and aloud before bed. Do this for 30 days, then go on to the next scroll, through all ten (about 10 months). The book's idea is that this repetition replaces your bad habits with good ones by planting the principles in your subconscious until you act on them automatically.
