# How *The Greatest Salesman in the World* tells you to read the scrolls

Og Mandino doesn't leave the reading to chance. The method is laid out in **Scroll I**, the scroll about forming good habits. Following the method is meant to be the first good habit you form.

## The schedule

**Rule 1: One scroll for 30 days.** You stay with a single scroll for thirty days in a row. Only then do you move on to the next one.

**Rule 2: Three readings a day, at fixed times.**

| When | How |
|---|---|
| When you get up in the morning | Read it **silently** |
| After your midday meal | Read it **silently** again |
| Just before you go to bed | Read it **aloud** |

**Rule 3: Don't skip a day.** The scroll warns that a missed day "cannot be retrieved." There's no catching up by reading twice as much the next day.

**Rule 4: Go through all ten in order.** Ten scrolls at 30 days each comes to about **ten months**. After that, the principles are meant to be part of how you think and act.

### What the ten months look like

| Month | Scroll | Key line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I: Habits | "Today I begin a new life." |
| 2 | II: Love | "I will greet this day with love in my heart." |
| 3 | III: Persistence | "I will persist until I succeed." |
| 4 | IV: Self-worth | "I am nature's greatest miracle." |
| 5 | V: Urgency | "I will live this day as if it is my last." |
| 6 | VI: Emotions | "Today I will be master of my emotions." |
| 7 | VII: Laughter | "I will laugh at the world." |
| 8 | VIII: Goals | "Today I will multiply my value a hundredfold." |
| 9 | IX: Action | "I will act now." |
| 10 | X: Prayer | The salesman's prayer: "Guide me." |

## Why it works, according to the book

**1. Habits decide success or failure.** Scroll I says the only real difference between people who fail and people who succeed is their **habits**. Good habits are "the key to all success," and bad habits are "the unlocked door to failure." So the first law is: *I will form good habits and become their slave.*

**2. Only a habit can beat a habit.** Willpower and good intentions won't break an old habit. You need a new habit to replace it. The daily reading is that new habit: a deliberate routine set up to push out the old patterns.

**3. Repetition reaches the "other mind."** The book says your conscious resolutions fade, but the words you repeat sink into a deeper, "mysterious" mind (we'd call it the subconscious) that never sleeps. Once the words are lodged there, they start to drive your reactions on their own. You don't have to remember to persist or to stay calm. You just respond that way. Reading aloud just before sleep hands the words to that mind at the end of the day.

**4. An act repeated becomes a pleasure.** The scroll lays out a progression. A repeated act gets **easy**, then it becomes a **pleasure**, and once it's a pleasure you do it by nature, as a **habit**. Thirty days of three readings a day comes to about 90 exposures per scroll. That's enough to carry each principle through those stages.

**5. Patience is built in.** Scroll I compares the olive tree, which takes a hundred years, with the onion plant, which takes nine weeks, and says the reader has "lived as an onion plant." The slow 30-day pace per scroll is on purpose. Lasting change comes from steady repetition, not a quick read-through.

**6. Fixed times make it automatic.** Linking the readings to waking, lunch and bedtime ties the new habit to things you already do every day. You don't have to decide each day whether to read. The time of day is the cue.

## Making it work today (practical adaptation)

These suggestions are mine, not the book's.

- **Set up the cues.** Put three 2-minute reminders in your calendar: morning, after lunch, bedtime.
- **Track the streak.** Mark off 30 boxes for each scroll and start the next scroll on day 31.
- **Add one behavior.** While you read a scroll, also practice what it teaches. During Scroll III, make one more call or attempt after you feel like stopping. During Scroll IX, start any task as soon as you think of it.
- **Keep the aloud reading.** It feels awkward at first, but the book treats it as an important part of the method, so don't swap it for another silent read.

A note: the book is an inspirational parable with religious themes, especially in Scroll X's prayer. If prayer doesn't fit your beliefs, you can read Scroll X as a daily intention or reflection and keep the same schedule.
