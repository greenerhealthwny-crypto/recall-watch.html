# Your 30-Day Plan After 40 Rejections (Og Mandino's Scrolls)

First, 40 rejections in one week does not say you are bad at this. It says you made at least 40 attempts. In *The Greatest Salesman in the World*, that is the raw material success is built from.

---

## 1. Which scroll to start with, and why

### My recommendation: Scroll III, "I will persist until I succeed"

Each scroll in the book targets a different problem. Yours is **rejection and the urge to stop**, and that is exactly what Scroll III is about:

- It gives you a new way to see each "no." It is not a verdict on you. It is a step closer to a "yes" (the law of averages, explained below).
- It hands you one concrete behavior: **make one more attempt** before you go home.
- It asks you to drop defeat words from your vocabulary: *quit, cannot, unable, impossible, hopeless*.
- Its image is a lion that refuses to walk with the sheep. The young bull is judged by how many times it charges back at the lance, not by whether it got hurt.

### An honest note on the book's order

Mandino does **not** tell you to pick a scroll. He tells you to read them **in order, starting with Scroll I** (form good habits). Scroll I explains the reading method itself, and it is the habit every other scroll depends on. A strict reading of the book puts Scroll III in your **third month**.

So you have two options:

| Option | What you read for 30 days | Best if… |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Targeted (my pick for you right now)** | Scroll III, using the Scroll I method | You need help with rejection *this month* and might quit before month 3 |
| **B. By the book** | Scroll I first, then II, then III | You want the full ten-month program as Mandino wrote it |

If you choose A, you still use Scroll I's core idea: the daily reading routine *is* the habit you are building. After the 30 days, go back and do the whole set in order (I → X). Re-reading Scroll III in month 3 does no harm.

## 2. The law of averages: what Mandino means

In Scroll III, Hafid treats failure as part of the price of success:

- **Each failure raises the chance of success on the next try.** In the book's words, each "nay" brings him "closer to the sound of yea."
- **"I must fail often to succeed only once."** Rejections are not a sign you are on the wrong path. They are the path.
- Each day's effort is **one blow of the blade against an oak tree**. No single blow fells it. Enough blows do.
- So the right response to a hard day is not to stop. It is to **make one more attempt**, and if that fails, one more.

### How to use it with your real numbers

Turn the idea into a ratio you can measure:

1. Write down how many **contacts** it takes you to get a meeting (or a sale). Example: if 40 noes led to 1 yes, your current ratio is about 1 in 41.
2. Work out what each "no" is worth. If a sale earns you $400 and takes 40 contacts, **every rejection "earns" about $10**. You can say that to yourself as you hang up.
3. Track the ratio each week. Your goal is not zero rejections. It is **more attempts** and a **slowly improving ratio**.

### A practical caveat *(std)*

Read the law of averages as motivation, not maths. If you make the same weak pitch to the wrong people, rejection #41 is not more likely to be a yes than #1 was. (Believing it is would be the gambler's fallacy.) The law works in sales because:

- more attempts means more chances, even at a fixed rate, and
- each "no" teaches you something (a better opening, a better list, a better answer to an objection), and that *raises* your rate.

So persist, **and** review your noes. That is the persistence of the lion, not the stubbornness of the ox.

## 3. The 30-day plan (Scroll III)

### The daily reading routine (from Scroll I, every single day)

| When | How | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **When you wake up** | Read Scroll III **silently** | ~3 min |
| **After lunch** | Read it **silently** again | ~3 min |
| **Just before bed** | Read it **aloud** | ~4 min |

Rules from the book:
- **Do not skip a day.** Mandino says a missed day cannot be retrieved. If you miss one, start again on the next day; don't quit the whole month.
- Do not move on early. **30 days on one scroll**, even once it feels familiar. Mandino says the repetition is what sinks the words into your "other mind" (the subconscious), until the reaction becomes automatic.

Tip *(std)*: write the key line, **"I will persist until I succeed,"** on an index card. Keep it by your phone or in your wallet. A paraphrased summary of the scroll is fine to read if you don't have the book on you.

### Daily behaviors (the scroll in action)

1. **A daily minimum of attempts.** Pick a number you can always hit (say, 15 calls or messages). Count them.
2. **The "one more" call.** When you are tired and want to stop, make **one more attempt**. If it fails, make one more after that. Log these separately; they are your persistence reps.
3. **Count the noes.** Use a tally sheet. Each "no" gets a mark, not a sigh. You are paying down the price of the next "yes."
4. **Ban the defeat words.** When you catch yourself saying *can't, impossible, hopeless,* or *I quit*, rephrase it out loud ("That one didn't work. Next.").

### Week by week

**Week 1 (Days 1–7): Install the routine**
- Focus: the three daily readings and your daily minimum. Nothing fancy.
- Start your tally sheet: attempts / noes / yeses / "one more" calls.
- End of week: compute your first ratio (contacts per meeting or per sale).

**Week 2 (Days 8–14): Reframe the "no"**
- Add the **value of a no**: calculate your $-per-rejection figure and write it at the top of your tally sheet.
- After each rejection, say (silently) something like "That's one closer," then dial.
- Each evening, write down **one lesson** from that day's noes: an objection, a bad lead, a weak opening line.

**Week 3 (Days 15–21): Stretch the effort**
- Raise your daily minimum by a few attempts.
- Make **two** "one more" calls each day instead of one.
- Fix the single most common objection from your lesson notes. Practice a better answer.

**Week 4 (Days 22–30): Lock it in**
- Compare your Week 1 and Week 4 numbers: attempts per day, ratio, "one more" calls.
- Note how rejection *feels* now compared with Day 1. Most people find the sting is smaller once a "no" has a job.
- Day 30: write a short note to yourself on what changed. Day 31: start the next scroll.

## 4. Help from the other scrolls (only when you need it)

You will still be reading Scroll III each day. If a specific problem comes up, these quick tools from other scrolls can help:

- **Bad mood after a harsh rejection → Scroll VI.** Answer the feeling with an action: "If I feel depressed I will sing… If I feel fear I will plunge ahead." Also: the buyer who snapped at you today may be in a different mood tomorrow. It may not have been about you.
- **A deal falls through → Scroll VII.** Say the four words, **"This too shall pass,"** laugh at yourself a little, then make the next call.
- **Stalling before the next call → Scroll IX.** Say "I will act now" and dial before you can think of a reason not to.
- **Feeling like a fraud → Scroll IV.** You are "nature's greatest miracle": nobody else has your voice or story. Use what makes you different instead of copying the top rep's script.

## 5. After the 30 days

- **Month 2 and beyond:** go through the whole program **in order (Scroll I → Scroll X)**, 30 days each, about ten months in all. That's how Mandino intended it.
- **Pair it with skills** *(std)*. The scrolls build attitude and habits; they are not a sales technique manual. Combine them with practical work: better prospect lists, stronger openers, objection handling, follow-up sequences. That is how persistence turns into a better ratio.
- A note: the book is a parable with religious themes (Scroll X is a prayer for guidance). You can use Scroll III without any of that. If it fits your beliefs, Scroll X's line about asking for **ability equal to your opportunities** is a good one for later.

---

**The one line to remember this month:** *"I will persist until I succeed."* Then make one more call.
