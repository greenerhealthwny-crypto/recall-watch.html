# Getting Through 40 Rejections: A 30-Day Plan Built on Og Mandino's Scrolls

First, about the 40 rejections. In your first weeks of selling, that number is normal. It mostly tells you how much activity you are putting in. It says very little about whether you're cut out for the job. Mandino's book was written for someone in your position: Hafid, a camel boy who is scared of failing at selling.

## How the scrolls are supposed to be used

In *The Greatest Salesman in the World*, the ten scrolls come with strict instructions:

- **One scroll at a time, for 30 days**, before you move to the next one. All ten take about ten months.
- **Read the scroll three times a day:**
  1. silently, when you wake up
  2. silently again, after your midday meal
  3. **out loud**, just before you go to bed. Mandino says this reading matters most.
- **Don't skip days.** The point is to repeat the words until they become automatic and start shaping how you act.
- **Go in order.** The first scroll explains how to use all the others.

That means a strict 30-day plan covers one scroll, not all ten crammed into a month. Spreading the ten scrolls over 30 days would go against the method the book teaches.

## Which scroll to start with, and why

**The book's answer is Scroll I: "Today I begin a new life."** Here's why:

- Scroll I is the instruction manual. It argues that **habits, not talent or knowledge, decide whether a person succeeds or fails.** It tells you to "form good habits and become their slave," and it sets up the 30-day, three-readings-a-day routine.
- Your real problem isn't one bad week. It's whether you'll keep making calls in week two, week three and month six. Scroll I is about building that daily discipline.
- If you jump ahead, you get the inspiring words without the system that makes them stick.

**The scroll that speaks most directly to rejection is Scroll III: "I will persist until I succeed."** This is where Mandino treats every "no" as a step toward a "yes" and where he says he will never think about defeat. If you feel close to quitting this week, it's fine to read Scroll III as emergency support. Just treat it as an extra on top of the plan. Your main 30-day reading should still be Scroll I, so the habit gets built in the right order. Scroll III gets its own full 30 days in month three.

## Your 30-day plan

### Every day (the core habit)
| When | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| When you wake up | Read Scroll I silently | 5 min |
| After lunch | Read Scroll I silently | 5 min |
| Before bed | Read Scroll I **out loud** | 5 min |
| During the day | Keep your call/prospecting block: a set number of attempts, whatever the results | your quota |
| End of the day | Log attempts, conversations, appointments, sales and one lesson learned | 5 min |

### Week 1 (days 1-7): Lock in the routine
- Goal: do all three readings every single day. Set phone alarms for them.
- Choose **one** small selling habit to build next to the reading, such as "I make my first call by 9:00 a.m." or "I make 20 dials before lunch."
- Start counting rejections as activity. A "no" shows up in your log as proof that you worked.

### Week 2 (days 8-14): Replace one bad habit
- Scroll I says the only way to get rid of a bad habit is to replace it with a good one. Pick the bad habit that rejection set off, like stalling after a "no", checking email to avoid the next call, or ending the day early, and set a replacement. Example: "After every no, I write one line about what happened and dial the next number within 60 seconds."
- Read your end-of-day log and look for patterns in where the "no" happens: at the opener, at price, or at "send me info."

### Week 3 (days 15-21): Improve skill as well as volume
- Ask your manager or a top rep to listen to two of your calls, or role-play with you.
- Rewrite your weakest part, which is usually the opener or your answer to the most common objection.
- Keep up the readings. Around this point the words often start coming to mind without effort, especially in the moment after a rejection.

### Week 4 (days 22-30): Review and commit
- Total your numbers: attempts, conversations, appointments and closes. Work out your own ratios.
- Compare week 1 with week 4. Most new reps find that the habit (showing up and dialing) got stable before the results improved.
- On day 30, write a short note to yourself: what changed, which habit stuck, and which one you'll keep.
- **Day 31: start Scroll II, "I will greet this day with love in my heart"**, for the next 30 days. Then Scroll III (persistence), and so on through all ten.

### The full sequence, for reference
1. I: Today I begin a new life (habits)
2. II: I will greet this day with love in my heart
3. III: I will persist until I succeed
4. IV: I am nature's greatest miracle
5. V: I will live this day as if it is my last
6. VI: Today I will be master of my emotions
7. VII: I will laugh at the world
8. VIII: Today I will multiply my value a hundredfold
9. IX: I will act now
10. X: The prayer for guidance

## The "law of averages" idea

In Scroll III, Mandino says that **every failure to sell moves you closer to the next success.** Every "nay" brings you nearer to a "yea," and every frown gets you ready for the next smile. He compares effort to chopping at a huge oak: no single blow fells it, but the blows add up.

**The useful part:** selling is a numbers game in the sense that results come from **volume × conversion rate**. Suppose you close 1 in 20 conversations. Then 40 rejections are what it costs to get about two sales, and you can treat each "no" as worth a small share of a commission. Seeing it this way takes the sting out of each rejection. A "no" is the expected toll on the road to a "yes", not a judgment of you. It also keeps you focused on what you control, which is attempts.

**Where to be careful (Mandino doesn't spell this out):**
- **A "no" doesn't literally make the next "yes" more likely.** Each prospect decides independently. Believing a sale is "due" after a run of rejections is the gambler's fallacy. The law of averages holds **over a large number of attempts**, not call by call.
- **Your averages can change, and you can change them.** Volume alone with a weak pitch or a poorly targeted list keeps your ratio low. The best version of the idea is: *keep the volume up, and use each "no" to raise your conversion rate.* That's why the plan above has you log and review every rejection.
- **Know your real numbers.** Ask your manager what a typical ratio looks like for your product. If 40 rejections in a week is normal for a new rep, you're on track. If it's well below normal, the logging will show you where to fix things.

## The short version
- **Start with Scroll I.** It builds the habit that makes everything else work. Read it three times a day for 30 days, with the last reading out loud.
- **Use Scroll III as extra support** if rejection is hitting hard. It gets its own full month later.
- **The law of averages:** each "no" is part of the price of a "yes" across many attempts. Keep your activity steady and use every rejection to improve your ratio.

You've made 40 attempts. That shows you're doing the part most people avoid.
