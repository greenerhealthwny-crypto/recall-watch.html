# 100 New Window-Cleaning Clients in 100 Days (the *Brick & Mortar Funnels* way)

Your industry matches one chapter almost exactly. **Joshua Latimer's** home-services chapter is built on his own window-cleaning company, which he says passed $180k a month once he put systems in place. His plan is the main one below. I've added pieces from **Tyler Shaule** (texting, reactivation and reviews), **David Frey** (local referrals) and **Alex Hormozi** (how to think about ad spend).

Most numbers below are **example assumptions**. Put in your own figures. Anything marked *(book)* comes from the book. Ad costs and platform features have changed since it came out (about 2020).

## Part 1: How much can you spend to get a customer? (maximum CAC)

### Latimer's formula *(book)*

> **Maximum CAC = annual revenue per client × profit margin**

His example is lawn care: $40 a visit × 30 visits = $1,200 a year. At a 25% margin, that's a **$300 maximum CAC**.

For a job that happens once (his roofing example: a $15,000 job at 20% margin leaves $3,000 profit), you decide how much of that profit you're willing to give up to win the job.

### Applied to window cleaning (example numbers)

| Input | Example | Your number |
|---|---|---|
| Average full exterior + interior clean | $350 | ___ |
| Cleans per year (for example spring and fall) | 2 | ___ |
| Add-ons per year (screens, tracks, gutters, pressure washing) | $150 | ___ |
| **Annual revenue per client** | **$850** | ___ |
| Profit margin after labor, fuel and supplies | 30% | ___ |
| **Maximum CAC (year one)** | **$850 × 30% = $255** | ___ |

So in this example you could spend up to **about $255 to win a recurring residential client** and still break even in year one. Everything after year one is profit. Latimer says one good client who moves up your value ladder can be worth **about $3,850 a year** *(book)*.

### Three checks from the other experts

1. **Spend only on clients who actually show up and buy.** Hormozi works out what each show is worth (cash per sale × close rate) and spends about half of that *(book)*. For you, work out what a **booked estimate or booked front-end job** is worth. Say 60% of $99 front-end customers go on to a $255-profit recurring plan. Then each front-end customer is worth about $150 in year-one profit, and you can afford roughly **$75–$150 in ads per front-end booking**.
2. **Aim to spend $1 and get $3 back** *(Hormozi, book)*. That pays for the ads, pays for the next customer, and leaves $1 to bank. Once a campaign does that, spend more on it. "He who can spend the most to acquire a customer wins" (Dan Kennedy, quoted by Hormozi).
3. **Track it every week, without emotion** (Latimer's step 7). Watch ad spend, leads, booked jobs, completed jobs, upgrades to recurring service, and cost per recurring client compared with your maximum CAC.

> Lesson from the book: "Amateurs focus on the front end but professionals focus on the back end." You can **lose money on the first job** and still win if your back end (recurring plans and add-ons) pays it back inside your maximum CAC.

## Part 2: The offers (your value ladder)

### Front-end offers (cheap and easy to say yes to)

Latimer's own window-cleaning example is **"10 exterior windows for $99"** *(book)*. Each offer gets its own landing page. Options to test:

| Offer | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **10 exterior windows for $99** | The book's proven window offer. A low-risk first taste |
| **Holiday or seasonal offer**, e.g. "4th of July Sparkle Special" or a spring pollen clean | Latimer ties offers to holidays for built-in urgency *(book)* |
| **"Only 10 spots this week in [your town]"** | Scarcity and urgency are part of his copy formula *(book)* |
| **Free window clean through a partner** (siding, roofing, realtor or HVAC company) | Shaule's "offer hack" example: a siding company gives a free window cleaning *(book)*. Partner businesses give it to their clients, and you get the lead |
| **Bring-a-neighbor deal**, e.g. both houses on the street get 20% off | A version of Frey's "viral deal", too good not to share *(book)* |

### Back-end offers (where you make your money)

Once the first job is done, offer the next steps, roughly in this order:
1. **The full house**: interior windows, screens and tracks, on the same visit if they agree.
2. **A recurring plan**: spring and fall, or quarterly, for a small discount or priority booking. This is what makes the maximum-CAC math work.
3. **Add-ons**: gutter cleaning, pressure washing, solar panels, hard-water stain removal, holiday light hanging.
4. **Commercial or storefront accounts**, if you serve them.

## Part 3: The seven steps (Latimer) *(book)*

1. **Choose one irresistible front-end offer.** Tie it to a holiday or season where you can.
2. **Write the copy**: a hook headline, body text, a list of benefits, and urgency or scarcity.
   - *Example:* "**[TOWN] HOMEOWNERS: Streak-Free Windows Before the 4th, 10 Exterior Windows for $99.** Insured, local, and done in one visit. Only 10 spots this week."
   - Call out the town and the reader by name, the way Mike Arce does ("WOMEN IN SPRINGFIELD!") *(book)*.
3. **Build a simple landing page for that one offer only.** "Your broad website is failing you." Collect name, phone, email and address, and add a booking calendar or a short quote form.
4. **Use great images.** Before-and-after photos of real jobs (yours only, never stock photos passed off as your work).
5. **Repeat for each offer you want to test** (for example the $99 offer against the partner offer).
6. **Drive traffic:**
   - 3–5 emails to your existing customers;
   - Facebook Live and posts;
   - Facebook and Instagram ads aimed at homeowners within your service area.
7. **Measure and record the data, without emotion.** Keep the winners, cut the losers, and then spend more on the winners.

## Part 4: The 100-day calendar

### Weeks 1–2: your warm list first (this is the fastest money)
- **Reactivate past customers and old quotes** by text and email (Shaule's reactivation funnel). Offer them a reason to book now, such as your new seasonal special or a priority spot *(book)*. Only text people who gave you consent (see the cautions below).
- **Send Latimer's 3–5 emails** to current customers announcing the front-end offer and the bring-a-neighbor deal.
- **Start the review and referral system now** (Shaule: put this in place early). After each job, send a short NPS survey: "How likely are you to recommend us (1–10)?" and "What would make it a 10?" Ask every customer for a Google review. Follow up personally with anyone who scores you low. Aim for many five-star reviews over time; Shaule's target is about 300 *(book)*.
- **Set up partners:** pitch the "free window clean" offer to 5–10 realtors, siding, roofing or HVAC companies.

### Weeks 3–6: build the funnel and start paid traffic
- Launch the **$99 landing page** and one **seasonal/holiday page**.
- Run **at least 2 ads that differ in one thing only** (the headline or the image). Judge each after about $50 of spend (Arce) *(book)*.
- **Call or text every lead the same day.** Most sales come from follow-up. Joe McCall plans 6–7 touches over 3–4 months *(book)*.
- **Local Facebook (Frey):** post before-and-after photos in local groups (where the rules allow), make about 20 local friend connections a day, keep everything positive, and keep the selling soft *(book)*.

### Weeks 7–10: scale what works
- Raise the budget on any ad that brings in recurring clients **below your maximum CAC**.
- **On every front-end job, offer the upgrades** in person: the full house, a recurring plan, add-ons. This is the moment they're most impressed.
- Add a second offer (gutters, or storefronts) as its own page.
- Door-hang the neighbors of every finished job: "We just cleaned your neighbor's windows at [address]..."

### Weeks 11–14: proof, reviews and repeat
- Build a **gallery of proof**: before-and-after photos, short customer videos and reviews. Use them in retargeting ads to people who visited the page but didn't book (Jennifer Sewell, Holmes) *(book)*.
- Ask happy customers for referrals and neighbor introductions.
- **Change the offer about every 90 days** (Shaule) so it stays fresh *(book)*.

## Part 5: Work backward from 100 clients (example rates)

| Stage | Assumed rate | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| New paying customers | target | **100** |
| Quotes or bookings that become jobs | 70% | about 143 booked |
| Leads that book | 35% | about **410 leads** |
| Leads from warm list, referrals and partners | perhaps 30–40% | about 120–160 |
| Leads from paid ads | the rest | about 250–290 |

That works out to about **4 leads a day** over 100 days. With the example of $15–$35 per lead, the ads might cost **about $4,000–$10,000**. That's roughly **$40–$100 per new customer**, well under the example $255 maximum CAC. If 50–60 of the 100 move to a recurring plan, the campaign pays for itself in year one.

Replace every rate with your own after the first 2–3 weeks of data.

## Cautions
- **These results are the authors' own claims,** not guarantees. Ad prices are higher now than when the book was written; check your local costs.
- **Texting and calling:** get prior express written consent before sending marketing texts (TCPA), and follow CAN-SPAM for email.
- **Reviews:** ask *every* customer for a review. Don't send only the happy ones to Google ("review gating"); Google's policies discourage it. Never post fake reviews or testimonials.
- **Facebook:** follow its rules on profiles, group posting and giveaways.
- **Price the front-end offer so it covers your crew's time.** Make sure your crew can deliver it fast and well, and that you offer every customer the upgrade.
