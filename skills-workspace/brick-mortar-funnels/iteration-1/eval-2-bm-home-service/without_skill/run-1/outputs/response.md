# Window Cleaning: What You Can Spend per Customer, and a Plan for 100 New Clients

**A note before you start:** I don't know the contents of *Brick & Mortar Funnels* well enough to quote its exact terms, formulas or numbered steps, and I don't want to make up quotes from it. What follows uses the method that local-business funnel books in this genre teach: work out lifetime value, set a target cost per customer, open with an irresistible front-end offer, follow up, then sell recurring service. I've applied it to window cleaning. If you have the book handy, check its chapter names against the sections below.

---

## Part 1: How much you can spend to get a customer

### Step 1: Work out what a customer is worth over time (lifetime value)

Don't base your ad budget on the first job. Base it on what a customer pays you over the whole time they stay with you.

| Input | Example (replace with your numbers) |
|---|---|
| Average ticket per clean | $250 |
| Cleans per year | 3 (spring, summer, fall) |
| Average years retained | 3 |
| **Gross lifetime revenue** | $250 x 3 x 3 = **$2,250** |
| Gross margin (after labor, supplies, fuel) | 50% |
| **Lifetime gross profit** | **$1,125** |

Then add:
- **Add-on revenue**: gutters, screens, pressure washing, solar panels, holiday lights. Add the average add-on spend per customer.
- **Referral value**: if each customer refers 0.5 new customers on average, that's extra value per customer acquired.

### Step 2: Set your maximum and target cost to acquire a customer

- **Maximum allowable acquisition cost:** the most you could pay and still break even over the customer's lifetime. In the example, that's about $1,125.
- **Target acquisition cost:** stay well under the maximum so you keep a profit and can afford mistakes. A common rule is 20-33% of lifetime gross profit, so about **$225-$375** in the example.
- **Cash-flow check:** you also need to know how long it takes to earn back the acquisition cost. If you spend $300 to get a customer and the first clean earns $125 gross profit, you're in the red until the third visit. Make sure you have the cash to carry that, or use a front-end offer (below) that pays you back sooner.

### Step 3: Work backward to cost per lead

Cost per customer = cost per lead / close rate.

- If you close 30% of leads, a $300 target cost per customer allows **$90 per lead**.
- If better follow-up raises the close rate to 50%, the same budget allows $150 per lead. That's why follow-up usually earns more than cheaper ads do.

**The point:** the business that can afford to pay the most for a customer can outspend competitors on ads. You get there by raising lifetime value (recurring plans, add-ons, retention), not by finding cheaper clicks.

## Part 2: The offers

Build a ladder of offers: one easy first yes, then steps up to the recurring revenue.

1. **Lead magnet / front-end offer (low risk for the customer):**
   - "First-floor exterior windows for $99" or "Free screen cleaning with any whole-home clean."
   - Free instant quote: they send photos of the house and you text back a price within an hour.
   - Make the offer specific, give it a deadline ("this month only, 20 spots per neighborhood"), and back it with a guarantee ("streak-free or we come back free").
2. **Core offer:** a whole-home inside and outside clean, presented in good / better / best packages (exterior only; interior and exterior; full package with screens, tracks and sills).
3. **Upsells at the job:** gutters, hard-water stain removal, solar panels, pressure washing. Show them on a simple menu or tablet before you start.
4. **Recurring plan (where the profit is):** a "Crystal Clear Club" with 2-4 visits a year, priority scheduling, a locked-in price, 10-15% off add-ons, and automatic billing. Pitch it on the doorstep as soon as you finish the first clean, while the customer is happiest.
5. **Referral offer:** "Give $50, get $50." Leave a referral card or door hanger and send a text after the job.

Make the offer strong enough that saying no feels foolish: a lot of value, little risk, and enough proof (before and after photos, reviews) that people believe it.

## Part 3: Step-by-step plan for 100 new clients

### Do the math first
- **Goal:** 100 new customers.
- **Close rate assumption:** 35% of leads book, so you need about **285 leads**.
- **Budget at a $300 target cost per customer:** about **$30,000** total, or about $105 per lead. Test small and scale up only what works.
- **Capacity check:** 100 jobs in 60-90 days is about 8-12 jobs a week. Make sure you have the crews and the schedule for that before you turn on the ads.

### Step 1: Fix what you already have (weeks 0-1)
- Optimize your Google Business Profile: photos, services, service area, and a review push.
- Build a one-page landing page for each offer with one call to action (book or get a quote), before and after photos, the guarantee, reviews, and a deadline.
- Set up a CRM with automated text and email follow-up, so each new lead gets a reply within 5 minutes.

### Step 2: Mine your existing list (fastest, cheapest wins)
- Contact past and inactive customers: "We're booking spring routes on your street. Reply YES for $X off."
- Ask current customers for referrals, using the referral offer above.

### Step 3: Turn on traffic (weeks 1-8)
- **Facebook and Instagram ads**, targeted by ZIP code and household income, with before and after videos and the front-end offer.
- **Google Local Services Ads and search ads**, to catch people who are already searching ("window cleaning near me").
- **Neighborhood saturation:** "We're cleaning windows on your street" door hangers and postcards (EDDM) in areas around current jobs, plus yard signs at jobs you're working.
- **Partnerships:** realtors (move-in and move-out cleans), property managers, HOAs, and other home-service businesses who can cross-refer customers.

### Step 4: Follow up hard
- Text right away, then call. Then send a 7-14 day sequence of texts and emails with reviews, a reminder of the deadline, and a last chance to book.
- Most of the profit in a funnel comes from follow-up. Many leads book on the third to sixth contact.

### Step 5: Turn customers into recurring members
- Pitch the recurring plan on every job and set a target, for example 40% of new customers join.
- Each member raises your lifetime value, which raises the amount you can afford to spend on the next customer.

### Step 6: Track your numbers every week and adjust
Track these each week:
- Leads by channel
- Cost per lead
- Booking rate
- Cost per customer
- Average ticket
- Upsell rate
- Recurring-plan sign-up rate

Cut channels that cost more than your target per customer, and spend more on the ones that come in under it.

---

## Summary
1. Base what you can spend on **lifetime gross profit**, not the first job. Target about 20-33% of it per customer.
2. Use an **irresistible, low-risk front-end offer** to get in the door, then **upsell** and sell a **recurring plan**.
3. Work backward from 100 customers to the leads and budget you need, start with your existing list and referrals, add local paid traffic, follow up hard, and track the numbers every week.
