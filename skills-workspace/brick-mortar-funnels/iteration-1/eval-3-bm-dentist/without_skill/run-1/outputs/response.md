# 100 New Patients in 100 Days: A Funnel Plan for a Dental Practice

**A caveat first:** I can't reliably recall the specific contents of a book called *Brick & Mortar Funnels*. I don't want to make up its chapter names, scripts or "official" steps. What follows is the standard local-business funnel approach that books in this genre usually teach (lead magnet, front-end offer, follow-up, reviews, referrals), applied to a dental practice. If you have the book, check the specifics against it. Treat the numbers below as planning assumptions, not quotes from the book.

---

## 1. Do the math backward from 100 patients

100 new patients in 100 days works out to about **1 new patient per day**, or **7 per week**. Work backward from that:

| Stage | Assumed rate | Weekly number needed |
|---|---|---|
| New patients who show up | target | 7 |
| Booked appointments (about 80% show rate) | 80% | ~9 |
| Leads who book (about 30–40% with fast follow-up) | 35% | ~25 |
| Landing-page visitors who become leads (10–20%) | 15% | ~170 |

That gives you a clear weekly scoreboard: about 170 visitors, 25 leads, 9 bookings and 7 patients who show up. Track these numbers every week and fix whichever stage is weakest.

## 2. Build the funnel

1. **Pick one irresistible front-end offer.** It should be easy to say yes to and pull in your ideal patient. Examples:
   - a new-patient special (exam, X-rays and cleaning at a low flat price)
   - a free whitening kit with a new-patient visit
   - a free consultation for implants or Invisalign (higher-value cases)

   Check that the offer complies with your state dental board's advertising rules and your insurance contracts.
2. **Build a single-purpose landing page.** It needs:
   - one offer and one call to action ("Claim your new-patient visit")
   - before-and-after photos, your face and your team
   - your review stars, insurance accepted, hours and location
   - a short form plus click-to-call
3. **Drive local traffic:**
   - Google Business Profile, fully optimized, with photos and weekly posts
   - Google Search ads for "dentist near me" and "emergency dentist [town]"
   - Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ads aimed at a 5–10 mile radius
   - local partnerships with gyms, schools, employers and realtors, where you offer a corporate or community version of the deal
4. **Respond to new leads fast.** Reply within 5 minutes by call and text. Leads go cold within hours.
   - Set up a follow-up sequence of 5–7 touches over about 10 days using SMS, email and phone.
   - Your front desk needs a booking script whose goal is to book the appointment, not just answer questions.
5. **Reduce no-shows.**
   - Send a confirmation text right away, a reminder 48 hours before and another 2 hours before.
   - Consider a small refundable deposit for promotional offers.
6. **Convert on the first visit.**
   - Deliver a great experience.
   - Present a treatment plan with financing options.
   - Pre-book the next hygiene visit before the patient leaves.

   The front-end offer gets patients in the door. Lifetime value comes from recare visits and treatment.

## 3. Get reviews systematically

- **Ask at the moment of peak happiness:** right after a good cleaning, a pain-free procedure or a smile reveal. Train the hygienist or assistant to ask in person ("Would you mind sharing your experience on Google?").
- **Make it one tap.** Text a direct link to your Google review form within an hour of the visit. Use a QR code at checkout as a backup.
- **Automate it** through your practice-management or reputation software, so every completed appointment triggers a review request plus one reminder.
- **Respond to every review**, positive or negative, and keep replies HIPAA-safe. Never confirm that someone is a patient or discuss their treatment in a public reply.
- **Don't buy or incentivize reviews.** Google's policies forbid it, and many state boards restrict it. Also, don't filter which patients you ask ("review gating"), which is against Google's policies.
- **Set a goal.** Aim for something like 1–2 new Google reviews per day during the 100 days. Review count, how recent your reviews are and your rating all drive both map-pack ranking and landing-page conversion.

## 4. Build a referral engine

- **Ask directly.** After a positive visit, say: "We're growing and would love more patients like you. Who in your family or at work could use a great dentist?"
- **Give referral cards or a shareable link** that carries the same new-patient offer, so friends get a gift.
- **Thank people who refer.** Send a handwritten card, a small gift or an entry into a monthly drawing. First check your state's rules on referral rewards and any anti-kickback rules that apply to government-insured patients. Many practices thank the referrer rather than paying per referral.
- **Run family and household offers.** Once one family member is a patient, invite the rest of the household.
- **Run "bring a friend" events or months,** such as a whitening day or a kids' back-to-school check.
- **Build professional referral relationships** with orthodontists, oral surgeons, pediatricians, physicians and local businesses.
- **Track the source of every new patient** ("How did you hear about us?") so you know which channels and patients drive growth.

## 5. A 100-day rhythm

- **Days 1–14:** Set up the offer, landing page, Google Business Profile, tracking, front-desk scripts, and the review and referral automations.
- **Days 15–60:** Launch ads and partnerships. Review the funnel numbers weekly and fix the weakest stage.
- **Days 61–100:** Scale the channels that work, push reviews and referrals hard, and keep reactivating lapsed patients from your own database. Reactivation is often the cheapest source of "new" bookings.

**In short:** have one compelling offer, a focused landing page and local traffic. Follow up within minutes and confirm appointments carefully. Then turn every happy patient into a review and a referral, so the funnel feeds itself after the 100 days end.
