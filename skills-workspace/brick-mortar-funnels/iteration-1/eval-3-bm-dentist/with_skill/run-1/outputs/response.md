# 100 New Dental Patients in 100 Days: the *Brick & Mortar Funnels* plan

The book gives one chapter to each industry. The dental chapter is by **Dr. Anissa Holmes**, and her plan runs in **four phases**. Reviews and referrals come mostly from two other chapters. **Tyler Shaule** gives the "rate, review and referral" text-message funnel. **David Frey** gives local referral methods. **Dr. Chad Woolner** (chiropractor) gives a "portfolio of proof" that carries over well to a dental office.

Points marked *(book)* come from the book. Points marked *(std)* are general practice or compliance notes, not from the book. The book uses figures from 2017–2019, so ad costs and platform features have changed since then.

---

## 1. Start with the numbers

The experts all work backward from what a new patient is worth.

- **Case value** = collections ÷ number of new patients. Use a yearly figure if you can (Woolner) *(book)*.
- **Maximum cost to acquire a patient (CAC)** = how much of that value you're willing to spend to get one patient (Latimer) *(book)*. Implant and Invisalign cases can carry a much higher CAC than a hygiene patient.
- **Work backward from 100** *(std, using the book's method)*. Suppose 50% of people who come in for a consult start treatment. Then you need about 200 consult shows. At a 60% show rate, that's about 333 appointments. If a third of leads book, you need about 1,000 leads. Put your own rates in place of these.
- **Hormozi's return target:** spend $1 to make $3 *(book)*.

Track five numbers every week: leads, booked appointments, shows, starts, and cost per new patient.

## 2. Dr. Anissa Holmes's four phases

### Phase 1: Days 1–45. Build warm audiences
Get local attention before you spend much on ads.

- **Gift-card giveaway with local businesses** *(book)*
  - Ask nearby businesses (salons, cafés, boutiques, gyms) to donate a gift card each.
  - Give away **one prize a day for 12 days**, announced on **Facebook Live**.
  - To enter, people **share and comment** on Facebook, or comment only on Instagram.
  - This builds your engaged audience, and each partner business promotes it to their own customers.
- **Smile makeover competition** for someone in your community who needs it *(book)*. This gives you a story with real emotion, before-and-after content, and local goodwill.
- **Set up retargeting audiences now** *(book)*. Include people who engage with your page and Instagram, website visitors, your patient list, and video viewers. Later ads go to these warm people first.
- **Train your front desk on the phone** *(book)*. The team should lead the call with questions instead of reading out prices:
  1. "What is your biggest challenge with your teeth or smile?"
  2. Ask what the biggest benefit of fixing it would be.
  3. Have them picture the result.
  4. "If you can fit [cost] into your budget, are you ready to start right away?"

Do your **existing patient list** first as well (from the book's shared principles and Shaule's reactivation funnel). Text and email past and overdue patients with a fresh offer, for example a hygiene and whitening special, and invite them to the giveaway. Shaule recommends **repackaging the offer every 90 days** *(book)*.

### Phase 2: Days 46–50. Funnels for high-value treatments
- Build a **separate funnel with email follow-up** for each high-value service: **implants**, **Invisalign**, and **people at risk of cavities** *(book)*.
- Use **one offer per landing page**. A general "we do everything" website converts poorly (Latimer) *(book)*.
- Use a short form (name, email, phone) and put a booking calendar on the page or the next page (Hormozi, Argue) *(book)*.
- For consult-heavy treatments such as implants and Invisalign, Shaule's **survey-to-appointment** format fits well: 3–5 questions, with the first two easy and one or two that qualify the person *(book)*.

### Phase 3: Days 51–80. The 30-day floss challenge
- Run a **30-day floss challenge** with **weekly prizes** *(book)*. It keeps your new audience engaged for a month and gives people a reason to come in.
- Run **video testimonial ads** to your warm retargeting audiences at the same time *(book)*.

### Phase 4: Days 81–100. More traffic and conversion
- Add more traffic, for example a community drive such as a **holiday clothing drive**, plus more ads *(book)*.
- **Convert the leads.** Call and text every new lead the same day (Mashore, Hormozi). McCall notes that most business comes from follow-up, with 6–7 touches over 3–4 months *(book)*.

## 3. How to get reviews

### Tyler Shaule's "rate, review and referral" funnel
Shaule says to set this up **early**, not at the end *(book)*.

1. **Text a short survey** after each visit using the Net Promoter Score (NPS) question:
   - "How likely are you to recommend us to a friend, on a scale of 1–10?"
   - "How could we earn a score closer to 10?"
2. **Ask for a Google review**, and follow up personally with anyone who isn't happy.
3. **Target: about 300 five-star reviews** *(book)*.
4. **Then ask for referrals.** People who just gave you a 9 or 10 are your best referral source.

**One important change to the book's version** *(std, verify)*: Shaule sends only the 9s and 10s to Google. That is "review gating", and Google's policies discourage or forbid it. A safer version:
- Send the review link to **every** patient.
- Use the NPS answers to find and fix problems, and call anyone who scores low.

Text messages have about a 98% open rate *(book)*, which is why this works well by text.

### Proof that keeps adding up (Woolner, adapted)
- **Film at least one patient success story a week** *(book)*: smile makeovers, implant cases, nervous patients who had a good visit.
- Put these stories on **"proof pages"** and use them in your retargeting and testimonial ads. Woolner calls the effect the "Position Ascension Loop": better proof leads to higher expectations and better results, which give you a stronger reputation *(book)*.

## 4. How to get referrals

- **From your giveaway partners.** The businesses that donated gift cards already share your posts. Keep the relationship going.
- **David Frey's five referral methods** *(book)*:
  1. **Refer a friend:** ask happy patients to introduce you.
  2. **Share a post:** ask patients to share their success story.
  3. **"Can I use your profile?":** a patient introduces you to their friends.
  4. **A "Team 12" group:** 12 local professionals who refer to each other, for example an orthodontist, a pediatrician, a realtor, a salon owner, a gym owner, and a financial adviser.
  5. **A "viral deal":** an offer so good people want to pass it on.
- **Be the "local celebrity" on Facebook** (Frey) *(book)*:
  - Friend local people and your **Dream 100** local influencers.
  - Join local groups.
  - Send about **20 friend requests a day**.
  - Post only positive, non-political content.
  - Frey says to check Facebook's Terms of Service first if you plan a second personal profile.
- **Bring-a-friend bonuses** (Cross) *(book)*. Good timing: during the floss challenge, or right after a patient gives you a high NPS score.

## 5. A simple 100-day calendar

| Days | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–14 | Work out case value and maximum CAC. Reactivate your patient list by text and email. Start the NPS review funnel. Line up giveaway partners. Phone training |
| 15–45 | 12-day gift-card giveaway on Facebook Live. Smile makeover contest. Build retargeting audiences. Local Facebook networking, 20 friend requests a day |
| 46–50 | Implant, Invisalign and cavity-risk funnels with email follow-up |
| 51–80 | 30-day floss challenge with weekly prizes. Video testimonial ads. Bring-a-friend offers. Film one success story a week |
| 81–100 | Holiday clothing drive and more ads. Same-day lead follow-up. Push for reviews, then referrals. Start the next 90-day offer |

## 6. Compliance points for a dental practice *(std, check with your state board and counsel)*

- **Patient privacy (HIPAA).** Get **written authorization** before you use any patient's photos, story or video in marketing. Don't reply to reviews in a way that confirms someone is a patient or shares any treatment details.
- **Testimonials (FTC and state dental boards).** Use only real reviews and testimonials; never invent one. Some boards also limit before-and-after photos or require disclaimers.
- **Giveaways and contests.** Follow Facebook and Instagram promotion rules and your state's contest and sweepstakes laws. Some states restrict free services or other inducements, especially for Medicare and Medicaid patients.
- **Paying for referrals.** Many state dental boards limit or ban paying people, including patients, for referrals. Check the rules before you offer cash or prizes for a referral.
- **Texting and email.** Get **prior express written consent** before sending marketing texts (TCPA), and follow CAN-SPAM for email.

The results in the book are the authors' own examples, not guarantees. Ad costs have risen since 2019, so test small, measure, and put more budget into whatever works.
