# 100 Gym Members in 100 Days on $1,000: Hormozi's Plan

Alex Hormozi's chapter in *Brick & Mortar Funnels* is titled **"100 Gym Members With Only $1,000 In The Next 100 Days,"** so it fits your situation closely. His main idea is that **$1,000 is not your marketing budget. It is seed money.** Your new members pay for the rest of your marketing.

> The dollar figures below are the book's own examples (from about 2019–2020). Ad costs have gone up since then. Use your own numbers once you have a week or two of data.

---

## 1. The core idea: client-financed acquisition

**You collect more cash when someone joins than it cost to get them. The extra pays for getting the *next* member.**

The book's example:

| Per sale | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cash collected at the point of sale | **$1,100** |
| Pays back the ads that brought in this member | $300 |
| Pays for the ads that bring in the next member | $300 |
| Left for fulfillment and profit | $500 |

His rule of thumb: **spend $1 to make $3.** $1 repays the ads, $1 buys the next customer, and $1 goes to the bank. If the cash comes in at the point of sale, your marketing spend is limited only by how fast you can recycle it.

He quotes Dan Kennedy: *"He who can spend the most to acquire a customer wins."*

---

## 2. Stop selling a $130/month membership

Hormozi calls a $130-a-month membership a **commodity**. A gym's advantage is **trust**, so sell a high-ticket **result**.

> "Better to close 50% at 5x the price than 100% at 1x."

**Weekly service tiers** (book example):
- **$149/week:** semi-private training plus 1-on-1 accountability
- down to **$49/week:** accountability only

**How to price a result:** A member wants to lose 50 lb. At 2 lb a week, that takes 25 weeks. Double it to 50 weeks for a realistic timeline. 50 weeks × $99/week = **$4,950** for the program.

---

## 3. Where the $1,100 per sale comes from

Out of **10 prospects who show up**, Hormozi expects about **5 sales** (a 50% close rate):

| Buyer type | Count | Cash up front |
|---|---|---|
| "Whale" | 1 | $2,500 |
| Above average | 1 | $1,200 |
| Normal | 3 | $600 each = $1,800 |
| **Total** | **5 sales** | **$5,500 → about $1,100 cash per sale** |

Each sale also comes with ongoing payments (continuity), so the monthly revenue is on top of this.

### The five-sale sequence (how you collect the cash)
1. **Service:** the program itself, sold at the consult.
2. **Food:** a nutrition orientation 24–48 hours later. **60–80% buy meal delivery.**
3. **Supplements:** "75%+ of USA residents buy supplements."
4. **12-month commitment:** offered to anyone who was downsold.
5. **Paid in full:** 14 days later, **20–30% take the "Year of Change" pay-in-full discount.**

Steps 2–5 raise the cash collected early, and that early cash funds the next round of ads.

---

## 4. How much you can spend per show

| Step | Math |
|---|---|
| Value of one show | $1,100 cash per sale × 50% close rate = **$550** |
| Most you should spend per show | about half of that = **$275** |

**Key metric:** for every prospect who actually shows up for a consult, spend **up to about $275** on ads. Don't track cost per lead or cost per click.

---

## 5. The funnel

**Ad → click → opt-in → schedule → show**

- **Ad:** a challenge with a deadline and a clear result instead of "join our gym." Hormozi's examples:
  - **6-Week Mommy Makeover Challenge**
  - **28-Day Bikini Blueprint**
  - **42-Day Detox**
- **Opt-in page:** name, email, phone.
- **Scheduling page:** book a consult. Offer slots **3–8 pm on weekdays, *including Friday*,** and **9 am–noon on Saturday.**
- **Free Facebook group** for people who opted in.
- **Contact everyone who opts in, whether they scheduled or not.** Call and text to confirm and get them excited, so they actually show up.

---

## 6. Working backward to 100 members

The book gives the close rate (50%). The show rate and booking rate below are planning assumptions, so swap in your own numbers.

| Stage | Assumption | Number needed |
|---|---|---|
| **New members** | target | **100** |
| Shows | 50% close (Hormozi) | **200** |
| Booked consults | ~60% show rate (assumption) | **~333** |
| Leads (opt-ins) | ~1 in 3 books (assumption) | **~1,000** |

**What that costs:**
- At about **$300 per sale** (the book's example), 100 members = **about $30,000 in total ad spend** over the 100 days.
- That works out to about **$30 per lead**, which is inside the $5–$35 Facebook lead cost that another author in the book (Mike Arce) reported at the time.
- Hard ceiling: 200 shows × $275 = $55,000. If you're spending more than that, something in the funnel is broken.

You don't need $30,000 up front. **Your first members pay for it.**

---

## 7. How $1,000 compounds to 100 members

This applies Hormozi's split: from each $1,100 sale, put **$600 back into ads** ($300 to repay, $300 for the next member). Each round then spends **double** the round before.

| Round | Ad spend | New members (at $300 each) | Cash collected (at $1,100) | Total members so far |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1,000 (your money) | ~3 | ~$3,700 | ~3 |
| 2 | $2,000 | ~7 | ~$7,300 | ~10 |
| 3 | $4,000 | ~13 | ~$14,700 | ~23 |
| 4 | $8,000 | ~27 | ~$29,300 | ~50 |
| 5 | $16,000 | ~53 | ~$58,700 | **~103** |
| **Total** | **$31,000** (only $1,000 from you) | **~103** | **~$113,000** | |

- **Your own money at risk: $1,000.** The other ~$30,000 in ad spend comes from member cash.
- **Left after ads:** about $82,000, which covers coaching, fulfillment and profit (about $500 per member in the book's split).
- **Timing:** five rounds of about 2–3 weeks each fit inside 100 days. Food sells within 48 hours, and pay-in-full comes at day 14, so cash from one round can fund the next.
- **Round 1 sanity check:** $1,000 at ~$30/lead ≈ 33 leads → ~11 booked → ~7 shows → ~3 sales. The math holds even at small scale.

---

## 8. A 100-day calendar

| Days | What to do |
|---|---|
| **1–7** | Build the offer: choose a challenge (for example the 6-Week Mommy Makeover), set the weekly tiers ($49–$149), price the full result. Write the five-sale sequence and the consult script. Build the opt-in page, the scheduling page and the Facebook group. |
| **8–21** | **Round 1:** spend the $1,000. Call and text every opt-in the same day. Run consults, then nutrition orientations 24–48 hours later. Track leads, bookings, shows, closes and cash per sale. |
| **22–35** | **Round 2:** put $600 from each sale back into ads (~$2,000). Make the day-14 pay-in-full offer to Round 1 members. Fix the weakest stage of the funnel: show rate or close rate. |
| **36–56** | **Round 3:** ~$4,000. Add consult slots and staff for the extra consults. Test a second challenge angle. |
| **57–77** | **Round 4:** ~$8,000. Your fulfillment capacity now limits growth as much as ad spend does. |
| **78–100** | **Round 5:** ~$16,000 → about 100 members in total. Then repeat ("rinse and repeat"; top gyms simply spend more). |

---

## 9. What has to be true for this to work

- **Close rate of about 50%.** At 25%, a show is worth only $275, so you can spend only about $137 per show. Practice your sales skills before you raise the budget.
- **Cash up front.** The model depends on collecting about $1,100 within the first two weeks. If you sell month-to-month only, you can't recycle the money fast enough.
- **Same-day follow-up.** Most lost sales come from people who opt in but never book or never show up.
- **Check your own numbers every week.** If your cost per show rises above about $275, pause ads and fix the offer or the follow-up before you spend more.
- **Compliance:** get written consent before sending marketing texts (TCPA). Follow CAN-SPAM for email, Facebook's ad policies (they restrict weight-loss and body-image claims), and the FTC rules on testimonials and results claims. Put challenge, refund and pay-in-full terms in writing.

---

**In short:** Hormozi would sell a high-ticket challenge, collect about $1,100 cash per sale through the five-sale sequence, and spend up to about $275 per show. He'd put $600 of each sale back into ads, so the $1,000 doubles each round and reaches about 100 members in five rounds. Only the first $1,000 is your own money.
