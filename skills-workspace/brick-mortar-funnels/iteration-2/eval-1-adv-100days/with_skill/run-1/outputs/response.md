# 100 Customers in 100 Days: One-Person Junk Removal

*A plan built from* Brick & Mortar Funnels *(ClickFunnels, about 2020)*. The main source is **Joshua Latimer** (home services: max CAC, front-end offers, one page per offer, test and measure). Three other experts fill gaps a solo hauler has:

- **Tyler Shaule:** the Phone Funnel System. Reactivation, a text-a-photo-for-a-quote survey funnel, and a rate, review and referral funnel.
- **David Frey:** the "local celebrity" Facebook referral machine, plus a Dream 100 of local partners.
- **Dr. Chad Woolner:** the "portfolio of proof", meaning one filmed success story a week.

Tags: **(book)** means the idea comes from the book. **(adapt)** means I've applied a book idea to junk removal. **(assume)** means a placeholder number you should swap for your own.

---

## 0. The constraint that shapes everything: you are one person

100 customers in 100 days is about **7 jobs a week**, or 1–2 a day on a 5–6 day week. One truck and one person can do that. It's also close to the limit once you count dump runs, estimates and marketing time. So the plan has to do three things:

1. **Fill the calendar with jobs that pay well.** Don't fill it with tiny jobs that eat a whole morning.
2. **Batch work by area and time slot.** For example, morning jobs on the north side, afternoons on the south side, and a dump run between them *(adapt)*.
3. **Automate the follow-up** with text and email templates, so marketing doesn't stop when you're on a job. Shaule: "Conversations = Conversions" *(book)*.

Shaule's point fits you well: for a business that sells in person, **"the closest to the customer wins"** *(book)*. Your edge over national franchises is that you're the owner, you answer the text, and you show up yourself. Market that. Make your face and name the brand (Frey's "local celebrity") *(book / adapt)*.

## 1. Know your numbers first (Latimer's maximum CAC)

Latimer's rule: before you spend a dollar, know the most you can afford to pay to get one customer *(book)*. Junk removal is mostly **one-off jobs**, so use his one-off formula: decide what share of a job's profit you'll give up to win it. Then add the **back end** (repeat jobs and referrals), because "amateurs focus on the front end but professionals focus on the back end" *(book)*.

Fill this in with your own figures. The numbers below are placeholders *(assume)*:

| Item | Placeholder | Your number |
|---|---|---|
| Average job revenue | $325 | |
| Direct costs per job (dump fees, fuel, wear, helper if used) | $135 | |
| Gross profit per job | **$190** (about 58%) | |
| Share of first-job profit you'll give up to acquire a customer | 35% | |
| **Max CAC on the first job** | **about $65** | |
| Expected extra jobs per customer in 12 months (repeat and referral) | 0.5 | |
| Back-end profit per customer (0.5 × $190) | $95 | |
| **Max CAC counting the back end** | **about $100** (stretch limit) | |

How to use it:
- **Day-to-day rule:** keep blended cost per booked job under **about $65**. Go up toward **about $100** only for channels that bring repeat or referral customers, such as realtors, property managers and contractors *(adapt)*.
- **Hormozi's test** *(book)*: "spend $1 to make $3". If $65 in ads brings a $190-profit job, you're well over that.
- **Latimer's step 7:** record every lead's source, quote, close and revenue in a simple sheet. Read it "unemotionally" every week *(book)*.

### Working backward from 100 customers

The book's logic (Hormozi and Argue's funnel math) is leads → quotes → booked jobs. Placeholder rates *(assume)*:

## 2. The value ladder and front-end offers (Latimer)

Latimer says to lead with **low-priced, irresistible front-end offers**, each tied to a reason to act now (he uses holidays), and then move customers up the ladder *(book)*. His home-services examples were a $20 45-minute power clean, a free spot clean, and 10 exterior windows for $99 *(book)*.

**Your ladder** *(adapt)*:

| Rung | Offer | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Front end A | **"Single-Item Pickup, $59"**: one mattress, sofa, fridge (where you can legally take it), or hot tub cover | A low-risk first job, easy to say yes to. Often turns into "while you're here, can you also take…" |
| Front end B | **"Garage Corner Clear-Out, $129"**: up to 1/6 of a truckload | Holiday and New Year declutter hook |
| Front end C | **"Free Text-a-Photo Quote in 15 Minutes"** | The main lead offer for everything else (Shaule's survey idea, see section 4) |
| Core | Partial and full truckloads | Where most of the profit is |
| Upper | Garage, basement and estate clean-outs; move-out and eviction clean-outs | Big tickets, often through partners |
| Back end | **Partner accounts**: realtors, property managers, landlords, contractors, senior-move managers, storage facilities | Repeat jobs. Latimer says one good client moving up the ladder can be worth about $3,850 a year in his trade *(book)* |

**Price discipline:** the front-end offer has to be small and clearly bounded ("one item", "1/6 load"), so it can't turn into an unpaid full haul. Upsell on site with a clear price from your load-size chart *(adapt)*.

**Hooks by season.** If you start about now (late November), roughly in order *(adapt)*:
- "Make room before the holiday guests arrive."
- "Post-holiday haul-away: boxes, old TVs, the sofa the new one replaced."
- "New Year, empty garage."
- "Spring head start / moving season."

Latimer's own example ties the offer to a holiday such as the 4th of July *(book)*.

## 3. One offer, one landing page (Latimer's 7 steps)

Latimer: "your broad website is failing you". Build **one simple page per offer** *(book)*. Repeat his seven steps for each offer:

1. **Pick one irresistible front-end offer**, for example "Single-Item Pickup $59".
2. **Write the copy:** a hook headline, body text, benefits, and urgency or scarcity *(book)*. With one truck, scarcity is real: "Only 10 single-item slots this week".
3. **Build a page for that one offer only:** headline, before-and-after photos, 3 benefits, price, "Text a photo to book", a form (name, phone, email, address or zip, photo upload), and a scheduler.
4. **Use strong images, especially before-and-after shots** *(book)*. You'll shoot these on every job (section 5).
5. **Repeat** for offers B and C.
6. **Drive traffic:** 3–5 emails to existing clients, Facebook Live and posts, Facebook ads *(book)*.
7. **Measure and record** in your sheet *(book)*.

**Sample page copy** *(adapt; Latimer's elements plus Arce's audience call-out and specificity)*:

> **[TOWN] HOMEOWNERS: That old couch is gone by Friday for $59.**
> One item. One flat price. Picked up by the owner, not a call center. I do the lifting, you point.
> ✓ Flat price, no surprise "load fees" ✓ Usable items donated where possible ✓ Same-week slots
> **Only 10 single-item slots this week.** Text a photo to [number] or book below.

Frey suggests a memorable URL with "free" and the city name in it *(book)*. For example, `[Town]JunkQuote.com` or `Free[Town]JunkQuote.com` for the photo-quote offer.

## 4. The phone funnel (Shaule): built for a one-person business

Shaule's three funnels fit a solo hauler well, because most of the work is done by text *(book)*:

### 4a. Reactivation funnel (week 1, your quickest customers)
- Upload every past customer and every old lead with a mobile number *(book)*. **Only text people who agreed to receive texts.** Under TCPA you need prior express written consent for marketing texts *(std)*. If you don't have it, call, email or mail them instead.
- Text a repackaged offer *(adapt)*:
  > "Hi [Name], it's [You] from [Business], I hauled your old [item] last spring. I'm doing holiday clear-outs in [area] next week: anything up to a garage corner for $129. Want me to save you a slot? Reply STOP to opt out."
- Shaule says to **repackage the offer every 90 days** *(book)*. For you, that's one fresh offer per season.

### 4b. Survey-to-appointment funnel ("Text a photo for a quote")
Shaule recommends a survey funnel for businesses that need an estimate, such as contractors. Use 3–5 questions: the first two easy, then one or two that qualify *(book)*. Yours *(adapt)*:
1. What do you need gone? (furniture / appliances / yard debris / mixed / full clean-out)
2. Where is it? (curb / garage / inside, upstairs / basement)
3. Roughly how much? (one item / a pickup-bed's worth / a room / a whole house)
4. When do you need it gone? (this week / this month / just pricing)
5. Upload or text a photo.

Then reply with a price range and two time slots within 15 minutes when you can. "This week" answers are your hottest leads.

**SMS entry points** (Shaule's tips) *(book)*: a text extension on Google ads, a "Text Me" button on Instagram and Facebook, your texting number printed large on the truck, yard signs and door hangers, and the number on every landing page.

### 4c. Rate, review and referral funnel (start in week 1, not week 10)
- After every job, send a short survey: "How likely are you to recommend us (1–10)?" and "What would make it a 10?" *(book)*.
- **Ask every customer for a Google review, not only the 9s and 10s.** The book suggests sending only high scorers to Google, but Google prohibits that kind of "review gating" *(std, verify)*. Use low scores to follow up personally and fix the problem.
- Then ask for a referral: "Know a neighbor with a garage like yours? They get $20 off and you get a $20 gift card." *(adapt)*. Check local rules on referral rewards.
- Shaule's long-term target is about **300 five-star reviews** *(book)*. In 100 days, aim for **60 or more** (a 60% review rate on 100 jobs) *(assume)*.

## 5. Proof portfolio (Woolner)

Woolner's "marathon" phase builds a **portfolio of proof**: film at least one success story a week and send traffic to "proof pages" *(book)*. Junk removal suits this, because before-and-after shots are very visual *(adapt)*:
- **On every job:** a 10-second "before" pan, a 10-second "after" pan, and the truck loaded. Ask permission, and leave out house numbers and faces unless the customer agrees.
- **Each week:** one 60-second customer story video, for example "Linda's late mom's garage: 3 hours, 2 loads, 40% donated". Real customers only, with their consent. **Never invent or stage testimonials** *(skill caveat; FTC rules, std)*.
- Post them to your Facebook profile, page, local groups and Google Business Profile. Run them as retargeting ads to people who visited your landing pages *(Jennifer Sewell and Holmes on retargeting with testimonials, book)*.
- Build a "Recent Clear-Outs in [Town]" proof page, and link it from every offer page.

Woolner's **Position Ascension Loop** *(book)* is positioning → perception → expectation → anticipation → superior outcomes → reputation. Applied to you: become "the [Town] junk guy who actually shows up, sweeps up and donates" *(adapt)*.

## 6. Local referral machine (Frey) and partner accounts

Frey's play is a lead-generating personal Facebook profile, friend everyone local, friend your **Dream 100**, join local groups, send **20 friend requests a day**, post positive content, and use referral methods *(book)*. Frey himself warns you to **check Facebook's Terms of Service** before creating a second personal profile *(book)*. The safest route is to use your one real profile.

**Your Dream 100** (the people who send repeat jobs) *(adapt)*:
- Realtors and listing agents (pre-sale clean-outs)
- Property managers and landlords (move-out and eviction clean-outs)
- Estate-sale companies, senior-move managers, estate attorneys
- Self-storage managers (abandoned units)
- Remodelers, flooring and roofing contractors (debris)
- HOA managers, church and PTA leaders (community clean-up days)

For each: comment on their posts for a couple of days, then send a friend request and keep engaging for 30 days *(Frey, book)*. Then offer a **partner deal**, for example "Your clients get priority same-week slots and a 10% client discount. You get one call and it's handled, with photos sent to you when it's done." *(adapt)*.

**Frey's referral methods, adapted** *(book / adapt)*:
- **Share a post:** ask happy customers to share their before-and-after post.
- **Refer a friend:** the $20 / $20 offer above.
- **"Team 12":** a referral group of 12 local trades (a realtor, organizer, cleaner, handyman, mover, painter…) who pass jobs to each other.
- **The "viral deal":** for example, "Free curbside pickup of one Christmas tree with any paid job in January." It's cheap for you and easy to share.

**Fishbowl twist** (Frey's restaurant fishbowl, adapted): put a "Win a free garage clear-out ($300 value)" box at a hardware store, coffee shop or storage facility. Draw once a month, and build your list from the entries *(adapt)*. Check local sweepstakes rules (free entry, posted rules) and only text entrants who ticked a consent box *(std)*.

## 7. The 100-day calendar

Organized into the skill's blocks: list and warm audience, then funnels and paid traffic, then scale and proof.

### Weeks 1–2 (days 1–14): Numbers, offers, existing list, warm audience
- [ ] Fill in the maximum CAC table and the funnel math (section 1). Set up the tracking sheet: source, quote, booked, revenue, review, referral.
- [ ] Choose offer A for the current season. Write it using Latimer's copy elements.
- [ ] Set up a business texting number with two-way SMS, saved reply templates and a consent line on every form (Shaule; TCPA) *(book / std)*.
- [ ] **Reactivation blast** to consented past customers and leads (section 4a). *Goal: 5–8 jobs.*
- [ ] Turn on the NPS, review and referral sequence now (section 4c).
- [ ] Polish your Facebook profile: a banner with your face, the truck and the offer URL. Fill in the "Details About You" section (Frey) *(book)*.
- [ ] Start **20 local friend requests a day** and join 5–10 local groups (Frey) *(book)*. Post a helpful tip, a before-and-after, and a "here's who I am" story.
- [ ] Make your Dream 100 list. Start engaging with 10 a day.
- [ ] Google Business Profile: photos, services, the text number, and the first reviews from past customers.
- **Target by day 14:** about 10 customers.

### Weeks 3–4 (days 15–28): One-offer pages and the photo-quote funnel
- [ ] Build landing pages for offers A and C (Latimer steps 3–5). Add a pixel so you can retarget (Arce's reason for using a landing page rather than a lead-form ad) *(book)*.
- [ ] Build the text-a-photo survey (section 4b) and put the "Text Me" entry points everywhere.
- [ ] Send 3–5 emails to your existing list about the offer (Latimer step 6) *(book)*.
- [ ] Facebook Live or posts: "Clearing a garage in [neighborhood] today, I have 2 slots left this week."
- [ ] Door hangers on 20–30 houses around every job: "I was just at your neighbor's" *(adapt)*.
- [ ] First Dream 100 partner offers: 5 realtors, 5 property managers, 3 contractors.
- [ ] Film the first weekly proof story (Woolner) *(book)*.
- **Target by day 28:** about 25 customers in total.

### Weeks 5–6 (days 29–42): Paid traffic within your maximum CAC
- [ ] Launch Facebook and Instagram ads to the offer A page, aimed at homeowners within your service radius. Run **at least 2 ads that differ in one thing** (the photo or the headline), and judge each after about $50 of spend (Arce) *(book)*.
- [ ] Google search ads for "junk removal [town]" with a text extension (Shaule) *(book)*. Cap spend so **cost per booked job stays under about $65** *(Latimer, applied)*.
- [ ] Retargeting: show proof videos to page visitors who didn't book (J. Sewell, Holmes) *(book)*.
- [ ] Speed-to-lead: answer every text within 15 minutes during working hours. Send automated replies while you're lifting.
- [ ] Follow up every unbooked quote 3 times over 10 days. McCall's rule is that 90% of business comes from follow-up (6–7 touches over 3–4 months) *(book)*.
- **Target by day 42:** about 42 customers in total.

### Weeks 7–10 (days 43–70): Scale what works and add a second offer
- [ ] Read the sheet unemotionally (Latimer step 7). Stop the worst channel and put that budget into the best.
- [ ] Launch offer B with its own page (Latimer step 5: repeat for each offer) *(book)*.
- [ ] Sign up 3–5 partner accounts with a simple "priority slot" agreement. Send photos to the partner after each job.
- [ ] **Run a community clean-up day** with an HOA, church or neighborhood group. This adapts Woolner's "Epic Event". Haul one curb pile for each house for a flat fee. Raffle a free garage clear-out, with prizes donated by Team 12 partners, to collect names. Give a "book within 7 days" price (his "Glue and Offer") *(adapt)*.
- [ ] Batch the schedule by area. If demand passes capacity, raise prices before adding hours, and think about a part-time helper for big jobs only *(adapt)*.
- **Target by day 70:** about 72 customers in total.

### Weeks 11–14 (days 71–100): Proof, reviews, referrals, and the back end
- [ ] Publish the "Recent Clear-Outs" proof page with 8–10 real stories (Woolner) *(book)*.
- [ ] Push for reviews: a personal text to every customer who hasn't left one yet (to everyone, with no gating).
- [ ] Referral push: "Know someone moving or downsizing this spring?"
- [ ] Partner check-in: give each Dream 100 partner a recap of the jobs you did for their clients.
- [ ] Repackage the offer for the next 90 days (Shaule) *(book)*.
- [ ] Plan the next 100 days from your real close, show and cost numbers.
- **Target by day 100:** 100 customers. **Stretch:** 60+ new reviews and 5+ active partner accounts.

### Daily rhythm (about 60–90 minutes a day of marketing)
- **Before the first job:** answer texts and quotes. Do 20 friend requests and 10 Dream 100 engagements (Frey) *(book)*.
- **On every job:** before-and-after shots, door hangers next door, and ask "anything else while I'm here?"
- **Evening:** post one piece of proof, send review and referral texts, and update the sheet.
- Mashore's **6 must-dos a day, hardest first** works well for a solo owner *(book)*.

## 9. Compliance and cautions

- **Texting and calls:** get prior express written consent for marketing texts (TCPA), honor STOP, and follow email law (CAN-SPAM) *(std)*.
- **Reviews:** ask everyone. No gating, no incentives in exchange for reviews, and no fake testimonials (Google policy and FTC rules) *(std, verify)*.
- **Facebook:** check the Terms of Service before running a second personal profile (Frey's own warning). Follow platform rules for contests and promotions *(book / std)*.
- **Sweepstakes and raffles:** offer free entry and post the rules. Raffles that require a purchase can be illegal gambling in some states *(std)*.
- **Disposal:** know what you legally can't take or must handle in a special way (paint, chemicals, tires, refrigerant appliances, e-waste), and keep dump and recycling receipts *(std)*.
- **Business basics:** liability insurance, a commercial vehicle policy, any local hauling license, and a written price chart so on-site upsells are clear *(std)*.
- **Numbers:** the experts' results are their own claims, not guarantees. Ad costs have risen since the book (Arce's lead costs of $5–35 on Facebook date from about 2019) *(book / std)*. Replace every placeholder in this plan with your real data by week 6.
