## The math: what "100 customers in 100 days" actually needs

| Metric | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | 100 (about 1 per day, about 7 per week) | You're one person, so 1–2 jobs a day is realistic capacity |
| Booked quotes needed | ~160 | Assumes about 60–65% of on-site or photo quotes close |
| Leads needed | ~400 | Assumes about 40% of leads book a quote |
| Leads per week | ~28 → ramping from 10 (wk 1–2) to 40+ (wk 8+) | |
| Avg ticket | $250–$450 | Mix of single items, half loads and full loads |
| Revenue goal | $30k–$40k | Use it to fund ads in phase 2 |

Your bottleneck is capacity, not leads. Make every job count three times: the job itself, a review and a referral.

## The funnel (home-services style)

### 1. Front-end offer (the "hook")
Don't sell "junk removal." Sell an outcome with no risk attached:

> **"Garage Back in 1 Afternoon — Guaranteed. Upfront photo quote in 15 minutes, no surprise fees, and we donate or recycle what we can. If we're late, $50 off."**

Pieces of the offer:
- **Photo/text quote**: the lead texts 3 photos and gets a price range within 15 minutes. That's low friction and faster than competitors.
- **Price anchor**: "Single-item pickups from $89. Truckload pricing by volume (1/8, 1/4, 1/2, full)."
- **Risk reversal**: on-time guarantee, a price-lock (the quote is the price unless the volume changes) and a sweep-up included.
- **Bonus stack**: free donation receipt, a free "one more item" add-on under 50 lb, and a photo of the empty space.
- **Scarcity (true)**: "I'm a one-truck operation, so I book only 8 jobs a week." This is honest and builds urgency.

### 2. Lead capture
- A one-page landing page: headline, before/after photos, a 3-step "how it works," "Text photos to (xxx)" plus a 3-field form, reviews and a guarantee.
- Google Business Profile (GBP): this is your #1 free asset. Pick the right categories, add service areas and post 3 times a week.
- Click-to-text and click-to-call on everything.

### 3. Speed-to-lead follow-up
- Reply within 5 minutes (auto-text: "Got it! Send 2–3 photos and I'll price it in 15 min. — [Name], owner").
- If they don't book, follow up on day 1, day 3 and day 7. Then put them in a monthly "clean-out season" text.
- Confirm the day before, text "on my way," and send the review request within 2 hours after the job.

### 4. Back end: reviews, referrals, repeat
- **Review ask**: leave a card on site with a QR code. Ask by text the same day. Goal: 1 review per 2 jobs.
- **Referral offer**: "$25 off your next haul or a $25 gift card for every friend who books."
- **Partner referrals** (covered below): realtors, property managers, estate-sale companies, contractors and storage facilities.
- **Repeat**: offer a seasonal clean-out reminder in spring and fall, and "move-out week" packages.

## Borrowed expert #2: the event / "one-day" funnel

Event and workshop funnels work by giving people a reason to act now and a place to gather. You can adapt that for junk removal like this:

- **"Neighborhood Clean-Out Day"**: pick one subdivision or HOA. Drop door hangers and post in the neighborhood Facebook group or Nextdoor. Run it like this: "On Saturday the truck is in [Neighborhood]. Shared-load pricing: 30% off when 3+ neighbors book the same day." This puts many small jobs on one route and spreads by word of mouth.
- **A free mini-workshop with a partner**: "Downsizing Without the Stress," held at a senior center, library or realtor office with an estate-sale or organizing partner. Collect sign-ups and offer an attendee-only bundle.
- **A charity tie-in**: "Every haul this month donates X to [local food bank or habitat store]." This gives you PR, a story to share in Facebook groups and goodwill.

## The 100-day plan

### Phase 1 — Days 1–20: Foundation and first 15 customers (warm market + free channels)
**Goal: 15 customers, 10 reviews**

| Days | Actions |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Build the landing page, GBP, a text-quote number and simple CRM/spreadsheet tracking (lead source → quote → booked → review). Write the offer. |
| 1–5 | **Warm launch**: text or call 100 people you know. "I just launched; first 10 jobs are 20% off and I'd love a review." Post the same on your personal Facebook. |
| 3–10 | Join 5–10 local Facebook groups, Nextdoor and buy/sell groups. Post before/after photos (not ads). Respond to every "anyone know a hauler?" post within an hour. |
| 5–20 | Drop 500 door hangers in 2–3 target neighborhoods (older homes, high owner-occupancy, near move-out zones). |
| 7–20 | Make a list of 30 referral partners (realtors, property managers, estate-sale companies, storage facilities, contractors, cleaning services). Visit 2 a day with a one-page "partner sheet" and a priority-booking promise. |
| Ongoing | After every job: before/after photos, a review ask and a referral card. |

### Phase 2 — Days 21–50: Turn on paid lead flow plus the first event
**Goal: running total of 45 customers, 30 reviews**

| Days | Actions |
|---|---|
| 21 | Launch Google Local Services Ads (pay-per-lead, "Google Guaranteed" badge) and/or Google Search ads on "junk removal [city]" and similar terms. Start at $20–$30 a day. |
| 21–30 | Run a Facebook/Instagram ad with a before/after carousel, the offer headline and a "Text photos for a 15-min quote" button. Target homeowners within a 15-mile radius. |
| 25–35 | Plan the **first Neighborhood Clean-Out Day** (scheduled for about day 35): door hangers, an HOA newsletter and a Nextdoor post. |
| 30–50 | Partner follow-ups: send every realtor a "move-out ready" package (a flat-rate 1/2-truck offer for listings). Ask each partner for 1 intro. |
| Weekly | Review your numbers: cost per lead, quote-to-close rate and capacity. Kill ad sets that cost more than $40 per booked job. |

### Phase 3 — Days 51–80: Scale what works and stack events
**Goal: running total of 75 customers, 50+ reviews**

| Days | Actions |
|---|---|
| 51–60 | Run the **Downsizing workshop** with an estate-sale or organizer partner. Offer attendees a bundle (clean-out plus donation run plus a haul-away at a flat rate). |
| 55–70 | Clean-Out Day #2 and #3 in new neighborhoods. Retarget ad viewers with "Truck in your area Saturday." |
| 60–80 | Double the budget on the best lead source. Send the "seasonal clean-out" text to every past lead who didn't book. |
| 60–80 | Ask your top 5 partners for a standing arrangement (for example, every vacancy turn for a property manager). |

### Phase 4 — Days 81–100: Close out, systematize, set up the next 100
**Goal: 100 customers**

| Days | Actions |
|---|---|
| 81–90 | Run a "last call" campaign to all open leads and past customers: "Fall clean-out: 3 Saturday slots left." |
| 85–95 | Hold a customer-appreciation or charity month. Every job donates and gets posted (social proof). |
| 90–100 | Document your SOPs (quoting, routing, dump/donation runs, follow-up). Decide on a part-time helper or a second truck day. Rebuild the plan for the next 100 days with the channel data. |

## Weekly scorecard (track every Friday)

- Leads (by source)
- Quotes sent / jobs booked / close %
- Jobs done, revenue, avg ticket
- Reviews received (goal: 50%+ of jobs)
- Referrals received (goal: 1 in 5 jobs)
- Ad spend → cost per booked job
- Partner touches this week

## Scripts you can use today

**Speed-to-lead text:**
> "Hi [Name], it's [You] with [Business]. Thanks for reaching out! Text me 2–3 photos of what's going and I'll send a price in 15 minutes. I've got openings [day] and [day]."

**Review ask (same day):**
> "Thanks again, [Name]! If you were happy with the job, a quick Google review helps a one-man business more than you know: [link]. Know anyone moving or cleaning out? They get $25 off, and so do you."

**Partner pitch (realtor):**
> "I help listings go on the market faster. Flat-rate half-truck clean-outs, booked within 48 hours, with photos sent to you when it's done. Want me to be your go-to?"

## Biggest risks and fixes

- **Capacity crunch**: if you hit 10+ jobs a week, raise prices or require minimums rather than burn out.
- **Ad leads that are low quality**: require photos before quoting, and use a price-range anchor in ads.
- **Dump fees eating margin**: build disposal costs into your volume pricing, and donate or recycle to cut tipping fees.
- **Weather and seasonality**: front-load events in good-weather months and keep a backlog of leads.

**Bottom line:** the home-services lesson is irresistible offer + speed + reviews/referrals. Add the event lesson of creating a local moment that gathers demand all at once. As a one-person shop, that combination can realistically reach about 1 job a day by day 100.
