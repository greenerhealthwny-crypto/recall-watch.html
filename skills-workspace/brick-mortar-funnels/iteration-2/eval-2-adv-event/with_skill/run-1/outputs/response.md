# Event Funnel for a Vintage-Furniture Pop-Up

*Combining Jaime Cross's ticketed retail class and Dr. Chad Woolner's workshop funnel and "Epic Event", from* Brick & Mortar Funnels *(ClickFunnels, about 2020).*

Tags: **(book)** means the idea comes from the book. **(adapt)** means my translation of it to a vintage pop-up. **(assume)** means a placeholder number to replace with yours.

Then use proof and the list to sell out the rest of the pop-up and the next one (Woolner's marathon).

## 2. Numbers first: the "case value" for a pop-up

Woolner: know what one new customer is worth before deciding what an event is worth *(book)*. Hormozi's related rule is to know what you can spend per person who shows up *(book)*. Placeholders *(assume)*:

| Item | Placeholder | Yours |
|---|---|---|
| Average order value (a piece plus small add-ons) | $420 | |
| Gross margin after sourcing and restoration | 55%, so **about $230 profit** per order | |
| Expected repeat purchases in 12 months (next pop-ups, online drops) | 0.6 | |
| **"Case value" in profit per buyer (12 months)** | about $230 + (0.6 × $230) ≈ **$370** | |
| Opening Night: ticket holders who buy that night | 35% | |
| **Profit value per attendee** | 35% × $230 ≈ **$80**, plus ticket income | |

What this tells you:
- You can spend up to roughly **$40 to get one attendee** (half of their value, Hormozi's spend-per-show logic) *(book / adapt)*. Ticket income covers a large part of that.
- One-of-a-kind stock **limits revenue**. If 60 attendees each wanted the same sideboard, you'd make one sale. Plan stock: about 3–4 sellable pieces per expected buyer, plus small, low-cost add-ons (lamps, mirrors, prints, barware) so everyone can buy *something* *(adapt)*.

## 3. The front-end offer: a Cross-style ticketed "First Look"

### The ticket
**"First Look: Vintage Opening Night", $25 per ticket, fully credited toward any purchase that night** *(adapt)*.

- Cross charged **$47–$97** for her class *(book)*. For furniture, where the real money is in the piece, a lower ticket that **turns into store credit** keeps the commitment, filters out casual visitors, and removes the "I paid to shop?" objection. Test $25 against $45. You could also offer a "$45 with a vintage cocktail glass to take home" tier.
- **Scarcity is built in:** limited capacity (fire code plus comfort), and every piece is one of a kind. Ticket holders get **first pick before public doors open**.

### What happens inside (Cross's class structure, translated)

| Cross's class element *(book)* | Vintage pop-up version *(adapt)* |
|---|---|
| Factory tour and origin story | "Where this came from": the story of 3 hero pieces (the estate they came from, the maker, the restoration). Show before photos on a screen or easel |
| "Seven Pillars of True Beauty" (a teaching framework) | **"The 5 Tells of Real Vintage"**: joinery, maker's marks, wood and veneer, hardware, wear patterns. Spend 15 minutes on it. This is the education-first part Woolner also stresses |
| Detox and juicing for skin (a lifestyle tie-in) | "Styling one vintage piece into a modern room": a 10-minute live styling demo |
| Hand massage and sampling stations | **Hands-on stations:** a "Spot the Reproduction" challenge table, a free wax-and-polish demo (a staff member finishes a small item), and a fabric and upholstery swatch bar |
| "Director of Happiness" | One named host whose only job is guests: greets, answers delivery questions, holds pieces, and follows up after the event |

### The thank-you-page bonus (Cross's free 14-day challenge)
After buying a ticket, the buyer immediately gets **"The 14-Day Room Refresh Challenge"** *(adapt)*: one short email or text a day (where they opted in) with tasks such as "Measure your one awkward wall", "Pick your wood tone", and "Send us a photo of your space". On day 14 it ends with "Bring your measurements to Opening Night and we'll pull 3 pieces that fit."

This warms them up *and* gives you their room sizes, which raises the chance they buy that night.

### Bring-a-friend and refer-two (Cross's referral mechanics)
- **Bring a friend** (a second ticket): both get a **free small vintage item** at the door, such as a brass candlestick, a vintage print or a coupe glass. They cost you little and feel valuable *(adapt of Cross's free full-size serum)*.
- **Refer two friends who buy tickets:** get **$50 more store credit** or **free local delivery** *(adapt of Cross's free products)*.

### The URL
A memorable, offer-specific domain, as Cross did *(book)* and Frey recommends (city name included) *(book)*. For example **`[City]VintageFirstLook.com`**, pointing to a one-offer landing page: hero photo of the best piece, date, ticket price and credit, "5 Tells" teaser, the bonus challenge, a photo gallery, ticket counter ("38 of 60 left"), and **real testimonials only** from past buyers or pop-ups.

## 4. Filling the room: Woolner's workshop funnel, adapted

Woolner's sprint phase is about **3 small workshops a week**, giving real value, booked through relationships before outreach *(book)*. For you, that means short free talks at other people's venues that send people to Opening Night tickets.

### The mini-workshop: "Vintage 101: How to Buy Vintage Without Getting Burned" (30 minutes) *(adapt of "The 30-Minute Reboot")*
- **Where:** coffee shops, wine bars, breweries, coworking spaces, interior-design studios, realtor offices (for new homeowners), library or community-center events.
- **Content:** 3 of the "5 Tells", one real-or-fake piece passed around, and how to measure your space. **No pitch.** End with: "We're doing a First Look night on [date]. Anyone here gets $10 off the ticket with code VINTAGE101."
- **Collect:** name, email, phone and a consent checkbox on a sign-up sheet or QR code. This is the start of Woolner's "ongoing conversation".

### How to book workshops (Woolner's order) *(book → adapt)*
1. **Existing relationships first:** your past customers, Instagram followers, friends who run venues, and your email list.
2. **Lead boxes:** Woolner uses "win a free lunch" boxes in restaurants. Yours is a **"Win a restored mid-century side table"** box at partner cafés and shops. Entries go on your list. Use free entry and posted rules. Text only people who tick the consent box *(std)*.
3. **"Welcome guest" outreach:** each week, contact 10–15 businesses and offer the free talk to their customers or staff. **Expect about 90% to say no**, as Woolner says *(book)*. Two yeses a week is enough.

### Target math *(assume)*
- 6 weeks × 2–3 mini-workshops × about 12 people = about 180 people reached in person.
- At a 20% ticket conversion, that's about 36 tickets. Add the list, social and ads (section 6) to reach **60 tickets**.

## 5. Opening Night as Woolner's "Epic Event"

Woolner's Epic Event was a **free movie screening** with about 200–300 attendees and about 20 new patients *(book)*. Your version is a **ticketed First Look night**, with an optional **"Vintage Movie Night"** later in the pop-up's run to bring people back *(adapt)*.

### Invite sequence (Woolner: 3 email invites → RSVP → reminders) *(book → adapt)*

**Invite 1 (about 21 days out): the story.**
> Subject: The sideboard that came out of a 1962 Palm Springs estate
> We're opening a 10-day vintage pop-up at [venue] on [date], and the night before, 60 people get first pick. Ticket is $25, **and it's fully credited to anything you buy that night.** Plus, the moment you grab a ticket, you'll start our free 14-Day Room Refresh Challenge. → [City]VintageFirstLook.com

**Invite 2 (about 10 days out): the proof and scarcity.**
> Subject: 22 tickets left, and a sneak peek
> Here are 6 pieces you'll only see at First Look (photos). Bring a friend and you both get a free vintage keepsake at the door.

**Invite 3 (about 3 days out): last call.**
> Subject: Last call: First Look is Thursday
> Doors 6:30, "5 Tells of Real Vintage" at 7:00, raffle at 8:15. Your $25 comes back as credit. [Link]

**RSVP / ticket form:** name, email, mobile (with a consent checkbox), **number of guests** (Woolner's field), and "What room are you shopping for?" so the host can prepare a shortlist *(book / adapt)*.

**Reminders:** 2 days before (email), the morning of (text), and 1 hour before (text with parking info). Every reminder is from the host, with their name ("This is Maya, your host").

### Run of show *(adapt)*

| Time | Segment | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 6:30 | Doors. Welcome drink, name tag, bring-a-friend gifts. Ticket holders get **first-pick shopping**, and pieces can be put on a 30-minute hold | Cross (experience), A. Sewell's "serve food first" |
| 7:00 | 15-minute "5 Tells of Real Vintage" talk plus the stories behind 3 hero pieces | Cross (class and origin story), Woolner (value, not a pitch) |
| 7:20 | Stations: Spot the Reproduction, polish demo, swatch bar, styling demo | Cross (hands-on stations) |
| 7:45 | Open shopping. The host goes to people's held items and shortlists. Delivery booking table | |
| 8:15 | **Glue and Offer:** a raffle with prizes donated by **joint-venture partners** (an upholsterer's gift card, a florist arrangement, a coffee roaster's bag, an interior designer's 1-hour consult). People must be present to win | Woolner *(book)* |
| 8:20 | **Tonight-only offer:** "Buy tonight and get **free local delivery** plus your ticket credit, or reserve any piece with 25% down until Sunday." | Woolner's limited-time offer *(book → adapt)* |
| 8:45 | Final sales and delivery scheduling. Take before-and-after "in your home" photo requests | Woolner (proof) |

**Why the raffle matters:** Woolner uses it as "glue" to keep people in the room until the offer *(book)*. Partners also promote the night to their own audiences, because their prize gets mentioned. **Legal caution:** a raffle where tickets are bought can be regulated gambling in many places. Make entry free with attendance (your ticket is store credit, not a raffle ticket), post the rules, and check local law *(std)*.

## 7. After the event: Woolner's marathon (the portfolio of proof)

A pop-up's biggest risk is starting from nothing every time. Woolner's answer is a **portfolio of proof** and an ongoing communication system *(book)*:
- **Film at least one story a week:** a piece's restoration, a customer's room after delivery (with permission), or the backstory of a find. Post it, and add it to a **proof page** on the event URL *(book → adapt)*.
- **Position Ascension Loop** *(book)*: positioning ("the pop-up where every piece has a story and is checked for authenticity") → perception → expectation → anticipation (a waitlist for the next drop) → superior outcomes (the delivery experience, the Director of Happiness follow-up) → reputation → stronger positioning.
- **Follow-up for non-buyers:** in the 7 days after, text or email the shortlist the host made for them, with "still available" or "sold, but here's a similar one". McCall's rule: most business comes from follow-up *(book)*.
- **Reviews and referrals** (Shaule's NPS idea): "How likely are you to recommend us (1–10)?", then **ask everyone** for a Google or Instagram review. Don't gate requests by score *(book; std, verify)*.
- **Next pop-up:** past ticket holders get first access before the public. Repackage the offer every time (Shaule's 90-day repackaging) *(book)*.

## 10. Cautions

- **Raffles and giveaways:** free entry, posted rules, and platform promotion rules. Paid raffles may count as gambling *(std)*.
- **Texting and email:** get consent before marketing texts (TCPA) and include opt-outs (CAN-SPAM) *(std)*.
- **Pop-up operations:** temporary event or vendor permits, sales tax registration, venue insurance, fire-code capacity, and a liquor license or caterer if you serve alcohol *(std)*.
- **Honest descriptions:** describe age, maker and condition accurately. "Authentic mid-century" needs evidence. Use only real testimonials (FTC) *(std)*.
- **Results:** Cross's and Woolner's figures (for example, about 200–300 attendees and 20 new patients at Woolner's event) are the authors' own claims, not guarantees *(book)*. Ad costs have risen since the book *(std)*.
