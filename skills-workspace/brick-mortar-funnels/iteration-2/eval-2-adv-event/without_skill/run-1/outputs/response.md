# Retail Event and Workshop Funnel for a Small Vintage-Furniture Pop-Up

## A note on sources first

I don't have the exact text of Jaime Cross's or Chad Woolner's chapters in *Brick & Mortar Funnels*, so I won't quote them. Below I adapt the core mechanics their approaches are known for:

- **Retail event (Cross-style):** a ticketed or limited "experience" for VIPs that creates urgency, builds a list and drives a big sales day. You give people a reason to show up on one specific day, not "whenever."
- **Workshop funnel (Woolner-style):** a low-cost or free educational workshop is the front-end offer. It builds trust and authority, and attendees then get a special offer at the end.

Check the details (pricing, scripts, timelines) against the book if you have it.

---

## The big idea

Pop-ups already have built-in scarcity: you're only open on certain days. Vintage furniture also has built-in scarcity: every piece is one of a kind. The funnel's job is to turn those two facts into a crowd at the door on opening hour, and to keep those people as buyers for the next pop-up.

**Combined funnel:**
`Ad / social / partner promo → VIP Early Access list (lead) → paid workshop or VIP preview ticket (front end) → pop-up sale day (core offer) → post-event follow-up and "next drop" list (back end)`

## Part 1: The retail event, adapted — "VIP Preview Night"

### The offer
> **"VIP First-Look Night — Friday 6–8pm, the night before we open to the public. 40 tickets. $15, credited toward any purchase. Includes a drink, first pick of every piece, and 10% off all night."**

Why this works for vintage:
- **First pick is the real value.** Collectors know the best pieces go in the first hour. You're selling access, not a discount.
- **A ticket credited toward a purchase** filters for serious buyers and makes the ticket feel free, while still getting people to commit.
- **A hard cap (40)** is honest scarcity in a small space. It also keeps the room comfortable to shop.

### Building the event
| Element | Vintage-pop-up version |
|---|---|
| Theme | "Mid-Century Night," "Moody Maximalist," "Cabin Modern." Curate each drop around a story. |
| Sneak peeks | Post 1–2 hero pieces a day for 10 days before ("This 1962 teak credenza will be there Friday. VIPs see it first.") |
| Hold requests | No holds before the event. That keeps the urgency real. VIP ticket holders can *reserve* 1 piece that night with a deposit. |
| Experience | Local DJ or vinyl, a wine/coffee partner, a styling corner, a "story card" on each piece (maker, era, provenance) |
| Logistics | Delivery partner on site with flat-rate local delivery, plus a "sold" tag system and a "pay now, pick up Sunday" option |
| Public day | Saturday open to everyone. Announce "VIPs were here last night," which shows social proof *and* sets up FOMO for the next drop |

### Promotion timeline (3 weeks)
- **Week -3:** Announce the date. Open the free "Early Access List" (email and SMS). Partner with 2–3 local businesses (coffee shop, plant store, interior designer) for cross-promo.
- **Week -2:** Open VIP tickets to the list first (24 hours), then to the public. Run a small local Instagram/Facebook ad ($10–$20 a day, 10-mile radius, interests in vintage, mid-century and interior design) to the ticket page.
- **Week -1:** Post daily sneak peeks. Send a "12 tickets left" update. Send a reminder text the day before and the day of.
- **Event week:** Go live on Instagram Stories during setup. Post "just sold" pieces to drive Saturday traffic.

## Part 2: The workshop funnel, adapted — "Vintage Hunting and Styling Workshop"

### The workshop (front end)
> **"How to Spot, Score & Style Vintage Furniture — a 75-minute hands-on workshop. $25 (or free with VIP ticket). Learn to date a piece, spot quality joinery and veneer vs. solid wood, price-check like a dealer, and style one statement piece in a small space."**

Workshop ideas (rotate them):
1. **Spot & Score:** identifying makers, joinery, condition red flags and fair pricing
2. **Refresh, Don't Ruin:** basic cleaning, oiling teak, fixing wobbly legs (with a "never do this" list)
3. **Style One Piece:** how to build a room around a single vintage anchor
4. **Small-Space Vintage:** apartments and rentals

### Workshop structure (Woolner-style teach → offer)
1. **Welcome and quick win (10 min):** everyone learns one trick right away (for example, how to tell a dovetail from a dowel joint)
2. **Teach (45 min):** hands-on with real pieces from the inventory. Every example piece is for sale.
3. **Story/proof (5 min):** before and after rooms from past customers
4. **Offer (10 min):** "Workshop attendees only: 15% off today, plus first shot at the 'held for workshop' pieces, plus free local delivery this weekend."
5. **Q&A and shop (open-ended):** people stay and buy, because they now understand *why* the pieces are worth the price

Why this fits vintage: the main objection is "it's used/old, why does it cost that?" Teaching people to recognize quality removes that objection on its own. It also turns casual browsers into confident buyers and future repeat customers.

## Part 3: Putting them together — one pop-up cycle

| When | Step | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Ongoing | Instagram/TikTok sourcing stories and "the hunt" content | Build an audience |
| Week -3 | Free Early Access List (lead magnet: "Vintage Buying Cheat Sheet: 10 signs of quality" PDF) | Collect emails and SMS numbers |
| Week -2 | Sell VIP Preview tickets and workshop seats (bundle: $35 for both, or VIP includes the workshop) | Front-end revenue and commitment |
| Friday 5pm | Workshop (5–6pm) → VIP Preview Night (6–8pm) | Warmest buyers, highest-value sales |
| Saturday–Sunday | Public pop-up | Volume, new list sign-ups at checkout (QR code) |
| Week +1 | Thank-you email, "what sold" recap, unsold-piece flash sale to the list, review ask | Close leftovers, social proof |
| Ongoing | "Next Drop" list and monthly sneak peeks | Repeat buyers for the next pop-up |

### Back-end and repeat offers
- **"Hunt List" sourcing service:** customers tell you what they're looking for (for example, "a walnut dresser under $600"), pay a $20 deposit, and you text them first when you find one.
- **Membership/"Collector's Club":** $10–$15 a month or $99 a year for VIP night access every drop, 10% off and first look at new finds.
- **Partner revenue:** an interior designer referral fee, plus delivery and refinishing upsells.

## Numbers to plan around (example for one pop-up)

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Early Access List sign-ups | 300–500 |
| VIP tickets (40 × $15, credited to purchases) | $600 committed |
| Workshop seats (20 × $25) | $500 |
| VIP Night conversion | 60–70% buy something; avg $350 |
| Public weekend | 150–300 visitors; 15–25% buy |
| Ad spend | $200–$400 |

Track: list growth, ticket sell-through, revenue per attendee (VIP vs. workshop vs. public), sell-through % of inventory and list sign-ups at checkout.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- **Selling out the best pieces online beforehand.** That kills the reason to come to VIP night. Show sneak peeks, but don't sell those pieces early.
- **Making the workshop a sales pitch.** Teach for real. The offer works *because* the teaching was valuable.
- **No capture at the door.** Every visitor should get a reason to join the list (a raffle for a small piece or the cheat sheet).
- **Fake scarcity.** You don't need it. One-of-a-kind inventory and a limited room are real constraints, so say so plainly.
- **Forgetting logistics.** Big furniture needs a delivery or pickup plan on the spot, or sales stall.

**Bottom line:** use the retail-event idea to create a *moment* (a ticketed VIP first-look with real scarcity). Use the workshop funnel to build *trust and buying confidence* (teach people to recognize quality, then make attendees an offer). Together they turn a small pop-up into a repeatable launch cycle with a growing list of collectors who show up every drop.
