# A GTD System for Your Marketplace Flipping Side Hustle

You're fitting this around a day job and a family. So the system has one main job: get flipping out of your head, so it only gets your attention at the times you choose. Below is a complete setup you can build in any tool (Todoist, Things, Notion, Apple Reminders, or paper). Expect it to take about 60–90 minutes to set up.

---

## 1. Ground rules (read these first)

1. **The side hustle gets its own area, not its own system.** Use the same trusted system as the rest of your life, with a "Flipping" tag or area. If you run two systems, one of them will go stale.
2. **Protect the boundaries with the calendar, not with willpower.** Block your flipping time, for example Tue/Thu 8:30–10pm and Sat 7–10am. Everything outside those blocks belongs to the job and the family.
3. **Two-minute rule, adjusted for buyers.** If replying to a buyer takes under 2 minutes and you're in a flipping context, do it now. Otherwise capture it and batch it.
4. **Nothing about flipping lives only in your head:** prices you paid, pickup promises, buyer names, or "I should list that dresser."

---

## 2. Capture: your inboxes

Keep as few inboxes as possible and empty each one at least every 48 hours.

| Inbox | What lands there | How to process it |
|---|---|---|
| **Phone quick-capture** (task app inbox or voice memo) | Ideas, "check that garage sale," pricing thoughts | Clarify during daily processing |
| **Marketplace messages** (FB Marketplace, OfferUp, Craigslist, eBay) | Buyer and seller messages | Batch-reply 2–3 times a day; capture any commitment as a next action |
| **Photo camera roll / "Sourcing" album** | Photos of possible buys and items to list | Turn each into an inventory record + a "List: ___" action |
| **Physical inbox tray / bin** (garage or shelf) | Receipts, new items not yet processed | Processed in the Weekly Review |
| **Email label "Flipping"** | Platform notices, payouts, shipping labels | Clarify during daily processing |

**Tip:** Turn off push notifications for marketplace apps during work hours and family time. Check them at set times. A buyer who waits two hours is fine. A boss or kid who gets half your attention is not.

---

## 3. Clarify and organize: the lists

### Core lists

1. **Inbox:** unprocessed items.
2. **Projects (Flipping):** any outcome that takes more than one step. For flipping, most items and every sourcing trip are small projects.
3. **Next Actions:** sorted by context (see section 4).
4. **Waiting For:** things others owe you. Include the date and the person each time.
   - "Buyer Jason: confirm Sat pickup for oak table (msg'd 11/20)"
   - "eBay payout for Nintendo lot (expected 11/24)"
   - "Spouse: OK on using garage corner for storage"
5. **Calendar (hard landscape only):** set pickups, buyer meetups, estate sale openings, your blocked flipping sessions. Don't put wishful to-dos here.
6. **Someday/Maybe (Flipping):**
   - Start an eBay store / move to a higher seller tier
   - Learn furniture refinishing
   - Rent a small storage unit
   - Sell on Mercari or Poshmark too
   - Build an LLC + separate bank account (move this to Projects once revenue is steady)
7. **Agendas:** topics to raise with specific people.
   - **Spouse/partner:** weekend sourcing schedule, garage space, budget cap for buys
   - **Accountant/tax person:** side-income reporting, mileage deductions, sales tax
   - **Regular buyer or picker friend:** what they're looking for

### Reference (not actionable, but you need it)

- **Inventory tracker** (spreadsheet or Airtable): item, date bought, cost, where bought, listing date, platforms, asking price, sold price, fees, profit, days to sell.
- **Pricing research notes:** sold comps by category, brands that move fast, brands to avoid.
- **Sourcing map:** thrift stores, estate sale companies, curb-alert days, and the best day or time for each.
- **Templates:** listing description template, buyer reply snippets ("Yes, still available. Pickup is at ___, cash or Venmo"), and a safe-meetup policy.
- **Finance:** monthly profit and loss, mileage log, receipts folder (for taxes).

### Tickler / recurring items

- Weekly: relist or refresh listings older than 7 days
- Weekly: check local estate sale listings (Thursday night, for weekend sales)
- Monthly: drop prices on items older than 30 days by 10–15%
- Quarterly: set aside and pay estimated taxes, if needed

## 4. Contexts built for a job + family life

Contexts answer one question: **"What can I do right now, given where I am, what I have with me, and how much energy is left?"** Keep the list short.

| Context | Use it when | Example actions |
|---|---|---|
| **@Phone (5 min)** | Lunch break, waiting in line, kid's practice | Reply to buyer re: dresser; renew 3 OfferUp listings; check sold comps for KitchenAid mixer |
| **@Computer** | Evening session at a desk | Write eBay listings for the 4 Lego sets; update inventory spreadsheet; print shipping labels |
| **@Garage / Workspace** | Flipping block at home | Photograph 5 items; clean and test the Dyson; pack eBay orders |
| **@Errands / Out** | Already driving for family errands | Drop packages at USPS; check Goodwill on Main St; buy furniture wax |
| **@Sourcing trip** | Planned Saturday mornings | Hit 2 estate sales on the list; Facebook pickup of the free desk |
| **@Agenda: Spouse** | Weekly check-in | Discuss holiday sourcing budget |
| **Low energy (tag)** | Tired after the kids' bedtime | Sort photos; renew listings; log sales |

**Two key ideas:**
- **Bundle errands with family trips.** Your USPS drop-off and thrift check go on the same run as groceries.
- **Low-energy tasks matter.** Most of your flipping time comes when you're tired. Keep a stock of brain-dead tasks so 9:30pm isn't wasted.

## 5. Sample project with natural planning

**Project:** *Flip the $40 estate sale mid-century dresser for $250+ within 3 weeks.*

The Natural Planning Model has five steps: purpose, vision, brainstorm, organize, next actions.

### 1. Purpose and principles: why are we doing this?
- **Purpose:** Prove that furniture flips can make $150+ profit per piece. If they do, this becomes a repeatable category.
- **Principles (the rules for how):**
  - No more than 4 hours of hands-on work. If it needs more, sell it as-is.
  - Work only in scheduled blocks. No refinishing during family dinner.
  - Meet buyers safely: porch or driveway pickup with someone home, or a public spot.
  - Keep total extra spend under $25.

### 2. Outcome / vision: what does "done" look like?
> The dresser is sold and picked up within 21 days for $250 or more. Profit (after supplies) is logged in the inventory sheet. I have before/after photos and a note on what worked, so I can repeat it.

### 3. Brainstorm (everything, unfiltered)
- Clean the drawers, fix the sticky bottom drawer
- Restain or just oil the top?
- Replace or polish the hardware
- Check sold comps for "mid-century dresser" in my area
- Good daylight photos, staged with a plant and lamp
- List on FB Marketplace, OfferUp, Craigslist
- Delivery for an extra fee?
- Need a dolly / helper to move it
- Ask spouse about moving it into the garage
- Keywords: "MCM," "walnut," "Broyhill-style," "dovetail drawers"
- Price high, then drop on a schedule
- Log costs: $40 + supplies

### 4. Organize (sequence and group)
**Phase A: Assess (Week 1)**
1. Research sold comps → set target price and floor price
2. Inspect for damage → decide: as-is, light refresh, or full refinish

**Phase B: Prep (Week 1–2, two sessions max)**
3. Clean and fix the drawer
4. Refresh the top (oil, or light sand + stain)
5. Polish the hardware

**Phase C: Sell (Week 2–3)**
6. Photograph in daylight (Saturday morning)
7. Write and post the listing on 3 platforms
8. Respond to buyers, then schedule the pickup
9. Price drop on Day 7 if there are no serious offers

**Phase D: Close**
10. Collect payment, confirm pickup, mark it sold everywhere
11. Log profit, hours spent, and lessons learned

### 5. Next actions (only the very next step for each open path)
- **@Phone:** Search FB Marketplace + eBay *sold* listings for "mid century dresser," save 5 comps to the Pricing note *(10 min)*
- **@Agenda: Spouse:** Okay to keep the dresser in the left garage bay for 3 weeks?
- **@Errands:** Pick up Howard Feed-N-Wax + 0000 steel wool at the hardware store (on the Saturday grocery run)
- **Calendar:** Sat 8–10am, Dresser prep session #1
- **Waiting For:** (none yet)

Every other step stays in the project plan until it becomes the next action.

## 6. Daily rhythm (15 minutes total)

- **Morning (3 min, phone):** Glance at the calendar. Any pickups or meetups today? Anything to grab on the way?
- **Lunch (5 min, @Phone):** Batch-reply to buyers. Capture any promises into Calendar or Waiting For.
- **Evening flipping block (when scheduled):** Start with a 5-minute inbox-to-zero, then work from the @Garage or @Computer list.
- **Non-flipping evenings:** Don't open the list. That's the whole point of a trusted system.

---

## 7. Weekly Review checklist (Flipping edition)

**When:** Same time every week, 45–60 minutes. Sunday evening works well because it's after weekend sourcing and before the work week. Put it on your calendar as a recurring appointment.

### Get Clear
- [ ] Collect loose items: receipts from wallet and car, sticky notes, items dumped in the garage
- [ ] Empty the physical inbox tray / new-items bin
- [ ] Process the phone capture inbox to zero
- [ ] Clear the camera roll "Sourcing" album → inventory records + "List: ___" actions
- [ ] Process the email "Flipping" label to zero
- [ ] Mind sweep: "What's bugging me about the business?" Write down everything

### Get Current
- [ ] **Review the past week's calendar:** any pickups that fell through? Follow-ups owed?
- [ ] **Review the next 2 weeks' calendar:** estate sales, holidays, kid events that block flipping time. Book your flipping blocks.
- [ ] **Review Next Actions:** cross off done items; remove stale ones; make sure every action is concrete ("Photograph Dyson," not "Dyson")
- [ ] **Review Waiting For:** nudge any buyer or payout that's more than 2 days late
- [ ] **Review Projects:** every active project (each item, each sourcing trip) has at least one next action
- [ ] **Review Agendas:** anything to raise with your spouse this week?
- [ ] **Inventory health check:**
  - [ ] Every item on hand is listed (or has a "List: ___" action)
  - [ ] Items listed 7+ days → refresh or relist
  - [ ] Items listed 30+ days → price drop, bundle, or donate
- [ ] **Money check:** log sales and payouts; update the weekly profit total; log mileage

### Get Creative
- [ ] Review Someday/Maybe: promote anything to an active project? Delete anything dead?
- [ ] What category sold best this week? What should I source more of or less of?
- [ ] **Capacity check:** Did flipping take time from family or sleep this week? Adjust next week's blocks if so.
- [ ] Pick the **1–3 outcomes** that matter most for next week (e.g., "List all 6 garage items," "Sell the dresser")

---

## 8. Higher horizons (a quick check each quarter)

- **Goal (1 year):** e.g., $1,000/month average profit with no more than 8 hours a week
- **Areas of focus for the side hustle:** Sourcing · Listing & Sales · Inventory & Space · Finances & Taxes · Customer Safety · Family Boundaries
- **Purpose:** Why are you doing this? Debt payoff, a vacation fund, a test before going full-time? Write it down. It tells you which opportunities to say no to.

---

## Quick-start: your first 3 actions

1. **Today (10 min):** Create the "Flipping" area, the contexts above, and the Waiting For list in your task app.
2. **Tonight (20 min):** Walk the garage and your phone photos. Capture every item and open commitment into the inbox.
3. **This week:** Put the Weekly Review and two flipping blocks on your calendar as recurring events, and run your first review.
