# A GTD System for a Marketplace Flipping Side Hustle

*Built on David Allen's* Getting Things Done *(revised edition, 2015).*

You have three areas competing for the same 24 hours: a job, a family, and a new business. GTD will not create more time. It gets every commitment out of your head and into a trusted system, so that in any free 20 minutes you can see what you could do and choose with confidence. Allen calls that state **"mind like water"**: you respond to each thing in proportion, without over- or under-reacting.

One design decision comes first: **build one system, not a separate "hustle system."** If flipping has its own app or notebook that you check only on hustle nights, you will still be carrying job and family loops in your head, and your mind won't trust either system. Use one set of lists. Mark side-hustle items with a tag or prefix (for example `FLIP:`) so you can filter them, but review everything together.

---

## Step 0: Pick your tools (keep them few)

The revised edition is tool-agnostic. Any tool works if you trust it and review it. For a flipper on the move, these work well:

| Need | Suggested tool |
|---|---|
| Capture on the go (at a garage sale, a thrift store, in the car) | Phone: a quick-capture inbox in your list app, plus voice notes |
| Lists | One list manager (Todoist, Things, TickTick, Apple Reminders, or a paper notebook) |
| Calendar | The calendar you already share with your family |
| Reference and project support | One notes or cloud folder area, e.g. `Flip / Reference` and `Flip / Projects` |
| Physical in-tray | A box or tray at home for receipts, item tags, parts, and cash envelopes |

Keep the number of **collection points** small: phone inbox, physical in-tray, and email. Every extra inbox is one more place you have to empty.

---

## Step 1: Capture (the initial mind sweep)

Set aside 1 to 2 hours. Write down **everything** that has your attention about the hustle, one item per line, with no judging or organizing yet. Then do the same quickly for job and family, because those loops are what will pull your attention away from your flipping time.

Trigger list for the side hustle:

- **Setup:** Marketplace profile and photo, payment methods, ratings, a separate bank account or card, bookkeeping, a business name, local rules on resale or sales tax, the tax treatment of side income
- **Sourcing:** garage sales, estate sales, thrift stores, curb alerts, Facebook "free" listings, auctions, liquidation pallets, a list of target categories (furniture, tools, electronics, kids' gear)
- **Inventory:** items you already own to sell first, storage space (garage corner, shelving), item tracking (a spreadsheet of cost, listed price, and sold price)
- **Listing:** a photo setup and lighting, a description template, a pricing method (searching sold comps), cleaning and repair supplies
- **Selling:** message response times, safe meetup spots, pickup windows, dealing with no-shows, delivery options
- **Money:** startup budget, the reinvestment rule, profit targets, receipts
- **Family and time:** agreed hustle hours with your partner, use of the garage and car, a kid-safe storage zone
- **Ideas:** a refurbishing niche, eBay cross-listing, a small van someday

Everything you write down goes into your inbox. You'll clarify it next.

---

## Step 2: Clarify (process inbox to empty)

Take each item **one at a time, from the top**. Don't skip ahead, and don't put anything back into "in." Ask:

1. **What is it?**
2. **Is it actionable?**
   - **No:** trash it, **incubate** it (Someday/Maybe or the tickler), or file it as **reference**.
   - **Yes:** What's the desired outcome? If it needs more than one action and can be finished within a year, it's a **project**. Then: **what's the very next physical, visible action?**
3. Then decide what to do with the action:
   - **Under 2 minutes? Do it now.** ("If an action will take less than two minutes, it should be done at the moment it's defined.") Replying "Yes, still available, can you do 6pm?" to a buyer is a classic two-minute action.
   - **Someone else can do it? Delegate it** and track it on **Waiting For**.
   - **Otherwise, defer it.** Put it on the **calendar** if it must happen on a specific day or at a specific time. Otherwise it goes on a **Next Actions** list by context.

Worked examples from a flipper's inbox:

| Stuff | Decision | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| "Dresser for $20 on curb alert, gone by tonight" | Actionable, date-specific | Calendar today, or do it now |
| "Learn about refinishing furniture" | Not now | Someday/Maybe |
| "Sold-comps notes for KitchenAid mixers" | Not actionable, useful | Reference: `Flip / Pricing comps` |
| "Set up separate bank account" | Multi-step outcome | Project, next action: "@computer: compare 3 no-fee business checking accounts" |
| "Buyer says she'll send pickup time" | Someone else's move | Waiting For: "Sara: pickup time for bike (asked Tue)" |
| "Estate sale Saturday 8am, Elm St" | Fixed time and place | Calendar |
| Receipt from a thrift store | Reference | Receipts folder, plus enter it in the tracking spreadsheet at the next @computer session |

## Step 3: Organize (the seven lists, with hard edges)

GTD uses **seven primary categories**. Keep firm boundaries between them: a Next Action never lives on the calendar "just in case," and reference never sits in the action lists.

### 1. Projects list
Every outcome that needs more than one step and can be done within a year. Write each one as a finished result.
- FLIP: Business setup complete (bank account, tracking sheet, tax notes)
- FLIP: First 10 items sold
- FLIP: Garage storage zone set up
- FLIP: Photo station built
- FLIP: Pricing reference sheet for top 5 categories
- FAMILY: Hustle schedule agreed with my partner
- (plus your job and family projects)

### 2. Project support material
Notes, plans, and files for each project, kept **outside** the action lists. Example: `Flip / Projects / First 10 items sold /` holds the item list, photos, and comps.

### 3. Calendar (the "hard landscape")
Only three kinds of things go here: **time-specific actions** (pickup with a buyer at 6:00pm), **day-specific actions** (call the auction house Tuesday), and **day-specific information** (city-wide garage sale weekend; trash day for big items). Block your agreed hustle windows here too, so your family can see them.

### 4. Next Actions lists (by context)
See the context list below. Each entry is the next physical step, starting with a verb.

### 5. Waiting For list
What you're waiting on, from whom, and since when.
- Buyer "Mike R.": confirm pickup for desk (messaged Mon)
- Partner: OK to use garage left bay (asked Sun)
- Bank: debit card in the mail (ordered 11/14)
- Payment: Venmo from buyer for lamp, $25

### 6. Reference material
Not actionable, but you want it. Sold-comp notes by category, listing and message templates, safe-meetup locations, local sales tax notes, receipts, and the inventory spreadsheet. Use a simple A–Z filing system you can file into in under a minute.

### 7. Someday/Maybe
Things you might want to do, but not now. List them so they stop nagging you.
- Cross-list on eBay or OfferUp
- Rent a storage unit
- Specialize in mid-century furniture
- Buy a utility trailer
- Make a "flip of the week" social account

### Tickler file
If you have paper (receipts, return-by slips, auction catalogs), use the **43 folders**: 31 daily plus 12 monthly. Drop an item into the day you want to see it again. Digitally, use deferred start dates or calendar reminders. Examples: "Re-list the sofa at a lower price" set for 7 days after listing, and "Quarterly estimated taxes" in the month it's due.

## Step 3b: Contexts that fit a job + family + hustle life

The four-criteria model says you choose actions first by **context**: where you are and what tools you have. Build contexts around the places and tools that limit what you can do:

| Context | What's on it | Example actions |
|---|---|---|
| **@phone (anywhere)** | Things you can do from your phone in a spare few minutes: lunch break, waiting rooms | Reply to buyer messages; post a quick listing from saved photos; check sold comps for a mixer |
| **@computer** | Things that need a keyboard | Update the inventory spreadsheet; write a description template; research business checking accounts |
| **@garage / workshop** | Physical work on items | Clean and test the lamp; tighten the dresser hardware; photograph 3 items at the photo station |
| **@errands / sourcing** | Out of the house with the car | Hit the Goodwill on Route 9; buy Magic Erasers and furniture polish; drop off the donate pile |
| **@pickup-meetup** | Handoffs with buyers (usually on the calendar instead) | Bring the bike to the police station lot |
| **@partner (agenda)** | Things to bring up with your spouse or partner | Agree on a weekend sourcing slot; talk about the budget cap |
| **@home** | Household (non-hustle) | Fix the kitchen drawer |
| **@work / @office** | Day-job context | (keep this separate so you're not flipping on company time) |

Two practical tips:
- **Low-energy list.** The four criteria are context, **time available**, **energy available**, then priority. After the kids are in bed you may have 30 minutes and very little energy. Tag simple tasks (`@phone`, quick replies, re-listing) so a tired evening is still useful.
- **Agendas** for people you talk to often (partner, a flipping friend, your accountant). Put discussion points there instead of in your head.

## Step 4: A sample project with the Natural Planning Model

**Project: "FLIP: First 10 items sold, with profit tracked"**

### 1. Purpose and principles (why?)
- **Purpose:** Prove the hustle can make money before spending real money on inventory. Build the habits and the system.
- **Principles (the rules I won't break):**
  - The family comes first: no sourcing during family dinners or kids' events.
  - Start with stuff I already own, then spend no more than $200 of my own money.
  - Meet only at safe public spots; no buyers in the house.
  - Track every dollar from day one.
  - Keep hustle work off the job's clock and equipment.

### 2. Outcome visioning (what does success look like?)
In 6 weeks: 10 items sold, every sale logged with cost, price, profit, and time spent. A repeatable listing template and a photo setup. A clear number for "profit per hour," so I know whether to scale up. My partner feels the hustle fits our life rather than taking it over.

### 3. Brainstorming (how? all ideas out, no judging)
Sell the unused treadmill; the kids' outgrown bike; the old TV stand; check the closets for things to sell · build a photo backdrop with a white sheet · learn to search sold comps · weekend garage sale runs · Facebook free section · ask friends if they're decluttering · make a listing template · put the price in the first line · bundle the small items · offer delivery for $15 · set a weekly re-list day · track time with a phone timer · cash box for change · ask a flipping subreddit for advice · consider an eBay crossover...

### 4. Organizing (components, order, priorities)
1. **Foundation:** tracking spreadsheet; separate account or card; listing template; photo station
2. **Phase A (weeks 1–2):** List 5 items I already own (zero cost, low risk, quick learning)
3. **Phase B (weeks 2–5):** Source 5 to 8 items within the $200 cap, focused on 1 or 2 categories
4. **Phase C (week 6):** Review the numbers and decide to scale, adjust, or pause

Dependencies: the photo station and template come before listing. Comps research comes before buying.

### 5. Next actions (the very next physical step, and who owns it)
- **@home:** Walk through the garage and closets with a notepad, and list everything sellable *(this is the project's single next action on the active list)*
- **@computer:** Create the inventory sheet with columns for item, cost, list price, sold price, fees, hours, and profit
- **@partner:** Agree on two weekly hustle windows (e.g., Tue 8–9:30pm and Sat 7–10am)
- **Waiting For:** nothing yet

Only the next actions go on your context lists. The rest of the plan lives in project support material until the next step is due.

## Step 5: Reflect — Weekly Review checklist

Pick a fixed time each week, for about 1 to 2 hours. **Sunday evening** suits many people with a day job, because it sets up both the work week and the next weekend's sourcing. Put it on the shared family calendar as a recurring appointment with yourself.

### GET CLEAR
- [ ] **Collect loose papers and materials.** Receipts in the car, item tags, sticky notes, cash from sales, garage sale flyers. Everything goes into the in-tray.
- [ ] **Get "in" to zero.** Process the physical in-tray, phone capture inbox, Marketplace messages, voice notes, email, and photos taken while sourcing. Use the clarify decision tree on each.
- [ ] **Empty your head.** Do a quick mind sweep of all three areas: hustle, job, family. What's nagging you?

### GET CURRENT
- [ ] **Review Next Actions lists.** Mark off what's done. Is each item still a clear physical next step?
- [ ] **Review previous calendar (last 1–2 weeks).** Any follow-ups from pickups, sales, family events, or work meetings?
- [ ] **Review upcoming calendar (next 2–4 weeks).** Garage sale weekends, kids' events, work travel. Block hustle windows around them.
- [ ] **Review Waiting For.** Unpaid buyers, pending pickups, partner decisions, mail-order items. Nudge whatever is stale.
- [ ] **Review the Projects list (and larger outcomes).** Make sure *every* project has at least one next action. Check the project support folders.
- [ ] **Review relevant checklists.** The hustle numbers check:
  - [ ] Log this week's sales and costs in the spreadsheet
  - [ ] Count active listings and stale ones (14+ days → drop the price or re-list)
  - [ ] Inventory on hand vs. storage space
  - [ ] Hours spent vs. the agreed budget
  - [ ] Profit this week and to date; profit per hour

### GET CREATIVE
- [ ] **Review Someday/Maybe.** Is anything ready to become active (eBay cross-listing, a niche)? Is anything dead? Delete it.
- [ ] **Be creative and courageous.** Any new ideas, a better sourcing route, a bolder price test? Capture them.
- [ ] *(Optional, monthly)* **Look up a horizon.** Is the hustle still serving your goals and your family's priorities? (See below.)

## Step 6: Engage — choosing what to do in the moment

Use the **four-criteria model** in this order:
1. **Context:** Where am I and what do I have? (At work at lunch → only `@phone`)
2. **Time available:** 10 minutes before school pickup → reply to messages, don't start a refinish
3. **Energy available:** Tired at 9pm → re-list items, update the spreadsheet; save sourcing and repairs for fresh hours
4. **Priority:** Of what's left, what matters most right now?

And the **threefold model**: each day you are doing predefined work (your lists), doing work as it shows up (a buyer messages, a kid gets sick), or defining your work (processing your inbox). Expect interruptions to win sometimes. Because everything is captured and you review weekly, you can drop the hustle for a night without anything getting lost.

## Keeping the hustle in balance (horizons of focus)

GTD is a workflow method, not a priority formula. To keep the hustle from swallowing your family time, look at it from higher horizons now and then:

- **Horizon 5 (Purpose and principles):** Why am I doing this? (e.g., financial breathing room for the family)
- **Horizon 4 (Vision):** What does life look like if this works?
- **Horizon 3 (Goals):** e.g., "$X/month in profit within a year, in 8 hours a week or less"
- **Horizon 2 (Areas of focus):** Job · Family/partner · Kids · Health · Home · Finances · *Flipping business*. Flipping is **one** area among several, not the center.
- **Horizon 1 (Projects):** Your Projects list
- **Ground (Actions):** Your Next Actions lists and calendar

If a weekly review shows the hustle crowding out the family or health areas, that's a signal to adjust. The system makes the tradeoff visible.

## Quick start: your first 7 days

1. **Day 1:** Set up the tools and create the seven lists and contexts above.
2. **Day 2:** Full mind sweep of the hustle, job, and family (1–2 hrs).
3. **Day 3:** Clarify the inbox to zero, doing every two-minute action.
4. **Day 4:** Run the natural planning model on "First 10 items sold."
5. **Day 5:** Agree on hustle windows with your partner and put them on the shared calendar.
6. **Days 6–7:** Do next actions by context; capture everything new.
7. **End of week:** Your first Weekly Review using the checklist.
