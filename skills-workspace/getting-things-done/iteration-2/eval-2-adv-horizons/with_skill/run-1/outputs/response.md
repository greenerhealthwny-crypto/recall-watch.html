# Mapping "Quit My Job in 2 Years and Run My Own Business" onto GTD's Horizons of Focus

*Based on David Allen's* Getting Things Done *(revised edition, 2015): the six-level model for reviewing your work.*

## First, a correction: this is not one project

The natural reflex is to put "Quit my job and start my business" on your Projects list. In GTD terms that's a category error. Allen defines a project as **"any desired result that can be accomplished within a year that requires more than one action step."** A two-year transition doesn't fit that definition. It is a **Horizon 3 goal**, possibly serving a **Horizon 4 vision**, and it will produce *many* projects over time.

If you treat it as one huge project, you get a list item with no clear next action that sits there making you anxious. If you place it at the right horizon, it gives direction to a steady flow of small, doable projects and actions.

## The six horizons (from the top down)

| Horizon | Altitude | Typical time frame* | Your question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | Purpose and principles | Lifetime | Why am I doing this at all? |
| **4** | Vision | 3–5 years | What does success look like? |
| **3** | Goals | 1–2 years | What has to be true by when? |
| **2** | Areas of focus and accountabilities | Ongoing | What must I keep up in every part of life? |
| **1** | Current projects | Within a year | What outcomes am I committed to now? |
| **Ground** | Current actions | Today/this week | What's the next physical step? |

\*The time frames are the usual GTD guidelines, not hard rules.

**Work top-down for direction, bottom-up for control.** Allen's advice is practical: if your inbox is overflowing and your head is full, the higher horizons won't feel real. Get Ground and Horizon 1 under control first (capture, clarify, the lists), then think about the higher levels. Below I go from the top down, because that's how you set the direction. The sequence that follows shows how to actually start.

---

## Horizon 5 — Purpose and principles

*Why does this matter to you?* Everything below should trace back to this.

Fill in your own. Prompts and examples:
- **Purpose:** "To build work that is mine, that uses my skills fully, and that gives my family security and freedom over our time."
- **Principles (the standards you won't trade away):**
  - "I don't bet the family's financial safety on the business."
  - "I keep doing excellent work for my current employer until the day I leave, and I leave on good terms."
  - "Health and relationships are not what I sacrifice to make this happen."
  - "I build things customers actually pay for, not things I just enjoy building."

**Why this matters:** In year 2, when you're tired and the business is slow, your principles decide what you do. They tell you whether to quit early, push harder, or adjust the plan.

---

## Horizon 4 — Vision (3–5 years)

*What does your life look like when this has worked?* Describe it in the present tense, with real detail.

Example:
> It's 2030. I've run my business full-time for about two years. It pays me at least what my old salary did, with 6+ months of operating reserve. I have [N] steady clients/customers, and I choose who I work with. I work about 45 hours most weeks, and I'm home for dinner. The work is [what you actually do day to day].

Note what the vision covers: income, time, the type of work, and lifestyle. Quitting your job is not the vision. It's a **milestone** on the way to it. Keep your eye on the life you're building, not just the exit.

---

## Horizon 3 — Goals (1–2 years)

This is where your "quit in 2 years" belongs. Make it concrete and testable, and add the conditions that make quitting a good decision rather than a jump into the unknown. Write them as specific outcomes with dates.

**The central goal:**
> **By [month, year + 2], I resign from my job and work full-time on my business.**

**Supporting goals (the "readiness criteria"):**
1. **Revenue proof:** The business earns at least $[X]/month on the side for [3–6] consecutive months before I resign.
2. **Financial runway:** [6–12] months of personal living expenses saved, plus a business reserve.
3. **Customer base:** [N] paying customers or a dependable pipeline, plus repeatable ways to find new ones.
4. **Foundations:** The business is legally set up, with bookkeeping, contracts/terms, and insurance in place. Health insurance is figured out for after I leave.
5. **Skills:** I can sell, price, and deliver without being stuck.
6. **Family alignment:** My partner/family and I agree on the plan, the risks, and the go/no-go checkpoints.

**Add go/no-go checkpoints** (for example, at 12 and 18 months). At each one, ask: "Am I on track? Do I proceed, adjust the date, or pivot the business idea?" These belong on your calendar now.

A year-1 slice of the goals makes it easier to define projects:
- **By month 12:** First paying customers, $[Y]/month in side revenue, half the runway saved.
- **By month 24:** All readiness criteria met → resign.

## Horizon 2 — Areas of focus and accountabilities

These are the ongoing roles and standards that don't end. No area is ever "finished," but each one needs attention. Allen's point: the projects you create should keep *all* your areas at an acceptable standard, not just one.

**Areas that the transition adds or changes:**
- **Business: Product/Offer** — what you sell and how good it is
- **Business: Marketing and Sales** — pipeline, outreach, audience
- **Business: Delivery and Customers** — doing the work and keeping clients happy
- **Business: Finance and Admin** — bookkeeping, taxes, legal, tools
- **Personal Finances** — runway, budget, debt, health insurance plan
- **Skills and Learning** — sales, your trade, running a business

**Areas you must keep up** (the ones that fall apart if you ignore them):
- **Current job** — performance, reputation, references. You're still accountable here for two years. Also check your employment contract for non-compete, IP, and moonlighting clauses.
- **Family/partner** — time together and shared decisions
- **Health and energy** — sleep, exercise. This is the engine for doing two things at once.
- **Home**
- **Friends and community/network** — which also becomes your referral base

Use this list as a **checklist** during your Weekly Review: "Is there an area I'm neglecting? Does any area need a new project?"

## Horizon 1 — Current projects (within a year)

Now break the Horizon 3 goals into projects you can finish in the next 12 months. Each one is a specific result that needs more than one action. Only put the ones you're committed to *now* on your active Projects list. Put the rest on **Someday/Maybe** and move them up in later reviews.

**Active now (sample):**
| Project (written as a finished outcome) | Serves goal / area |
|---|---|
| Business idea validated with 10 customer interviews | Revenue proof / Product |
| First paid offer launched with a price and a sales page | Revenue proof / Marketing |
| Monthly budget and runway target set; automatic savings running | Runway / Personal finances |
| Employment contract reviewed for non-compete, IP, and moonlighting rules | Foundations / Current job |
| Two-year plan agreed with my partner, including checkpoints | Family alignment / Family |
| Weekly schedule set with protected business hours (e.g., 2 evenings + Saturday morning) | All goals / Health, Family |

**Someday/Maybe (for later quarters):**
- Form an LLC and open a business bank account
- Get the website and portfolio built
- Set up bookkeeping software
- Plan health insurance for after resigning
- Find a mentor or mastermind group
- Plan the resignation and transition handover (month ~22)

For a big or unclear project, run the **natural planning model**: (1) purpose and principles, (2) outcome visioning, (3) brainstorming, (4) organizing, (5) next actions.

## Ground — Current actions

Every active project needs **at least one next action**: the very next physical, visible step, on a context list. This is where the two-year vision becomes something you can do on a Tuesday night.

| Project | Next action | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Idea validated with 10 interviews | Write a list of 15 people who fit the target customer profile | @computer |
| First paid offer launched | Draft 3 possible offer names and prices in my notebook | @anywhere |
| Budget and runway set | Download the last 3 months of bank statements | @computer |
| Contract reviewed | Find my employment agreement in email and read the IP section | @computer |
| Plan agreed with partner | Ask my partner for a 1-hour Sunday planning slot | @partner agenda |
| Weekly schedule | Block Tue/Thu 8–10pm and Sat 7–10am on the calendar as "Business" | @calendar |

Also:
- **Calendar:** the 12- and 18-month go/no-go checkpoints; protected business blocks
- **Waiting For:** responses from people you've asked for interviews, your partner's input
- **Two-minute rule:** "If an action will take less than two minutes, it should be done at the moment it's defined." For example, texting a friend to ask for one interview contact.

## Keeping the horizons alive: review rhythm

| Horizon | How often to review | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Ground & Horizon 1 | **Weekly** | Weekly Review: get clear, get current, get creative |
| Horizon 2 | Monthly, or weekly at a glance | Areas checklist in the Weekly Review |
| Horizon 3 | Quarterly | Goals review, and the go/no-go checkpoints |
| Horizons 4–5 | Yearly, or when making a big decision | Personal retreat or planning day |

**The Weekly Review** is what holds it all together:
- **Get clear:** collect loose papers, process the inbox to zero, empty your head
- **Get current:** review action lists, the previous and upcoming calendar, Waiting For, project (and larger outcome) lists, and relevant checklists (including your Horizon 2 areas)
- **Get creative:** review Someday/Maybe and be creative and courageous. This is where "launch a podcast" or "LLC" moves from Someday to active when the time is right.

## Choosing what to do each day

The horizons give **direction**. In the moment, use the **four-criteria model**, in order: **context** (where am I and what tools do I have?), **time available**, **energy available**, then **priority**. Priority is where the horizons come in: when you have a free evening and enough energy, a next action on "First paid offer launched" usually beats reorganizing your desk, because it moves your Horizon 3 goal.

## How to start this week (bottom-up first)

1. **Mind sweep:** write down everything on your mind about the job, the business idea, money, and family.
2. **Clarify and organize** it into Projects, Next Actions, Waiting For, Someday/Maybe, calendar, and reference.
3. **Write a first draft of Horizons 5, 4, and 3** (one page total, messy is fine).
4. **List your Horizon 2 areas.**
5. **Choose 3 to 6 active projects** from the goals. Give each one a next action.
6. **Put the go/no-go checkpoints and your first Weekly Review on the calendar.**

You don't need the perfect plan for the next two years. You need clear goals at Horizon 3, a handful of real projects at Horizon 1, and a next action for each, reviewed every week. Over 104 Weekly Reviews, that's how you get to your resignation day.
