# Your Life Mapped onto GTD's Horizons of Focus
**The goal: quit your job in 2 years and run your own business**

David Allen's Horizons of Focus look at your commitments from six heights, from the runway (today's actions) up to 50,000 feet (purpose). A big life change like this usually fails in the middle. People have a dream at the top and a to-do list at the bottom, with nothing linking them. The map below builds that link.

I don't know your business idea, finances, or family situation yet, so I've filled in a realistic structure with placeholders in **[brackets]**. Replace them with your own details. Use the questions at the end to fill the gaps.

---

## Horizon 5 (50,000 ft): Purpose and Principles
*Why do you exist? What are your non-negotiables?*

This is the anchor. When the business gets hard in Year 2, this is what keeps you going, and what tells you when *not* to quit.

**Draft purpose statement (edit this):**
> "I want to build work that I own, that uses my strengths in **[skill]**, gives me control over my time, and supports my family without depending on one employer."

**Principles (how you'll pursue it):**
- I won't risk the family's basic security. Quitting depends on numbers, not feelings.
- I'll test with real customers before I bet on an idea.
- Health and key relationships are not the price of the business.
- I'll leave my current job on good terms (no burned bridges, no breach of contract or non-compete).

**Action:** Write this in your own words in one sitting (30 min). Read it at every quarterly review.

---

## Horizon 4 (40,000 ft): Vision (3–5 years)
*What does success look like if things go well?*

Write it in the present tense, as if it's already true, about 3–5 years out:

> "It's **[year + 4]**. I've run **[business]** full-time for two years. It brings in **[$X]** per year, pays me **[$Y]**, and has **[N]** steady clients/customers. I work about **[hours]** a week, mostly on **[the work I enjoy]**. We have **[6–12]** months of expenses saved. I pick up my kids from school on **[days]**."

Include:
- **Business:** revenue, what you sell, who buys, what your role is
- **Lifestyle:** hours, location, flexibility
- **Financial:** income floor, savings, benefits sorted (health insurance especially)
- **Personal:** who you are as a result (skills, confidence, reputation)

---

## Horizon 3 (30,000 ft): 1–2 Year Goals
*The specific objectives that make the vision real.*

This is where your 2-year deadline lives. Turn "quit in 2 years" into a **quit trigger**, a set of conditions you can measure. The date becomes the target, and the conditions decide it.

### The Quit Trigger (all must be true)
1. Side business revenue ≥ **[60–100%]** of your current take-home pay for **3 months in a row**
2. Emergency fund = **[6–12]** months of family expenses, kept separate from business capital
3. Health insurance plan picked and priced (spouse's plan, marketplace plan, etc.)
4. A repeatable way to get customers (you know where the next 10 come from)
5. Spouse/partner agrees and is on board with the plan

### Milestone goals by stage

| Timeframe | Goal |
|---|---|
| **Months 0–3** | Idea validated: 10+ conversations with target customers; first paying customer |
| **Months 4–6** | Offer defined and priced; **[$500–1,000]**/month revenue; business legally set up |
| **Months 7–12** | **[$2–3K]**/month; first repeatable marketing channel; emergency fund at 50% |
| **Months 13–18** | **[$X]**/month (≈50% of salary); systems documented; emergency fund at 100% |
| **Months 19–24** | Quit trigger met → give notice; transition plan done |

**Fallback goal:** If the trigger isn't met by month 24, decide on purpose. Extend 6 months, cut to part-time at your job, or pivot the idea. Don't just drift.

## Horizon 2 (20,000 ft): Areas of Focus and Responsibility
*The roles and standards you must keep up. They never get "done."*

Your life will have **two sets of areas at once** for the next 2 years. That's the main source of overload, so list them clearly.

### Day job (keep it up; it's funding the plan)
- **Job performance:** meet expectations, stay employable, keep your reputation and references
- **Professional network:** relationships that could become clients, partners, or a safety net
- **Legal/ethical boundaries:** no company time or equipment for the side business; review any non-compete or IP clause

### Business-in-progress
- **Product/Offer:** what you sell and how good it is
- **Customers & Sales:** pipeline, conversations, closing
- **Marketing & Audience:** visibility, content, referrals
- **Finance:** business bookkeeping, pricing, taxes, separate accounts
- **Operations & Legal:** entity, contracts, tools, insurance
- **Skills & Learning:** the gaps you need to close (sales, accounting, your craft)

### Life (don't let these go)
- **Family / Partner:** time, communication, shared decisions
- **Health:** sleep, exercise, stress (this is a long effort; burnout ends more businesses than competition does)
- **Personal finances:** budget, savings rate, emergency fund, debt
- **Home & Admin**
- **Friends / Community / Rest**

**Weekly check:** For each area, ask: "Is anything here below standard? Do I need a project to fix it?"

## Horizon 1 (10,000 ft): Current Projects
*Outcomes you can finish within about 12 months that need more than one step.*

Keep **3–5 active business projects at a time**. Put the rest on Someday/Maybe. Here's a realistic first slate:

**Business**
1. **Validate the business idea:** 10 customer interviews done and summarized
2. **Land the first paying customer**
3. **Build a simple one-page website / landing page**
4. **Set up the business legally:** LLC or sole proprietorship, EIN, business bank account
5. **Create a 24-month financial runway plan:** a spreadsheet of salary, expenses, savings target, and break-even

**Life (supporting the transition)**
6. **Build the emergency fund to [X] months:** automated monthly transfer set up
7. **Hold the "2-year plan" conversation with spouse/partner** and agree on guardrails (budget, hours, decision checkpoints)
8. **Research health insurance options after leaving the job**
9. **Review the employment contract** for non-compete, IP, and moonlighting clauses

**Day job**
10. **[Any current work project]:** keep a strong track record while you build

**Someday/Maybe (not now):** hire a VA, write a book, second product line, office space, podcast.

## Ground (Runway): Next Actions and Calendar
*The visible, physical next steps.*

Each active project gets **one concrete next action**, sorted by context:

**@Computer**
- Draft a list of 20 people who match my target customer *(Validate idea)*
- Download last 3 months of bank statements to calculate monthly family spending *(Runway plan)*
- Search the state business registry to see if **[business name]** is available *(Legal setup)*

**@Phone**
- Text **[name]** to ask for a 20-min chat about how they handle **[problem]** *(Validate idea)*
- Call HR or check the employee handbook for the moonlighting policy *(Contract review)*

**@Home**
- Find and read the employment agreement; highlight non-compete and IP clauses *(Contract review)*

**@Agenda: Spouse**
- Propose a time this weekend for the 2-year-plan conversation

**@Bank / Online**
- Set up an automatic transfer of **[$X]** per paycheck to a high-yield savings account *(Emergency fund)*

**Calendar (hard landscape)**
- Recurring business blocks: e.g., **Tue/Thu 5:30–7am, Sat 8–12** (protect these like meetings)
- Weekly Review: **Sunday 7pm**, 60 min
- Quarterly review: first weekend of **Jan / Apr / Jul / Oct**
- **Month 12 checkpoint:** "Am I on track for the quit trigger?"
- **Month 18 checkpoint:** "Do I set a notice date?"

---

## How the horizons connect (the "why" test)

Every next action should trace up to your purpose:

> **Text Sarah to ask for a 20-min chat** (Runway)
> → *Validate the business idea* (Project)
> → *Customers & Sales* (Area)
> → *$2–3K/month by month 12* (Goal)
> → *Full-time business supporting the family* (Vision)
> → *Owning my work and time without risking family security* (Purpose)

If an action doesn't trace up, ask whether it belongs at all. If a goal has no projects under it, it's only a wish.

## Review rhythm (keeps the map alive)

| Horizon | Review how often | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Runway / Next Actions | Daily | What's the next thing I can do right now? |
| Projects | Weekly (Weekly Review) | Does every project have a next action? Am I working on the right 3–5? |
| Areas of Focus | Weekly–monthly | Is any area slipping, especially health and family? |
| 1–2 Year Goals | Monthly + quarterly | Am I on pace for the quit trigger? What needs to change? |
| Vision | Quarterly | Do I still want this future? Has it changed? |
| Purpose & Principles | Yearly (or at a crisis) | Why am I doing this? Am I living by my principles? |

**Monthly business dashboard (5 numbers):** revenue, profit, # customers, savings balance (months of runway), hours spent. Track them on one page.

---

## Questions to make this map yours

Answer these and swap out the brackets:
1. What business are you thinking of, and how sure are you of the idea (just an idea / tested / already making money)?
2. What's your monthly family "must-pay" spending? That sets the revenue target and the size of the emergency fund.
3. Who depends on your income, and who needs to agree to the plan?
4. How many hours a week can you really give it, after work and family?
5. Does your employment contract have a non-compete, IP, or moonlighting clause?
6. What would make you decide *not* to quit, even at month 24?
