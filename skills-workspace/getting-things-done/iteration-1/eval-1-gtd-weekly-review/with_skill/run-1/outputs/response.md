# The GTD Weekly Review (David Allen, *Getting Things Done*, revised edition, 2015)

In the revised edition, the Weekly Review is part of **Reflect**, the fourth of the five steps (capture, clarify, organize, reflect, engage). Allen describes it as the practice that keeps your system trustworthy. It has **three phases and eleven steps**, in this order:

## 1. Get Clear
1. **Collect loose papers and materials.** Gather every receipt, business card, scrap note and stray document, and put them in your in-tray.
2. **Get "in" to zero.** Process every item in your in-trays, inboxes, notes and voicemail. Clarify each one (What is it? Is it actionable? What's the next action?) and put the result where it belongs: done (if under two minutes), delegated, deferred, filed, incubated or trashed.
3. **Empty your head.** Do a mind sweep. Write down any new projects, actions, waiting-fors and someday/maybes that haven't been captured yet, then clarify them.

## 2. Get Current
4. **Review action lists.** Go through your Next Actions lists. Mark off finished actions and look for reminders of further actions to capture.
5. **Review previous calendar data.** Look at the past couple of weeks for leftover actions, follow-ups and reference information, and move them into the system.
6. **Review upcoming calendar.** Look at the weeks ahead and capture actions about arrangements and preparation for upcoming events.
7. **Review Waiting For list.** Record follow-up actions that are needed and check off anything you've received.
8. **Review project (and larger outcome) lists.** Check the status of each project, goal and outcome one at a time. Make sure each active project has at least one current next action on your lists. Look through project plans and support material for new actions, deadlines, waiting-fors and so on.
9. **Review any relevant checklists.** Use them as triggers for anything you haven't done but should.

## 3. Get Creative
10. **Review Someday/Maybe list.** Move any projects that have become active onto your Projects list, and delete items that no longer interest you.
11. **Be creative and courageous.** Add any new, wonderful, harebrained, creative, thought-provoking or risk-taking ideas to your system.

## Practical notes
- **Timing:** block out the same time each week, often about an hour or two. Many people choose Friday afternoon so they can close the week and leave it behind. (The hour-or-two figure is a common guideline, not a fixed rule.)
- **Why it matters:** the review is what keeps your lists complete and current. When it happens regularly, you can trust your lists and keep a "mind like water" instead of using your head to track open loops.
- **Higher horizons:** the Weekly Review mainly covers the Ground (actions) and Horizon 1 (projects). Allen suggests reviewing Horizons 2–5 (areas of focus, goals, vision, purpose) less often, for example monthly, quarterly or yearly.

*Source: the phase and step names (Get Clear, Get Current, Get Creative, and the eleven items under them) follow the revised edition's Weekly Review checklist. The short descriptions for each step paraphrase the book's guidance and are not word-for-word quotes.*
