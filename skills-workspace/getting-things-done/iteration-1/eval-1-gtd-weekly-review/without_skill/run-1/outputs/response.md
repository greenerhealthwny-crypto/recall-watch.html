# The GTD Weekly Review (revised edition, 2015)

In the revised *Getting Things Done: The Art of Stress-Free Productivity* (2015), David Allen splits the Weekly Review into three phases: **Get Clear, Get Current, Get Creative**. It has 11 steps in all.

## 1. Get Clear

1. **Collect loose papers and materials.** Gather every receipt, business card, scrap of paper and other physical item that has piled up, and put it all in your in-tray.
2. **Get "In" to empty.** Process everything you collected and all your inboxes: notes, email, voicemail, meeting notes and so on. Decide what each item is and what the next action is, then put the results in your system. Do not leave anything in the inbox.
3. **Empty your head.** Write down any new projects, actions, waiting-fors, someday/maybes and other open loops that you have not captured yet, and process them too.

## 2. Get Current

4. **Review Next Actions lists.** Mark off completed actions. Look for reminders of further actions you need to capture.
5. **Review previous calendar data.** Look back over the past two or three weeks of your calendar for leftover action items, reference information and other loose ends. Move anything still relevant into your active system.
6. **Review upcoming calendar.** Look at the coming weeks for events you need to prepare for, and capture the actions they need, such as arrangements or preparation.
7. **Review Waiting For list.** Record any follow-up actions you need to take. Check off items you have received.
8. **Review Project (and larger outcome) lists.** Go through each project, goal and outcome one at a time. Make sure each one has at least one current next action in your system. Look through any project plans and support materials for new actions, completions and waiting-fors.
9. **Review any relevant checklists.** Check whether there is anything you have not done that you should be doing, for example in your areas of responsibility or your routine checklists.

## 3. Get Creative

10. **Review Someday/Maybe list.** Move projects that have become active onto your Projects list. Delete anything that no longer interests you.
11. **Be creative and courageous.** Add any new, wonderful, harebrained, creative, thought-provoking or risk-taking ideas to your system.

---

**Note:** The original 2001 edition had a slightly different and longer list. It included a separate step to review "Pending" and support files, and it put the steps in a different order. The three-phase structure above comes from the revised edition and from the David Allen Company's published Weekly Review checklist. Allen recommends doing the review once a week and setting aside about one to two hours for it. He often suggests Friday afternoon.
