# What *The Ministry of Healing* says about gardening and outdoor work

Here are the main passages, quoted word for word with page numbers. Page numbers follow the original 1905 edition. That is also the standard pagination used in Ellen White citations, so "MH 265" means *The Ministry of Healing*, page 265. I only fixed obvious scan errors (for example, "pleasT ant" became "pleasant"). The book is in the public domain, so you can quote it freely on your handout.

---

## 1. The key passage: "nothing better than the cultivation of the soil"
**Chapter: "In Contact with Nature," MH 264–265**

> "Plans should be devised for keeping patients out-of-doors. For those who are able to work, let some pleasant, easy employment be provided. Show them how agreeable and helpful this outdoor work is. Encourage them to breathe the fresh air." (MH 264)

> "Exercise in the open air should be prescribed as a life-giving necessity. And for such exercises there is nothing better than the cultivation of the soil. Let patients have flower beds to care for, or work to do in the orchard or vegetable garden. As they are encouraged to leave their rooms and spend time in the open air, cultivating flowers or doing some other light, pleasant work, their attention will be diverted from themselves and their sufferings." (MH 265)

> "Surround him with the beautiful things of nature; place him where he can see the flowers growing and hear the birds singing, and his heart will break into song in harmony with the songs of the birds. Relief will come to body and mind." (MH 265)

## 2. God's first gift was a garden, and the work in it brought health
**Chapter: "In Contact with Nature," MH 261–262**

> "God appointed them their work in the garden, 'to dress it and to keep it.' Each day's labor brought them health and gladness, and the happy pair greeted with joy the visits of their Creator, as in the cool of the day He walked and talked with them." (MH 261; quoting Genesis 2:15)

> "The plan of life which God appointed for our first parents has lessons for us. Although sin has cast its shadow over the earth, God desires His children to find delight in the works of His hands." (MH 261–262)

## 3. Nature as healer
**Chapter: "In Contact with Nature," MH 263–264**

> "Nature is God's physician. The pure air, the glad sunshine, the flowers and trees, the orchards and vineyards, and outdoor exercise amid these surroundings, are health-giving, life-giving. Physicians and nurses should encourage their patients to be much in the open air." (MH 263–264)

## 4. Gardens as a place to meet God
**Chapter: "In Contact with Nature," MH 266**

> "He who cares for the birds and the flowers will care for the beings formed in His own image. Out-of-doors, amid the things that God has made, breathing the fresh, health-giving air, the sick can best be told of the new life in Christ." (MH 266)

## 5. Outdoor work as exercise
**Chapter: "The Use of Remedies," MH 238–240**

> "But labor, to be of the greatest advantage, should be systematic and agreeable. Outdoor exercise is the best; it should be so planned as to strengthen by use the organs that have become weakened; and the heart should be in it; the labor of the hands should never degenerate into mere drudgery." (MH 238–239)

> "Light employment in useful labor, while it does not tax mind or body, has a happy influence upon both. It strengthens the muscles, improves the circulation, and gives the invalid the satisfaction of knowing that he is not wholly useless in this busy world." (MH 240)

> "Those whose habits are sedentary should, when the weather will permit, exercise in the open air every day, summer or winter." (MH 240)

## 6. Teaching others to grow food (a good fit for a garden *ministry*)
**Chapter: "Help for the Unemployed and the Homeless," MH 189–200**

> "The tilling of the soil, the employment that God appointed to man in Eden, opens a field in which there is opportunity for multitudes to gain a subsistence." (MH 189)

> "Christian farmers can do real missionary work in helping the poor to find homes on the land, and in teaching them how to till the soil and make it productive. Teach them how to use the implements of agriculture, how to cultivate various crops, how to plant and care for orchards." (MH 193)

> "Keep up the culture of your own land. Drop a word to your neighbors when you can, and let the harvest be eloquent in favor of right methods. Demonstrate what can be done with the land when properly worked." (MH 193)

> "Even the desert places of the earth, where the outlook appears to be forbidding, may become as the garden of God." (MH 194)

> "He who taught Adam and Eve in Eden how to tend the garden, desires to instruct men to-day. There is wisdom for him who drives the plow and sows the seed." (MH 200)

> "We should work cheerfully, hopefully, gratefully, believing that the earth holds in her bosom rich treasures for the faithful worker to garner, stores richer than gold or silver." (MH 200)

## 7. Plants around the home
**Chapter: "Choice and Preparation of the Home," MH 370**

> "God loves the beautiful. He has clothed the earth and the heavens with beauty, and with a Father's joy He watches the delight of His children in the things that He has made. He desires us to surround our homes with the beauty of natural things." (MH 370)

## 8. What comes out of the garden
**Chapter: "Diet and Health," MH 296**

> "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator. These foods, prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible, are the most healthful and nourishing." (MH 296)

## How to cite
- **Short form (common in Adventist materials):** *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 265, or simply **MH 265**.
- **Full reference:** White, Ellen G. *The Ministry of Healing*. 1905. Page numbers follow the original edition, which is also the standard pagination used in later printings and on EGW Writings online.

Before you print, check each quote against your own copy or EGW Writings (egwwritings.org). These quotes come from a scanned copy of the 1905 edition, so a quick look will catch any scan error I missed.

## Ideas for the handout
- **Headline quote:** "There is nothing better than the cultivation of the soil" (MH 265) is the strongest single line.
- **Ministry angle:** MH 193 ("let the harvest be eloquent… Demonstrate what can be done with the land") fits a community garden that shares produce or teaches neighbors.
- **Practical note:** White describes "light, pleasant work" (MH 265) and work that is "systematic and agreeable" (MH 239). You could pair that with a reminder to pace yourself, drink water, use sun protection, and talk to your doctor before starting heavy work if you have a health condition.
