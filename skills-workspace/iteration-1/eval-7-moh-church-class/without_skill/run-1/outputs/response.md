# "God's Prescription for Health": A 4-Week Class Based on *The Ministry of Healing*

**Audience:** Adults in their 50s and 60s, including several with type 2 diabetes
**Format:** 4 weekly sessions, about 90 minutes each
**Core text:** Ellen G. White, *The Ministry of Healing* (1905), mainly the chapters "General Hygiene," "Hygiene Among the Israelites," "Diet and Health," "Flesh as Food," "Extremes in Diet," "Mind Cure," and "In Contact With Nature"

> **Theme text for the series:** "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power—these are the true remedies."
> — *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 127

---

## Before You Begin: Ground Rules for a Class That Includes People With Diabetes

Put these on the first handout and repeat them out loud. They let the class encourage real change without anyone getting hurt.

1. **The natural remedies work alongside medical care, not instead of it.** Ellen White herself told people to cooperate with physicians and use sensible remedies. Nobody should stop or change diabetes medication, insulin, or blood-pressure medication without talking to their doctor.
2. **Diet and exercise changes can lower blood sugar quickly.** That is good news, but anyone on insulin or a sulfonylurea (glipizide, glyburide, glimepiride) can develop **hypoglycemia** as they eat better and walk more. Ask these members to check their glucose more often during the series and to tell their doctor they are changing their lifestyle. Their medication may need to come down.
3. **Keep a "low kit" in the room:** glucose tablets or juice boxes, plus a glucometer if a nurse or health professional can be there.
4. **Make no promises of cure.** Talk about "improved control," "less medication for some people," and "possible remission for some," and avoid claims of guaranteed healing.
5. **Invite a health professional** (nurse, dietitian, or physician from the congregation) to co-teach or review the material if you can.

---

## Standard Session Flow (about 90 minutes)

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 10 min | Welcome, prayer, check-in on last week's challenge |
| 10 min | Devotional reading: the week's *Ministry of Healing* quote plus a Bible text |
| 30 min | Main teaching: White's principle, then what current research says, then how it applies to diabetes |
| 20 min | Hands-on segment: food demo, short walk, water tasting, and so on |
| 15 min | Small-group discussion |
| 5 min | Weekly challenge and closing prayer |

**Optional measurements:** Offer voluntary weight, waist, and blood-pressure checks in Week 1 and Week 4. People with diabetes can bring their own fasting glucose logs if they want to. Keep all of it private and optional.

## Week 1: "The True Remedies" (God's Design for Healing)

**Focus:** Introduce all eight natural remedies, with a special emphasis on **trust in divine power**.

**Quote of the week:**
> "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power—these are the true remedies. Every person should have a knowledge of nature's remedial agencies and how to apply them."
> — *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 127

**Bible text:** 3 John 2; 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

**Teaching points**
- Ellen White saw health as part of redemption. Christ "went about healing," and caring for the body is a way of worshipping God.
- Go through the eight remedies one by one. Many people know the acronym NEWSTART (Nutrition, Exercise, Water, Sunlight, Temperance, Air, Rest, Trust).
- **The diabetes connection:** Type 2 diabetes is largely a lifestyle-responsive disease. Large trials such as the Diabetes Prevention Program found that diet changes plus about 150 minutes of walking a week cut progression to diabetes by roughly 58%, and by even more in adults over 60. The habits White described in 1905 are the habits doctors recommend today.
- Present trust in God as a remedy in its own right. It is not a last resort after everything else fails.

**Hands-on segment:** "Health self-inventory." Each person rates themselves from 1 to 5 on each of the eight remedies and circles the two they most want to improve.

**Discussion questions**
1. Which of the eight remedies comes easiest to you? Which is hardest?
2. Why do you think White put "trust in divine power" in the same list as water and sunlight?
3. What has your health journey taught you about dependence on God?

**Weekly challenge:** Pick **one** small change from your weakest area and do it every day this week. Pray each morning for strength to keep it.

## Week 2: "As Simple and Natural as Possible" (Proper Diet and Temperance)

**Focus:** A plant-forward, whole-food diet; regular meals; and temperance, all applied to blood-sugar control.

**Quote of the week:**
> "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator. These foods, prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible, are the most healthful and nourishing."
> — *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 296

**Bible text:** Genesis 1:29; Daniel 1:8–16

**Teaching points**
- **Whole foods versus refined foods:** White warned repeatedly against rich, highly refined, and sugary foods. Whole grains, beans, vegetables, and nuts digest slowly and raise blood sugar more gently than white flour and sweets.
- **Regular mealtimes:** White taught regular meals, no snacking between them, and a lighter evening meal. For people with diabetes, regular timing makes glucose more predictable. **Caution:** anyone on insulin or a sulfonylurea should not skip meals or take up a two-meal pattern without medical guidance.
- **Temperance:** White defined it as moderation in good things and avoiding harmful things entirely. Here that means sugar, sugary drinks, alcohol, and excess fat.
- **Avoid extremes:** In the chapter "Extremes in Diet," White warns against diets so restrictive that people become undernourished. That fits this audience well. The goal is balance, not starving yourself.
- **Diabetes specifics:**
  - Beans and lentils are an excellent way to control blood sugar.
  - Fruit is part of God's original diet, but people with diabetes should eat **whole** fruit in sensible portions with meals rather than drinking juice.
  - Use the "plate method": half non-starchy vegetables, one quarter whole grain or starchy vegetable, one quarter beans, lentils, tofu, or nuts.
  - Read labels for added sugars.

**Hands-on segment:** Cook and taste. Good options:
- A lentil-vegetable soup with whole-grain bread
- Steel-cut oatmeal with berries and walnuts, compared with a sweetened instant packet (talk about how fiber slows the glucose rise)
- A big colorful salad with chickpeas and a nut-based dressing

**Discussion questions**
1. Which foods would be hardest for you to cut back on? Why?
2. How can we be temperate without becoming legalistic or judgmental about food?
3. How can church potlucks better support members who are managing diabetes?

**Weekly challenge:** Eat at regular times every day. Replace one refined food (white bread, sugary cereal, or soda) with a whole-food alternative, and add one serving of beans or lentils every day.

## Week 3: "Heaven's Choicest Blessings" (Water, Exercise, Fresh Air, Sunlight)

**Focus:** The "outdoor" remedies and how they help blood sugar, the heart, and mood.

**Quote of the week:**
> "In health and in sickness, pure water is one of heaven's choicest blessings."
> — *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 237

**Bible text:** Isaiah 40:31; Psalm 19:1–6

**Teaching points**
- **Water:** White recommended drinking water freely and using it to bathe. Drinking water in place of sweetened drinks is one of the simplest ways to bring down glucose and weight. **Caution:** members with heart failure or kidney disease may be on fluid limits and should follow their doctor's guidance.
- **Exercise:** White called useful physical activity essential and especially commended walking in the open air. Working muscles take sugar out of the blood even without insulin, so a **10–15 minute walk after meals** noticeably lowers the glucose rise after eating. Aim to build toward about 150 minutes a week and add light strength work (chair squats, wall push-ups) twice a week.
- **Fresh air and nature:** In "In Contact With Nature," White describes how the outdoors restores body and mind. Walking outside combines exercise, fresh air, and sunlight in one.
- **Sunlight:** Moderate sun exposure supports vitamin D and mood. Use sun sense: avoid burning and protect your skin.
- **Diabetes safety:**
  - Wear proper shoes and check your feet daily, because neuropathy can hide blisters.
  - Carry a glucose source on walks if you take insulin or a sulfonylurea.
  - Get a doctor's clearance before vigorous exercise if you have heart disease, retinopathy, or severe neuropathy.

**Hands-on segment:** Take a 15-minute group walk outside, or around the fellowship hall if the weather is bad, right after a light snack. Anyone who wants to can check their glucose before and after. Then serve water infused with cucumber, lemon, or mint as an alternative to soda.

**Discussion questions**
1. What keeps you from moving more: pain, time, motivation, or weather? What could help?
2. Where do you feel closest to God in nature?
3. Who could be your walking partner this week?

**Weekly challenge:** Walk 10–15 minutes after at least one meal every day. Drink a glass of water first thing each morning and replace sweetened drinks with water.

## Week 4: "A Merry Heart" (Rest, Peace of Mind, and Trust in God)

**Focus:** Sleep, the Sabbath, stress, gratitude, and how a peaceful mind affects physical health, including blood sugar.

**Quote of the week:**
> "Courage, hope, faith, sympathy, love, promote health and prolong life. A contented mind, a cheerful spirit, is health to the body and strength to the soul."
> — *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 241

**Bible text:** Proverbs 17:22; Matthew 11:28–30; Philippians 4:6–7

**Teaching points**
- **Mind-body connection:** In "Mind Cure," White wrote that grief, anxiety, discontent, and guilt break down the life forces. Modern research agrees: chronic stress raises cortisol, and cortisol raises blood sugar.
- **Rest:** Poor or short sleep worsens insulin resistance and increases appetite. Encourage a regular bedtime and a light, early supper, which White also advised. Snoring and daytime sleepiness can point to sleep apnea, which is common alongside type 2 diabetes and worth raising with a doctor.
- **Sabbath rest:** Think of the Sabbath as a weekly "reset" for body and soul.
- **Gratitude and service:** White taught that thankfulness and helping others bring healing. Feeling lonely or discouraged makes it harder to keep up health habits, so the church family itself can be a remedy.
- **Trust in divine power, revisited:** Finish the series where it began. Lasting change comes by God's grace, not by willpower alone.

**Hands-on segment**
- Write a gratitude list and share a few items.
- Lead a short guided breathing and Scripture meditation using Matthew 11:28.
- Have each person write a personal "Health Covenant" with God listing 2–3 habits to keep going.
- Optional: repeat the Week 1 measurements and celebrate progress of every kind, including more energy, better sleep, and lower readings.

**Discussion questions**
1. What worries do you most need to bring to God?
2. How has this class changed how you think about health and faith?
3. How can we keep encouraging one another after the series ends?

**Weekly challenge (ongoing):** Keep your Health Covenant. Go to bed at a consistent time, write down three things you are grateful for each day, and check in with a class partner every week.

---

## After the Series

- **Start a monthly follow-up group** for potlucks, walks, and sharing. Long-term success depends on support.
- **Offer a health-focused potluck** where every dish follows the Week 2 principles, with labels for diabetes-friendly items.
- **Point people to further resources:** a diabetes self-management education (DSMES) program through a local hospital, a registered dietitian, or a structured lifestyle program such as CHIP or a church-run NEWSTART series.
- **Ask members to share their results with their doctors.** Several may qualify for medication adjustments, which is a powerful testimony.
