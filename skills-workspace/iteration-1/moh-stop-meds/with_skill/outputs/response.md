Your aunt is right that *The Ministry of Healing* is very critical of "drugs" and puts natural remedies first. But reading it as "stop your blood pressure pills" mistakes what Ellen White was writing about. It could also put your aunt in real danger. Here is what the book says and how to read it.

## What the book says

**1. She criticized the "drugs" of her own time.** In the chapter "The Physician an Educator" (*MH* ~126), White warns against the "free use of poisonous drugs" and the patent nostrums of her day. People took these to cover up symptoms while the cause of the illness went untreated.

**2. Her main point is to find the cause and help the body heal.** The best-known passage is on *MH* 127:

> "In case of sickness, the cause should be ascertained. Unhealthful conditions should be changed, wrong habits corrected. Then nature is to be assisted in her effort to expel impurities and to reestablish right conditions in the system."

> "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power,—these are the true remedies. Every person should have a knowledge of nature's remedial agencies and how to apply them."

These are the "eight natural remedies." Adventist lifestyle programs such as NEWSTART are built on them.

**3. She did not set prayer and natural means against using means at all.** In "Prayer for the Sick" she says Christ "made use of the simple agencies of nature" and "sanctioned the use of simple and natural remedies" (*MH* 225 ff.). The theme is working *with* the body's healing, not refusing treatment as a matter of faith.

**4. She warned against extremes.** In other chapters she criticizes health "reformers" whose narrow, extreme ideas hurt people and "have been a great injury to the cause of hygiene" (*MH* 318–320). Her consistent message is moderation and good sense.

## What "drugs" meant in 1905

When White wrote, the "drugs" doctors and patent-medicine sellers handed out often included mercury compounds (calomel), opium, strychnine, arsenic, and tonics that were mostly alcohol. Many of them were poisons. They did more harm than good, and her criticism of them was largely justified. Medicine in her day had no blood pressure medications at all. The first effective ones appeared about 50 years after the book was published.

Modern blood pressure medicines are a completely different kind of thing. They have been tested in large trials and greatly reduce the risk of stroke, heart attack, kidney failure and heart failure. Nothing in *The Ministry of Healing* addresses them, because they did not exist yet. Adventist hospitals and doctors, including Loma Linda University Health, prescribe these medicines every day while also teaching lifestyle medicine.

## Why stopping on her own is dangerous

- **High blood pressure usually has no symptoms.** She may feel fine while her pressure climbs. That is why it is called the "silent killer."
- **Stopping some medicines suddenly is risky in its own right.** Some blood pressure drugs, especially beta-blockers and clonidine, can cause a rebound spike in blood pressure or heart symptoms if they are stopped abruptly.
- **The main danger is stroke or heart attack.** These can happen suddenly and cannot always be undone.

## The good news: the book and her doctor can work together

White's natural remedies really do help lower blood pressure, and modern research backs many of them up:

| Natural remedy (*MH* 127) | What helps blood pressure today |
|---|---|
| Proper diet | Plant-based eating rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, beans and nuts (very close to the DASH diet), and **less salt** |
| Exercise | About 30 minutes of brisk walking on most days |
| Abstemiousness / temperance | Avoiding alcohol and tobacco; reaching a healthy weight |
| Rest | Getting enough sleep, and having sleep apnea checked if she snores |
| Trust in divine power, gratitude | Lowering stress through prayer and peace of mind (*MH* 241, 251 on how the mind affects the body) |
| Fresh air, sunlight, water | Time outdoors, gardening, staying well hydrated |

For some people, these changes lower blood pressure enough that **their doctor can safely reduce the dose or even stop a medicine.** That is a realistic goal she can work toward, but the doctor has to guide it.

## A practical way forward for your aunt

1. **Keep taking the pills for now.** Starting the natural remedies does not require giving up the medicine.
2. **Tell her doctor** she wants to follow the eight natural remedies and would like to need less medication over time. Many doctors will gladly support this.
3. **Get a home blood pressure monitor** and keep a log, so she and her doctor can see whether the lifestyle changes are working.
4. **Find a lifestyle program.** Many Adventist churches and hospitals offer CHIP (Complete Health Improvement Program), NEWSTART or cooking classes.
5. **Let the doctor lower or stop the medicine**, step by step, if her readings stay in a healthy range.

A gentle way to put it to her: White told readers to find the cause, correct wrong habits, and *assist* nature. Taking a medicine that keeps her blood pressure safe while she builds those healthier habits follows that advice. Her own warnings against extremes support taking it one careful step at a time.

*This is general information, not medical advice. Please encourage your aunt to talk with her doctor or pharmacist before changing any medication.*
