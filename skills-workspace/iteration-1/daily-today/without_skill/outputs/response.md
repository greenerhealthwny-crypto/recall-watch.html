## October 4: The Emotional Self

In Robert Greene's *The Daily Laws*, October is about **the Emotional Self**: seeing how your feelings take over your thinking, and learning to put some distance between what you feel and what you do. I can't reliably recall the exact title and wording of the October 4 entry, so I won't make up a quote. Here is the law from that month that fits your situation best, put in my own words:

> **Today's Law: Don't react while you're still angry. Wait before you answer.**
> When something gets under your skin, the first thing you want to do is the worst thing you could do. Notice the heat, name it, and don't act until it has passed.

### Why this hits home for a bakery owner

Greene's main point about emotions is that we feel first and think second. Our thinking then invents reasons for whatever we already wanted to do. A one-star review hurts because the bakery isn't just a job. It's your early mornings, your recipes, your name. So it feels like a personal attack, and your reply comes out as self-defense.

The trouble is who reads those replies. Not the reviewer, who has mostly moved on. **Your future customers** read them. When someone is checking out your shop, an angry reply tells them more than the bad review does. Greene would say you're letting a stranger set your emotional state, and through it, your reputation. That's power you hand away.

### What to do

1. **Make a 24-hour rule.** Don't reply to a negative review on the day you read it. Write the angry version somewhere private if that helps, then delete it.
2. **Name the feeling.** Before you reply, finish this sentence: "I'm feeling ___ because ___." Just naming it puts some distance between you and the anger. Usually it's hurt pride more than real injustice.
3. **Look for the useful part.** Ask: *Is any of this true?* Was the croissant dry? Was the line slow at 8 a.m.? A complaint that's 20% fair still gives you something to fix.
4. **Write the reply for future customers, not the reviewer.** Keep it short, calm and specific:
   > "Thanks for letting us know. We're sorry the [item] wasn't up to our usual standard. We bake everything fresh each morning and want every visit to be a good one. Please ask for me next time you're in. — [Your name]"
5. **Some reviews need no reply.** Obvious trolls, rants that don't match anything that happened, and reviews you have no facts to answer can be left alone or flagged with the platform.
6. **Fix the urge to fire back at its source.** Don't check reviews late at night, when you're tired, or in the middle of a rush. Pick one calm time each week to read and answer them.

### Something to think about today

*Think of the last angry reply you posted. What were you really defending, and did the reply protect it or hurt it?*

If you're calm in public while others are rattled, people will remember your bakery for that. Being calm won't feel as good as winning the argument, but it's a much better advertisement.
