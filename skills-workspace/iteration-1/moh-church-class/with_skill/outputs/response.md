# "Eight Remedies, Four Weeks": A Church Health Class from *The Ministry of Healing*

Here's a 4-week class built on Ellen White's eight natural remedies (MH 127). It's written for adults in their 50s and 60s, and it's adapted for the several class members who have type 2 diabetes. Each week covers two remedies and opens with a quote from the book. It then gives the modern evidence in plain language, one practical habit to try, and time to talk in small groups.

**The theme text for the whole series:**
> "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power,—these are the true remedies. Every person should have a knowledge of nature's remedial agencies and how to apply them." (*The Ministry of Healing*, p. 127)

---

## Before you start

- **Format:** about 75–90 minutes per week.
  - Welcome and prayer (5 min)
  - Opening reading (5 min)
  - Teaching (25 min)
  - Demo or activity (20 min)
  - Small-group discussion (15 min)
  - Weekly habit challenge and closing prayer (10 min)
- **Bring in a professional if you can.** A nurse, physician, registered dietitian or certified diabetes educator from your congregation or community makes a big difference, especially in Weeks 1 and 2.
- **Make one safety statement at the first session and repeat it on every handout:** *"This class supports your medical care. It doesn't replace it. Please don't stop or change any medication on your own. Talk with your doctor before making big changes to your diet or exercise. If you take insulin or certain diabetes pills, your doses may need adjusting as your habits improve."* White's warnings about "drugs" were aimed at the poisonous patent medicines of 1905 (mercury, opium, alcohol-based tonics). They were not about modern medications such as metformin or insulin.
- **Keep health information private.** Invite people to track their own blood sugar, steps or sleep in a personal journal. Never ask anyone to share their numbers in front of the group.
- **Use a spirit of grace.** Present the remedies as gifts, not as rules to measure people by. Type 2 diabetes comes from genetics, age and environment as well as lifestyle, so never imply that anyone is to blame for their illness.

## Week 1 — Proper Diet & Temperance: "The Diet Chosen for Us"

**Opening quote:**
> "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator. These foods, prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible, are the most healthful and nourishing." (MH 296)

**Key ideas**
- **White's message:** eat simple, whole plant foods. Eat regularly and in moderation, and don't overeat. "Regularity in eating is of vital importance. There should be a specified time for each meal." (MH 303)
- **Temperance (abstemiousness)** means two things: avoiding what harms you (tobacco, alcohol) and using good things in moderation.
- **Avoid extremes.** White warned that "reformers" who eat a meager diet with little variety damage their own health and discredit the cause (MH 318–319). Healthy food should be satisfying and taste good.

**The modern evidence, with a diabetes focus**
- Eating patterns built mostly on whole plant foods (beans, lentils, whole grains, vegetables, fruit and nuts) are linked to better blood sugar control, weight and heart health. Both diabetes guidelines and the Adventist Health Studies support this.
- **Fiber is your friend.** Beans and intact whole grains raise blood sugar more slowly than white bread, white rice or sugary cereals.
- **Teach the "plate method":** fill half the plate with non-starchy vegetables, a quarter with beans, lentils or tofu, and a quarter with whole grains or starchy vegetables. Add a small serving of fruit and a few nuts.
- **Regular meal times** help keep blood sugar steady, and many people with diabetes find that grazing between meals makes control harder. However, **don't recommend White's "two meals a day" (MH 304) to people with diabetes.** Long gaps between meals can cause dangerous low blood sugar for anyone on insulin or sulfonylureas (for example, glipizide or glyburide). Meal timing should be planned with their clinician.
- **Watch for B12.** People on a mostly plant-based diet need a reliable source of vitamin B12, from fortified foods or a supplement. B12 matters even more for people taking metformin, which can lower B12 levels. Suggest they ask their doctor about checking it.

**Demo:** Make a simple bean-and-vegetable soup or a lentil and brown-rice bowl. Show how to read a nutrition label for fiber and added sugar.

**Discussion**
1. What does "prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible" look like in a busy kitchen today?
2. Why do you think White warned against extreme diets as strongly as against overeating?

**Habit for the week:** Eat meals at regular times and add one serving of beans or lentils each day.

## Week 2 — Exercise, Pure Air & Sunlight: "Action Is a Law of Our Being"

**Opening quote:**
> "Inactivity is a fruitful cause of disease. Exercise quickens and equalizes the circulation of the blood." (MH 238)

**Key ideas**
- White taught that "action is a law of our being" (MH 237). She called outdoor exercise, especially gardening, "a life-giving necessity" (MH 265).
- **Pure air:** "The lungs are constantly throwing off impurities, and they need to be constantly supplied with fresh air" (MH 272). Air out your home, breathe deeply and spend time outdoors.
- **Sunlight:** let it into your home and spend time outside.

**The modern evidence, with a diabetes focus**
- Movement is one of the most powerful tools for type 2 diabetes. Working muscles take up glucose even without much insulin.
- **A 10–15 minute walk after meals** noticeably lowers the rise in blood sugar after eating. This is an easy, encouraging habit for this age group.
- Build toward about **150 minutes a week** of moderate activity, such as brisk walking. Add light strength or balance work twice a week (chair exercises or resistance bands) to protect muscle and help prevent falls.
- **Safety for people with diabetes:**
  - Get medical clearance before starting a new exercise program.
  - Anyone on insulin or sulfonylureas should carry glucose tablets or juice, since exercise can cause low blood sugar.
  - Wear good, well-fitting shoes and check your feet every day, especially if you have neuropathy.
  - People with eye disease from diabetes (retinopathy) should ask their doctor before heavy lifting.
- **Sunlight:** morning daylight helps set the body clock and improves sleep, and sunlight helps the body make vitamin D. People in this age group also have higher skin-cancer risk, so encourage moderate sun and sun protection rather than long exposure.

**Activity:** Take a group walk outdoors, or around the church if the weather is bad. Do 10 minutes of chair exercises together, then close with deep breathing.

**Discussion**
1. Jesus often withdrew to the hills and the lakeside (*With Nature and with God*, MH 51). How could time outdoors be a spiritual practice for you?
2. What gets in your way when you try to move more? What small change could you make this week?

**Habit for the week:** Walk for 10 minutes after at least one meal each day, outdoors if you can.

## Week 3 — Water & Rest: "Heaven's Choicest Blessings"

**Opening quote:**
> "In health and in sickness, pure water is one of heaven's choicest blessings." (MH 237)

**Key ideas**
- White encouraged drinking water freely and using it outwardly, through bathing and simple water treatments (hydrotherapy).
- **Rest:** "Some make themselves sick by overwork. For these, rest, freedom from care, and a spare diet, are essential to restoration of health." (MH 236)

**The modern evidence, with a diabetes focus**
- **Swap sugary drinks for water.** Replacing soda, sweet tea, juice and specialty coffees with water is one of the simplest steps toward better blood sugar. High blood sugar also pulls fluid from the body, so drinking enough water matters.
- **Be careful with water treatments.** Warm and contrast baths can be soothing. But people with diabetic neuropathy may not feel heat on their feet, and burns are a real danger. **No hot foot soaks.** Always check water temperature with a thermometer or your elbow, never your feet. People with heart conditions should avoid extreme temperatures.
- **Sleep and blood sugar:**
  - Short or poor sleep raises insulin resistance and increases cravings.
  - Aim for 7–8 hours, with a regular bedtime and a wind-down routine.
  - Loud snoring or daytime sleepiness may point to sleep apnea, which is common with type 2 diabetes and treatable. Encourage people to mention it to their doctor.
- **The Sabbath as a weekly rest:** a gift of time to rest and refocus that is built into the week.

**Demo:** Make a "flavored water" station with cucumber, mint, citrus slices or berries. Hand out a simple sleep-routine worksheet.

**Discussion**
1. Do you rest well, or do you "make yourself sick by overwork"? What would "freedom from care" look like this week?
2. How can the Sabbath be a time of real physical and emotional renewal, not just a busy day at church?

**Habit for the week:** Replace one sugary drink a day with water, and set a consistent bedtime.

## Week 4 — Trust in Divine Power: "Gratitude and Praise"

**Opening quote:**
> "Nothing tends more to promote health of body and of soul than does a spirit of gratitude and praise. It is a positive duty to resist melancholy, discontented thoughts and feelings." (MH 251)

**Key ideas**
- **Mind and body are linked.** White wrote: "The relation that exists between the mind and the body is very intimate... Grief, anxiety, discontent, remorse, guilt, distrust, all tend to break down the life forces." (MH 241)
- **Prayer and natural remedies work together.** White wrote that Christ "sanctioned the use of simple and natural remedies" (MH 233). Trusting God and caring for our bodies, including with medical care, go hand in hand.

**The modern evidence, with a diabetes focus**
- Chronic stress raises stress hormones such as cortisol, and those raise blood sugar.
- **"Diabetes distress"** (the burnout of managing a chronic condition every day) is very common, and depression is more common in people with diabetes. Name it gently. Point people to their doctor, a counselor or a diabetes support group when needed. Faith and professional help aren't in competition.
- Gratitude practices, social support, purpose and belonging to a faith community are all linked to better health and longer life. The Adventist community in Loma Linda is one of the well-known "Blue Zones."

**Activity**
- Start a gratitude journal together.
- Sing a hymn of praise. White encouraged singing praises.
- Pair class members as "health partners" who will check in on each other weekly after the series ends. This puts into practice the book's call to minister to one another.

**Discussion**
1. Which emotion from White's list (grief, anxiety, discontent, guilt, distrust) weighs on you most? How might gratitude change it?
2. How can our church keep supporting each other's health after this class ends?

**Habit for the week (and beyond):** Write down three things you're thankful for each evening, and pray for your health partner.

**Closing celebration:** Share a potluck of recipes from Week 1, and have each person share one habit they plan to keep. Repeat the theme text from MH 127 together.

## Handout template (one page per week)
1. The week's quote with its MH page number
2. A plain-language explanation (2–3 sentences)
3. The modern evidence (2–3 bullet points)
4. One simple step to take this week
5. A diabetes safety tip
6. This footer: *"This class supports your medical care. It doesn't replace it. Talk with your health-care provider before making major changes to your diet, exercise or medication."*

## A few notes for the class leader
- **Some content is White's 1905 view rather than current science.** Present it as her conviction, not as settled fact. Examples: two meals a day, avoiding tea and coffee, and rules about food temperature. Current research on moderate coffee and tea is mostly neutral to favorable.
- **As people's habits improve, their blood sugar may drop.** That's good news, but it may mean medication doses need lowering. Remind the class to keep their doctors informed and to monitor as they've been told to.
- **For extra depth,** pair each week with a Scripture passage, for example:
  - Week 1: Daniel 1
  - Week 2: Mark 6:31
  - Week 3: John 4:13–14
  - Week 4: Proverbs 17:22
