# 7 Days in the Court: A Daily Laws Plan for Your First Week at the Hospital

Most of this plan comes from **April ("The Perfect Courtier")** and **May ("The Supposed Nonplayers of Power")** in *The Daily Laws*. Those two months cover office politics: how power actually moves through a workplace, and how to spot people who are playing games. I skipped the most manipulative entries on purpose. Your aim is to **see the game clearly without becoming a player you'd be ashamed of.** Greene's own point supports that: people who pretend there is no game get eaten, and so do people who play it cynically. The defensible ground is awareness plus competence.

**How to use it:** read the day's title in the morning, do the one action, and spend 3 minutes in the evening on the journal question. Keep notes in a private notebook. Don't keep them on a work device.

---

### Day 1: *Find Out Who Holds the Strings* (April 3)
Org charts in hospitals are mostly fiction. The person who really decides things might be a long-tenured admin assistant, the charge nurse everyone defers to, or the scheduler who controls the OR calendar.
- **Action:** Pay attention to who people go to for answers, whose emails get replied to first, and who gets mentioned when someone says "check with ___ first." Write down three names.
- **Journal:** Who has influence here that their title doesn't show?

### Day 2: *Judge Them on Their Behavior, Not on Their Words* (May 3)
New hires get love-bombed by some people and quietly tested by others. Words cost nothing. Patterns tell you who someone is.
- **Action:** Don't form alliances this week. Be warm to everyone and committed to no one. Watch how people treat those who can't help them, like environmental services, the front desk, and patients' families.
- **Journal:** Who has said one thing and done another? Who has been consistently decent when nobody important was watching?

### Day 3: *Always Say Less Than Necessary* (April 10)
In a hospital this is good politics and also good compliance. Gossip travels fast, and patient information should never travel at all. When you talk too much as a new person, you show your hand and get pulled into feuds you don't understand yet.
- **Action:** When someone vents about a colleague or another department, listen, acknowledge it, and offer no opinion ("Sounds frustrating. I'm still figuring out how everything works."). Ask questions and hold back judgments.
- **Journal:** When today did I feel the urge to over-share or agree just to fit in?

### Day 4: *Never Outshine the Master* (April 1)
The non-snake version of this law is to **make your manager's life easier and give them credit freely.** It doesn't mean dimming your work or flattering anyone. New hires often bring "at my last job we..." ideas that sound like criticism.
- **Action:** Ask your manager: "What's the one thing that would take the most off your plate in the next month?" Then do it well. Save your improvement ideas for after you've earned trust, and frame them as building on what they've already built.
- **Journal:** How does my boss's success get measured, and how can my work show up in that measure?

### Day 5: *Stay Above the Fray* (April 17), paired with *Do Not Be the Court Cynic* (April 7)
Every hospital has old fault lines: clinical vs. administrative, one department vs. another, the people who survived the last reorg vs. the new leadership. Pick a side early and you inherit that side's enemies. Being the cynic who mocks "corporate" or the doctors also has a cost, because it marks you as someone who doesn't believe in the place.
- **Action:** Find one recurring conflict and work out each side's legitimate interest (e.g., billing wants accuracy, clinicians want time with patients). Out loud, stay neutral and constructive.
- **Journal:** Which faction am I being recruited into, and what would joining it cost me in a year?

### Day 6: *Master Your Emotional Responses* (April 8), reinforced by *Increase Your Reaction Time* (November 3)
Someone will eventually snap at you, dump a mess on your desk, or take credit for your work. Greene's point is that a reaction in the moment gives other people power over you. Pausing is a strategy.
- **Action:** Make it a rule this week to answer any email that stings only after a 2-hour delay, and to give any face-to-face jab a calm "Let me look into that and get back to you."
- **Journal:** What triggered me this week? Was it really about me, or about their pressure?

### Day 7: *So Much Depends on Reputation* (April 9), paired with *Create the Need for You* (February 21)
This is the strongest anti-snake strategy there is: **become known as reliable, discreet, and good at something hard.** The best hospital admins are the ones who understand the credentialing backlog, the prior-auth workflow, or the scheduling system. People protect those who make their days easier.
- **Action:** Pick one thing nobody wants to learn deeply (a system, a process, a regulation) and start mastering it. Then write one sentence describing the reputation you want in six months, e.g. "the person who follows through and doesn't gossip."
- **Journal:** What did people see me do this week, and does it match that sentence?

## Your guardrails ("not a snake" rules)

Greene's books describe some tactics (sowing doubt, crushing enemies, cultivating a false image) as things to *recognize*, not things to use. Some lines to hold:
- **Defend, don't attack.** Use these laws to read people and protect yourself, not to undermine anyone.
- **Discretion isn't deception.** Saying less is fine. Lying or making people think things that aren't true is not.
- **Give credit generously and accurately.** Never take credit for someone else's work. Never let your own work disappear quietly either. Document it.
- **Remember the mission.** In a hospital, office politics sits downstream of patient care. When you're unsure, the option that serves patients and colleagues honestly is almost always the strategically sound one too.

**After week one:** keep going with the rest of April, then May for reading people. For more ideas on persuasion that rely on listening rather than tricks, August is the next month to read (e.g., *Transform Yourself into a Deep Listener*, Aug 27).
